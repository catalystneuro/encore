Reproducing Kondo, Sehara, Harukuni, Aoki et al. (2025) from dandiset 001425¶

Paper: Kondo M, Sehara K, Harukuni R, Aoki R, Sugimoto S, Tanaka YR, Matsuzaki M, Nakae K. "A multimodal dataset linking wide-field calcium imaging to behavior changes in mice during an operant lever-pull task", bioRxiv, doi:10.1101/2025.02.03.631599. The claims file (claims/kondo-2025.yaml) was written from the preprint, version 1 (JATS full text with the Methods and the legends of Figures 1 to 6 and S1 to S4; the tables' contents and the figures themselves were not available to the reader).

Dataset: DANDI:001425, "BraiDyn-BC: mouse cued-lever-pull task dataset", pinned at version 0.250705.0947 (doi:10.48324/dandi.001425/0.250705.0947). The paper cites no version (it predates the dandiset). There are two published versions, 0.250514.0602 and 0.250705.0947; the later one is the most recent and the only one that holds the day-0 resting-state sessions of all 25 mice, which claim figs2b-no-aberrant-transients uses (the earlier version lacks those of six mice; the difference is shown below). So the most recent published version is pinned, as the protocol asks when the paper cites none. Every file is streamed from the archive; nothing is downloaded to disk. Loading and the per-session measures are in bdbc.py.

Claims: 22 claims. Tested: 20. Blocked before analysis: 2 (data missing).

Revised after the first verification round: the raw blue fluorescence is now sampled from 16 blocks of 10 frames and checked against every frame of four sessions; the data check tests the excitation wavelengths against the data and lists the processing groups, the lever's sampling interval, zero T_pull values and the sessions with many extra tone TTLs; the sensory maps add a reading of each TTL channel over only the sessions where its name fits; the auditory map gets a chance level; the movement onsets get a synthetic test of the rule, a sustained-crossing variant and the grand traces; and fig3e-cluster-b-similar-rates is blocked, as its criterion says when the readings of "similar" differ. Revised after the second round: the data check states that the pull response separates the two dF/F series in three of the four sessions read frame by frame; the searches of the linked repositories, the Google Drive folder and the lab data platform for the manual annotations are run in the notebook; the forepaw session count is printed without the two sessions lacking face video; and some statements are corrected to the printed numbers. The outcome of each is in the summary at the end and in the attempt record.

Checks of the description against the data, as the outputs above show them:

  • The files hold 364 task, 98 resting-state and 33 sensory-mapping sessions of 25 subjects; each subject has one sex and one date of birth; the session date in each file name matches its session_start_time. No two files share their first 600 frames of dF/F, so no recording was found deposited twice.
  • The 30 Hz series have the frame interval of 30 Hz, and the raw blue and violet stacks have the length of the dF/F in every file. Each file has the same 44 ROIs (22 per hemisphere).
  • The archive holds only NWB files and MP4 videos (the asset count by file extension). Every file's processing/ophys holds the dF/F series (dFF, dFF_B, dFF_V) and the ROI masks, and no raw ROI fluorescence series; its analysis group holds only atlas_to_data_transform.
  • In every file ImagingPlane_blue states an excitation wavelength of 405 nm and ImagingPlane_UV 470 nm, while their own descriptions (and the Methods) say the reverse. The data are consistent with the names and descriptions, not with excitation_lambda, in the four sessions read frame by frame (the table after the clock check): the stack named blue is about three times as bright as the one named UV, its ROI means follow dFF_B (median r 0.72 to 0.82), the frame-to-frame changes of the UV stack follow dFF_V (median r 0.43 to 0.48). The data are taken by their names and descriptions (widefield_blue, dFF_B "473 nm excitation").
  • The lever and its timestamps are sampled every 0.2 ms (5 kHz) in every task session, as the analysis assumes. The lever is stored with unit "a.u."; its range (about -0.4 to 4.3) fits the 4 mm travel of the Methods and the unit mm that the paper's code gives (bdbc-nwb-explorer, data/daq.csv).
  • The trial table's pull_onset follows the first lever sample above 1 mm by about 14 ms in every session (the online threshold), and reaction_time is pull_onset - start_time. The raw tone TTL rises 2 to 18 ms after start_time (median per session). Most sessions have a tone TTL or two more than trials, and nine have more than 10 more (up to 145, VG1-GC#78 day 4; listed). The down-sampled tone series rises once per trial in these sessions too, so the trial table is used as the count of trials.
  • The trial tables give T_pull values of 0.0 s (in 10783 of 48549 trials, 157 sessions), where the Methods give a start of 1 ms; T_pull is used as stored, for the clustering only.
  • Reward TTLs outnumber the "success" labels in more than half of the sessions, usually by one, in a few sessions by tens, so the two ways of counting successes differ; both are shown. # The onset rule on synthetic trial averages, 30 Hz, -1 to 3 s, 1000 runs each: (i) no movement,
# the trial average of 50 trials of white noise; (ii) no movement, smooth noise (white noise
# filtered with a 100 ms exponential kernel, then averaged over 50 trials); (iii) as (ii) plus a
# ramp that starts at +0.3 s and rises by 10 noise s.d. over 0.5 s. Displacement is taken as in the
# data: the distance of the trace from its mean over -1 to -0.5 s.

    x = rng.standard_normal((50, len(tt), 2))
    if kind != "white":
        a = np.exp(-1 / (0.1 * 30))
        for k in range(1, len(tt)):
            x[:, k] = a * x[:, k - 1] + np.sqrt(1 - a ** 2) * x[:, k]

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The raw blue fluorescence is sampled from 16 blocks of 10 frames per session, checked against every frame in four sessions; the sensory maps use the ROI series only (the pixel maps do not fit the time limit); the Figure 6 claims are tested over all sessions, as the example session is not identified; the stimulus identity of the sensory-mapping trials is inferred from the stimulus pattern and the evoked maps, as the files do not name the codes; the movement onsets are also shown under a rule that requires a crossing to last 100 ms, an assumption, as the rule the criterion gives fires on traces without movement.

What was found about the dataset. The excitation wavelengths of the two imaging planes are swapped in every file (the stacks' intensities and correlations with the dF/F series fit their names, not the stated wavelengths); the lever's unit is "a.u." where the code says mm; the sensory-mapping trial codes have no description, six sessions have no stim_visual channel and carry the visual train on stim_whisker and the tactile stimulus on stim_auditory, with no third stimulus, and in the other sessions the trial order is visual, auditory, tactile against the Methods' visual, tactile, auditory; reward TTLs outnumber success labels in more than half of the task sessions; some pupil diameters are infinite or of 1e5 pixels and more; four sensory-mapping sessions have 36000 frames, not 27000; 357 task sessions have body keypoints against the paper's 355; the session-initial T_pull does not follow the Methods' rule (previous final minus 100 ms) in about a third of consecutive sessions; the trial tables hold T_pull values of 0.0 s where the Methods give a 1 ms start; nine task sessions have more than 10 tone TTLs beyond their trials (up to 145).
