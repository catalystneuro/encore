Reproducing Even-Chen et al. (2019) from dandiset 000121¶

Paper. Even-Chen N, Sheffer B, Vyas S, Ryu SI, Shenoy KV (2019). Structure and variability of delay activity in premotor cortex. PLOS Computational Biology 15(2), e1006808. doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006808. This attempt used the full text, which is open access.

Dandiset. DANDI:000121, version 0.220124.2156. The paper cites no DANDI version (it predates the dandiset; its data statement points to a Stanford zip file that now returns HTTP 403). This is the most recent published version. The earlier published version, 0.210815.0703, holds the same 12 files (same blobs and sizes) and differs only in metadata; the draft holds no files (see the data check).

What the dandiset holds. 12 sessions of delayed center-out reaching with two Utah arrays: all 7 sessions of monkey R and 5 of the 16 of monkey J (ring 1 of 4, vertical 2 of 4, 3-rings 2 of 4, horizontal none). Each file has a trials table, hand, cursor and eye positions, threshold crossings of the 192 electrodes as units, and LFP.

Code read. The paper names no analysis code. The conversion code, catalystneuro/shenoy-lab-to-nwb at commit 319acb472372c9e4359ebacf13c8df3652a822b1 (center_out_task/), was read. It builds the units from the rig's 1 ms threshold-crossing rasters of the two arrays (units 0 to 95 from spikeRaster, 96 to 191 from spikeRaster2), takes go_cue_time from the rig's timeTargetOn and target_shown_time as that minus delayTime, assigns the units' electrode_group by the order of a dictionary of groups, and stores positions with conversion NaN. It settles no analysis choice.

Outside inputs. None.

No claim uses data from outside the dandiset. Random numbers come from generators seeded per section (the seeds are named at the top of each section).

Electrode groups: 1 = M1 ; 2 = PMd (from general/extracellular_ephys)

Units 0 to 95 carry electrode_group 2 (PMd), but their electrodes column points to electrode rows 0 to 95, which belong to group 1 (M1); units 96 to 191 are labelled M1 and point to rows of group 2 (PMd), the rows the PMd LFP uses. The conversion code assigns electrode_group by the order of a dictionary of groups, not from the recording. The next cell asks which array carries the delay-period target signal.

In every J file unit 1's spike times decrease once, from the end of the session back to its start, unit 1 holds two to five times the median number of spikes, and unit 0 stops long before the session ends: the boundary between units 0 and 1 in spike_times_index is misplaced, and unit 1 holds the rest of unit 0's train followed by its own. Both are on the array of units 0 to 95, not the one used here as PMd. The loader sorts any unsorted train before counting.

Spikes, like hand samples, exist only within trials: 99.93 to 99.95% of the PMd spikes of every session fall inside a trial interval, although each file's span exceeds its summed trial time by 484 to 1963 s (the gaps between trials hold almost no spikes). The recorded time of the hand samples equals the summed trial time to within 7 s, so the day's mean rate, the spike count over the recorded time, is the rate over trial time. The per-session median PMd rates still vary widely, from 4.1 to 12.0 Hz in J and 4.6 to 47.4 Hz in R.

ARRAY_SEED = 1
rows = []
for s in sessions:
    t = s["trials"][s["trials"].analysed]
    y = (np.round(np.degrees(np.arctan2(t.ty, t.tx))) * 1000 + np.round(np.hypot(t.tx, t.ty))).values
    keep = pd.Series(y).map(pd.Series(y).value_counts()) >= 5
    for name, units in (("units 0-95", premotor.FILE_PMD_UNITS), ("units 96-191", premotor.PMD_UNITS)):
        row = {"monkey": s["monkey"], "date": s["date"], "array": name}
        for window, counts in (("delay", s["delay_counts"]), ("move", s["move_counts"])):
            X = counts[:, units].astype(float)
            Z = np.c_[np.ones(len(t)), t.tx, t.ty]
            n_sig = 0
            for ch in range(X.shape[1]):
                if X[:, ch].std() == 0:
                    continue
                beta = np.linalg.lstsq(Z, X[:, ch], rcond=None)[0]
                rss = ((X[:, ch] - Z @ beta) ** 2).sum()
                tss = ((X[:, ch] - X[:, ch].mean()) ** 2).sum()
                F = ((tss - rss) / 2) / (rss / (len(X) - 3))
                n_sig += stats.f.sf(F, 2, len(X) - 3) < 0.05 / 96
            _, acc = premotor.svm_cv(X[keep.values], y[keep.values], ARRAY_SEED, folds=5)
            row[f"{window}_sig_channels"] = n_sig
            row[f"{window}_accuracy"] = round(acc.mean(), 3)
        row["chance"] = round(1 / len(np.unique(y[keep.values])), 3)
        rows.append(row)

def plane_shares(means, plane):
    """Per task, share of the variance of its condition means in the plane: (a) of all channels,
    (b) of the top PCs."""
    Q = premotor.plane_basis(plane)
    return {task: {"a": 100 * ((Mc @ Q) ** 2).sum() / (Mc ** 2).sum(),
                   "b": 100 * ((Mc @ Q) ** 2).sum() / ((Mc @ plane["A"].T) ** 2).sum()}

def refit_with_targets(X, plane, tx, ty):
    """The plane refit from the same PCs to other target positions."""
    S = (X - plane["mu"]) @ plane["A"].T
    ones = np.ones((len(S), 1))
    bx = np.linalg.lstsq(np.hstack([ones, S]), tx, rcond=None)[0]
    null = np.eye(S.shape[1]) - np.outer(bx[1:], bx[1:]) / (bx[1:] @ bx[1:])
    by = np.linalg.lstsq(np.hstack([ones, S @ null]), ty, rcond=None)[0]
    return {**plane, "wx": plane["A"].T @ bx[1:], "wy": plane["A"].T @ (null @ by[1:])}

def speed_axis(Xc, speed, Q):
    """Regression of maximum speed on the activity with the plane projected out (unit-free axis)."""
    Xp = Xc - (Xc @ Q) @ Q.T
    fit = LinearRegression().fit(Xp, speed)
    return fit, Xp

How the analysis differs from the paper's¶

  • Monkey J is represented by 5 of its 16 sessions and 96 of its 120 conditions; its PCA, plane and classifiers are fit on these alone. Monkey R's data are complete.
  • The PMd array is taken as units 96 to 191, against the units' electrode_group labels, from the data check; results on the labelled array are shown as variants where they bear on a claim.
  • The paper does not say how the day's average rate, movement onset, maximum speed, the SVM hyperparameters, the folds or the null of its p-values were set; the choices here are listed in the claims file and in premotor.py. The day's mean rate is the rate over trial time, as spikes exist only within trials.
  • The spatial plane is fit on all trials, so the plane-projected distance classifier sees its test folds through the plane; the paper does not say how it handled this.
  • R's horizontal and vertical tasks are named by the Methods' dates; the file geometry is shown as a variant. R's horizontal targets lie at 5.2 + 10.4 k mm, so its 12 distances are matched by rank.

What was found about the dandiset¶

  • The units' electrode_group labels are swapped: the array labelled PMd is the one whose units point to M1 electrode rows and which, in R, has 29 silent channels and almost no target information in the decoding (at most 6 significant channels per session in the delay window), though 40 of its 96 channels regress on target position when R's sessions are pooled.
  • In every J file the boundary between units 0 and 1 is misplaced, so unit 0 stops early and unit 1's spike times run past the session's end and restart.
  • Positions are stated in meters with conversion NaN but are in mm; task_id is the same in every trial, so the configuration must be inferred from the targets; is_successful carries a description of something else; electrode positions and impedances are empty.
  • The archive's summary gives 15 files and 187.5 GB for a version of 12 files and 39.5 GB.
  • 11 of J's 16 sessions, including all its horizontal sessions, are not in the dandiset.
  • The files place R's horizontal and vertical tasks on the dates the Methods give the other way round; R has almost no trials without a delay and delays up to 1000 ms; its 3-rings session holds 1340 successful delayed trials against the 912 the paper used.
  • Spikes and hand samples exist only within trials; the gaps between trials (484 to 1963 s per session) hold almost no data.
