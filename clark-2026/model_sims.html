figs1-s2-spectrum-does-not-fix-symmetry¶

Eigenvalue degeneracy suggests but does not fix the symmetry group, since ensembles matching the model's second-order statistics, and hence its spectrum, can differ beyond second order and give different symmetry groups and attractors (a single ring for Gaussian loadings, the full torus for mixture-of-Gaussians loadings, in a rank-four network).

As weight_matrix_analyses.ipynb, cells 16 and 19: N 10,000, J = M M^T / N, loading standard deviations 10 and 5 for the two doublets; the mixture gives each neuron one doublet with standard deviation sqrt(2) times as large, so that the second moments are the same. Dynamics x <- (1 - dt) x + dt J tanh(x), dt 0.1 for 10 tau, 40 initial conditions with evenly spaced angles in both doublets, permuted independently (amplitude 5 for the Gaussian, 0.45 in kappa units for the mixture). J tanh(x) is computed as M (M^T tanh(x)) / N, the same product. The nonzero eigenvalues of J = M M^T / N are those of the 4 by 4 matrix M^T M / N, which is how they are computed.

Measures (the claims file fixes collapse; the rest are standard choices). kappa in units of the loading standard deviation, as the paper's notebook. A doublet forms a ring when its 40 final radii vary by less than 10% (coefficient of variation) and their angles leave no gap wider than 2 pi / 10; it collapses when its median final radius is below one tenth of the ring radius of the dominant doublet.

N1, STD1 = 10000, np.array([10., 5.])
def gaussian_M(draw):
    return draw((N1, 4)) * np.repeat(STD1, 2)[None, :]
def mixture_M(group, z):
    M = np.zeros((N1, 4)); comp = np.sqrt(2) * STD1
    for d in range(2):
        M[group == d, 2 * d:2 * d + 2] = comp[d] * z[group == d]
    return M
def eig_lowrank(M):
    return np.sort(np.linalg.eigvalsh(M.T @ M / N1))[::-1]
def run_gauss(M, perm, r0=5.0, n_init=40, dt=0.1, T=10):
    even = np.arange(n_init) * 2 * np.pi / n_init
    ang0 = np.stack([even, perm(even)])
    sub = [M[:, 2 * d:2 * d + 2] / np.std(M[:, 2 * d:2 * d + 2], axis=0) for d in range(2)]
    x = sum(r0 * sub[d] @ np.array([np.cos(ang0[d]), np.sin(ang0[d])]) for d in range(2))
    for i in range(1, int(T / dt)):
        x = (1 - dt) * x + dt * (M @ (M.T @ np.tanh(x)) / N1)
    P = np.tanh(x)
    return [(M[:, 2 * d:2 * d + 2].T @ P) / N1 / np.std(M[:, 2 * d:2 * d + 2], axis=0)[:, None] for d in range(2)], ang0
def run_mix(M, perm, r0=0.45, n_init=40, dt=0.1, T=10):
    even = np.arange(n_init) * 2 * np.pi / n_init
    ang0 = np.stack([even, perm(even)])
    sc = np.repeat([np.std(M[:, 2 * d:2 * d + 2]) for d in range(2)], 2)
    kap0 = np.zeros((4, n_init))
    for d in range(2):
        kap0[2 * d] = r0 * np.cos(ang0[d]); kap0[2 * d + 1] = r0 * np.sin(ang0[d])
    x = (M / sc[None, :]) @ kap0
    for i in range(int(T / dt)):
        P = np.tanh(x)
        traj = (M.T @ P) / N1 / sc[:, None]     # kappa before the step, as the paper's loop
        x = (1 - dt) * x + dt * (M @ (M.T @ P) / N1)
    return [traj[2 * d:2 * d + 2] for d in range(2)], ang0
def ring_stats(kap, ang0):
    r = np.linalg.norm(kap, axis=0); a = np.arctan2(kap[1], kap[0]) % (2 * np.pi)
    s = np.sort(a); gap = np.diff(np.r_[s, s[0] + 2 * np.pi]).max()
    drift = np.abs(np.angle(np.exp(1j * (a - ang0))))
    return dict(radius=np.median(r), cv=r.std() / r.mean(), gap=gap, drift_max=drift.max(),
                ring=bool(r.std() / r.mean() < 0.1 and gap < 2 * np.pi / 10))
def judge_s1(eg, em, kg, ag, km, am):
    rel = np.abs(eg - em) / eg
    g = [ring_stats(kg[d], ag[d]) for d in range(2)]; m = [ring_stats(km[d], am[d]) for d in range(2)]
    g_collapse = g[1]["radius"] < 0.1 * g[0]["radius"]
    m_collapse = min(m[0]["radius"], m[1]["radius"]) < 0.1 * max(m[0]["radius"], m[1]["radius"])
    dyn = g[0]["ring"] and g_collapse and m[0]["ring"] and m[1]["ring"] and not m_collapse
    return rel, g, m, g_collapse, m_collapse, dyn

Result: partially reproduced. The dynamics differ as the legends describe under all five seeds: with Gaussian loadings the final states form a ring in the dominant doublet (radius 0.80, radii varying by at most 0.6%, largest angle gap 0.32 to 0.45 rad) and collapse in the subdominant one (radius 0.012 to 0.024, below one tenth of 0.80); with mixture loadings both doublets form rings (radii 0.56 to 0.57, the mean-field prediction 0.5642 and 0.5636). The eigenvalues of the two matrices agree within 5.2% under the claim's seed, just outside the 5% asked (5.18%; 2.4% to 5.2% over the five seeds, within 5% under two), so that part fails narrowly. The two ensembles share their second moments (population eigenvalues 100, 100, 25 and 25), and each finite matrix deviates from them by sampling; the paper's own seed-42 matrices agree within 3.3%. The criterion's 5% applies to two finite matrices, whose eigenvalues scatter around the shared population values by sampling, so this part measures sampling noise more than a difference between the ensembles; whether the criterion should test the population (large-N) spectrum is asked of a reader. The criterion has no rule for a partial result; partially reproduced is the protocol's reading of a result that holds in part.

figs11-s12-global-stability-model¶

In the generative-process network of 2,500 neurons, initial conditions far from the manifold converge to it with no spurious attractors, and with growing N the dynamics approach the mean-field behaviour, reliably converging to a ring by N = 1,000.

As spurious_fixed_points.ipynb: N 2,500, 500 angles, lambda 1e-8, nonlinearity 1 + erf, no uniform inhibition, tau 50 ms, Euler step 5 ms, 30 s; 34 initial states i.i.d. Gaussian with standard deviation 1, 10, 50, 100 and 1,000 (Supplementary Fig. 11a); and for N 50, 100, 250, 500 and 1,000, the 34 regularly spaced manifold points plus noise of standard deviation 0.1 sqrt(N) (Supplementary Fig. 12). The distance to the manifold is the distance of the firing rates to the nearest of the 500 target points, over sqrt(N), as in figs11b-data-network-global-stability.

The criterion judges "as in figs11b", whose threshold is the largest distance at 20 s of the fig2ef protocol. For this network that protocol is run on the network itself (assumption): the 100 regularly spaced manifold points plus noise of standard deviation 3, with the same step (5 ms), and the largest distance at 20 s is the threshold. For Supplementary Fig. 12 the threshold is that of the 2,500-neuron network, a fixed normalized distance (assumption); the threshold of each N's own reference run is shown as a variant. On the manifold means at least 95% of final states within the threshold, as in figs11b.

STDS = (1, 10, 50, 100, 1000)
def model_net(n, rng):
    X, P = H.gen_idealized(n, N_THETA, rng, SCALE, BETA)
    return X, P, H.compute_J(X, P, LAMDA)
def dist_to(P32, Psq, x):
    return H.nearest_target(P32, H.nonlin_idealized(x.astype(np.float32), BETA), Psq)
def reference_run(X, P32, Psq, J, rng):
    # the fig2ef protocol on this network: 100 regularly spaced manifold points plus noise sd 3
    n = X.shape[1]
    x0 = X[::N_THETA // 100].T + rng.standard_normal((n, 100)) * 3.0
    rec = {}
    def on_save(tt, x):
        if abs(tt - 20.0) < 1e-6 or tt == 0.0:
            rec[round(tt)] = dist_to(P32, Psq, x)[1]
    xf = H.simulate(J, x0, T=30.0, dt=0.005, c=0.0, phi=lambda v: H.nonlin_idealized(v, BETA), save_every=0.1, on_save=on_save)
    rec["final"] = dist_to(P32, Psq, xf)[1]
    return rec
def s11a(X, P, J, sd):
    P32 = P.astype(np.float32); Psq = (P32 ** 2).sum(1)
    ref = reference_run(X, P32, Psq, J, np.random.default_rng(sd + 1))
    rng = np.random.default_rng(sd + 2)

    xf = H.simulate(J, x0, T=30.0, dt=0.005, c=0.0, phi=lambda v: H.nonlin_idealized(v, BETA))

figs10-robustness-comparable¶

Under added weight noise the disordered network and a circulant network with proportional eigenvalues fragment into discrete states alike, so the two are comparably robust.

As weight_matrix_analyses.ipynb, cell 10: the generative-process network (N 2,500, 500 angles, lambda 1e-8) and the circulant network built from the mean-field interaction profile (DMFTModel(N_theta=2500).J - 1, scaled so that its diagonal is 10 / N); weight noise of standard deviation f times the matrix's standard deviation, f 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0; 34 initial conditions (regularly spaced manifold points for the disordered network, shifted Gaussian bumps for the circulant one) with noise of 0.025 times each state's standard deviation; 120 s, step 5 ms, no uniform inhibition. The encoded angle is the argmax of the overlap m (disordered) or of the input currents (circulant), as the notebook's theta_max.

Final angles are merged into states as in fig2gi-circulant-noise-collapses (within 2 pi / 50). The criterion does not say how many states make a continuum among 34 initial conditions spaced 2 pi / 34 apart; a network keeps a continuum here when at least 31 (90%) remain distinct under all three merging readings (assumption).
