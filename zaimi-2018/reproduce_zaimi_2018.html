Paper. Zaimi A, Wabartha M, Herman V, Antonsanti P-L, Perone CS, Cohen-Adad J (2018). AxonDeepSeg: automatic axon and myelin segmentation from microscopy data using convolutional neural networks. Scientific Reports 8, 3816. doi:10.1038/s41598-018-22181-4.

Dataset. DANDI:001436, "TEM Images of Corpus Callosum in Control and Cuprizone-Intoxicated Mice with Axon and Myelin Segmentations", version 0.250512.1625 (doi:10.48324/dandi.001436/0.250512.1625). The paper cites no version of the dandiset (the dandiset was created in 2025, after the paper). 0.250512.1625 is the only published version, and the draft (modified 2026-09-23) has the same 660 assets and size, so the most recent published version is pinned. The dataset is a BIDS microscopy dataset of png images, not NWB; the files are read from the archive into memory.

Text the claims were written from. The version of record (PMC5830647), with Methods, figure legends and Tables 1 to 3; the paper has no supplement. Claims file: claims/zaimi-2018.yaml.

Outside inputs. (1) The paper's released TEM model, default_TEM_model_v1, at commit 7f6013221b2a862e107a7e23f3c8662eba3ba64b of https://github.com/neuropoly/axondeepseg, with the inference code at the same commit (claims data: with_inputs). (2) The folder names of the NYU mouse component (OSF node adrcs) of the White Matter Microscopy Database (https://osf.io/yp4qg/, doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/YP4QG), which the paper's Data availability statement names, to label each mouse as control or cuprizone (data: with_labels, tested by the link claim osf-group-labels-link). (3) The macaque TEM images and masks of ref. 7 in the same database (OSF node yp4qg, folder MontrealNeurologicalInstitute/histology/Macaque), for the two macaque claims (data: elsewhere). (4) The SEM dataset of AxonDeepSeg, https://github.com/axondeepseg/data_axondeepseg_sem at tag r20211130 (commit d7884f9, doi:10.5281/zenodo.5498378), whose rat images include two with the pixel size, field of view and fixation of the paper's SEM test images, for table3-sem-rat-accuracy (data: elsewhere).

Ground truth. The dandiset's masks are not those the paper scored against: its CHANGES file (printed below) says that in 2025 the masks of 155 of the 158 images were changed "to separate all fibers with a 2px delineation", and one mask was corrected in 2022. The earlier masks are not public. A variant that fills the delineation lines bounds the effect on the mouse scores.

The inference steps of the original code¶

The original pipeline (Python 2, TensorFlow 1) cannot be installed here, so its output is not compared end to end.

The SEM rat test images (data elsewhere)¶

The SEM test images are not in the dandiset. The AxonDeepSeg project publishes an SEM dataset, https://github.com/axondeepseg/data_axondeepseg_sem (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.5498378 in its dataset_description.json, whose authors include the paper's authors): 10 rat cervical spinal cord SEM images, "small cropped sections extracted from larger mosaic images" acquired from 2015 to 2017, with manual axon, myelin and axonmyelin masks. It is read here at commit d7884f9 (tag r20211130, the release archived under that DOI), the last before the masks of 6 samples were changed in 2025 to separate touching fibres; the 2025 masks are a variant. Each image is matched to the rows of Table 1 by pixel size, field of view and fixation. The images that match the two SEM test rows are segmented with the paper's default_SEM_model_v1 as segment.py does (0.1 um working pixel size for a 512 patch, generate_resolution; overlap 25; no contrast inversion, which segment.py applies only for default_TEM_model_v1).

The macaque image (data elsewhere)¶

The macaque TEM image of Table 3 is not in the dandiset. The White Matter Microscopy Database, which the paper's Data availability statement names, holds the macaque corpus callosum TEM images of ref. 7 (folder MontrealNeurologicalInstitute/histology/Macaque of OSF node yp4qg): eight samples of three images each, 2300 x 3040 pixels (20.7 x 27.4 um at the 0.009 um of Table 1, which gives 27 x 21 um2), and one myelin and one axon mask per sample. The paper names neither the sample nor the image it tested. Each mask is matched here to the image of its sample that it fits (myelin is the darkest structure, so the grey-level contrast between the rest of the image and the myelin mask is large for the right image and near 0 for the others), and every matched image is one reading of "the macaque test image".

Claims not tested¶

The dandiset holds only the mouse TEM images and their masks. The human SEM test image (Table 3, row Human; 0.13 um, 715 x 735 um2 in Table 1) and the full rat cervical spinal cord slice of Figures 6 and 7 are not in it. The White Matter Microscopy Database (OSF node yp4qg), which the paper names, holds human spinal cord images (PolytechniqueMontreal/Human, one zip file) whose description, printed below, gives a pixel size of 230 nm, not the 0.13 um of the test image, and the AxonDeepSeg SEM dataset holds rat images only. The database holds two SEM panoramas of an adult rat cervical spinal cord, listed below: PolytechniqueMontreal/RatSpinalCordNov2016 (0.095 um, dated 2016-11-17) and PolytechniqueMontreal/RatSpinalCord2017 (0.13 um, dated 2017-04-27, next to a map of axon equivalent diameter). Either could be the slice of Figures 6 and 7, but the paper gives no pixel size, date or deposit for that slice, so neither can be shown to be it, and the Figure 7 claims also leave the tract boundaries and the white matter mask open. They are not run here, and these claims are not tested.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper's network runs in TensorFlow 1, which cannot be installed with Python 3.13; it is rebuilt here from the released checkpoint, and its output matches the model's own saved graph to below 1e-04 on the four patches of the first mouse image, with the same class on every pixel, and the SEM network matches its saved graph to below 1e-04 on the patches of Rat 1 (the largest differences are printed in the model check and in the SEM section). The difference changes slightly from run to run, at the level of floating-point round-off. Resampling uses the settings of scikit-image 0.13; the original lines of the code are printed, but the original pipeline could not be run end to end. The paper's test set (3 held-out mice) is not named, so the mouse claims are tested on all mice and on the control mice, both of which include training mice, with every set of 3 mice shown. The dandiset's masks are those of 2025 (fibres separated by a 2-pixel line), not the masks the paper scored against; filling those lines changes the mouse accuracy by less than 0.002. The SEM rat test images come from the AxonDeepSeg SEM dataset, matched to Table 1 by pixel size, field of view and fixation. The macaque images and masks come from the White Matter Microscopy Database, and which of them the paper tested is not known.

What was found about the dataset. The dandiset holds 158 TEM images of 20 mice (8 per mouse, 6 for mouse 25) with three masks each, and the masks fit their images (myelin contrast median 84.4 grey levels in place against 11.8 when shifted). No image is deposited twice. The pixel size is 0.00236 um, not the 0.002 um of the paper (the README says so; at 0.002 um the images would be too small for the model's 512-pixel patches). The files do not say which mice are controls; the White Matter Microscopy Database does, by mouse number. The archive's summary counts 11 samples where there are 158 images (sample ids repeat across mice). 21 axonmyelin masks are grey plus alpha pngs, the rest grey. The test split of the paper is not recorded.
