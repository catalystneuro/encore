Reproducing Wirth et al. (2017) from CRCNS hc-12¶

Paper. Wirth S, Baraduc P, Plante A, Pinede S, Duhamel JR (2017). Gaze-informed, task-situated representation of space in primate hippocampus during virtual navigation. PLoS Biology 15(2): e2001045. doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.2001045. The claims file (claims/wirth-2017.yaml) was written from the version of record (PMC5328243), Results, Methods and the legends of Figs 1 to 7 and S1 to S7; S1 Appendix was not available.

Datasets. CRCNS hc-12 (189 cells, the new-maze sessions; every claim rests on it) and CRCNS hc-21 (101 cells, a familiar and a new maze per file), streamed from the public bucket crcnsarchive (hc-12/live/data.tar.gz, hc-21/live/data.tar.gz). CRCNS data sets are not versioned; the version is recorded as unversioned, and the MD5 sums from each data set's checksums.md5 for the files read are: hc-12 data.tar.gz 426c7d00c0358f2cc9873e0dbb331e2f (descriptor version 0.7, 17 Jan 2017); hc-21 data.tar.gz dee5e8aa4a7b3c44b61b8cecfe622a0a (descriptor version 0.52, 21 Dec 2018). Each data set has one published state (live/, with only documentation under orig/), so the version pinned is the only one there is, and hc-12 is the deposit the paper cites. hc-21 is read only to check whether it holds recordings that are also in hc-12 and whether it holds the probe sessions hc-12 lacks; no claim rests on it.

One pass over hc-12¶

The archive is streamed once. For each cell file the helper keeps the new-maze trials, the camera rows inside the new-maze session, the spikes and the eye signal, computes every map measure with 999 surrogates under both surrogate readings, the landmark ANOVAs, and, once per session, the behaviour. Seeds are fixed per cell (CRC32 of the file name).

The descriptor (hc-12, version 0.7) gives the positions as virtual X, Y. The values span x from -16 to 12.9 and y from -15.2 to 15.2, five arm ends at radius 16 m (Methods: radius 16 m), so positions are in metres. The camera orientation is in degrees, on 0 to 360 in some files and -180 to 180 in others; the analysis takes it modulo 360. Column 5 (curvilinear abscissa along the branch) is, on the entry and exit paths (tags 1xy and 4xy), the distance from the centre divided by the 16 m arm: it runs from 0 at the centre to 1 at the arm end (median absolute difference at most 0.0023 in every file, printed above). On the passive returns (tags 5xy) it does not follow the distance from the centre; it runs along the return path. Camera rows are at 60 Hz and eye samples at 200 Hz (5 ms), where the descriptor says 250 Hz.

Every file holds the 12-column camera layout of the descriptor (not the 11 columns that star_remapped_cell_r.m documents), with transition tags 101 to 555 and a state-space radius of 0.81 to 3.0, inside the -3 to 3 grid of the state-space maps. The polar angle is on -pi to pi in some files and 0 to 2 pi in others; the maps use its cosine and sine. The mean orientation on each entry path falls on 18, 90, 162, 234 or 306 degrees in every session, the five arms 72 degrees apart. ke20140207 spans only -126 to 90 degrees because its headings (18, 90, 234 and 306, that is -126 and -54) all fall in that range. In sn20110610 3.6% of the frames have an abscissa up to 1.618, none of them on an entry path, so the landmark ANOVAs do not use them.

Checks against the description:

  • The descriptor says the learning curve's middle column is the probability of a correct response, with the bounds in columns 1 and 3. In every file column 2 is the lowest and column 3 the highest, so column 1 is the probability, column 2 the lower bound and column 3 the upper bound. The field learningtrial, which the descriptor does not describe, equals the first trial whose column 2 exceeds 1/4 (S3B legend's criterion), which confirms that reading.
  • The descriptor says code 43 is the rewarded start arm and code 53 the rewarded chosen arm. The arm chosen on rewarded trials differs between sessions (printed above), as each day had a new reward location (Results); 53 is the rewarded arm only in some sessions.
  • The eye signal is in undocumented units, on a +-5000 scale in some sessions and a +-5 scale in others, with a rail at the bound; no calibration to degrees is given. Eye samples are 5 ms apart (200 Hz), where the descriptor says 250 Hz.
  • The camera rows share the trial clock. At the trial start (code 21) the camera is at the arm end (distance over 16 m, 1.00), and the row still belongs to the passive return (0.99 of them), whose abscissa has run down to 0.01; one second later the camera is on the entry path near the centre (0.12). One second before the arrival at the choice point (code 22) it is at 0.72 on the entry path, at the arrival it is at the centre (0.00), and it is still there one second later, while the view rotates. At the reward (code 11) it is at the arm end (1.00), and one second earlier at 0.21 on the exit path (printed above).
  • The trial ends (code 23) at the reward (code 11): the median time between them is 0 s in both monkeys, and the return lasts until the next trial start (median 3.6 to 3.8 s).

A probe session is read from the start arms: the first 10 trials use one or two entries and later trials use others (Results; S3C legend: one entry for 36 trials, or two entries until trial 60). The side of the first entries is checked from the arm-end positions, relative to the rewarded arm (north by the paper's convention). def arm_angles(b):
    """Angle of each start arm relative to the rewarded arm, degrees, negative = clockwise seen from
    above (x east, y north, as a right turn lowers the camera orientation): with the reward north,
    the eastern entries are at -72 (northeast) and -144 (southeast)."""
    ang = np.degrees(np.arctan2(b["pos_start"][:, 1], b["pos_start"][:, 0]))
    rew = b["pos_reward"][b["correct"]]
    ra = np.degrees(np.arctan2(np.nanmedian(rew[:, 1]), np.nanmedian(rew[:, 0])))
    out = {}
    for a in np.unique(b["start_arm"][np.isfinite(b["start_arm"])]).astype(int):
        m = b["start_arm"] == a
        out[a] = int(round(((np.nanmedian(ang[m]) - ra + 180) % 360) - 180))
    return out

During the 500 ms before each joystick action, gaze anticipated the direction of the subsequent movement.

Each left (code 2) or right (code 1) joystick move; mean horizontal eye signal in the 500 ms (reading a) or 300 ms (reading b) before it, for successful trials (Method W) and, as a variant, all trials. Per-session differences are also tested across sessions (Wilcoxon signed-rank), as the turns of one session are not independent.

Sign of the eye signal. The files do not say which sign of the horizontal signal is rightward. It is taken from the slow phases. During a turn the scene flows across the screen opposite to the turn, and the slow phases of optokinetic nystagmus and smooth pursuit follow the scene, so the eye drifts to the left during a right turn. The median eye velocity from 0 to 600 ms after the code is positive during right turns and negative during left turns, in 40 of 40 sessions of K and 20 of 20 of S (session signed-rank p = 1.8e-12 and 1.9e-6). The slow phases are therefore leftward when the signal increases, and a negative value is rightward. This uses the eye velocity only, not the eye position that the claim tests. The mean position during the turn moves the other way, toward the turn (profile above), as the mean eye position does during optokinetic stimulation. The sign is still an assumption, now supported by this check.

Is the view already rotating in the 500 ms before the code? In both monkeys it is, slowly: the camera orientation changes by a median of -0.49 and 0.54 degrees (K) and -0.75 and 0.69 degrees (S) over those 500 ms, in the direction of the coming turn in 95% to 99% of turns, as if the code came after the turn had begun. In K the eye velocity before the code also differs between right and left turns (0.250 against -0.189 session SD, 38 of 40 sessions, p = 2.3e-9), in the direction of the slow phases; in S it does not (9 of 20 sessions, p = 0.40). The position effect remains in the turns with the least rotation before the code: with less than 0.25 degrees, K -0.27 session SD (p = 3.3e-5, 355 and 328 turns) and S -0.54 (p = 0.0059, 32 and 106 turns); with less than 1 degree, K -0.37 and S -0.695. In K the velocity difference also remains with less than 0.25 degrees (0.248, p = 2.6e-10), so it is not explained by the rotation of the view, and part of K's position effect may continue an eye movement under way before the window. In K's low-rotation turns the position effect is a third to a half of that over all turns (-0.77). Information content in the coding spaces¶

The point-of-gaze space needs the gaze direction in degrees, which the files cannot give (the eye signal is uncalibrated, data check). Position, direction and state space are computed for the cells with more than 100 spikes in the new-maze session. Readings: trial window "full" (start to next start), "toreward" (start to reward, no return) or "toreward_ret" (start to reward plus the return that follows); surrogates "permute" (chunk order permuted) or "shift" (each 5 s chunk rotated within itself by its own random offset) (Method W). Significance is the bilateral surrogate p < 0.01; the upper tail alone is shown as a variant. The responsive set of the paper needs all four spaces; the set responsive in the three spaces here is contained in it, and all cells contain it, so counts over these two sets bound the count over the paper's set.

The surrogates are first checked on synthetic spike trains that code nothing: a homogeneous Poisson train on a smooth random trajectory over 200 bins. A valid surrogate gives about 1% of such trains p < 0.01. The first version of the "shift" reading, which moved each chunk by an offset over the whole session so that chunks overlapped, is run alongside for comparison.

def shift_overlapping(s, rng, L=300):
    """The first version of the "shift" reading: each chunk moved by an independent offset
    over the whole session, circularly, so that chunks overlap (replaced)."""
    T = len(s)
    src = np.arange(T)
    ch = src // L
    tgt = (src + rng.integers(0, T, size=ch[-1] + 1)[ch]) % T
    return np.bincount(tgt, weights=s, minlength=T)

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The point-of-gaze space is missing, as the eye signal has no calibration, so the responsive set of the paper cannot be formed; counts over it are bounded by counts over the three-space responsive cells and over all cells. The map resolution steps, the surrogate shifting (two readings), the trial windows (three readings), the move types of the state-space selectivity (three typings), the per-session standardization of the eye signal and its sign are choices the paper does not fix. The learning-curve method of the paper's reference 60 is not known, so curves were not recomputed.

What was found about the data. The learning curves' columns are probability, lower and upper bound, not the order the descriptor gives; eye samples are 200 Hz, not 250 Hz; the eye signal is on two different scales with no units, sign or calibration; the rewarded arm is not always code 53; two hc-12 files have no new-maze data; 87 of the 101 hc-21 files hold the same spike trains as hc-12 files; hc-12 holds 12 of monkey K's 15 probe sessions and none of monkey S's 9, and in 3 of the 12 the first entries lie on both sides of the rewarded arm.
