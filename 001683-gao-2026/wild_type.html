def position(m):
    lab = m["label"].split("_", 1)[1]

        if "S1" in lab:
            return "S1:" + lab
        if key in lab:
            return key
    return "?" + lab

The EP files hold the 64 intracranial channels of left S1 only; there is no EEG in them. The resting BASELINE recordings last about 4 to 5 minutes and carry 120 to 151 intervals every 2 s, tagged tFUS like the stimulated ones. The PLV EEG files are recordings with 20 ms pulses (1Hz_20ms_CC), the pulse of the epilepsy experiment (Methods D), made during the wild-type phase, of 20 of the 23 rats. Their intervals come every 2 s in most files, every 1 s in a few. No EEG recording of a resting baseline (the sham of Figs. 5E-F) is in the dandiset.

def mean_response(m, n=100, pre=0.05, post=0.2):
    x = tfus.read_epochs(m, np.sort(m["intervals"].start.to_numpy())[:n], pre=pre, post=post)
    x = x - x[:, :int(pre * tfus.RATE)].mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)
    return np.abs(x.mean(axis=0)).max(axis=1) * 1e6     # peak over channels of the trial mean, uV

# Alignment of the EP intervals with the bursts (verifier, round 1). The intervals last 2.2 s and start
# 2 s apart; a burst at another lag would still show at a fixed lag of the 2 s cycle. Two checks on the
# first 40 cycles of every CENTER and BASELINE file: (1) the RMS over the 64 channels of the
# trial-mean LFP (3-300 Hz) over the whole cycle, -100 to 1900 ms from the interval start, in 10 ms
# bins; (2) the power at 1400-1520 Hz, the band of the bursts' 1.5 kHz pulse repetition frequency (just
# under the 1526 Hz Nyquist frequency), where an ultrasound artifact would mark the 67 ms burst.
from scipy import signal as sg
for kind, files in (("EP", ep), ("PLV", plv)):
    L = np.concatenate([(m["intervals"].stop - m["intervals"].start).to_numpy() for m in files])
    print(f"{kind} files, interval stop - start: {L.min():.4f} to {L.max():.4f} s; start spacing:",
          sorted({round(float(np.median(np.diff(np.sort(m['intervals'].start)))), 3) for m in files}))
SOS_LFP = sg.butter(4, [3, 300], btype="bandpass", fs=tfus.RATE, output="sos")
SOS_PRF = sg.butter(4, [1400, 1520], btype="bandpass", fs=tfus.RATE, output="sos")

def cycle_profile(m, n=40):
    on = np.sort(m["intervals"].start.to_numpy())[1:n + 1]
    pre, L, b = int(0.1 * tfus.RATE), int(2.0 * tfus.RATE), int(0.01 * tfus.RATE)
    i0, i1 = int(on[0] * tfus.RATE) - pre, int(on[-1] * tfus.RATE) - pre + L
    x = tfus.read_rows(m, i0, i1, threads=8)
    idx = [int(t * tfus.RATE) - pre - i0 for t in on]
    lfp = sg.sosfiltfilt(SOS_LFP, x, axis=0)
    mu = np.mean([lfp[i:i + L] for i in idx], axis=0)
    rms = np.sqrt((mu ** 2).mean(1)) * 1e6
    hf = (sg.sosfiltfilt(SOS_PRF, x, axis=0) ** 2).mean(1)
    pw = np.mean([hf[i:i + L] for i in idx], axis=0)
    nb = L // b
    return rms[:nb * b].reshape(nb, b).mean(1), pw[:nb * b].reshape(nb, b).mean(1)

In the PLV EEG the mean response after the interval start is many times the pre-onset level, so the interval starts are the pulse onsets, in seconds (these files' tables are in seconds, unlike the seizure labels). In the intracranial EP files the LFP shows no clear response at any lag of the 2 s cycle, in CENTER as in BASELINE (second table). The bursts can still be located: power at 1.4 to 1.5 kHz, the band of their 1.5 kHz pulse repetition frequency, peaks 10 to 60 ms after the interval start in most CENTER recordings and in none of the resting ones (counts printed above), so the interval starts are the burst onsets. An EP absent from the LFP is therefore not a misalignment of the intervals.

fig5b-tfus-evoked-potentials¶

tFUS of the CC in wild-type rats evokes potentials in the left S1 LFP that differ significantly from the sham (resting) recording at some time points.

For each rat, the LFP around the first 200 bursts of the target-confirmation recording at the chosen coordinates, and around pseudo onsets in the resting BASELINE recording, each trial baseline-subtracted over -20 to 0 ms, averaged over trials. Readings of the claims file: the channel, (a) the mean over the 64 channels or (b) each channel; the sham onsets, (a) every 2 s from the start of the resting recording or (b) 200 onsets drawn at random (seed 0); the ANOVA, ordinary or repeated measures (tfus.timepoint_tests; the repeated reading adds a random intercept per rat to the same two-way ANOVA), Bonferroni over the time points from 0 to 200 ms. The dandiset does not say which position was chosen. The paper says "the coordinates with the clearest EP were used" (supplement, Target Confirmation); two readings are run: (a) the CENTER recording, the first estimate, and (b) the position whose channel-mean EP has the largest peak from 0 to 200 ms. Reading (b) picks the position on the data it then tests, so it is also run with the position picked on the odd trials and the test made on the even trials (largest, split). The BASELINE file's own interval starts are shown as a third sham variant.

Wild-type PLI¶

PLI (M6) of the PLV EEG files, first 200 pulses, in the windows pre (-20 to 0 ms), tFUS (0 to 20 ms) and post (20 to 200 ms), for every pair of one ipsilateral and one contralateral channel: per trial and averaged (reading a) or over the concatenated samples of all trials (reading b). The PLI of a short window is biased upwards, the more so the fewer samples it holds, so the 549 samples of 20 to 200 ms cannot be compared with the 61 of the pre window (the first version of this attempt did, and its post-window decreases were that bias). The post window is therefore the mean PLI of its nine 20 ms sub-windows, each as long as the pre window; the whole window is shown as post_full. Each window's PLI is also given with a noise floor, the same PLI with the contralateral channels taken from the next trial. The change from pre is a difference (reading a) or a ratio (reading b), of the mean over the pairs of a region (for the ratio, the region's mean PLI over its mean pre PLI, a standard choice, as pair-wise ratios explode where the pre PLI is near 0). The dandiset's PLV files are the recordings with 20 ms pulses, which fits the Methods' "tFUS (20 ms of stimulation)"; the claims file reads the epochs as the 200 bursts of the target confirmation, whose EEG is not in the dandiset. These files are used, and this is recorded as a deviation.

The decreases the first version of this attempt reported in the 20 to 200 ms window (S1 -0.029, non-S1 -0.016 concatenated, 100% of pairs per trial) were the bias of comparing 549 samples with 61: they appear again with the whole window (post_full) and vanish with its 20 ms sub-windows.

Result: not reproduced, resting on an assumption. In no band and under no combination of readings does the S1 PLI fall while the non-S1 PLI does not, with equal-length windows or with the whole 20 to 200 ms window. In the 0 to 20 ms window the S1 PLI is not below pre in theta, alpha or beta under any reading (differences +0.0002 to +0.070). After it, with 20 ms sub-windows over concatenated samples, both regions fall slightly in alpha (S1 -0.010, non-S1 -0.014) and beta (-0.016, -0.026). The per-trial PLI is at its trial-shuffled floor (theta S1 pre 0.976, floor 0.976), so the per-trial reading cannot show a change, and the concatenated PLI (0.10 to 0.20) is about 0.02 above its floor. The outcome rests on the assumption that the PLV EEG files, the recordings with 20 ms pulses, stand for the target-confirmation EEG that the claims file names and the dandiset lacks; without it the claim would be blocked (data missing), as fig5cdj-wild-type-gamma-pli-decrease is.

Summary¶

A failure to reproduce here may be a failure of this attempt, not of the paper: see the reasoning and the differences from the paper's method.

The outcomes of the other nineteen claims are in seizure_metrics.ipynb and eeg_blocks.ipynb.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The position whose bursts form the tFUS EPs is not recorded; the CENTER recording and the position with the largest EP are both run. The wild-type PLI uses the dandiset's PLV EEG files, recordings with 20 ms pulses, since the EEG of the target-confirmation bursts is not in the dandiset; the Methods' "tFUS (20 ms of stimulation)" fits these files; fig5ghi-low-band-pli-s1-only rests on this assumption. The repeated-measures reading of the EP test adds a random intercept per rat to the group by time point ANOVA. Short windows bias the PLI upwards, so the 20 to 200 ms window is compared with the pre window through its nine 20 ms sub-windows, and each PLI is given with a trial-shuffled noise floor.

What was found about the dataset. The EP files of all 23 rats are there, one per transducer position plus a resting recording, with interval tables in seconds whose starts are the burst onsets (1.4-1.5 kHz power, the pulse repetition frequency, peaks just after them in most CENTER recordings). The LFP shows no clear evoked response at any lag of the 2 s cycle. The resting recordings hold no EEG, so the sham of Figs. 5E-F cannot be computed. The PLV EEG files cover 20 of the 23 rats (not BH638, BH642, BH644). The chosen position is not recorded.
