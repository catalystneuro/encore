Temmar et al. (2025), LINK: same-day and cross-day decoding (Figure 4, Appendix C.1)¶

Paper: doi:10.52202/085713-4642. Dataset: DANDI:001201, version 0.261026.1912, streamed (the most recent published version; the paper cites none, and the three published versions hold the same assets, see signals.ipynb). Claims file: claims/temmar-2025.yaml. Code read: github.com/chesteklab/LINK_dataset at 5e1c36a.

Claim Tested here
fig4a-sameday-stable RR on all 312 sessions, the LSTM on every 16th session
fig4a-lstm-beats-rr on every session with an LSTM: every 16th, and the 12 with the highest RR R2
fig4bc-dof-accuracy RR on all sessions, LSTM on its sessions
fig4d-cross-day-decay RR and LSTM decoders of the LSTM sessions, tested on every session within 100 days
suppc1-shuffle-baseline RR on all sessions, LSTM on its sessions
fig2a-irregular-shifts all days, with the R2 above

Why a subset for the LSTM. One LSTM of the paper's size (2000 iterations, batch 64, 20 bins, 300 hidden units) takes 77 to 223 s to train when four train at once on this runner, depending on the run: 77 s in round 1 and 207 to 223 s in round 2; the cell below prints this run's time of each fit and its estimate for all sessions. At those times 312 fits, four at a time, take 1.7 to 4.8 hours, over the 60-minute limit of a notebook. The LSTM is therefore trained on every 16th session in date order (20 sessions), chosen before any result was seen; the claims on trends and distributions over the dataset are tested on those. After round 1 of the verification, 12 sessions are added for fig4a-lstm-beats-rr: those with the highest RR R2 not already among the 20, where an LSTM is likeliest to fall below RR, chosen by that rule before their result. An LSTM on the 42 other sessions from July to November 2022 is 11 more rounds of four fits, 14 to 41 minutes at those times; at round 2's times that would take this notebook (48 minutes then) over its limit, so the LSTM part of fig2a-irregular-shifts is also run in window.ipynb, on 24 of the 42 with the 20 every-16th sessions refit there as the reference. Ridge regression is cheap and runs on every session.

Load and fit ridge regression¶

Per session: the first 300 trials train and the remaining 75 test (Appendix C.1). SBP is z-scored per channel on the training bins. Ridge regression (alpha 0.1, intercept, 8 bins of history, 768 inputs) is fit with that normalisation (Appendix C.1) and, as a variant, on the raw SBP as the code does.

def load(i):
    r = A.iloc[i]
    s = link.read_session(r["asset_id"], tcr=False)
    (xtr, ytr), (xte, yte) = link.split(s)
    mu, sd = link.zparams(xtr)
    rr = link.rr_fit(((xtr - mu) / sd).astype(np.float32), ytr)
    rr_raw = link.rr_fit(xtr, ytr)
    rec = {"i": i, "date": r["date"], "style": s["style"], "path": r["path"],
           "n_train_bins": len(xtr), "n_test_bins": len(xte),
           "n_test_trials": len(s["trials"]) - link.N_TRAIN_TRIALS,
           "mu": mu, "sd": sd, "rr": rr, "rr_raw": rr_raw, "xte": xte, "yte": yte,
           "sbp_mean": s["sbp"].astype(np.float64).mean(0), "n_bins": len(s["sbp"]),
           "test_trial_index": s["trial_index"][link.N_TRAIN_TRIALS:] - s["trial_index"][link.N_TRAIN_TRIALS]}
    rec["xtr_z"] = ((xtr - mu) / sd).astype(np.float32)   # kept for every session: the extra LSTMs are chosen after the RR fits
    rec["ytr"] = ytr
    return rec

Train the LSTMs¶

Each LSTM is trained as Appendix C.1 and the claim's criterion give (96 inputs, 300 hidden units, one layer, linear readout of the four outputs at the last of 20 bins; Adam, batch 64, 2000 iterations, learning rate linear from 2e-4 to 1e-5, weight decay 0.001; outputs z-scored on the training data; white noise of SD 0.1 per bin and a bias of SD 0.2 per sequence and channel), seed 42 as in the code. Each worker then evaluates its decoder on its own hold-out, on the shuffled hold-out (suppc1), and on the hold-out of every session within 100 days under both normalisations of fig4d (every 5th bin).

def shuffles(x_raw, y, mu, sd, tti, rng):
    """The hold-out shuffles of suppc1-shuffle-baseline: (a) permute the raw bins of SBP, then build the
    history; (b) permute whole input windows; and (trials) permute whole trials; kinematics are permuted
    separately in each case."""
    n = len(y)
    pa, pk = rng.permutation(n), rng.permutation(n)
    out = {"a": (x_raw[pa], y[pk], None)}
    out["b"] = (x_raw, y[pk], rng.permutation(n))       # window permutation applied to the predictions
    bounds = np.append(tti, n)
    tr = [np.arange(bounds[j], bounds[j + 1]) for j in range(len(tti))]
    o1 = np.concatenate([tr[j] for j in rng.permutation(len(tr))])
    o2 = np.concatenate([tr[j] for j in rng.permutation(len(tr))])
    out["trials"] = (x_raw[o1], y[o2], None)
    return out


def lstm_task(i, seed=42, cross=True):
    t0 = time.time()
    s = SES_BY_I[i]
    state, onorm = link.lstm_train([(s["xtr_z"], s["ytr"])], seed=seed, iters=ITERS)
    fit_s = time.time() - t0
    res = {"i": i, "seed": seed, "fit_s": fit_s}
    p = link.lstm_predict(state, onorm, z(s["xte"], s["mu"], s["sd"]))
    res["r2"] = link.r2_per_output(s["yte"], p)
    if not cross:
        return res
    rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
    sh = {}
    for k, (xs, ys, perm) in shuffles(s["xte"], s["yte"], s["mu"], s["sd"], s["test_trial_index"], rng).items():
        pr = link.lstm_predict(state, onorm, z(xs, s["mu"], s["sd"]))
        if perm is not None:
            pr = pr[perm]
        sh[k] = link.r2_per_output(ys, pr).mean()
    res["shuffle"] = sh
    cr = []
    for j, (day, xte, yte, mu, sd) in TEST.items():
        off = day - s["day"]
        if abs(off) > MAX_OFFSET:
            continue
        sel = slice(None, None, EVAL_EVERY)
        for norm, (m_, s_) in (("train", (s["mu"], s["sd"])), ("test", (mu, sd))):
            pr = link.lstm_predict(state, onorm, z(xte, m_, s_), every=EVAL_EVERY)
            cr.append({"dec": i, "test": j, "offset": off, "norm": norm, "decoder": "LSTM",
                       "r2": link.r2_per_output(yte[sel], pr).mean()})
    res["cross"] = cr
    res["total_s"] = time.time() - t0
    return res

fig4d-cross-day-decay¶

Decoders tested on hold-out sets of other days within 100 days of training lose accuracy sharply in the short term, with the decay stabilising at longer offsets; neither RR nor LSTM generalises well, the LSTM is better at every offset, and both follow a similar decay.

The decoders are those of the LSTM sessions, for both RR and LSTM, so the two curves come from the same training days; each is tested on every session within 100 days, scored on every 5th test bin. Readings: offset (a) signed or (b) absolute; normalisation (i) the training day's or (ii) the test day's own training-trial parameters.

def shift_counts(by_sessions):
    out = []
    for n, d in enumerate(DM.index):
        if by_sessions:
            win = DM.iloc[max(0, n - 30):n]       # variant: the preceding 30 recording days
        else:
            win = DM[(DM.index < d) & (DM.index >= d - pd.Timedelta(days=30))]
        if len(win) < 3:
            out.append(np.nan); continue
        med = win.median(); mad = (win - med).abs().median()
        out.append(int(((DM.loc[d] - med).abs() > 3 * mad.where(mad > 0, np.nan)).sum()))
    return pd.Series(out, index=DM.index)

How the analysis differs from the paper's: the LSTM is trained on 20 of the 312 sessions (every 16th), and on the 12 other sessions with the highest RR R2 for fig4a-lstm-beats-rr, for time; cross-day R2 is scored on every 5th test bin; the cross-day curves use the decoders of those 20 sessions for both RR and the LSTM; the LSTM's noise is 0.1 white and 0.2 bias as the Methods state, while the code passes them the other way round; the test set is the 75 trials after the first 300, to the end of the file.

What was found about the dataset: every session has 375 trials, so the 300/75 split is exact; same-day decoding is stable across the 3.5 years.
