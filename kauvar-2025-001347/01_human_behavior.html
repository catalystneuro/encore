Data check¶

The non-iEEG files (sub-h*) hold two series of 40 eye-closure trials each, pre_trials_data and post_trials_data, with a time axis time_reference/tt (seconds from the puff). The iEEG behaviour files (sub-SD*_ses-puff_behavior.nwb) hold, per session, the continuous eye closure, the video frame of each puff (events-*-on), the trial windows (by_trial_closure, 800 samples by trials), the stored per-trial early, late and late/early values, the early and late windows, the frame rate, and which_trials.

Checks of the stored values against the traces they come from. (1) Each trial window of by_trial_closure should be the continuous trace from 400 samples before to 400 after the puff frame. (2) The stored early and late values should be the window means of the trial windows, and the stored late/early their ratio. (3) The puff frames should be spaced as the Methods describe: blocks of 8 puffs 3 to 8 s apart, 35 s between blocks.

        s = b[sess]
        ec, ev, tr = s["eyeclosure"], s["events_on"], s["trials"]

The trial windows equal the continuous trace around each puff frame, and the stored per-trial values equal the window means and their ratio to rounding, in every participant and session, so the stored values and the traces agree (the table above). The shortest gap between puffs is 3.3 to 3.5 s and the median about 5.5 s, with pauses of about 36.5 s between blocks (4 pauses in 40-puff sessions, 3 in 32-puff sessions), consistent with the Methods (3 to 8 s between puffs, 35 s between blocks). Some sessions hold fewer than 40 puffs (SD178, SD185 and SD186 have 32, SD207's infusion session 14). Some trace values exceed 1, which a fraction of eye closure cannot (h009 ketamine series up to 2.74, SD186 infusion up to 2.58), and are tracking artefacts; they are kept, as in the paper's code. Whether the puff frames are the true puff times cannot be shown from the eye alone; the iEEG coding-dimension notebook (04) shows the puff times share the LFP clock.

which_trials holds 0-based trial indices; in most files it starts at 1, so the first trial is left out, as the Methods say ("the first trial was excluded").

p50-condition-labels-link: which non-iEEG series is saline¶

The non-iEEG files do not say which of the two series is the saline visit. The paper's code (Zenodo 14920570, human_ieeg/scripts/analyze_airpuff_p50.py) reads pre as saline and post as ketamine (treatment saline for pre_trials, highdose for post_trials), and lists the eight sessions by participant with their treatment and CADSS. The link holds when the code's participants are the dataset's, each with both treatments, and the series have the trial counts the paper reports (40, of which 38 are used). The meaning of pre and post itself cannot be checked against anything in the data; the visit order was crossed over (the code's session names put saline first for h009, h012 and h013 and ketamine first for h011), so pre does not mean the first visit.

def inclusion_tests(sub):
    b = beh[sub]
    v = {s: he.per_trial(b, s)["late_over_early"].to_numpy() for s in he.SESSIONS}
    res = {"subject": sub, "n which_trials": len(b["which_trials"])}
    ps = []
    for a, c, lab in [("preinfusion", "infusion", "pre"), ("postinfusion", "infusion", "post")]:
        ok = ~np.isnan(v[a]) & ~np.isnan(v[c])
        t = st.ttest_rel(v[a][ok], v[c][ok])
        res[f"t {lab}-ket"] = t.statistic
        res[f"n {lab}-ket"] = int(ok.sum())
        ps.append(t.pvalue)
    pc = multipletests(ps, method="fdr_bh")[1]
    res["p pre-ket (BH)"], res["p post-ket (BH)"] = pc
    for s in he.SESSIONS:
        res[f"mean {s[:4]}"] = np.nanmean(v[s])
        res[f"sem {s[:4]}"] = st.sem(v[s], nan_policy="omit")
    res["decrease"] = (res["t pre-ket"] > 0) and (pc[0] < 0.05)
    res["recovery"] = (res["t post-ket"] > 0) and (pc[1] < 0.05)
    res["meets both"] = res["decrease"] and res["recovery"]
    return res
