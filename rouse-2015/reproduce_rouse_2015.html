Reproducing Rouse & Schieber (2015) from dandiset 000941¶

Paper. Rouse AG, Schieber MH (2015). Spatiotemporal distribution of location and object effects in reach-to-grasp kinematics. J Neurophysiol 114:3268-3282. doi:10.1152/jn.00686.2015. The claims file (claims/000941/10.1152-jn.00686.2015.yaml) was written from the version of record (Methods, Results, legends, Tables 1 and 2; the paper has no supplement).

Dandiset. DANDI:000941, "FALCON Benchmark M1-A", version 0.241029.1405. The paper cites no version of the dataset (it predates it), and this is the only published version, so it is the one pinned. The draft has the same 11 assets and was last modified within a second of the publication of this version.

What the dandiset holds. Monkey L only: trial tables, 16 channels of preprocessed EMG at 50 Hz and multiunit spike times, for four sessions (one of them the paper's session L20120928) split for the FALCON benchmark, and three later sessions with ten trials each. It holds no marker positions and no joint angles, the data every kinematic claim of the paper is about, and no data of monkeys X and Y. Only the trial table can be used here.

Claim Tested here
table2-trial-counts counts shown; blocked, data missing (the dataset holds a part of the session's successful trials and no failed trials)
table2-movement-time movement time shown; blocked, metadata insufficient (the dataset's onset is not shown to be the paper's, and its medians fall about 55 ms short of the authors' own in every session)
fig2-trial-count-coax-45 count shown; blocked, data missing (a part of the session's trials)
fig2-joint-patterns, fig2-hand-opening-two-phases blocked, data missing (no marker positions or joint angles)

Its description says data of another monkey are in dandiset 001209 (FALCON M1-B, monkey X, a draft with no published version); the claims file does not name it, so it is not used.

Every trial in every file has result R (rewarded): the files hold no failed trials, although the column's description says it holds success, failure or abort. In the paper's session (held-in-calib, 20120928) the 412 trials carry numbers from 201 to 673, apart from one numbered 127, and span about 3,200 s. The paper's Table 2 gives this session 768 trials, 717 of them successful, over 1:26:11 (5,171 s). The trial with number 127 sits between 254 and 257 in the same session (and between 254 and 256 in two others), in time order, so its number is very likely 255 or 256, stored wrong; the number 127 is the largest value of a signed 8-bit integer. Session 20120927 also has a true trial 127 at its start, so in that file the number does not identify a trial.

All event times lie on a 20 ms grid, the step of the EMG, so each is known only to 20 ms; the paper times movement from 200 Hz video (5 ms) and contact from strain gauges sampled at 1 kHz. Each trial starts exactly 1 s before the go cue and stops exactly 1 s after the reward, so start_time and stop_time are a window cut around the events, not the trial's own start and stop. The reward time comes 0 to 0.92 s after contact (median 0.10 s), where the paper's task requires a 1,000 ms hold of the manipulated object before the water reward; reward_time therefore cannot be the time of the paper's reward, and the file does not define it. The events are in the expected order in every trial (no interval is negative; reward minus contact is 0 in some trials).

table2-movement-time¶

The median duration from movement onset to contact differs among the monkeys (255, 235 and 335 ms for L, X and Y; Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.001), giving 52, 48 and 68 normalized samples from M to C.

Criterion: movement time from onset to contact over the analyzed trials (a) or all successful trials (b); the claims file says that onset times given by the dataset are used. The dataset gives move_onset_time and contact_time, so the marker-speed onset is not recomputed (and could not be: there are no markers). The number of samples from M to C, inclusive of both, at 200 Hz is median / 5 ms + 1 (Results, paragraph 1: "inclusive of the first sample at movement onset and the last sample at peripheral object contact"; 255 ms gives 52). Only monkey L is in the dataset. Reading (b) uses the 412 successful trials in the file; reading (a) cannot be formed exactly, as the error trials are absent, and is approximated by the trials whose preceding trial number is present. The approximation treats every absent number as an error trial; many absent numbers are more likely successful trials held back in FALCON splits not on DANDI, so (a) here drops more trials than the paper's rule would.

Is the dataset's move_onset_time the paper's movement onset M?¶

The claims file uses the dataset's onset times when it gives them. The dataset does not define move_onset_time, and its median movement time is 55 ms short of the paper's, so three checks compare it with the same authors' later papers on the same monkey and sessions (in paper/): Rouse & Schieber 2016, J Neurophysiol 115:3238, Table 2 (median movement time per session, with L20120928 the 2015 paper's session), and Rouse & Schieber 2016, J Neurosci 36:10640, Methods ("For monkey L, the minimum median times were as follows: I-M, 243 ms"; the onset "was identified from motion-capture data as the monkey released the center object"). The 2015 paper's task software also marked the release of the center object as an event marker (Methods, Behavioral Task), separate from the motion-capture onset M. The third check aligns the EMG on the dataset's onset.

The file's median movement time falls short of the authors' own median for the same session in every one of the four sessions, by 52 to 58 ms, three times the 20 ms step of the event times. Its median time from cue to onset is 300 to 320 ms, 57 to 77 ms longer than the shortest session median of I-M the J Neurosci paper gives for monkey L (243 ms), so no session of the file has a median as short as that. The median time from cue to contact, by contrast, is 500 to 520 ms, 4 to 25 ms above 243 ms plus the session's published movement time, as expected if contact is the same event, since 243 ms is the shortest of the session medians of I-M (medians do not add exactly, so this is a consistency check, not a proof). Contact and cue thus agree with the papers and the onset does not: the dataset's onset comes about 55 ms after the paper's M. In the EMG aligned on the dataset's onset, the shoulder and elbow muscles DLTa, BCPs and TCPlat are at half their peak 40 ms before it (DLTa and BCPs peak 0 to 20 ms after it), and 8 of the 16 muscles reach half their peak before it; this fits an onset later than the start of motion, but EMG leads motion under any definition, so the EMG alone does not decide. Taken together, the dataset's move_onset_time is very likely a later event than the paper's marker-speed onset M, such as the task software's center-release event marker (2015 Methods, Behavioral Task). This rests on session medians on a 20 ms grid compared with another paper's medians over a different set of trials (676 against 412), so it indicates, and does not prove, a later event.

Result: blocked, metadata insufficient. Taken at face value, the median time from the dataset's move_onset_time to contact_time in session L20120928 is 200 ms (quartiles 160 and 240 ms) over the 412 successful trials in the file, and 200 ms over the 387 trials whose preceding trial number is present, outside the tolerance of 229.5 to 280.5 ms around the paper's 255 ms (41 samples from M to C against 52). But the dataset does not define its onset, and the checks above show it is not the paper's: in all four monkey L sessions the file's movement time is 52 to 58 ms shorter than the authors' own median for the session (J Neurophysiol 2016, Table 2), its cue to onset is 57 to 77 ms longer than the shortest median I-M the authors report (243 ms), and its cue to contact agrees within 4 to 25 ms. A movement time measured from a later event cannot test the paper's, and the paper's onset cannot be recomputed without markers. The comparison among monkeys and its Kruskal-Wallis test cannot be made, as X and Y are not in the dataset.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. Movement onset and contact are the dataset's own event times, at 20 ms resolution, not the paper's onset from marker speed (5 ms steps) and contact from strain gauges (1 kHz); the checks above indicate that the onset is very likely a later event, so the movement time is shown but not compared. Only monkey L is in the dataset, and only 412 of the 717 successful trials of its session. The objects are named from obj_id by the apparatus order of the paper's Fig. 1 legend.

What was found about the dataset. It holds trial tables, EMG and multiunit spikes of monkey L, and no kinematics, so it cannot support the paper it names first. The session L20120928 is there only in part (trials 201 to 673, about 53 of 86 minutes, no failed trials). tgt_obj does not name the grasped object; obj_id does, without names. Trial number 255 or 256 is stored as 127 in each of the four calibration files. The minival files repeat the first two trials of the calibration files. move_onset_time, contact_time and reward_time are not defined; move_onset_time gives movement times about 55 ms shorter than the authors' own in each of four sessions, so it is very likely not the paper's marker-speed onset; and reward_time comes a median 0.10 s after contact, where the paper's task requires a 1 s hold before the reward.
