Reproducing Padilla-Coreano et al. (2022) from dandiset 001749¶

Paper. Padilla-Coreano N, Batra K, Patarino M, et al. (2022). Cortical ensembles orchestrate social competition through hypothalamic outputs. Nature 603, 667-671. doi:10.1038/s41586-022-04507-5. The claims file was written from the PMC author manuscript (PMC9576144) with its Extended Data legends, and the Nature supplementary information, which holds the Methods.

Dandiset. DANDI:001749, version 0.260311.1727. The paper cites no version of the dandiset, which was created in 2026, after the paper. This is the only published version and the most recent one. The draft (modified 2026-09-23) lists the same 105 assets with the same paths, sizes and modification times, so the two do not differ in content the analysis uses.

What the dandiset holds. 53 NWB files, one per implanted mouse and competition: 10 from the "ephys" cohort (spikes and tone times only), 21 from the mPFC-LH ("chrimson") cohort and 22 from the mPFC-BLA cohort (with AlphaTracker poses, port entries and, in 34 files, the nine hand-scored behaviour labels of Figure 1f), and 52 behaviour videos (mp4). No file holds an alone session, a phototagging light pulse, an optogenetic stimulation, the unimplanted cohort, the ex vivo recordings or a body weight (file contents, in the data check).

Code and outside input. The HMM-GLM is ported from the paper's MATLAB code (github.com/Tyelab/HMMGLM at commit 3fc3567815b9c891402635664a844320136d2fac) to hmmglm.py. The paper's processed HMM-GLM input in that repository is read as a variant input and as a check of the alignment; no outcome rests on it.

HMM-GLM decoding of behaviour from mPFC multi-unit activity¶

Input (Methods 17)¶

"Multiunit firing rate activity was determined by applying a common reference average and determining spikes using 4 standard deviations from the mean. [...] The dataset consists of 965 trials, with spiking information from 32 channels across 70 time stamps of 200 ms intervals." (Methods 17, Dataset)

The input is computed here from the raw ElectricalSeries of the labelled files: 600 to 6,000 Hz band (Methods 11), common average reference, crossings of 4 SD from the channel mean (reading a, negative crossings below mean minus 4 SD; reading b, crossings in either direction), counted in 70 bins of 200 ms from -2 s to +12 s around tone onset (competition.mua_trials). Labels are the files' 20 Hz labels taken every fourth bin (labels change only on whole seconds, so this is exact).

The recording does not share the behaviour clock, and the offset is in no file (data check). A file is used when the offset can be placed: a clear port-entry offset (more than twice the 99th percentile over offsets), an offset within 10 s of the median of the other clear files recorded that day in that cage, scaled spikes matching the recording (peak above 10 SD), and labels matching the port entries (r of at least 0.3 at the file's best tone shift k). The window of trial j starts in the recording at tone j + k + offset to the spikes - offset of the spikes to the recording - 2 s. As checks, the multi-unit counts are correlated with the scaled spikes in the same bins (a check of the recording's offset to the spikes), and both are compared with the paper's own processed input (dataset_forHMMGLM_proc.mat in the code repository at the pinned commit, a variant input that the claims file names), whose trials are matched to the files' trials by their labels.

Per behaviour, the AUC is the mean over the 10 folds (folds where a label is absent give no AUC and are left out of its mean, a standard choice). def zscore(X):
    m, s = X.mean(axis=(0, 2), keepdims=True), X.std(axis=(0, 2), keepdims=True)
    return (X - m) / np.where(s > 0, s, 1)

def judge(res, key, label):
    A = {k: np.nanmean(v, axis=0) for k, v in res[key].items()}
    h_, g_, n_ = A["HMM-GLM"], A["GLM"], A["9 GLMs"]
    sign = stats.binomtest(int((h_ > 0.5).sum()), 9).pvalue
    rs = lambda a, b: stats.ranksums(a, b).pvalue
    out = {"reading": label, "auc": key, "HMM-GLM min": h_.min(), "HMM-GLM median": np.median(h_), "GLM median": np.median(g_),
           "9 GLMs median": np.median(n_), "HMM > 0.5": int((h_ > 0.5).sum()), "sign p": sign,
           "HMM vs GLM p": rs(h_, g_), "HMM vs 9 GLMs p": rs(h_, n_), "GLM vs 9 GLMs p": rs(g_, n_),
           "HMM > GLM, all 9": bool((h_ > g_).all()), "HMM > 9 GLMs, all 9": bool((h_ > n_).all())}

    return out, A

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The behavioural tests use the hand-scored labels of the 34 labelled files (and the port entries for latency), not the paper's AlphaTracker distance or its GUI scoring of whole sessions; the labels cover -2 to +12 s of each trial, so "whole competition" means the labelled bins. Rewards and port occupancy are compared per session (20), with the 34 files as a variant. Pushing success and time displaced need both mice's files and use 13 and 14 sessions. Reward delivery is taken at 2 s, from the Methods; the files give 0.49 s. Latency (ED Fig. 5d) is judged on the trials whose labels can be placed on the port entries. The HMM-GLM claims use the paper's code, ported, on multi-unit counts computed here from the raw recording of the 17 labelled files (512 trials, 10 mice) whose recording can be placed on the behaviour clock, against the paper's 965 trials and 13 mice; the paper's own processed input is run as a variant, and on it the port reproduces Figure 1i, selects 6 states for ED Fig. 5j, and passes all four train and test combinations of ED Fig. 5k. The SVM of Figure 1i is not run.

What was found about the dataset. The stored spike times are two thirds of their time in the recording (divided by 30 kHz, with a 20 kHz recording). In 18 of the 21 mPFC-LH files and one "ephys" file the scaled spikes match the recording only after an offset of 5 to 28 s, and in the other 3 mPFC-LH files at no offset within 60 s. The offset between the neural recording and the behaviour clock (the video_gap the description names) is in no file; the data put it at 4 to 45 s in 25 of the 26 files where it can be estimated (294 s in the other), differing by session, and the paper's processed input confirms it in 16 of the 17 files used for the HMM-GLM. Every cluster is labelled unsorted (5,879 against the paper's 998 cells), so the paper's cells cannot be selected. In 18 labelled files the labels hold fewer trials than tones, and in several of them the label trials start at a later tone; nothing in the files says which tones were scored. The "ephys" files have no port entries, labels or poses; the BLA files have poses with every value missing; no file holds alone sessions, phototagging pulses, the optogenetic experiments or body weights. The tone schedule is the program's, the same in all files of a cohort, deliveries are 0.49 s after onset against the paper's 2 s, pair_in_dataset says "not included" where the competitor's file is present, one session start time and one subject description contradict the file names.
