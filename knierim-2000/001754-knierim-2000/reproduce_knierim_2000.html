Dandiset description:
 This dataset contains extracellular recordings from hippocampal place cells in three freely moving rats during the Neurolab Space Shuttle mission (STS-90) in April 1998. The experiment investigated whether the hippocampus can maintain stable spatial representations during three-dimensional navigation in microgravity. Rats were implanted with multi-electrode recording arrays and trained to traverse a three-dimensional track called the "Escher staircase," in which three 90-degree yaw turns were interleaved with three 90-degree pitch turns, creating a closed loop after only 270 degrees of total yaw rotation. This design placed the spatial information from external landmarks in conflict with the expected input from the head-direction system, which normally requires 360 degrees of yaw to signal a return to the starting direction. The dataset includes both preflight recordings on a flat rectangular track and in-flight recordings on the Escher staircase track from flight days 4 and 9. Place cells eventually demonstrated normal spatially specific firing, with spatial information content comparable to preflight levels, though initial exposures on flight day 4 showed abnormal spatial selectivity patterns. Hippocampal EEG was also recorded, showing normal theta rhythm during locomotion and normal sharp waves and ripples during quiet rest. Rats received medial forebrain bundle stimulation as a reward for traversing the track.

# Shared clock of spikes and position, and whose position the trace is. Each unit with >= 50 spikes at a
# position in a task window: spatial information of its spike-count map over the window's deposited
# occupancy (as in reading M-b) with its real spike times, against the same with its spikes shifted by
# +-30, 60, 90, 120 and 150 s (circularly within the window; each shifted spike takes the nearest sample
# within 0.25 s, else it is dropped). Shifted spikes are dropped more often than real ones, so for each
# shift the real spikes are subsampled to the same count (fixed seed). These are standard choices for a
# check; they decide no claim.
# Every unit is run with its own file's trace in every ES and MC window of both rats of a flight day.
SHIFTS = [s_ * k for k in (30, 60, 90, 120, 150) for s_ in (1, -1)]
rng_clock = np.random.default_rng(1754)

def shift_contrast(s, a, b, occ):
    occ = nl.clean_occupancy(occ)
    t, valid = s["t"], s["valid"]
    out = []
    for _, u in s["units"].iterrows():
        sp = u.spikes[(u.spikes >= a) & (u.spikes <= b)]
        ok, r, c = nl.spike_positions(s, sp)
        if ok.sum() < 50:
            continue
        def info(rows, cols):
            counts = nl.count_map(rows, cols)
            counts[occ == 0] = 0
            return nl.skaggs_info(np.where(occ > 0, counts / np.where(occ > 0, occ, 1), 0.0), occ) if counts.sum() else np.nan
        real = info(r, c)
        shifted, matched = [], []
        for sh in SHIFTS:
            x = a + (sp - a + sh) % (b - a)
            i = np.clip(np.searchsorted(t, x), 1, len(t) - 1)
            j = np.where(np.abs(t[i - 1] - x) <= np.abs(t[i] - x), i - 1, i)
            keep = (np.abs(t[j] - x) <= 0.25) & valid[j]
            shifted.append(info(*nl.bin_xy(s["xy"][j[keep]])) if keep.sum() else np.nan)
            # the real spikes subsampled to the number of shifted spikes kept, as information rises when spikes are fewer

            matched.append(info(r[pick], c[pick]) if len(pick) else np.nan)
        diff = np.array(matched) - np.array(shifted)
        out.append({"real": real, "shift_median": np.nanmedian(shifted), "above_all": bool(np.all(diff[np.isfinite(diff)] > 0)), "matched_median": np.nanmedian(matched)})
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

Spatial information in flight against preflight¶

The three comparisons (si-comparable-to-preflight, si-rat1-fd9-vs-preflight, si-rat3-fd4-vs-preflight) share their method, from the claims file: active cells (mean rate above 0.05 Hz on the track), Skaggs' information per spike with off-track positions kept, flight against the same rat's preflight cells by a two-sided Mann-Whitney test at p < 0.05, run under every reading.

How the readings apply here:

  • M-a, the deposited maps. Each map, identical copies counted once, over its visited bins, without the lost-position bin. A map is active when its occupancy-weighted mean rate exceeds 0.05 Hz, as maps cannot be linked to units in this version (data check).
  • M-b, maps recomputed from spikes and position. Position exists only at spike times and only in flight, so spike counts are divided by the deposited occupancy map of the epoch, as the criterion now says (answer of 2026-10-20), and M-b cannot be computed for any preflight cell. A unit is active when its spikes over the epoch's duration exceed 0.05 Hz. Spikes in bins with no deposited occupancy cannot be divided by an occupancy and are left out of M-b (33 to 1,188 per epoch over the active units, printed below). The criterion has both sides of a comparison use the same map reading, so M-b is not run for these comparisons; its flight values are shown, with a test of M-b flight values against M-a preflight values as a variant, not judged.
  • S-a and S-b. Each preflight file is one recording day with one ES and one MC epoch, so S-a has two candidates, ES1 and MC1. S-b, the day closest before launch, is that same day, and the criterion runs each of its epochs on its own (answer of 2026-10-20), so S-b gives the same two readings as S-a. Each flight session has one ES epoch (for rat 3, session 2 is ES1).

The criterion's rule is applied to the M-a readings, the only ones with preflight values.

fig3-stability-across-sessions¶

For rat 3 on flight day 4, most of the 7 cells kept the same firing fields in a third session that followed the second immediately; the fourth cell lost its field, and a few other cells gained a field in session 3.

Cells are the active cells of session 2 (ES1), followed into session 3 (ES2). A cell keeps its field by (a) when the correlation of its session 2 and 3 maps over bins visited in both exceeds the 95th percentile of the correlations between different cells across the two sessions, and by (b) when its session 3 peak lies in its session 2 field (bins with at least 20% of the session 2 peak). "Most" is more than half of the active cells of session 2 (answer of 2026-10-20; with the paper's 7 cells this was at least 4). Reproduced when more than half keep their field under every reading, not reproduced when half or fewer do under every reading, and blocked when the readings disagree.

Reading M-b uses the unsmoothed maps of the active units. Reading M-a needs the deposited maps of one cell in both sessions, and the dandiset gives no link (every Rat3 FD4 ES map has cell_number -1). The criterion now allows a pairing through the unit whose spike-count map a deposited map best matches, when each map is tied to one unit and no unit to two maps (answer of 2026-10-20). The code ties every distinct map of session 2 and of session 3 that has a unit on its tetrode (17 of 19 in session 2, all 11 active maps among them, and 23 of 23 in session 3) to the unit it best matches, checks that no unit is tied to two maps in either session, and runs M-a as a judged reading only if so.

def stability(maps2, maps3, cells, fraction=nl.FIELD_FRACTION):
    """Readings (a) and (b) for the cells; maps2/maps3 map a cell to its session 2 and 3 maps (None when absent)."""
    C_ = np.array([[nl.map_correlation(maps2[i], maps3[j], both) if maps3[j] is not None else np.nan for j in cells] for i in cells])
    off = C_[~np.eye(len(cells), dtype=bool)]
    thr = float(np.percentile(off[np.isfinite(off)], 95))
    same = np.diag(C_)
    peak = [maps3[i] is not None and nl.peak_in_field(maps2[i], maps3[i], both, fraction) for i in cells]
    return pd.DataFrame({"cell": cells, "r_same_cell": same, "keeps_a": np.nan_to_num(same, nan=-2) > thr, "keeps_b": peak}), thr, len(off[np.isfinite(off)])

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper gives no bin size, smoothing or occupancy rule; the deposited maps (64 x 64, smoothed with an unstated kernel) are used as the authors' maps (M-a), and recomputed maps (M-b) can only be made for flight, with the deposited occupancy. M-a counts a map as active by its own mean rate, as maps cannot be linked to units. The active-cell counts differ from the paper's in most comparisons (for example 11 against 7 for rat 3 in session 2, 38 against 24 for its preflight ES maps), so the cells are not the paper's set, and the spatial information of the deposited maps is above the paper's means for rat 2 and for rat 1's FD9 and preflight ES maps. Which preflight epoch the paper used is not stated, and the criterion runs both; for rat 1 the choice decides the test. The stability claim's "most" is more than half of the active cells, which here are 11 maps or 16 units, not 7. Its M-a reading is not run, as tying the deposited maps to the units they best match is not one to one in session 3, so it rests on unsmoothed recomputed maps (M-b) alone, with few spikes per 4-pixel bin. Maps with cell_number -1 are counted in M-a; in flight they behave as one cell's maps, and leaving them out changes no test.
