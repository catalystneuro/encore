Most of the paper's touch and whisking findings held up in this data, but its cutting and muscle results could not be tested. Moment and its rate of change predicted touch spiking (mean r 0.71, as in the paper), a viscoelastic model came close to the statistical one, and the WT* afferents were strongly modulated by whisk phase. Direction selectivity was only partly reproduced, with 65% of afferents selective against the 80% asked for, and moment with its rate of change ranked second, not first, among variable pairs. Preferred phases occupied all four quarter cycles, but the test for a single preferred direction was borderline. The progressive cutting and intrinsic protractor EMG results were blocked because the files label every cutting trial intact and hold no EMG signal.
