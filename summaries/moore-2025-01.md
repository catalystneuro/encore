Most main findings held in this data, the juvenile ones least firmly. In adults, Fmr1-/y rats showed more drift of the head-direction signal with unchanged tuning, as reported. In juveniles, sharper tuning held for head-direction information but not for tuning width, and the reduced drift fell short of significance (p = 0.085) in the paper's direction. The juvenile effects depend on regrouping one rat whose archive label contradicts the paper's counts, and one adult Fmr1-/y file duplicates another rat's recording. Stability of tuning within and between sessions mostly did not reproduce, and probe placement could not be tested.
