Five of eight findings held up in this data, two did not, and one could not be tested. VPM neurons respond to touch and whisking, fast-spiking interneurons respond to both, and thalamic input drives them (from only 2 mice). Suppressing the interneurons raised excitatory firing during whisking, on thin evidence. That excitatory neurons respond mainly to touch, and that suppression sharpens touch selectivity, did not reproduce, but their criteria used measures the paper does not. The role of sensory input could not be tested because no file marks the nerve-transected mice, and the claims came from the abstract only, so this attempt is deprecated.
