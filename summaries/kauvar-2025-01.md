Most main findings held: ketamine spared fast activity but removed the persistent, accumulating brain-wide response, and it shortened regional decay times while rise times stayed unchanged. The twelve response clusters did not reproduce (11 found, 6 of 12 sizes in range), nor did the stronger ketamine effect on slow clusters, and the clustering shifted with the random seed and BLAS thread count. Partly held: the late emotion-like drop and weaker coupling under ketamine, and the eye-closure reduction, which depends on the code's normalization. Human iEEG persistence, timescale and phase locking were not tested for lack of time. Participant reports, dissociation scores and licking data are absent from the archive.
