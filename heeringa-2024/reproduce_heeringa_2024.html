Reproducing Heeringa (2024) from dandiset 001262¶

Paper. Heeringa AN (2024). Single-unit data for sensory neuroscience: Responses from the auditory nerve of young-adult and aging gerbils. Scientific Data 11, 411. doi:10.1038/s41597-024-03259-3. The claims file was written from the version of record (open access, PMC11035566), which has no supplement, and from the paper's MATLAB toolbox on Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.10370064).

Dandiset. DANDI:001262, version 0.241205.0959. The paper cites no DANDI version: its data statement points to the Dryad deposit (doi:10.5061/dryad.qv9s4mwn4), which the dandiset lists as a variant form. Two versions were published on 2024-12-05, 30 minutes apart; this is the more recent. The data check below shows that both hold the same 1160 files (same blobs), so the choice changes no number; the later one adds metadata only (the paper as relatedResource and protocol, keywords, funder, ethics).

What the dandiset holds. One NWB file per single unit, 1160 units from 104 gerbils, with per trial the spike times, the stimulus (frequency, level, delay, duration), the raw voltage trace, and per recording type one row of analysed outcomes (best frequency, spontaneous rate, threshold). The other analysed outcomes the paper describes (characteristic frequency, vector strength and its p-value, click latencies) are not in the files, so they are recomputed here from the spike times with the paper's own functions (named in its Methods), as described in the helper gerbil_an.py. Raw voltage traces are read only for the trials that hold very short inter-spike intervals (isi-exclusion).

Outside inputs. None. Method details come from the paper's Methods and from its code (Zenodo record 10370064), as cited in the helper and under each claim.

The ABR threshold is not readable as written. In every file general/notes ends with "threshold (db SPL): " followed by a single character, or by "NaN". Read as code points, the characters are 10 to 85 in steps of 5 (for example "#" is 35 and "K" is 75; some are control characters such as a line feed, 10), which is the range Table 1 gives (10 to 85 dB SPL). The number appears to have been written as a character in the conversion to NWB. This is a format error in the files; the claims that use the ABR threshold read it as the character's code point, an interpretation the data support (the values are multiples of 5 and in the paper's range) but do not prove. 11 animals have "NaN". Ages are given in days (P85D), weights as text with a unit (65g, 6.110000e+01g), and oxygen as "yes" or "no" in general/surgery.

Spike times are in seconds from the start of each trial: tone trials start the tone at 7.75 ms and last 50 ms, and no spike falls outside its trial's length (taken from the length of the voltage trace at 48,828.125 Hz, the rate the Methods give). Spike times are sorted within trials, and no unit's spike train duplicates another's, so no recording was deposited twice. Empty trials hold a single NaN. The CLICK trials, and the SR, NOISE and CVC trials, carry no delay (NaN): the click's onset in the trial is not in the files.

The next cell checks that spikes and the trial table share a clock, with a contrast between conditions: the rate in tone trials minus the rate in the interleaved silent trials, in 5 ms windows from the start of the trial, over the BF and RLF recordings. If the clock is shared, the difference is near zero before 7.75 ms and large from a few milliseconds after it. It then checks that the stored best frequency agrees with the frequency of the highest tone-evoked rate computed from the spike times.

Before the tone (0 to 5 ms) the tone and silent trials do not differ; from 10 ms the tone trials fire far more (median difference 147 spikes/s at 10 to 15 ms, with tone above silent in 96% of recordings), and after the tone ends (57.75 ms) the tone trials fall below the silent ones, the offset suppression expected of auditory nerve fibres. The spikes and the trial table share a clock in the BF and RLF recordings, the ones whose timing matters for the claims (PH and CF use the same delay and duration). The stored BF is close to the raw peak of the spike-time frequency-response curve (the stored value is the peak of a smoothing spline, Methods), so the analysis table and the spike times describe the same recordings.

def local_maxima(h):
    out = []
    for i in range(len(h)):
        left = h[i - 1] if i > 0 else -1
        right = h[i + 1] if i < len(h) - 1 else np.inf   # the last bin is never a mode (criterion)
        if h[i] > left and h[i] > right:
            out.append(i)
    return out

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper's scripts read stored analysed outcomes from the MATLAB files. The NWB files keep only the best frequency, spontaneous rate and threshold per recording, so the characteristic frequency, vector strength and click latency are recomputed from the spike times with the paper's functions, without the experimenter's manual corrections for the CF. The click delay is not in the files and is assumed to be 7.75 ms. The ABR threshold is read from a character code. What was found about the dataset. All 1160 units and 104 animals of Table 1 are there, with ages, sexes, weights, oxygen, and the recording types of Fig. 7, and every count and mean of Table 1 matches. The ABR threshold is stored as a single character whose code point is the threshold. The CF, vector strength with its p-value, and click latencies that the paper describes as stored outcomes are not in the NWB files, and the click delay is missing from the trial table. Many units have within-trial inter-spike intervals below 0.6 ms; about half of these intervals lie on one spike waveform by the dip-ratio rule. Those below 0.1 ms (all on one waveform) are double detections of one spike. For those from 0.3 to 0.6 ms this is a plausible reading, not a demonstration, since the rule was checked only against pairs from 0.8 to 1.5 ms. The archive's summary lists the recordings as voltage clamp, while they are extracellular recordings of single fibres.
