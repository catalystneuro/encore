Reproducing Markowitz, Gillis, Jay et al. (2023) from DANDI:000559¶

Paper: Markowitz J.E., Gillis W.F., Jay M. et al., "Spontaneous behaviour is structured by reinforcement without explicit reward", Nature 614, 108-117 (2023), doi:10.1038/s41586-022-05611-2. The full text was used (open access, CC BY 4.0).

Dandiset: DANDI:000559, version 0.260528.0858. The paper cites no dandiset version (its Data availability names a Zenodo record). The archive has two published versions, 0.240502.0456 and 0.260528.0858; they hold the same 4,425 assets (identical asset ids, blobs and sizes, checked below) and differ only in dandiset-level metadata (anatomy terms, study targets, ORCIDs, related resources). The most recent one is pinned.

Every file is streamed from the archive; nothing is downloaded to disk. Claims and criteria are in claims/000559/10.1038-s41586-022-05611-2.yaml.

Code read before the criteria were written: the paper's analysis code, dattalab/dopamine-reinforces-spontaneous-behavior at commit 14ed714 (notebooks for every panel and the rl_analysis package; it reads the paper's Zenodo parquet files, not NWB), and the conversion code, catalystneuro/datta-lab-to-nwb at commit 47c681e, which shows how the NWB series were made from those parquet files. Where the Methods leave a parameter open, the analysis follows the paper's code; spontda.py says which.

Clock checks¶

dLight, speed and syllable labels share one frame clock in the files (the timestamps match, see above), but that does not show that the labels are aligned to the signals in time. Two contrasts check it. (1) The dLight waveform averaged around syllable onsets, with the true labels and with the labels shifted by a random 5 to 15 s within each session; the paper describes a dip around onset followed by a peak. (2) The fraction of stimulation pulses with an online onset of the target syllable in the 0.5 s before, with true and with shifted labels.

def future_usage(inst_area, rng, n_shuf=100, segments=5):
    # Mean over mouse-syllable pairs of the correlation between an instance's dLight peak and
    # the count of the same syllable among the next n syllables, with a within-group shuffle null.
    g = inst_area.sort_values(["session", "seq_pos"])
    sess, lab, pk, pos = g.session.values.astype(np.int64), g.syllable.values.astype(np.int64), g.peak.values, g.seq_pos.values.astype(np.int64)
    sess_len = g.groupby("session").seq_pos.transform("max").values + 1
    seg = np.minimum((g.t_rel.values / (1800.0 / segments)).astype(int), segments - 1)
    named = (lab < sd.N_NAMED_SYLLABLES) & np.isfinite(pk)
    # occurrences sorted by (session, syllable, position)
    key = sess * 100 + lab
    big = 10 ** 6
    comb = key * big + pos
    order = np.argsort(comb, kind="stable")
    comb_sorted = comb[order]
    n_upto_self = np.searchsorted(comb_sorted, comb, side="right")  # occurrences up to and including i
    gid = pd.factorize(sess * 1000 + lab * 10 + seg)[0]
    mouse_of_sess = g.groupby("session").mouse.first()
    # shuffled peaks within (session, syllable, segment) groups; only finite peaks are permuted,
    # among the instances that have one, so that the instances without a peak stay the same
    fin = np.isfinite(pk)
    by_group = np.lexsort((np.arange(len(gid)), ~fin, gid))
    shuffles = []
    for _ in range(n_shuf):
        o = np.lexsort((rng.random(len(gid)), ~fin, gid))
        x = np.empty(len(gid)); x[by_group] = pk[o]
        assert np.array_equal(np.isfinite(x), fin)
        shuffles.append(x)
    out = []
    for n in n_bins:
        idx = np.flatnonzero(named & (pos + n < sess_len))
        cnt = (np.searchsorted(comb_sorted, comb[idx] + n, side="right") - n_upto_self[idx]).astype(float)
        gg = pd.factorize(gid[idx])[0]
        k = gg.max() + 1
        first = pd.Series(np.arange(len(gg))).groupby(gg).first().values
        g_sess, g_syl = sess[idx][first], lab[idx][first]
        nn = np.bincount(gg, minlength=k).astype(float)
        sy, syy = np.bincount(gg, cnt, k), np.bincount(gg, cnt * cnt, k)
        def summarize(x):
            sx, sxx, sxy = np.bincount(gg, x, k), np.bincount(gg, x * x, k), np.bincount(gg, x * cnt, k)
            vx, vy = sxx - sx ** 2 / nn, syy - sy ** 2 / nn
            ok = (nn >= 3) & (vx > 1e-9 * np.maximum(sxx, 1)) & (vy > 1e-9 * np.maximum(syy, 1))
            r = np.full(k, np.nan)
            r[ok] = (sxy - sx * sy / nn)[ok] / np.sqrt(vx[ok] * vy[ok])
            n_groups.append(int(ok.sum()))
            df = pd.DataFrame(dict(session=g_sess, syllable=g_syl, r=r))
            df = df.groupby(["session", "syllable"]).r.mean().reset_index()
            df["mouse"] = df.session.map(mouse_of_sess)
            return df.groupby(["mouse", "syllable"]).r.mean()
        n_groups = []
        pp = summarize(pk[idx])
        null = np.array([summarize(s_[idx]).mean() for s_ in shuffles])
        pm = pp.groupby(level=0).mean()
        out.append(dict(n=n, mean_r=pp.mean(), n_pairs=int(pp.notna().sum()), groups_obs=n_groups[0],
                        groups_null_min=min(n_groups[1:]), groups_null_max=max(n_groups[1:]), null_mean=null.mean(),
                        null_95=np.percentile(null, 95), p=(null >= pp.mean()).mean(),
                        mice_positive=int((pm > 0).sum()), mice=len(pm)))
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

    z = (z - np.nanmean(z)) / np.nanstd(z)

def speed_change(d, align, post_s):
    t, v = d["t"], d["speed_mm_per_frame"] * sd.FS
    out = []
    for a in align:
        pre = v[(t >= a - 1) & (t < a)]
        post = v[(t >= a) & (t < a + post_s)]
        out.append(np.nanmean(post) - np.nanmean(pre) if np.isfinite(pre).any() and np.isfinite(post).any() else np.nan)
    return np.array(out)

def trials(r, d, pulsed, post_s, catch_exclusion=None, isolate=None):
    # isolate: keep only catch trials with no stimulation pulse (any pulse of a train) within this many s
    p_all = np.asarray(d["pulse_on"])
    p = p_all[np.r_[True, np.diff(p_all) > 1.0]] if pulsed else p_all
    lab = d["online"]
    on = sd.onsets(lab)
    ton = d["t"][on[lab[on] == r.targets[0]]]
    k = np.searchsorted(p, ton)
    nxt = np.where(k < len(p), p[np.minimum(k, len(p) - 1)], np.inf)
    catch = ton[(nxt - ton) > 0.5]
    if catch_exclusion is not None:
        prv = np.where(k > 0, p[np.maximum(k - 1, 0)], -np.inf)
        catch = catch[(catch - prv[(nxt - ton) > 0.5]) > catch_exclusion]
    if isolate is not None:
        j = np.searchsorted(p_all, catch)
        dprev = np.where(j > 0, catch - p_all[np.maximum(j - 1, 0)], np.inf)
        dnext = np.where(j < len(p_all), p_all[np.minimum(j, len(p_all) - 1)] - catch, np.inf)
        catch = catch[np.minimum(dprev, dnext) >= isolate]
    return speed_change(d, p, post_s), speed_change(d, catch + LAT, post_s)

def per_mouse(pulse_sel, pulsed, post_s, catch_exclusion=None, isolate=None):
    rows = []
    for mouse, g in C[C.stim & pulse_sel & C.passes].groupby("mouse"):
        st, ca, frac = [], [], []
        for i, r in g.iterrows():
            a, b = trials(r, cl[i], pulsed, post_s, catch_exclusion, isolate)
            st.append(a); ca.append(b); frac.append(len(a) / (len(a) + len(b)))

How the analysis differs from the paper's:

  • dLight is re-referenced and z-scored here from the stored dF/F series, following the Methods; the paper's analyses used a pre-computed z-scored trace that is not in the files.
  • The dandiset holds fewer photometry sessions than the paper's code used: 177 DLS sessions without stimulation are used here (224 files, 210 with syllables, 193 after leaving out habituation sessions, 177 passing the quality criteria), where the Methods give 379 experiments.
  • Habituation sessions (17 DLS, 1 DMS) are left out of the photometry analyses, as they are of the closed-loop analyses; the Methods do not say whether the paper's experiments include them.
  • Fig. 2e: five equal time segments stand in for the paper's five behavioural segments, and r is compared with shuffles directly instead of being z-scored to them.
  • Fig. 2b and 2i: 20 repeats of fivefold cross-validation and 200 shuffles, where the paper used 100 repeats and 1,000 shuffles; a plain Shannon entropy where the code used a Dirichlet estimate.
  • Fig. 1l: a 100-tree sklearn random forest (at least 5 instances per leaf) on 1,000 instances per syllable and one label shuffle, where the paper used cuML with 1,000 trees on all instances and 100 shuffles.
  • Fig. 3: the baseline and post days are identified from session dates and stimulation, as the files give no session number or session type.
  • Fig. 3l and 3m: catch trials are a substitute (target onsets with no pulse in the next 0.5 s), which covers more detections than the paper's 25 % and fails in no-opsin controls.

What was found about the dandiset:

  • It holds the 14 DLS dLight mice, 8 DMS dLight mice, 20 Opto-DA mice and 12 no-opsin controls of the paper, and the closed-loop sessions reproduce the paper's Fig. 3e and 3f statistics closely.
  • Catch trials of the closed-loop experiments are not marked, nor are the opsin and genotype of each mouse; Opto-DA and control mice can be told apart only by the stimulation site.
  • Of the dLight+ChrimsonR mice, only the 250-ms sessions are present, not the 2-s and pulsed sessions the Methods describe; DLS photometry files without stimulation number 224 (177 usable), where the Methods give 379 experiments.
  • 14 files hold empty syllable series; 28 photometry and 547 closed-loop files store their frames out of time order (in all but two, one backward step, at least 0.95 of the session length in 558 of 575), and two closed-loop files of mouse 3155 store every frame twice; 5 files with stimulation are described as recording sessions; every closed-loop file states a 635-nm stimulator, including those of Ai32 mice stimulated with 470-nm light; the dLight-only mice carry the subject description of the DAT-IRES-Cre and Ai32 mice.
  • No food-reward sessions or consumption times are in the dandiset.
