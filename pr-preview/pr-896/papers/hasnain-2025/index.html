Summary: The paper's main findings on layer 5 extratelencephalic neurons, that they resemble other motor cortex neurons in selectivity and subspace preference, could not be tested, as the paper's own recordings are not public. On the reused recordings behind Figure 4, the session selection could not be recovered, leaving 5 ALM and medulla sessions where the paper has 16. Medullary movement-null activity explained less variance than movement-potent activity under only one of several readings, and medullary selectivity, similar null and potent variance in ALM, and ALM patterns absent from the medulla held in part. Prediction of medullary null activity from ALM and its lower dimension did not reproduce; the decoder as described overfit, and the paper leaves its method open.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Paper inconsistentThe paper gives two counts for the ALM and medulla sessions of the MAP data (16 sessions in the Results and the Figure 4 statistics, 11 sessions from 7 mice in the Methods), two counts for the bilateral ALM units (1116 in the Results, 2016 in the Methods), and two decoding windows (the previous 40 ms in the Results, t and 8 preceding 5 ms bins, 45 ms, in the Methods).
  • Methods not availableCross-region decoding (Figure 4d). With ridge lambda = 0.01 and the source projections at t and 8 preceding bins of 20 ms-smoothed rates, the predictors are nearly collinear and held-out R2 is far below zero on these data, falling with the number of predictors per training trial. Was lambda applied to standardized predictors, was R2 computed on held-out single trials at each bin and over which variance (the across-trial variance at each bin or over the whole span), and were trials or bins pooled before the fit?
  • Methods not availableMovement and stationary bins in the MAP data (Figure 4c to f). How was the hand-set threshold placed, were the keypoints smoothed before taking velocity, and do supra-threshold runs shorter than 100 ms count as moving? The medulla null against potent result here holds only when they count as moving.
  • Methods not availableMAP trials and units. Were photostimulation, early-lick and ignore trials used, and the trials with a 1.8 s or 0.3 s delay? Is the 'good' label of the selection the `classification` or the `unit_quality` column, with CCF regions assigned to which units? Here Figure 4c (ALM) is reproduced and Figure 4e is reproduced under N2 with `unit_quality`, and neither with `classification`.
  • Methods not availableVariance explained and dimensionality (Figure 4c, e). Which covariance and which d normalise the variance explained of each subspace (the reported null and potent values add to the total, which the Methods' formula does not give), and which measure of dimensionality, at which scale, does Figure 4e use?
  • Methods not availableThe baseline z-score (Methods, Subspace identification) divides by the baseline s.d. across trials; for MAP units with near-silent baselines this gives values up to 278 s.d. among units classified good and above 50000 among units with `unit_quality` good. Were such units excluded, or was a floor applied?

Questions about the claims 2

Where the author or a verifier believed the claims file misread the paper, they asked, citing the paper, and a reader who had not seen the data answered from the paper alone, amending the claims file where it was wrong.   • openasked 2026-11-21about fig3-1c-alm-early-residual

    The criterion scores the early-window residual in "the M4, M5 window", which reads as one 20 to 120 ms window, and the claims file does not list the binning as open. The Methods divide the primary window into 50 ms bins, score the same pairs in the early window, and compute the distance curve in 50 ms bins. Should the criterion name the binning of the early window as open, with readings (a) one 100 ms window and (b) two 50 ms bins with the per-bin distances averaged as for the pre-lick window (M9)?

    Passage. The primary window was the 200 ms before the lick, divided into 50 ms bins. The same pairs were scored in the early window (Methods, Matched distance); Distance used 50 ms bins (Methods, Time courses).

    Why it matters. The outcome turns on it. On the matched pairs the ALM early residual is 12.59 (SEM 2.0) in two 50 ms bins, within 12.6 +- 2.0 (reproduced), and 18.36 (SEM 2.7) as one 100 ms window, outside tolerance (partially reproduced). The attempt adopts the binned reading as an assumption. The verifier of round 1 reported that the claims file misses this open detail.

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked 2026-11-21about fig2d-choice-share-flat

    The reproduced clause uses the SEM as the line between "rose" and "stayed flat" (the pre-lick mean exceeds the early mean by more than its SEM). The partial clause, "the mouse-level choice fraction does not rise in at least four regions", does not say whether the same SEM rule applies or whether any increase of the mean counts as a rise. Which is meant?

    Passage. Before the lick, the share fell or stayed flat in four regions and rose only in medulla, from 0.036 to 0.058 (Results, Lick decoding rises without a matching rise on the choice axis, paragraph 2).

    Why it matters. In midbrain the mean rises by 0.006, less than its SEM of 0.006. With the SEM rule four regions do not rise and the outcome is partially reproduced; counting any increase as a rise, three regions do not rise and the outcome is not reproduced. The attempt applies the criterion's own SEM rule to both clauses, as an assumption. The verifier of round 1 reported the ambiguity.

  • The claims file's U1 is applied with `classification` as the 'good' label (G1), the only label whose units carry a CCF annotation.
