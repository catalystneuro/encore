Encore for journals

Your journal asks authors to deposit their data. Encore tells you whether what they deposited is enough to support the paper.

In the public registry of 307 papers, 36% of the claims could not be tested at all, and for 67% of those the data the claim rests on were not in the dataset. A check finds this while the authors can still fix it.

Request a pilot How a check is made

What an editor receives

  • Each key finding of the paper as a claim, with an outcome: reproduced, partially reproduced, not reproduced, or blocked.
  • Each problem found in the dataset, such as missing data, errors in the metadata, or a selection of units or sessions the paper does not document.
  • The notebook that ran each test, which the authors or a reviewer can run again.
  • For a full check, the verdict of an independent verifier on every outcome.

Four ways to work with us

A pilot on your own papers

Send us a few papers with deposited data, published or in review. We run a full check on each and return the reports, so you see what a check finds on your journal's papers before you decide anything.

Checks during review

At the stage of review you choose, your editorial office sends us the manuscript and the location of its data. We return the report for the editor to share with the reviewers and the authors. A quick check takes one to three hours of computing. A full check, with two rounds of independent verification, takes several hours more.

A public record for published papers

For accepted papers, we add the check to the public registry. The paper gets a page with every claim, its outcome and the code behind it, and a badge for the article page that links there. Authors can respond, and their response is shown beside the check.

Run it yourselves

The encore command is open source and runs on your own machines, with your own Claude account, on files in a directory, files at a URL, or a draft dataset in an archive. We help your team set it up.

Confidential manuscripts

A manuscript under review does not go through the public registry. We run its check with the encore command on our own machines, and the result stays there until we send it to you. The agents' commands run in a sandbox that can write only inside the run's directory and can reach only the archives, Python's package index, GitHub and doi.org.

The manuscript and the data are sent to Anthropic's models under its commercial terms. Under those terms Anthropic does not train models on them and keeps them for 30 days (Anthropic's data usage policy). Nothing goes on the public registry unless the journal and the authors ask for it.

If the manuscript must stay on your own systems, your team can run the same check itself.

What a check does and does not say

A failure to reproduce is not a finding that the paper is wrong. A check reports what one attempt could recover from the deposited data, and the agents that make it can be mistaken.

A blocked claim usually points to data the authors still need to deposit. A claim that did not reproduce is a question to put to the authors, and the report gives them the notebook that ran the test. The editor and the reviewers decide what follows. More on reading outcomes

Most checks so far are of neuroscience papers, on data in the DANDI Archive, CRCNS.org, Dryad, EBRAINS, Figshare, OpenNeuro and SPARC. The registry also holds checks of papers in ecology, microbiology, political science, food safety and planetary science, and we take papers from other fields.
