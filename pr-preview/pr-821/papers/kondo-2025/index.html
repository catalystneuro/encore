A multimodal dataset linking wide-field calcium imaging to behavior changes in mice during an operant lever-pull task

Masashi Kondo, Keisuke Sehara, Rie Harukuni, Ryo Aoki, Shoya Sugimoto, Yasuhiro R. Tanaka, M Matsuzaki, Ken Nakae

Outcomes of 22 claims

  • ✓15 reproduced
  • ◐1 partially reproduced
  • ⊘6 blocked

Summary: Most of the paper's main findings held up in this data. The response rate to the tone rose over training (median 0.556 to 0.856), the mice split into two clusters by their final pull duration, visual and whisker responses peaked in right VISp and left barrel cortex, and the tracked lever tip followed the encoder. Whether the success rate stayed constant, and matched between clusters late in training, could not be tested, because the paper gives no test and the readings disagree. The Figure 6 statements on movement order and on areas tracking the pupil could not be tested, as the example session is not identified. The landmark and DeepLabCut validations need manual annotations that are not deposited.

  • openasked 2026-11-10about fig6b-movement-order

    The criterion's onset rule (the first time the trial average leaves the mean +/- 2 or 3 s.d. of the -1 to -0.5 s window) is not in the paper, and it cannot tell movement from noise. On synthetic trial averages with no movement it gives an onset before 0 in 66% (white noise) to 85% (smooth noise) of runs at 2 s.d., and in 28% to 58% at 3 s.d. Requiring the crossing to last 100 ms brings white noise down to 3%, but smooth noise stays at 42%. The paper judges the order from the traces of Figure 6b. Should the criterion judge onset some other way, for example against a threshold scaled to the trace's range, or by the displacement at a fixed time before 0 against the baseline spread?

    Passage. "While some body parts, such as forepaws and nose, began moving before the lever-pull onset, others, including jaw and pupil diameter, moved afterward at least in this session" (Technical Validation, paragraph 5; Figure 6b). The Methods give no onset rule.

    Why it matters. Under the rule as written every keypoint's grand onset is -0.5 s, the first sample after the baseline window, so the test says nothing about the order. The claim is blocked either way, as the example session is not identified, but a sound rule would show whether the order fails in the mean: the grand jaw displacement is 6.4 pixels at -0.2 s against a baseline s.d. of 0.27, so the jaw moves before the pull in the mean over sessions.

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked 2026-11-10about fig4d-auditory-map

    The criterion is reproduced when the 80th-percentile region of the mean map over 44 ROIs includes a parietal ROI (VISa, VISrl or PTLp, either hemisphere) and more than one ROI. That region always holds about 9 ROIs, so "more than one ROI" always holds, and a random set of 9 ROIs includes one of the 4 parietal ROIs with probability 0.61. The criterion gives no chance level. Should it compare the parietal response with a null, such as the response at shifted stimulus onsets or a random set of ROIs?

    Passage. "Auditory stimuli evoked spreading activity, including a multimodal sensory area in the parietal cortex" (Technical Validation, paragraph 3; Figure 4d).

    Why it matters. The claim is reproduced under the criterion as written (left VISa is in the region under every reading), but the pass carries little evidence. With a null the outcome could differ: the code-2 response of VISa is larger at the true onsets than at onsets shifted by 3 or 6 s in only 10 of 19 mice.

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked 2026-11-10about fig6b-movement-order

    The criterion takes the baseline of each displacement trace from -1 to -0.5 s before lever-pull onset. In the grand traces that window already holds movement: the jaw displacement is 0.54 pixels at -1 s, 0.05 at -0.7 s and 1.46 at -0.5 s, so the trace is V-shaped across the window and any rule built on it fires at -0.5 s, the first sample after it. Should the baseline window be earlier, for example before the tone, or should onset be judged without a pre-pull baseline?

    Passage. "While some body parts, such as forepaws and nose, began moving before the lever-pull onset, others, including jaw and pupil diameter, moved afterward at least in this session" (Technical Validation, paragraph 5; Figure 6b). The Methods give no baseline window.

    Why it matters. Raised by the second verification round. Every keypoint's grand onset is -0.5 s under every onset rule tried, so the order cannot be read from onsets measured against this window. The claim is blocked either way, as the example session is not identified.

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked 2026-11-10about fig6b-motor-somatosensory-after-reward

    The claim says MOs activity "returned quickly to baseline" after the pull, while MOp, SSp-ul and SSp-n stayed slightly above baseline after reward delivery. The criterion tests only that MOs's post-reward mean minus baseline is smaller than those of the three other areas and that theirs are above baseline. Should it also test that MOs returns to its baseline, for example that its post-reward mean is within the spread of its baseline?

    Passage. "The secondary motor cortex (MOs) quickly recovered to the baseline level. In contrast, the primary motor (MOp) and somatosensory cortex (SSpul and SSpn) showed slightly higher activity than the baseline level after the reward delivery" (Technical Validation, paragraph 5; Figure 6b).

    Why it matters. Raised by the second verification round. The claim is reproduced under the criterion as written, but in the mean over sessions MOs stays above its baseline (0.0131 to 0.0140 in the 0.5 to 2 s after reward), about half of SSp-n's 0.0244 to 0.0266, so the return to baseline is not shown.

    No reader has answered it yet.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • F_B0 and the session mean are taken over 16 evenly spaced blocks of 10 consecutive blue frames (160 of 54000), not over every frame; reading every frame of every session does not fit the time limit. The error of the subsample is measured on four sessions read frame by frame.methods-no-signal-degradation
  • Only the ROI reading was run. The pixel-map reading needs 60 to 120 frames around each of about 30 stimuli per modality for 25 sessions; a spread frame takes about 0.3 s to reach over the network, which does not fit the time limit with the other claims.fig4d-visual-map
  • In 6 of 25 first sessions there is no stim_visual channel; there the visual train is on the stim_whisker channel and coded 1, so the code-1 reading uses all 25 mice and the channel reading 19.fig4d-visual-map
  • The files do not name the trial codes. Code 3 is on the stim_whisker channel in the 19 sessions with three channels and on the stim_auditory channel in the other six; the code reading, the channel over the 19 sessions, and the channel over all 25 are shown and agree.fig4d-tactile-map
  • Only the ROI reading was run (see fig4d-visual-map). Code 2 exists only in 19 first sessions; the six sessions of the earliest cohort have two stimulus types.fig4d-auditory-map
  • The peak detection and the K-means settings (scipy.signal.find_peaks; scikit-learn KMeans with 10 initialisations, random_state 0) are not given in the paper; the claims file names find_peaks.figs2b-no-aberrant-transients
  • The lever-tip keypoint is rightbartip, which the paper's linked code describes as the tip of the lever (bdbc-nwb-explorer data/body_video.csv); the NWB files give no description.fig5f-lever-tip-correlation
  • The criterion's "displacement ... trial average" was run both as the trial average of each trial's displacement and as the displacement of the trial-averaged position; they agree.fig6b-movement-order
  • Pupil diameters that are not finite, not positive or above 300 pixels are set missing.fig6b-movement-order
  • A variant of the onset rule that requires the trace to stay outside the band for at least 100 ms (3 samples) is also shown, an assumption added after the first verification round; it changes no onset of the grand traces. The outcome is blocked under every rule, so it does not rest on this assumption and is not marked assumed.fig6b-movement-order
