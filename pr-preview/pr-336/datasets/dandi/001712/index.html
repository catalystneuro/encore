  • ✓ReproducedFigure 2B and 2C; Results, Nrxn1α mice exhibit slower adaptation to shifting reward contingencies, paragraph 1

    Mice use the block prior; rightward choices are more frequent in right-likely blocks than in left-likely blocks, most clearly when contrast is low, shifting the psychometric curve toward the likely side (shown for WT, and present but smaller in KO).

    This reliance on priors is demonstrated in WT animals by a pronounced shift in the change of rightward choices conditioned by block-type (right-likely - left-likely blocks) and in their psychometric curves towards the higher probability stimulus side ...   • ✓ReproducedFigure 2E to 2G; Results, Nrxn1α mice exhibit slower adaptation to shifting reward contingencies, paragraph 1

    After a block switch, mice adapt their choices toward the newly likely side, and the proportion of choices toward the block side rises from an initial value A to an asymptote B with a time constant tau.

    Mean proportion of probability of choices towards block side (e.g. right choices during right likely block and left choices for left likely block). WT mice adapt more strongly and consistently than KO mice

    Figure 2E to 2G; Results, Nrxn1α mice exhibit slower adaptation to shifting reward contingencies, paragraph 1

    Over 14 switches between biased blocks, choices toward the new block side are 0.543 in trials 0 to 4 and 0.827 in trials 20 to 39 (one-sided Fisher p = 2.7e-06). The fit gives A = 0.480, B = 1.000 (at its bound) and tau = 25.12 trials (SE 16.14), so tau is poorly determined. Resampling the switches gives 95% intervals of 0.279 to 0.643 for A, 0.836 to 1.000 for B and 6.41 to 38.37 trials for tau; B exceeds A in every draw and tau is 40 or more in 0.018 of them. A maximum-likelihood fit on single trials gives A = 0.479, B = 1.000, tau = 25.03. At contrasts of 12.5% or less, 0.452 against 0.820, tau 21.57.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper's parametric curve is not given; B - (B - A) exp(-t / tau) over trials 0 to 39, all contrasts, was chosen here.

    Reaction time (time to first movement) is longer at lower contrasts in both genotypes.

    Time to first movement (reaction time) as a function of contrast. Both genotypes showed longer latencies at lower contrasts

    Figure 1E

    Over 750 trials with a movement onset after the stimulus, the Spearman correlation of absolute contrast with reaction time is -0.053 (p = 0.15); the median at 0% (0.089 s) exceeds that at 100% (0.081 s), the second part of the criterion. Medians are 0.081 to 0.095 s at every contrast, and 161 trials have no movement onset after the stimulus (149 with an onset at or before it and 12 with no onset). With reaction times above 0.15 s only (226 trials) the correlation is -0.226 (p = 0.00062), so the relation appears among slower responses. Of the 899 onsets, 149 fall at or before the stimulus and 346 within 0.08 s after it, so many may mark movements begun before the stimulus could guide them. The response time (choice registration minus stimulus onset) is shorter at higher contrast over all 911 choice trials (rho -0.134, p = 5.2e-05; medians 0.326 s at 0% and 0.281 s at 100%), a variant that does not decide the outcome. The paper's text calls reaction time the time to first movement and does not say how that movement is detected or how earlier movements are treated.

    • Trials with a movement onset at or before the stimulus onset are left out; the paper does not say how it treats them.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds one session of one mouse (sub-FD-28). The paper analyses 10 sessions from each of 11 wild-type and 8 Nrxn1a knockout mice. Eleven of the paper's findings, all comparisons between genotypes, cannot be tested.
  • Description missingThe genotype of the mouse is not given in the NWB files, the dandiset metadata, or the public IBL database (subject FD_28 has an empty genotype list, from a query made by hand during the attempt). The subject description is the paper's general description of the cross. A comment in the conversion script lists the session under knockout sessions, but next to a note that the list is a placeholder.
  • Data missingNeither NWB file holds the cortical landmarks or the registration to the Allen atlas that the conversion code's documentation describes for the processed file (lab_meta_data localization and atlas_registration), so no pixel can be assigned to a cortical area.
  • Metadata errorBoth imaging planes give the indicator as GCaMP6f; the paper's Methods and the file's keywords give GCaMP6s (CamKII-tTA x TRE-GCaMP6s).
  • Metadata errorThe raw file's imaging planes state an imaging rate of 15 Hz; the timestamps give 31.2 Hz per channel (32.03 ms intervals), and the Methods give 30 Hz.
  • Metadata errorThe dandiset's keywords and description name "CNTNALP2", which is neither the gene of the paper and the files (Nrxn1a) nor a correct spelling of Cntnap2.
  • Metadata errorThe wheel series state that counter-clockwise is positive, but trials whose choice is labelled counter_clockwise turn the wheel with negative velocity (95.5% of them 0.1 to 0.3 s after movement onset). The choice labels agree with the rewards, so the sign convention of the wheel or of the labels is misdescribed.
  • Metadata errorThe wheel movement onset is described as the first movement after the go cue, but 151 of 899 onsets precede the go cue (by up to 0.18 s).
  • Description missingThe ROI motion energy series give the ROI position in pixels but not the body part it covers; the paper's model uses whisker-pad and body motion energy.
  • Description missingThe SVD spatial components cover the whole 540 x 640 frame with no brain mask, and the corners of the frame carry large values in stimulus-evoked maps.
  • OtherThe dandiset has no published version; only the draft exists, so no version can be cited or pinned.
  • Methods not availableThe paper's analysis code is to be made public on publication. The encoding code it links (churchlandlab/chiCa) implements the model for single-cell data in another task, not the widefield analysis.
  • Paper inconsistentIn the Figure 3 legend the correlation panel is labelled (E) twice and (D) is missing, the correlation panel's legend says the KO minus WT matrix shows reduced inter-regional correlations while the Results say correlations are increased, and the legend of panel C describes variance difference maps, as does panel E.

Reproduction Prior use and cortical dynamics in a Nrxn1a mouse model (Davatolhagh et al. 2025)

Tests the findings of Davatolhagh et al. (2025) that can be tested on dandiset 001712, which holds one widefield imaging and behaviour session of one mouse of unstated genotype in the IBL biased choice task. It tests the psychometric curve, reaction times, the use of the block prior, adaptation after block switches, lateralized visual responses and a linear encoding model of the imaging with task and movement predictors. The comparisons between knockout and wild-type mice cannot be tested. All files
