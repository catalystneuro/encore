Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • Rests on the averaging of repeated measurements assumed in table2-fit-statistics.fig1-linear-components fig2-changing-field
  • The paper does not say how the repeated measurements of a step (2 to 19 per step) enter Table 2. Assumed here that they are averaged as vectors, the usual treatment of repeated measurements and the reading under which the MADs of six specimens come within 0.3 deg of Table 2. The first measurement of each step is shown as the other reading and gives a different outcome. The deposit's Supplementary Figure 1 supports averaging; it takes NRM as the mean of the rows' moments, not the moment of the mean vector, which changes R2 by at most 0.08 here and is shown as a second reading.table2-fit-statistics
  • MAD is Kirschvink's principal component measure under the claims file's three readings, and the pTRM check difference is taken on the TRM-gained axis, where Figures 1 and 2 plot the checks (legends, "red triangles represent partial thermal remanent magnetization checks"). The paper gives no formula for either; it names PmagPy and "standard paleointensity definitions" (Technical Validation).table2-fit-statistics
  • ss6's 250.1 step holds 19 rows in two groups of directions (inclination near 71 and near -13 deg); all are averaged, as for every step. Ten other steps of six glass sheets also hold two groups of directions more than 45 deg apart (s11 360.1, 550 and 550.1, ss10 250 and 360, ss18 100, ss24 NRM and 550, ss29 175.1, ss36 450.1) and are averaged the same way.table2-fit-statistics
  • Repeated measurements averaged as in table2-fit-statistics; first measurements give the same counts.table2-no-fit
  • The great circle is fit through the origin (Kirschvink's plane MAD), a standard choice; the paper gives no fitting method.fig2-changing-field
  • The p-value of Watson's test is computed exactly by simulation (200000 sets of uniform directions, fixed seeds), a standard form of the test the claims file names.glass-directions-not-random
  • The example's crystal name is read as a misprint for 12040g1, as the criterion's reading (b) allows; as written, for 14053g1, the example does not match the data.example-14053g1-threshold
  • The cutoff of 2 on the ratio is the claims file's, not the paper's; the largest ratio, 1.17, is far from it. The cutoff fails a crystal only when the 40 uT TRM exceeds four times the 20 uT TRM, although the criterion's text says "about twice". Without the factor of two, TRM40/TRM20 is 1.54 to 2.35 and three crystals are above 2 (12053g1 2.35, 14053g3 2.08, 12053g2 2.06), so a cutoff of 2 on that ratio would fail reading (b) for them and no reading would pass every crystal. This is asked of a reader in questions/figshare-24639285.yaml; the claim is tested as the file has it.no-alteration-recording-efficiency
