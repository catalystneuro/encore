ed9b-octant-decoding¶

Position within the maze can be decoded with octant resolution from the activity, above decoders trained on shuffled data.

Criterion: eight octants (direction not coded), lambda 6.75e-8, the average matrix of 200 fits; the null is 1,000 decoders on their own shuffles, under the readings (n1) one fit per shuffle and (n2) the average of 200 fits per shuffle. Reproduced when the real median accuracy exceeds every null value (p < 0.001) and exceeds chance (1/8) under both; not reproduced when it fails under both; otherwise methods_underspecified.

ed9c-local-decoding-map¶

Decoders built from the 32 footprints closest to a point decode octant position significantly in parts of the field of view, with accuracy that varies across it.

Criterion: map points every 5 image pixels over rows 1 to 608 and columns 140 to 700; a point is used when at least 32 footprints lie within 270 um (45 pixels at 5.94 um, or 50 at 5.35); at each, the average matrix of 40 fits on its 32 nearest footprints; the null is 1,000 decoders on shuffled data from 32 random footprints (one fit or 40 each); Benjamini-Hochberg 5%. It depends on ed9b-octant-decoding. Its cost is measured here.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. All 2948 footprints are used where the claims name the 2,382 curated ones, which the dandiset does not mark; position is tracked from the behaviour video with the code's rule; MATLAB's linear SVMs are replaced by scikit-learn's SGD (10 passes, for more than 100 predictors) and LinearSVC (exact, for 100 or fewer), as MATLAB's defaults choose. Only null reading (n1) is run in full; (n2) and the analyses that depend on these claims are costed, not run (more n2 decoders and reduced runs are in the added notebooks). The first attempt (-01) judged ed9b and ed9c against decoders with shifted labels; the claims file now uses the paper's null, shuffled activity, with two readings of a null decoder.
