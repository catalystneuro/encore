Mah et al. 2023 · Nature Communications

Distinct value computations support rapid sequential decisions

doi:10.1038/s41467-023-43250-x · paper key mah-2023

Datasets

  • DANDI:001169

The behavioural findings hold up: rats wait less and initiate trials faster in high-reward blocks, and wait times follow the inferred block while initiation times follow recent rewards. The model findings hold only in part, as the paper's fitting code is not public: in the agent's refits the inferential model's wait times depend on the previous offer, and rats split by inference quality lack the claimed dissociation. Comparing inferential and retrospective models was too large to run. That wait times become block-sensitive only with training could not be tested, as the dataset holds only sessions passing the inclusion criterion. With a cue before initiation, the previous block still shapes initiation times, but the previous-reward effect fails the population test.

  • ✓Reproduced The dataset comes from 291 rats, with a median of 33,493 behavioral trials and 71 sessions per rat.
  • ✓Reproduced In mixed blocks, rats wait longer for larger offered rewards; mean wait time on catch trials rises with offer across 5, 10, 20, 40 and 80 uL.
  • ✓Reproduced Rats wait about 10% less for 20 uL in high blocks than in low blocks, across the population.
  • ✓Reproduced Rats initiate trials more quickly in high blocks than in low blocks, across the population.
  • ✓Reproduced The block sensitivity of trial initiation times comes from trials after unrewarded trials; after rewarded trials initiation times do not differ between high and low blocks in the same direction.
  • ✓Reproduced Wait times show no major differences after rewarded and after unrewarded trials.
  • ✓Reproduced After a transition into a mixed block, wait times converge to a common value whether the previous block was high or low, while trial initiation times in mixed blocks keep depending on the previous block (slower after high blocks, faster after low blocks).
  • ✓Reproduced Longer timescale contrast effects also appear at transitions from mixed into high or low blocks for trial initiation times, but not for wait times.
  • ✓Reproduced In mixed blocks, wait times are explained by the current offer and barely by previous offers, while trial initiation times reflect an exponentially decaying effect of previous rewards (larger recent rewards, faster initiation).
  • ✓Reproduced The time constants of exponential fits to the previous trial coefficients differ between trial initiation times and wait times, with longer time constants for initiation times (wait time time constants are mostly 0).
  • ✓Reproduced Across rats, the time constants for wait times and trial initiation times are not correlated.
  • ✓Reproduced Rats split by fast or slow temporal integration of initiation times (tau below the 20th or above the 80th percentile) differ in their initiation time regression coefficients but not in their wait time coefficients.
  • ✓Reproduced In mixed blocks, wait times for 20 uL do not depend on whether the previous offer was smaller or larger than 20 uL, for 87% of rats (253 of 291) and for the population.
  • ✓Reproduced In mixed blocks, trial initiation times are slower after offers smaller than 20 uL than after offers larger than 20 uL, significantly so for 89% of rats (256 of 291) and for the population.
  • ✓Reproduced Sensitivity to the previous offer is larger for trial initiation times than for wait times across rats.
  • ✓Reproduced Satiety effects on trial initiation times are modest, and the results, including the dynamics at block transitions, do not change qualitatively without detrending.
  • ✓Reproduced Rats' wait times in mixed blocks depend in a graded way on the catch probability, as the wait time model predicts (shorter waits at higher catch probability).
  • ◐Partially reproduced A retrospective model with a learning rate scaled by changes in the inferred block posterior captures the slow and fast dynamics of trial initiation times at block transitions and their dependence on previous rewards, while a static learning rate or an unsigned prediction error gain does not.
  • ✓Reproduced In simulations of a Bayes optimal observer, hearing the reward offer reduces the uncertainty of beliefs about the hidden block (posterior variance below prior variance).
  • ✓Reproduced Male and female rats do not differ in their wait time ratios or trial initiation time ratios.
  • ✓Reproduced Without removing wait times more than 1 SD above the pooled mean, the contextual effects on wait times are qualitatively similar, with longer average wait times, mainly in low blocks.

  • ✓Reproduced With the reward cue played before trial initiation, trial initiation times become sensitive to the offered reward.
