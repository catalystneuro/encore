# Light and dark tuning, aligned with the light-on cross-validated shifts (Methods).
def light_dark_aligned(i):
    r = recs[i]
    lt = rsc.trial_tuning(r["sp"], r["heading"], r["seg_l"])
    dt = rsc.trial_tuning(r["sp"], r["heading"], r["seg_d"])
    if lt.shape[2] == 0:
        return None, None
    sh = rsc.cv_shifts(lt) if lt.shape[2] > 1 else (rsc.SHIFT_POINT - lt[:, :, 0].argmax(1))[:, None]
    la = rsc.apply_shifts(lt, sh).mean(2)
    # light-off trials take the shift of the mean light-on curve (all light trials)
    sh_all = (rsc.SHIFT_POINT - lt.mean(2).argmax(1))[:, None]
    da = rsc.apply_shifts(dt, np.repeat(sh_all, max(dt.shape[2], 1), 1)).mean(2) if dt.shape[2] else np.full_like(la, np.nan)
    return la, da

aligned = [light_dark_aligned(i) for i in range(len(recs))]

RSC somata: Gaussian fits and clustering¶

For each soma, light-on and light-off curves aligned with the light-on shifts are rescaled together to 0 to 1 and each fitted with the two-term Gaussian (code LightDarkExperiment.calculateTuningFits2); features are a1, a2, c1, c2 of both fits. Cells are (a) the heading-selective somata, (b) all somata.

    L, D, mice, recid = [], [], [], []

Clustering the Gaussian-fit coefficients of RSC somatic tuning curves in light-on and light-off conditions gives an optimum of three functional classes (heading, landmark and alignment cells), though the responses span a continuum.

fig5-class-proportions¶

12.1% of RSC neurons are tuned in the dual-cue experiment; of these, landmark cells are the plurality (33%), followed by alignment cells (28%) and heading cells (22%).

Three-component GMM on the features of reading (a); outlier rule (a) squared Mahalanobis distance to the nearest component beyond the chi-square 95th percentile (8 df) goes to a null cluster, or (b) none; archetypal light and dark curves from each component's mean coefficients (b1 = pi/2, b2 = 3 pi/2, a0 = 0); each cell to the archetype pair with the smallest summed cosine distance. Classes are named from the archetypes: heading is the one whose light-on archetype is the least bimodal (a2/a1), and of the other two, landmark is the one with the weaker light-off archetype. Shares are given (i) as the mean over recordings, (ii) the mean over mice, (iii) pooled.

def archetype(coef):
    a1, a2, c1, c2 = coef
    x = rsc.X
    return a1 * np.exp(-((x - np.pi / 2) / c1) ** 2) + a2 * np.exp(-((x - 3 * np.pi / 2) / c2) ** 2)

def cosdist(A, b):
    with np.errstate(invalid="ignore", divide="ignore"):
        return 1 - (A @ b) / (np.linalg.norm(A, axis=1) * np.linalg.norm(b))

fig5e-alignment-peaks-coregistered¶

In alignment cells the single light-off peak is always at the location of one of the two light-on peaks, so landmark and heading responses are coregistered.

Per alignment cell (no outlier step), offset of the light-off peak bin from the nearer of the two light-on peaks (the peak bin, and the peak bin of the other half circle), V-test against 0.

Taking the nearer of two peaks 180 degrees apart folds the offsets into +-90 degrees, so the V-test against 0 is positive even for light-off peaks unrelated to the light-on curve. The V of each class is therefore compared with two nulls: light-off peaks paired at random with other cells' light-on curves within the class, and light-off peaks drawn uniformly.

def peak_offsets(L, D):
    out = []
    for l, d in zip(L, D):
        p1 = int(np.argmax(l))
        other = [(p1 + 30 + k) % 60 for k in range(-15, 16)]
        p2 = other[int(np.argmax(l[other]))]
        pd_ = int(np.argmax(d))
        o = [rsc.wrap180((pd_ - p) * 6) for p in (p1, p2)]
        out.append(o[int(np.argmin(np.abs(o)))])
    return np.array(out)

# Null for V: taking the nearer of two peaks 180 degrees apart folds every offset into +-90, which
# by itself gives V > 0. (1) light-off peaks of the class paired with the light-on curves of other
# cells of the class at random; (2) light-off peaks drawn uniformly over the 60 bins.
def nearer(p1, p2, pdk):
    o1, o2 = rsc.wrap180((pdk - p1) * 6), rsc.wrap180((pdk - p2) * 6)
    return np.where(np.abs(o1) <= np.abs(o2), o1, o2)

How the analysis differs from the paper's: inferred spikes from raw fluorescence without neuropil correction; selection with the centred fit; Gaussian fits reimplemented in numpy; light-off curves take the shift of the mean light-on curve (leaveOneOutAligner is not public); the GMM, LDA and mixed models are scikit-learn and statsmodels in place of MATLAB; the class names come from a rule on the archetypes stated above. The classes differ from the paper's, so the claims that use them test other cell groups.

What was found about the dataset: 35 dual-cue soma recordings where the paper reports 36; no light-on and light-off labels; no matching of ROIs between the standard and the visual-cue-only recordings of Figure 5K.
