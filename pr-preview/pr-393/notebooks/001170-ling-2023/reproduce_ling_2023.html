Reproducing Ling et al. (2023/2024) from dandiset 001170¶

Paper. Ling D, Moss EH, Smith CL, Deng F, Kroeger R, Reimer JL, Raman B, Arenkiel BR. Conserved neural dynamics and computations across species in olfaction. bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2023.04.24.538157. This attempt used the full text of version 2 of the preprint (2024-02-23), which is open access (CC BY-ND); its Table S1 and key resources table are images that could not be fetched. The paper was later published in iScience (2026, doi:10.1016/j.isci.2026.115373) with a new title; the claims tested here are those of the preprint, which the dandiset names.

Dandiset. DANDI:001170, version 0.260528.1016. The paper cites no DANDI version (its data statement promises Figshare). This is the most recent published version; the only other one, 0.251111.1532, holds the same 12 assets (same asset ids, sizes and digests, shown in the data check) and differs only in metadata (anatomy terms, keywords, funders, affiliations and a longer description), none of which the analysis uses.

What the dandiset holds. Two-photon imaging of the dorsal olfactory bulb of awake mice: raw fluorescence of glomerular ROIs, the raw movies, a trials table of odor valve openings, and treadmill (and in one file respiration) traces. Seven sessions from four mice follow the paper's block protocol (Figures 2, 4, 6, 7); five sessions from five mice (one of them, sub-134, also in the block sessions) follow a random-order protocol with 11 odors. The locust recordings of the paper are not in the dandiset; they are on Figshare with the paper's code.

Outside inputs. The paper's Figshare record (doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.31435543.v2): its MATLAB scripts (read for method choices), the deposited mouse array data1_mouse_glom198.mat (used only by the link claim), and the locust files data2_locust_PN66.mat, data4_locust_PN85.mat and data5_locust_PN85.mat (used by the locust claims, data elsewhere). Each file is checked against the md5 the record gives.

Random numbers (random vectors and label permutations) come from a generator seeded per claim.

All seven block sessions hold four solitary blocks (methyl salicylate, acetophenone, allyl sulfide, eugenol; ten 5-s pulses every 60 s, so 55 s between pulses) and three sequential blocks in which acetophenone, allyl sulfide or eugenol is followed 6 s after its onset (1 s after its offset) by methyl salicylate, ten pairs every 60 s. Blocks are about 15 min apart (the last onset of a block to the next block's first onset is 961 to 967 s, and 913 s between the last sequential and the first solitary block in sub-148 and sub-149). This agrees with the Methods. No block presents acetophenone, or any odor other than methyl salicylate, as the second odor of a pair. The order of blocks differs between mice: sub-134 and sub-141 start with the solitary blocks, sub-148 and sub-149 with the sequential ones.

def reference_angles(name, top=3):
    blocks = []
    for odor in olfdyn.BLOCK_ODORS:
        med = olfdyn.median_subtracted(resp[name, odor])
        blocks += [med[ON], med[OFF]]
    x = np.vstack(blocks)
    x = x - x.mean(axis=0)
    if top:
        u, sv, vt = np.linalg.svd(x, full_matrices=False)
        x = x @ vt[:top].T
    means = np.stack([x[i * 80:(i + 1) * 80].mean(0) for i in range(8)])   # odor1 ON, odor1 OFF, ...
    on, off = means[0::2], means[1::2]
    out = []
    for i, odor in enumerate(olfdyn.BLOCK_ODORS):
        a_on = olfdyn.angles(on[i:i + 1], on)[0]
        a_off = olfdyn.angles(on[i:i + 1], off)[0]
        others = np.delete(a_on, i)
        out.append({"session": name, "ref": odor, "on_on_mean": others.mean(), "on_off_mean": a_off.mean(),
                    "on_on_max": others.max(), "on_off_min": a_off.min(), "own_off": a_off[i]})
    return out

How the analysis differs from the paper's.

  • dF/F is computed from the dandiset's raw F with F0 from the 2 s before each onset (responses) or the session median (ITI and IBI). The paper's deposited arrays are close to F z-scored within each 57-s trial window. With that z-scoring the contrast enhancement (fig6e) and the negative correlation of later activity with the ON response from 5 s after offset (fig7d) appear, and neither appears with dF/F or with F z-scored on the 2 s before onset. The ON/OFF anticorrelation of fig2f does not appear with any of these (median r 0.068 with the whole-window z-score, 12 of 28 below 0). On synthetic glomeruli with excitation only, the whole-window z-score gives negative correlations between ON and later activity by itself. The z-scored results are shown as variants and do not decide the outcomes.
  • The ON-OFF angles follow the code's centring, which makes the mean ON and OFF vectors opposite (uncentred, the mouse mean is 71.8 degrees; in the locust, with the pre-odor baseline subtracted and no centring, it is 91.9, with 9 of 11 odors above 90); the same procedure gives about 101 degrees between the two halves of the mouse ON window and 96 between those of the locust ON window, so angles above 90 here do not show inversion.
  • Windows are placed in seconds; the paper's code counts frames at a nominal 16 per second, which lasts 51.2 s to 66.4 s for a nominal 57-s trial (912 frames) at the sessions' 13.7 to 17.8 Hz, so the windows drift by 6 to 9 s at the extremes (about 1.8 s in the 15.5-Hz session of the deposited array). Frame-counted windows are shown as a variant for fig2h, fig2f and fig7d and give the same conclusions.
  • Sessions are matched to figures by ROI count. The contrast claim uses all seven block sessions, as the preprint does not identify its three; the three that the published version's Table 1 names are shown as a variant. The random-order claim uses five sessions, of which two have ROI counts that appear in that table.
  • The k-NN choices follow the Methods where they can be run; the Methods' k = 50 cannot be run on the 40 ITI averages and is degenerate on the 60 IBI bins. With k at or below the class size, the ITI averages and the IBI bins are both classified at 69% to 100%. In the block sessions every trial of one odor lies in one block, and pseudo-trials within the 15 min after a block, with no odor, are classified nearly as well as the ITIs, so these classifiers cannot separate the odor from slow differences between blocks.

What was found about the dandiset.

  • The seven block-protocol sessions in four mice match the paper's mouse dataset, and the raw F of the 198-glomerulus session matches the paper's deposited array (median within-trial r 0.968, same ROI order).
  • The fluorescence is raw F (unit "n.a.") with no description of its extraction or of the normalisation the paper used, which decides several outcomes here.
