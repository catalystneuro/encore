Schwab et al. 2020 · Other journals

Neural activity during a simple reaching task in macaques is counter to gating and rebound in basal ganglia-thalamic communication

doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3000829 · paper key schwab-2020

Datasets

  • DANDI:000947

The paper's basic findings held in this data: GPi rests faster and has shorter spikes than VLa, and nearly all units change rate around movement. The timing and correlation findings mostly did not hold. VLa did not clearly lead GPi, and GPi–VLa spike correlations exceeded jittered controls, so they were not rare. VLa rate did not fall after GPi bursts, but the burst detector differed from the paper's, so that result says little. Several outcomes shift with the paper's own unit list, which the dandiset does not identify, and the VLa localization, EMG onset and model claims could not be tested.

  • ✓Reproduced The units the paper analysed can be found in the dandiset, and the dandiset's spike times and trials reproduce the per-unit peri-movement activity the paper's code stored.
  • ?Not reproduced 209 single units met the criteria to be included as GPi neurons (105 in NHP G, 104 in NHP I) and 185 as VLa neurons (66 and 119).
  • ✓Reproduced The resting firing rate of GPi neurons (mean 70.1 spikes/s) is significantly higher than that of VLa neurons (13.5 spikes/s), and the rates are consistent between the two animals.
  • ✓Reproduced Action potentials of GPi neurons are shorter than those of VLa neurons (trough to peak 0.24 against 0.50 ms; rank sum p = 1e-33).
  • ◐Partially reproduced The firing rate of most units ramps during the start-position hold period (97% of GPi and 98% of VLa units with a significant linear trend); slopes are distributed symmetrically around zero, without a difference between GPi and VLa (rank sum p = 0.29).
  • ✓Reproduced Nearly all units change their discharge around movement for at least one direction (GPi 99%, 206 of 209; VLa 88.1%, 163 of 185 in Table 1, 94% in the text).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Monophasic increases are the most common form of response in both GPi and VLa (48.7% and 65.3% of responses), and increases make up most detected changes (61.5% and 68.8%, biphasic responses counted twice), more than decreases (chi-squared p = 1.2e-17).
  • ?Not reproduced Monophasic changes are more common in VLa than in GPi (88.9% against 66.7% of responses; chi-squared p = 2.8e-12), and polyphasic responses more common in GPi.
  • ◐Partially reproduced The mean integrated change in firing over the peri-movement epoch is positive in both GPi and VLa (3.89 and 1.75 spikes), significantly different from zero.
  • ?Not reproduced Peri-movement changes begin later in GPi than in VLa (median onsets -47 and -90 ms; rank sum p = 2.4e-5).
  • ✓Reproduced GPi decreases lag behind VLa increases (median onsets -27 and -88 ms; rank sum p = 4.9e-4), against the gating prediction.
  • ?Not reproduced VLa decreases precede GPi increases (median onsets -108 and -62 ms; rank sum p = 0.01).
  • ✓Reproduced Comparing every GPi response with every VLa response, 96% of comparisons conflict with gating (178,846 of 187,154); only 4% have a GPi onset 0 to 50 ms before the VLa onset with the opposite sign.
  • ◐Partially reproduced A majority of GPi neurons (109 of 206) and some VLa neurons (56 of 179) respond significantly more strongly to one movement direction.
  • ?Not reproduced Short-time-scale spike-to-spike correlations between simultaneously recorded GPi and VLa units are rare (3.5% of 427 pairs over whole recordings, 2.2% of 183 at rest, 5.2% of 172 during movement) and their peaks are not larger than those of jittered controls (p = 0.975, 0.675 and 0.998).
  • ?Not reproduced Trial-to-trial noise correlations between GPi and VLa are rare (5.4% of 332 pairs at rest, 7.2% of 372 during movement), not different from shuffled controls (p = 0.07 and 0.388), and equally often positive and negative.
  • ?Not reproduced At rest, GPi burst onsets, burst offsets and pause onsets are each followed by a small, slow decrease of the population VLa firing rate.
  • ✓Reproduced Reaction times do not differ between the animals or reach directions; movement durations are longer for reaches to the right target, and NHP G moves more slowly than NHP I.
  • ⊘Blocked Agonist EMG activity begins well before movement onset, at similar times in both animals (-123 and -135 ms).
  • ⊘Blocked Thalamic neurons inhibited by GPi stimulation lie dorsal and anterior to neurons excited by SCP stimulation, with a narrow border between VLa and VLp.
