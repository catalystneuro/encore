Watson et al. 2016 · Neuron

Network Homeostasis and State Dynamics of Neocortical Sleep

doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2016.03.036 · paper key watson-2016

Datasets

  • DANDI:000041
  • CRCNS:fcx-1

DANDI:000041: Several main findings held in this data: pyramidal cell rates are lower in nonREM than in waking with a slope below 1, and they fall across sleep, most in the fastest cells. The effects of intervening microarousals, waking and REM on rates from one nonREM packet to the next did not reproduce, most likely because the dataset has no microarousal labels and the derived ones were about half the paper's count. The UP/DOWN state and spindle findings could not be tested because their detection methods are only in the unretrieved supplement, and the movement result could not be tested because the dataset has no movement data. Only 22 of the 27 sessions are in the dataset, and two outcomes depend on a few sessions.

CRCNS:fcx-1: The paper's central finding, that sleep evens out cortical firing as fast-firing pyramidal cells slow down and slow-firing cells speed up, held up in this data across sleep and within nonREM packets (first-to-last packet slope 0.872), as did the overall decline in population rate. The reported drop in rate variability over sleep and the firing changes within single UP states did not reproduce. Most of the lasting effects of individual sub-states (REM, microarousals, waking between packets) did not reproduce, likely because the paper does not say how sequences of states were formed and no rule gave its cell counts. Lower firing in waking after sleep held only weakly, as most cycles lack scored waking right after sleep.
