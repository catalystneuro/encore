Rodgers et al. 2014 · Neuron

Neural correlates of task switching in prefrontal cortex and primary auditory cortex in a novel stimulus selection task for rodents

doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2014.04.031 · paper key rodgers-2014

Datasets

  • CRCNS:pfc-1

Every main finding held up in this data: hold-period rule encoding in mPFC (76 of 121 units) and A1 (36 of 99), its earlier onset in mPFC, weaker encoding on interference trials, the link between baseline and evoked changes, impaired performance after mPFC disruption, and the network model. The minor behavioral, posture and control claims held too. The dataset does not say which of its 2977 clusters were analysed, so the unit set comes from a table in the paper's code repository. Responsiveness recomputed from spikes gave 46 and 61 units, not 31 and 49, so the responsive-unit counts and the mPFC latency lag hold only with that table. The single-unit onset medians also depend on the code's smoother and on how nine units are counted.

Attempt rodgers-2014-01

2026-11-06 · on CRCNS:pfc-1

  • ✓Reproduced The unit and session tables of the paper's code (metadata/unit_db.csv and metadata/sessions_df.csv of github.com/cxrodgers/Rodgers2014 at commit 390b608) match the data set's spike files and session folders, and the matched units have the paper's counts per region.
  • ✓Reproduced Trained rats perform significantly above 50% correct in both localization and pitch discrimination blocks in the recording sessions, with a median of 698 trials per session; the best rats reach about 85% in both blocks. Sessions not above 50% in either block (4 of 55) were discarded.
  • ✓Reproduced Performance is typically better on go trials than on nogo trials.
  • ✓Reproduced After each block change rats switch rapidly to the new target; performance dips briefly during the cue trials at the start of a block and recovers within a few trials.
  • ✓Reproduced The median intertrial interval is 4.0 s (interquartile range 2.7 to 5.3 s).
  • ✓Reproduced In the hold period of correct trials, 63% (76 of 121) of mPFC neurons individually encode the selection rule; 36 prefer localization and 40 pitch discrimination, and neither preference is significantly more common.
  • ✓Reproduced In the hold period of correct trials, 36% (36 of 99) of A1 neurons encode the selection rule; 13 prefer localization and 23 pitch discrimination, and neither preference is significantly more common.
  • ✓Reproduced In A1, rule encoding is not significantly more common among stimulus-responsive neurons (14 of 49) than among nonresponsive ones (22 of 50).
  • ✓Reproduced Among rule-encoding neurons, the median increase in hold-period firing rate in the preferred block is 74.7% in mPFC and 99.7% in A1.
  • ✓Reproduced Two simultaneously recorded mPFC units encode the rule with opposite preferences; one fires 12.1 Hz in localization against 7.2 Hz in pitch discrimination (483 and 295 trials), the other 5.4 Hz in pitch discrimination against 2.7 Hz in localization, both p < 0.001.
  • ✓Reproduced Two simultaneously recorded A1 units encode the rule; one fires 8.0 Hz in localization against 4.8 Hz in pitch discrimination, the other 10.1 Hz in pitch discrimination against 2.0 Hz in localization, both p < 0.001.
  • ✓Reproduced In mPFC rule-encoding neurons, rule encoding is weakened on interference trials; the hold-period response on interference trials is higher than on correct trials in the non-preferred block and lower in the preferred block (57 neurons).
  • ✓Reproduced In A1 rule-encoding neurons the rule encoding is reversed on interference trials; the hold-period response on interference trials is greater in the non-preferred block than in the preferred block (16 neurons).
  • ✓Reproduced In rule-encoding neurons of both regions, hold-period activity does not differ between correct go, correct nogo and go-on-nogo error trials within a block, while interference trials differ from all other trial types.
  • ✓Reproduced Across rule-encoding neurons in both regions, the hold-period rate is significantly above the spontaneous rate in the preferred block and significantly below it in the non-preferred block.
  • ✓Reproduced The median rule-encoding unit first shows a significant block preference 0.55 s before the stimulus in A1 and 0.625 s before it in mPFC, so most develop it before the trial is initiated.
  • ✓Reproduced The population activity of rule-encoding neurons in their preferred block ramps up before the stimulus and is significantly elevated earlier in mPFC (from 2.7 s before stimulus onset) than in A1 (from 0.88 s).
  • ✓Reproduced In the preferred block, after the rat leaves the center port, the population rate of rule-encoding neurons stays above baseline for several seconds on nogo trials and falls below baseline on go trials, in both mPFC and A1.
  • ✓Reproduced Auditory-responsive neurons are significantly rarer in mPFC (31 of 121) than in A1 (49 of 99).
  • ✓Reproduced Onset responses are stronger in auditory-responsive A1 neurons than in mPFC neurons, 0.12 against 0.02 extra spikes per trial (median) and 209% against 171% of baseline.
  • ✓Reproduced The median response latency of auditory-responsive mPFC neurons is slightly but significantly longer than in A1, 19.75 against 16.75 ms.
  • ✓Reproduced Across auditory-responsive neurons, the change in hold-period rate between blocks correlates with the change in evoked rate, in A1 (43 neurons, trend slope close to 1) and in mPFC (17 neurons), p < 0.001 in each.
  • ✓Reproduced After subtracting each block's baseline, only 6 of 43 A1 neurons (14%) change their evoked response significantly between blocks, 4 of the 6 from one rat (Rat 1), with higher rates in pitch discrimination.
  • ✓Reproduced Sound identity is decoded more accurately from A1 ensembles (21 ensembles, 49 neurons) than from mPFC ensembles (17 ensembles, 31 neurons), but for neither sound nor region is decoding better in one block than the other.
  • ✓Reproduced There is no correlation between a neuron's change in anticipatory activity and its tuning for the noise burst or the warble, in A1 or mPFC.
  • ✓Reproduced No stimulus pair shows a greater change in evoked response between blocks than the others, with or without baseline subtraction, and in absolute value; and no stimulus pair's change differs from zero.
  • ✓Reproduced Anticipatory firing rate does not correlate with reaction time or movement time; few neurons correlate individually and the mean correlation does not differ from zero.
  • ✓Reproduced The rat's head angle during the center poke differs between blocks by about 30 degrees, 26.9, 36.6 and 24.1 degrees in the three scored sessions.
  • ✓Reproduced For most rule-encoding neurons, block explains far more of the hold-period variance than head angle; the median fraction explained by block is 94.1% in mPFC and 83.0% in A1 (head angle 5.9% and 17.0%), and only 3 of 16 mPFC and 1 A1 neuron are mostly explained by head angle.
  • ✓Reproduced On posture-equalized trials the rule encoding persists; its population spread (0.471 in mPFC, 0.318 in A1) exceeds that of shuffles (p < 0.005), and only 1 of 16 mPFC and 0 of 8 A1 cells switch preferred block (binomial p = 0.0005 and 0.008).
  • ✓Reproduced The mean magnitude of rule encoding is not significantly altered on posture-equalized trials, in mPFC or A1.
  • ✓Reproduced Electrical disruption of mPFC impairs performance; across sessions the impairment is significant in pitch discrimination for all three rats and in localization for two of three.
  • ✓Reproduced Disruption impairs nogo trials in particular; every rat is significantly impaired on nogo trials in one block or the other, and Z2 also on localization go trials.
  • ✓Reproduced Rats perform as well when the distractor is replaced by a novel neutral sound as on standard trials, so they select the target and do not memorize the four stimulus pairs.
  • ✓Reproduced In the network model with 320 neurons, a task signal alone lets the network perform above 80% correct with a sensory signal-to-noise ratio as low as 0.0625, and 100% at an SNR of 0.25 for virtually any positive task signal.
