Diverging Network Architecture of the C. elegans Connectome and Signaling Network

Sophie Dvali, Caio Seguin, Richard F. Betzel, Andrew M. Leifer

Outcomes of 38 claims

Summary: With the signaling network taken from the atlas released with Randi et al. 2023, the paper's main network comparisons held up: its size and small-world structure, triad motifs that differ from the connectome's, and signaling and anatomical rich clubs that share only AVEL and AVER. The atlas could not be rebuilt from the dandiset, which lacks the unstimulated control recordings behind its q-values. The community findings, six signaling communities mostly distinct from the anatomical ones, could not be tested because the block model's software could not be installed. Pharyngeal neurons as broadcasters fell short of significance (p = 0.073), and AVE as the most connected pair in both networks rests on an assumed choice of connectome datasets.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Methods not availableThe paper names DANDI:001075 version 0.240920.1434 as the data of the signaling network but does not say how the q-values were obtained. They cannot be computed from that version (no stimulated-neuron identities, no control recordings), and the released atlas has 1154 edges at q < 0.05 where the paper reports 1151. Question for the authors. Which q-value matrix or atlas file did the analysis use, and can the q-values be published so that the q < 0.01 and q <= 0.18 controls can be built?#
  • Methods not availableThe paper does not name its four EM datasets ("three adults and one L4") or how gap junctions are given a direction. With the four sources of Randi et al.'s code (White 1986 whole adult and L4, Witvliet 2021 datasets 7 and 8) and gap junctions in both directions all reported values match; with the White 1986 N2U adult, or with gap junctions in one direction, several do not (edges 2893, clustering 0.233, sigma 2.93 or 2.21, r = 0.169, AIB as the most connected class). Question for the authors. Which datasets and which direction did the analysis use?#
  • Paper inconsistentThe Results describe the signaling rich club as "16 neurons of degree 25 or higher" and "five neurons of degree 40 or higher". In the network 22 and 7 neurons have degree 25 or higher and 40 or higher; 16 and 5 have degree above 25 and above 40, as the Figure 5 legend and richclub.py define them.#
  • Methods not availableThe paper reads the rich club thresholds off local maxima of the normalized coefficient by eye (richclub.py) and does not give the thresholds of the control networks' rich clubs (Extended Data Figures 11b, 12a and 13a). For the 1.25-contact anatomical network the attempt takes the local maximum with the largest normalized coefficient among those leaving at least 10 neurons (k = 28, 13 neurons), a rule that gives back 25 and 62 on the original networks; the legend's 9 of 15 kept and 4 new is close to the 8 of 14 and 5 new found. Question for the authors. Which degree thresholds define the rich clubs of the control networks?#
