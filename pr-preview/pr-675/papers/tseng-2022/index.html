Tseng et al. 2022 · Neuron

Shared and specialized coding across posterior cortical areas for dynamic navigation decisions

doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2022.05.012 · paper key tseng-2022

Datasets

  • DANDI:000579

The behavioral findings largely held up in this data, but most neural findings could not be tested. Performance dropped after rule switches and recovered, strategy shifted toward bias then back to rule-following, and running became more variable, all as reported. Visually guided trials updated rule belief more than normal trials, unlike the paper, and the paper does not describe how the update was computed. The photoinhibition experiments are not in the dataset, and the single-neuron encoding gradients depend on GLM fits that were too large to rerun. Population decoding reproduced the V1-to-A cue gradient, while RSC being most active in the stem held only in part. I did not create `summaries/tseng-2022-01.md`.

  • ✓Reproduced The neural analyses use 93,881 layer 2/3 neurons from 141 sessions of 8 mice, parcellated into V1 (14,373), PM (9,564), AM (14,974), MM (9,885), RSC (23,036) and A (22,049).
  • ✓Reproduced Performance is high before a rule switch, typically falls below chance after it, and recovers gradually to high accuracy by the end of the block.
  • ✓Reproduced The strategy LSTM predicts the mouse's choices in held-out sessions with 80.2% ± 5.1% accuracy (mean ± SD).
  • ✓Reproduced The LSTM predicts choices better (higher log likelihood) than the best reinforcement learning model (model 4, separate learning rates for rewarded and unrewarded trials).
  • ✓Reproduced Before a switch mice show little choice bias and follow the rule; after a switch the probability of the actual choice falls near chance, bias-following rises moderately, and rule-following then recovers gradually.
  • ?Not reproduced Visually guided trials do not update rule belief more than normal trials in the 30 trials after a switch (0.013 ± 0.0055 against 0.0080 ± 0.0023; p = 0.34).
  • ⊘Blocked Inhibiting RSC or PPC lowers task performance compared with control trials and with S1 inhibition; the RSC against PPC difference is not significant (p = 0.058).
  • ⊘Blocked Inhibition of PPC and RSC decreases rule-following compared with control trials and with S1 inhibition; bias-following increases, strongly in a subset of mice.
  • ⊘Blocked Across sessions, the change in rule-following under RSC or PPC inhibition is not correlated with the change in bias-following.
  • ✓Reproduced Running trajectories of choice-matched trials become less correlated with one another after a rule switch.
  • ✓Reproduced Dynamic choice crosses threshold sooner on trials with higher rule-following or bias-following.
  • ✓Reproduced With high rule-following, choice commitment starts near chance and rises during the maze; with low rule-following it stays low until late; with high bias-following it is high from trial onset.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Cue-biased running varies across mice and sessions and is not correlated with task performance or with the time course of dynamic choice.
  • ◐Partially reproduced RSC is most active in the maze stem and least active in the feedback period/ITI, when area A is more active.
  • ⊘Blocked The GLM explains a fraction 0.25 ± 0.17 (mean ± SD) of the Poisson deviance of single neurons on held-out test data.
  • ⊘Blocked 42,998 neurons have test fraction deviance explained above 0.2 (V1 7,775, PM 4,934, AM 6,878, MM 3,991, RSC 10,329, A 9,091); 68,532 pass a threshold of 0.1.
  • ⊘Blocked Fraction explained deviance on the CV held-out data differs very little from that on the test data (0.0010 ± 0.0006, mean ± SEM), so encoding measured on CV held-out data is not inflated by overfitting.
  • ⊘Blocked Area A has higher encoding of instantaneous movement and lower encoding of task variables than every other area, and V1 has higher task-variable encoding than every other area.
  • ⊘Blocked Cue encoding during stem traversal is strongest in V1 (higher than every other area), strong in AM and PM, weaker in RSC and weakest in A (lower than every other area).
