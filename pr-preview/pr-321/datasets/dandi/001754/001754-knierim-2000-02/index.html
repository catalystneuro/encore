      How the test differed from the paper’s method

      • The paper's Methods were not available; the selection (50 spikes at a known position), the 32 x 32 binning, the shuffle and the thresholds are common choices fixed in the claims file.
      • Occupancy is the original analysis's occupancy map for the epoch, and shuffled spikes take the nearest position sample within 0.25 s, as the dandiset gives position only at spike times. A spike whose nearest sample is the lost position is dropped.
      • Robust is read as at least half of the units significant in each flight day 9 session.
      • Rat2's flight day 9 session is judged although whose position the trace holds in it could not be established.

    • answered 2026-10-20asked 2026-10-20about si-comparable-to-preflight, si-rat1-fd9-vs-preflight, si-rat3-fd4-vs-preflight, fd4-abnormal-selectivity, fig3-stability-across-sessions

      Reading M-b recomputes rate maps from spikes and position. The dandiset gives position only at the spike times of each file's units, so no occupancy time can be computed from it, and the preflight files hold no position at all. Should M-b divide spike counts by the deposited occupancy map of the epoch (the attempt does this for the flight epochs), and when M-b cannot be computed for the preflight cells, is a comparison with preflight judged on M-a alone?

      Passage. The Fig. 1 legend says all statistical analyses kept off-track positions; the paper gives no bin size, smoothing or occupancy rule.

      Why it matters. Unsmoothed 64 x 64 maps give a spatial information about twice that of the deposited maps (2.9 to 4.0 bits per spike in flight against 0.7 to 1.6 for M-a), so a test of M-b flight values against M-a preflight values finds a difference in every rat, and judging on M-a alone does not. The attempt judges these claims on M-a and shows the M-b flight values and the mixed test as variants.

      The reader’s answer. The paper does not say. It is a two-page brief communication with no Methods, no supplement and no code; it gives the measure only by citation to Skaggs et al. (ref. 13), whose formula needs occupancy, and says only that "all statistical analyses included these off-track data" (Fig. 1 legend). Dividing spike counts by the epoch's deposited occupancy map is a reading it allows for M-b. It compares one index computed alike for both groups ("An index of spatial tuning specificity ... did not significantly differ between cells recorded preflight and those recorded on FD9"), so a comparison uses the same map reading on both sides; where M-b cannot be computed for preflight, the comparison is judged on M-a and the mixed test is a variant. Written into the criteria of si-comparable-to-preflight (which the rat 1 and rat 3 claims follow) and fd4-abnormal-selectivity, and into the opening comment.

      Each preflight file holds one recording day with one epoch labelled ES and one labelled MC. Is each epoch a candidate session under S-a (as the attempt reads it), and under S-b, is the session closest before launch the later epoch of that day (MC in every rat) or the whole day?

      Passage. The paper says rat 2 ran a flat rectangular track 4 days before launch (Fig. 2b) and names no track for rats 1 and 3.

      Why it matters. For rat 1 the flight day 9 cells do not differ from the preflight ES epoch (p = 0.50) and do differ from the preflight MC epoch (p = 0.0055), so the claim is blocked until the session is fixed.

      The reader’s answer. The paper does not say. It never mentions ES or MC epochs; for rat 2 it says only that the preflight cells were "recorded 4 days before launch as the rat ran clockwise on a flat, rectangular track", and for rats 1 and 3 it says "that rat's preflight data" and "this rat's place cells before the flight". Each epoch is a candidate under S-a. Under S-b each epoch of the day closest before launch is run on its own, as the paper describes one track for rat 2 and prefers neither epoch; the later epoch is not given. When the epochs disagree the claim stays blocked as the criterion says. Written into the criterion of si-comparable-to-preflight.

      The criterion reads "most" as at least 4 of the 7 active cells of session 2. On the dandiset session 2 has 11 active cells under M-a and 16 under M-b, not 7. Is "most" then a count of at least 4, or a fraction of at least 4/7 (or a majority) of the active cells?

      Passage. Fig. 3 legend, "Most place cells maintained the same firing locations, although the fourth cell lost its field".

      Why it matters. Under M-b, 6 of 16 cells keep their field by reading (a) and 1 of 16 by reading (b); as a count reading (a) passes and (b) fails, as a fraction both fail.

      The reader’s answer. The paper gives it as a fraction of the cells shown. "Most" refers to "the same seven cells" of Fig. 3, the active cells of session 2, and the legend says "Most place cells maintained the same firing locations, although the fourth cell lost its field", so 6 of 7. "Most" is more than half of the active cells of session 2; the count of 4 in the criterion was that majority for the paper's 7 cells, not a fixed count. Cells that gained a field in session 3 are "not shown" and are not in the denominator. The criterion now reads more than half; the statement and the expected count of 7 are unchanged.

    • answered 2026-10-20asked 2026-10-20about direction-selectivity

      The criterion computes, per bin, a counterclockwise rate and a clockwise expected count, which need the time spent in each bin in each direction. The dandiset has position only at spike times and one occupancy map per epoch, not split by direction, and nothing gives the track's layout in the image from which the circuit's direction could be read. Is there a reading the paper allows with these data, or does the claim stay blocked?

      Why it matters. Without a time base per direction no rate can be formed, so the attempt blocks the claim.

      The reader’s answer. The paper gives no method, only "Some cells demonstrated place fields when the rat was moving only counterclockwise through a single location", and no track layout or time base by direction. It allows no reading that does without time spent per bin in each direction, so the claim stays blocked as methods_underspecified; the criterion now says so.

It compares spatial information in flight with preflight for each rat, tests abnormal selectivity on flight day 4 and the stability of fields across two sessions, and records why the direction, EEG and three-surface claims could not be tested.
