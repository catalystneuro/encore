Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Published code disagreesThe data set's Figure 4 script differs from the paper's Methods and Results in three places. Its detection threshold is the 99th percentile of the noise projections (1% false positives) where the paper reports rates at 5%; its events need more than 0.8 s without spikes after the last spike (interval_post=800*fs/1000) where the Methods, and the script's own comment (">1000ms after the last spike"), say more than 1 s; and its noise traces are one per spike-free gap longer than 4 s, taken at the gap's midpoint and dropped if any |dF/F0| reaches 0.3, which the Methods do not say. The script's 0.8 s gives the paper's jRGECO1a event counts exactly (199, 70, 29, 34, 35, 22, 14, 21), the Methods' 1 s does not (191, 62, 28, 30, 36, 20, 12, 17), so the published counts appear to come from the script. With the script's isolation and its own threshold the jRGECO1a single-spike rate is 60.8% (60.7% under the Methods' isolation), not the 82% reported; with the script's isolation and the 5% threshold it is 82.4%.
  • Methods not availableFor the paper's authors, on the jRCaMP1a analysis of Figure 4. Which isolation intervals, noise rule and frame window were used? The jRGECO1a script reproduces the paper's jRGECO1a event and noise counts exactly, but the same rule on the jRCaMP1a files gives 162, 89, 86, 62, 44, 26, 15 and 11 events and 246 noise traces, and the Methods' 1 s isolation 158, 83, 79, 58, 38, 24, 16 and 11, against the reported 135, 65, 71, 52, 33, 20, 14, 11 and 274. This attempt assumed the script's 30-frame window for jRCaMP1a, which gives detection rates of 63.3% and 80.7% (reported 65% and 80%); the windows the Methods allow (4 or 5 s after the event, or 1 s) give at most 41.6% for one spike. Was data_20150707_cell1_001.mat, whose fluorescence does not follow its spikes, included? This attempt assumed it was, and the jRCaMP1a detection outcome depends on it.
  • Methods not availableFor the paper's authors, on Figure 4g. How was the exponential fitted (from the peak or from the spike, over what window), which fitting error selected the 16 traces, and how was the half rise time defined? The function the script calls for this, fit_dynamic_fitting, is not in the data set. This attempt fitted from the peak to the end of the isolation interval, kept the 16 traces with the smallest RMS residual, and measured the half rise from the first spike by linear interpolation; jRGECO1a is faster under these and under a 2 s window.
  • Paper inconsistentThe Results say 353 of 934 single RCaMP1h mutations and 187 of 689 single R-GECO1 mutations improved the 1 AP response (p < 0.01, Wilcoxon rank sum), citing Supplementary files 1 and 2. Those tables list 934 and 692 single mutants, but only 230 and 58 with a 1 AP ratio above 1 and a 1 AP p-value below 0.01, and no other rule tried (p < 0.05, any stimulus, all variants) gives both. For RCaMP1h, p < 0.05 and all variant rows give 334, within 10% of 353; for R-GECO1 none comes within 10% of 187 (a ratio above 1 alone gives 207).
