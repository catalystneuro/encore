  • ✓ReproducedData Availability Statement (doi:10.5281/zenodo.14826579)checks the link to the paper’s data

    The dandiset reproduces the processed data the paper deposited on Zenodo; the trial selections and the beta power of the paper's example trial computed from the dandiset agree with the paper's own files.

    Data used in this study are available at zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14826579).

    Data Availability Statement (doi:10.5281/zenodo.14826579)

    Trials are matched by number. The decoded selections agree with the four Zenodo lists for 569 of 570, 602 of 602, 594 of 594 and 577 of 578 trials; the only difference is trial 1501, the last of the session, absent from the Zenodo large-reward and right lists. The Zenodo example "trial35" is the 35th trial of the large-reward list (trial 84). Beta power computed here correlates with its trace at r = 0.943 at a lag of 6 ms, with a median amplitude ratio of 0.943, so the dandiset's LFP, its trial times and the beta pipeline used here agree with the paper's files.     How the test differed from the paper’s method

  • ✓ReproducedFigure 3B; Results and Discussion, paragraph 4

    Single-trial bipolar LFP from site c3bs-c3a shows beta bursts (one to a few cycles of high beta power) whose frequency changes around the cue and other task events.

    Single-trial LFPs measured from another s-μIP are shown to display fluctuations in the frequency of β-band “bursts” (i.e., 1–few cycles of high β-band power) around the cue and other task events

    Figure 3B; Results and Discussion, paragraph 4

    With the threshold at the 75th percentile (356 µV²) and bursts of at least 50 ms, the burst rate of 570 large-reward trials is 1.51 per s in the second before the value cue and 1.21 in the second after it (Wilcoxon p = 9.5e-10). The rate changes around the cue at the 50th and 90th percentiles too (p below 1e-40), rising at the 50th and falling at the 90th, so its direction depends on the threshold while the change does not. The rate dips just after the cue (0.51 per s in 0 to 0.25 s) and falls again from 6 s after it, after the feedback. On the smoothed power 95.1% of runs above the 75th percentile last at least 50 ms (median 206 ms), so the minimum length does little; on the unsmoothed envelope the rate also falls after the cue (3.07 to 2.11 per s, p = 6.8e-34). At the deciding threshold the fall in bursts coincides with the drop in beta power after the cue, so it is not separate evidence from the suppression of fig4b-beta-reward.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper shows a single trial and does not define a burst; the threshold, minimum length and test were chosen here before the analysis.
  • ✓ReproducedFigure 4A

    Trial-averaged dopamine at site c8dg is higher after large-reward than after small-reward value cues, in successful forced trials of the shape task.

    Higher dopamine levels are observed following the display of large reward associated value cues as compared to small reward cues in monkey T

    Figure 4A

    Over 0 to 3 s after the value cue, the re-referenced dopamine has a median of 1.28 in 85 large-reward trials and 0.19 in 80 small-reward trials (Mann-Whitney p = 0.023; 3 trials of 168 have no valid samples in the window). The difference lies in 1.5 to 3 s (p = 0.0072), not 0 to 1.5 s (p = 0.41). The effect is weak. Before the cue the groups differ the other way (p = 0.064). Without re-referencing (the stored da) there is no difference (p = 0.3), nor in the re-referenced oxidation current (p = 0.41). It holds in trials 1 to 367 (p = 0.013) and not in trials 368 to 734 (p = 0.51). With a baseline of the mean over -1 to 0 s in place of the sample at the cue the result is the same (1.38 against 0.10, p = 0.022).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Only the first 734 of 1468 trials of the session have trial-aligned FSCV in the dandiset, so 168 successful forced trials are used.
    • The stored da is not referenced at the value cue; it is re-referenced here by subtracting its value at the cue, which equals background subtraction there only if the PCA projection was linear and the nulling (NaN) masks are ignored.
    • Small reward is inferred from the absence of the big-reward code, confirmed by the cue condition codes 35 and 36 in all 303 successful forced trials.
    • The paper gives no window or test; 0 to 3 s and the Mann-Whitney test were chosen before the analysis.

    Over 0 to 0.5 s after the value cue, median beta power is 275 µV² in 570 large-reward trials and 327 µV² in 602 small-reward trials (ratio 0.84, Mann-Whitney p = 1e-08), with no difference before the cue (ratio 1.006, p = 0.82). Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • File format errorEach event is stored about five times in the events tables of both Monkey T sessions (85665 stored times, 17686 distinct, in 09/13/2024; 59702 and 12434 in 09/26/2024), once for every overlapping 60 s trial window that contains it.
  • Description missingThe trials tables hold only times, no trial conditions. Conditions must be decoded from the event codes. Session 09/13/2024 has no small-reward code, and the labels of codes 35 and 36 ("right cue reward condition 1/2") do not say that they code large and small reward, which the data show in all 303 successful forced trials.
  • Data missingThe trial-aligned FSCV table of session 09/13/2024 has rows for only the first 734 of its 1468 trials (from a source file named "firsthalf"), and the dandiset has FSCV from c8dg only; the second site of Figure 3A (c8ds) is missing.
  • Description missingThe trial-aligned FSCV columns (da, ph, m, iox) carry no units, and da is not referenced at the value cue as the Methods describe; the reference point is not stated.
  • Metadata errorThe raw FSCV current is labelled amperes, but its values range from -1527 to 1557; the unit is likely nA or the data are not current.
  • Metadata errorThe raw FSCV scan times are an exact 0.1 s grid starting at the first trial start and running 24120 s, past the last trial, and the raw current cannot be matched to the trial-aligned oxidation current at any offset or to the task at the stored times. The conversion notes say the times were interpolated between trial-start codes; the data do not support that they are on the trial clock.
  • Metadata errorThe bipolar LFP of session 09/26/2024 stores values already in volts (around 1e-4) with a conversion factor of 3.05e-8, so that read as NWB prescribes it is in picovolts.
  • Metadata errorThe raw ephys of session 09/26/2024 has starting_time 0 and no timestamps, but it matches the stored LFP (r 0.92 to 0.98) only when read 9831.35 s later on the trial clock, consistently from the first trial to the last.
  • Metadata errorIn Monkey P's file the spike times (6800 to 18785 s) are 6800.666 s later than the raw signal's timestamps (0 to 11985 s); the spike-triggered average of the raw signal shows the spike only with that shift. The file does not say the two use different clocks.
  • File format errorIn Monkey P's units table, unit 0 merges the unsorted (0) and poor-quality (-1) thresholded spikes, including 7 spike times below 1 s and 3 repeated times; the poor-quality spikes of the thresholded table are not in time order.
  • Data missingMonkey P's file has no trials or events, so the reward-related firing of Figure 5A cannot be tested. The paper's Zenodo events for this session are raw TTL values without a code map.
  • Description missingNo implantation dates are given, so the days post-implant of each session cannot be checked.
  • Metadata errorMonkey P's site is named cl3 in the file and dandiset description, c33 in the Figure 5 legend; the files' related_publications cite the bioRxiv preprint, not the article; the acquisition high cut is 8000 Hz in the Neuralynx settings, not the 7500 Hz the description states.
  • OtherThe applied FSCV waveform runs from -0.347 to 1.340 V, as the dandiset description says; the paper's Methods give -0.4 to 1.3 V.

Reproduction Microinvasive probes for dopamine, LFP and spikes in monkeys (Amjad et al. 2025)

Tests the findings of Amjad, Mahajan, Choi, Shrivastav, Murray, Somich, Coyne and Schwerdt (2025) on dandiset 002013, which holds the three example sessions of the paper. It checks the recording parameters and task structure, the dopamine response to large and small reward cues at site c8dg, beta-band LFP suppression by reward and cue side at c3bs-c3a, beta bursts around the cue, and the isolation of the sorted unit of monkey P, and compares the dandiset with the paper's Zenodo deposit.
