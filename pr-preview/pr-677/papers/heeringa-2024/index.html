  • ✓Reproduced The dataset holds 104 experiments (gerbils) with 1160 single auditory nerve fibres; 60 young-adult (32 female), 13 middle-aged (7 female) and 31 old (11 female) gerbils, with 759, 87 and 314 single units.
  • ✓Reproduced Mean ages of the groups are 5.5, 22.5 and 38.3 months, mean ABR thresholds 18.1, 34.2 and 49.0 dB SPL, and mean weights 74.6, 83.4 and 86.4 g (young adult, middle aged, old).
  • ✓Reproduced Best frequencies range from 428 to 15,825 Hz in young adults, 350 to 15,091 Hz in middle-aged and 620 to 15,762 Hz in old gerbils.
  • ✓Reproduced ABR thresholds rise with age, and hearing loss varies widely among the old animals.
  • ✓Reproduced Old gerbils are skewed towards males (11 of 31 female), which the paper attributes to ovarian cancer and early death of old females.
  • ✓Reproduced Of the 104 gerbils, 78 received additional oxygen throughout the experiment.
  • ✓Reproduced Responses to paired noise bursts were recorded in 21 of 104 experiments, Schroeder-phase stimuli in 5, and consonant-vowel-consonant logatomes in 17.
  • ◐Partially reproduced CF recordings were obtained for 119 fibres, CLICK for 261, SR for 203, NOISE for 143, SPS for 22 and CVC for 135.
  • ✓Reproduced No SR, SPS or CVC recordings were obtained in middle-aged gerbils, and SPS responses were recorded only in young adults.
  • ?Not reproduced Units with multiple inter-spike intervals below 0.6 ms, the absolute refractoriness of auditory nerve fibres, were excluded from the dataset.
  • ⊘Blocked No median spike waveform showed a prepotential, which would mark a ventral cochlear nucleus bushy cell.
  • ⊘Blocked Rate-level functions were checked for atypical shapes (auditory nerve rate-level functions are typically straight, sloping saturating or flat saturating), and units whose rate-level function was nonmonotonic at levels below 80 dB SPL were excluded.
  • ✓Reproduced The example fibre of Fig. 5b (animal G220908, unit 3p_181) had a best frequency of 7.6 kHz and a threshold of 19 dB SPL.
  • ✓Reproduced Fibres of young-adult gerbils show two regions of best sensitivity separated by a region around 3 to 4 kHz with higher thresholds and fewer fibres; the highest thresholds are at 3.5 kHz.
  • ?Not reproduced Among young-adult fibres, the fewest best frequencies fall in the bin bordered by 2.5 and 3.0 kHz.
  • ✓Reproduced Among high-frequency fibres (BF above 3.5 kHz) there is a cluster with low spontaneous rates, and low-frequency fibres have a bimodal distribution of spontaneous rate with modes near 5 and 60 spikes/s.
  • ✓Reproduced The highest best frequency at which significant phase locking was recorded in young-adult gerbils was 4.6 kHz.
  • ✓Reproduced The highest vector strengths are found among fibres with low best frequencies (below 1.5 kHz) and low spontaneous rates (below 18 spikes/s).
  • ✓Reproduced Characteristic frequency from the CF recordings correlates strongly with best frequency from the BF recordings, with no systematic deviation towards higher or lower frequencies.
  • ✓Reproduced Click latency is strongly negatively correlated with best frequency, reflecting the travelling wave delay along the cochlea.
  • ?Not reproduced The best-frequency distributions of the units with CF and with CLICK recordings are representative of the distribution of the full dataset.
  • ⊘Blocked Successful single-fibre recordings were obtained in 43% of old gerbils that were tried, against 88% of young adults.
  • ⊘Blocked Responses to tones at 20 and 30 dB above threshold were examined for non-primary-like shapes, using BF, RLF and PH recordings combined when available, and units with clearly non-primary-like responses were excluded.
