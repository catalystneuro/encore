Dataset version 0.231012.2129 · tested 2026-11-21 to 2026-11-22 · 11 open findings

  • OtherWith the dataset's own CCF labels and `classification`, the paper's selection (at least 30 good units in each region) gives 5 ALM and medulla sessions from 4 mice, with 300 ALM and 340 medullary reticular units, where the paper reports 16 sessions (or 11 from 7 mice) with 604 and 724 units; and 17 bilateral ALM sessions from 9 mice where the paper reports 20 from 10. With `unit_quality` and CCF labels transferred by electrode coordinates from the nearest classified unit it gives 19 ALM and medulla sessions from 7 mice with 1501 and 1745 units, and 31 bilateral sessions. No reading of the files recovers the paper's selection.#
    Found by the attempt on Hasnain et al. 2025 (hasnain-2025-01, 2026-11-22)
  • Description missingThe units' CCF annotation (`anno_name`) is filled only for units whose `classification` is good (69453 of 272227 units); the others have an empty label and only the probe's target region, though the electrodes table holds each electrode's CCF coordinates (x, y, z). The column description does not say this.#
    Found by the attempt on Hasnain et al. 2025 (hasnain-2025-01, 2026-11-22)
  • Description missingUnits carry two quality labels, `unit_quality` (good or multi, no description) and `classification` (good or unlabelled), with no statement of how they differ; 1852 units have no `classification` value.#
    Found by the attempt on Hasnain et al. 2025 (hasnain-2025-01, 2026-11-22)

    Found by the attempt on Hasnain et al. 2025 (hasnain-2025-01, 2026-11-22)
  • OtherThe spike, video and trial clocks agree in all 22 selected sessions. The tongue becomes visible a median 56 to 262 ms after the go cue, lick direction is decoded from spike counts after the go cue well above shuffled labels, and jaw and nose speed rises at the go cue. The units of the session compared are the same in versions 0.230822.0128 and 0.231012.2129.#
    Found by the attempt on Hasnain et al. 2025 (hasnain-2025-01, 2026-11-22)
  • Description missingThe trial table has no column for the delay duration. In the ALM and medulla sessions and the 2021 bilateral sessions used here, 13 to 57 correct and error trials per session (446 over 22 sessions) have a 1.8 s or 0.3 s delay in place of 1.2 s, which shows only in the sample and delay event times (`sample_start_times`, `delay_start_times`).#
    Found by the attempt on Hasnain et al. 2025 (hasnain-2025-01, 2026-11-22)
  • OtherThe pinned version holds what the paper describes. 168 of 174 files have photostimulation trials, 112 late-delay bilateral sessions come from 19 mice, and the mice and sessions per region equal Table 1 of the paper under its stated rules. No recording is deposited twice (spike counts, trial counts and trial timing all differ). Every photostimulation trial has one onset event inside it. Spikes and trials share a clock (the ALM laser-control difference 20 to 120 ms after onset exceeds that before onset in all 99 sessions with ALM units), and licks and video share one (the tongue is detected in 97% of frames in the 50 ms after a lick against 40% at times shifted by -0.5 s, in all 112 sessions).#
    Found by the attempt on Yang et al. 2026 (yang-2026-01, 2026-11-21)
  • File format errorFour units in the late-delay sessions have spike times that are not in ascending order. Analyses that assume sorted spike times (binary search for counts) must sort them first.#
    Found by the attempt on Yang et al. 2026 (yang-2026-01, 2026-11-21)
  • Description missingThe brain region of a unit is given two ways, as the probe's target in the electrode location (`brain_regions`, e.g. "left ALM", "right Thalamus") and as a CCF annotation per unit (`anno_name`), and neither is described as the region used for analysis. The probe targets reproduce the paper's per-region counts exactly.#
    Found by the attempt on Yang et al. 2026 (yang-2026-01, 2026-11-21)

Dataset version 0.230822.0128 an older version · tested 2026-11-20 · 6 open findings

Problems that change results 1

The data are wrong in a way the files do not show, so an analysis that takes them as given comes out wrong. Each was checked against the notebook’s outputs by someone other than the attempt’s author.

  • OtherChanges resultsIn five sessions, sub-440959 (SC011) on 2019-02-24, 2019-02-25 and 2019-02-26 and sub-442571 (SC022) on 2019-02-27 and 2019-02-28, the DeepLabCut marker timestamps advance 3.4 ms per frame but the video frames are 6.8 ms apart. As deposited, the tongue is visible at 6 to 13% of the lick events, and no shift of the lick times within 1 s brings this above 0.49; stretching each trial's marker timestamps by 2 from its first frame puts the tongue at 99.9 to 100% of the licks. Their trial videos hold 934 to 946 frames (median per session), against 1864 to 1886 in the other eight sessions of the same two mice, although the trials last as long (median 4.83 to 4.90 s in all 13) and each video starts at its trial's start; the median over all sessions is 1904. The video in the later version holds the same frame counts and carries no timing (a starting time with a rate of 1.0, no timestamps), so the 6.8 ms spacing is inferred from the frame counts and the licks, not read from the video. The markers of these sessions are on a time base half as long as the trial table's, and nothing in the files shows it; any analysis of movement around task events in them comes out wrong.#
    Checked: The printed outputs show it. Cell 8: the tongue-after-go-cue contrast is negative in 5 sessions. Cell 10: as deposited, the tongue is visible at 0.063 to 0.132 of lick events; the best fraction at any shift within 1 s is 0.484; stretching the timestamps by 2 gives 0.999 to 1.0. These sessions have 934 to 946 frames per trial video, against a median of 1904. Caveat: 6.8 ms apart is inferred from the x2 stretch, not measured; these sessions have video in 0.231012.2129 (cell 14), which could confirm it directly. The rating is right: signals on different clocks, the marker time base half as long as the trial table's, and nothing in the files labels it. The short videos are visible in the data but do not reveal the timing error. Any marker-to-event analysis of these sessions would come out wrong, although none of them is in the authors' 106.
    Found by the attempt on Wang et al. 2025 (wang-2025-01, 2026-11-20)
