  • The representative neuronal sequence (unit 9 of session Achilles-10252013) has 228 356 spikes, with IETs from 1.05 ms to 1.82 x 10^4 ms.

    Criterion

    The values are read from the labels of Figure 2. The unit is found as in O1, using all spikes of the session as in O2. Reproduced when, under one reading of O1, all three values are within tolerance. The other claims on the representative unit use that reading.

  • The IETs of each process, the representative neuron included, extend across several orders of magnitude.

    Section III, paragraph 6; Figure 2, top leftfig2-iet-broad
    From the paper

    First, the range of IETs in each process extends across several orders of magnitude.

    Criterion

    For the representative unit, reproduced when tau_max over tau_min is at least 10^3 (three decades). The paper says "several" and gives about 4.2 decades (1.05 to 1.82 x 10^4 ms). Three decades is the registry's reading of "several". The ratio is reported.

  • For the representative neuron, the burst size distributions are heavy-tailed across different timescales.

    Section III, paragraph 6; Figure 2, top center and legendfig2-neuron-burst-size-heavy-tail
    From the paper

    we con ... firm previous findings that burst size distributions Pδ(b) are heavy-tailed across different timescales

    Criterion

    Bursts as in the Methods, at each delta of the grid (O4) over the relevant range. The fit and test follow O3. Figure 2 marks a supported power law with a filled circle but shows no count. The criterion is therefore the registry's reading of "across different timescales". Reproduced when the power law is supported (R > 0, p < 0.05) at more than half of the relevant timescales, and at every one below 3000 ms, the range in which the paper says beta is stable. Partially reproduced when it is supported at more than half but not at every one below 3000 ms. The result at each delta is shown.

  • For neuronal spikes the fitted burst-size exponent stays near 2.35 for several decades of timescales, up to about 3000 ms.

    Section III, paragraph 6; Section III, paragraph 9; Figure 2, top, beta panelfig2-neuron-beta-stable
    From the paper

    our analysis shows that the estimated value of the power ... law exponent fitted to the empirical distribution remains stable for several decades of timescales ... stable around βδ ≃2.35 for neuronal spikes

    Criterion

    Beta is fitted at each delta of the grid (O4) between the lower end of the relevant range and 3000 ms, with the fit of O3. "Stable around 2.35" allows two readings, (a) every estimate in that range within 2.35 +/- 0.235, (b) the median of the estimates within that band and their range under 0.47 (twice the tolerance). Reproduced when (a) or (b) holds under one reading of O3. The estimates and their standard errors are shown.

  • For neuronal spikes, beta is stable below 3000 ms, where beta at the smaller timescale is below gamma. Above 3000 ms beta decreases, apparently driven by a decreasing gamma.

    Section III, paragraph 9; Figure 2, top, beta and gamma panelsfig2-neuron-beta-decrease-follows-gamma
    From the paper

    for the range of δ < 3000 ms, for which we observe βδ′ < γδδ′. For δ > 3000 ms, the decreasing tendency of βδ seems to be driven by the decreasing γδδ′

    Criterion

    Beta(delta) on the grid of O4 and gamma for the pairs delta = 3.3, 5.9 and 11 delta' (Figure 2), with the fit of O3. The paper names no test. The registry reads the claim in three parts. (1) For pairs with delta < 3000 ms, beta at delta' is below gamma in most pairs (more than half). (2) For delta > 3000 ms within the relevant range, beta has a negative Spearman correlation with log delta. (3) Over the same range, gamma has a negative Spearman correlation with log delta for at least one of the three ratios. Reproduced when all three hold under one reading of O3. Partially reproduced when (1) and (2) hold. If the relevant range above 3000 ms has fewer than three grid points, (2) and (3) are reported and not judged.

  • For the representative neuron, the merging-number distributions are heavy-tailed for various pairs of timescales, and their fitted exponents gamma stay fairly constant across a wide range of timescales.

    Section III, paragraph 6; Figure 2, top right and gamma panelfig2-neuron-merging-heavy-tail
    From the paper

    the merging number distributions Pδδ′(k) also ... heavy tails for various timescale pairs ... although the estimated exponents are not as stable as in burst size distribution, they remain relatively constant across a wide range of timescales

    Criterion

    Merging numbers for the pairs delta = 3.3, 5.9 and 11 delta' (Figure 2 legend), with delta' on the grid of O4 and both timescales within the relevant range. The fit and test follow O3. The paper gives no count or band. Reproduced when the power law is supported (R > 0, p < 0.05) for more than half of the pairs, and the gamma estimates of those pairs have an interquartile range below 0.5. The second condition is the registry's reading of "relatively constant". Partially reproduced when only the first holds. All estimates are shown.

  • Rescaled merging-number distributions for different pairs of delta and delta' collapse onto each other, so the burst tree is self-similar across levels.

    Section III, paragraph 6; Figure 2, top rightfig2-neuron-merging-collapse
    From the paper

    the rescaled distributions for different pairs of δ and δ′ collapse on top of each other, implying that the structure of the burst tree is self-similar across hierarchical levels or timescales

    Criterion

    The figure plots P(k) times the mean of k against k over the mean of k. The paper shows the collapse by eye, and it is supported by the fitted tails ("This is supported by fitting power-law tails"). How to measure it is open, and two readings are named. (a) The complementary cumulative distributions of k over its mean for the pairs of Figure 2 (delta' = 55, 100, 180, 330, 590 ms, each with delta = 3.3, 5.9 and 11 delta') differ by no more than a factor of 2 at every point where all are above 10^-3. (b) The gamma estimates of those pairs lie within +/- 0.5 of their median. Reproduced when either holds. The curves and the estimates are shown.

  • In most cases both gamma and the burst-size exponent at the smaller timescale exceed 2, so the scaling relation reduces to beta at delta equal to the smaller of gamma and beta at delta', which agrees with the observations.

    Section III, paragraphs 8 and 9 (Eqs. 7 and 8); Figure 2fig2-exponents-above-two
    From the paper

    Since γδδ′, βδ′ > 2 in most cases as shown in Fig. 2, we have

    Criterion

    For the representative unit and each pair of fig2-neuron-merging-heavy-tail, with the fit of O3. (1) In more than half of the pairs, both gamma and beta at delta' exceed 2. (2) In more than half of the pairs, beta at delta is within 10% of the smaller of gamma and beta at delta' (Eq. 8). "Most" is read as more than half, and "agrees well" as within 10%. Both readings are the registry's. Reproduced when both hold, partially reproduced when only (1) holds.

  • Other neurons of the recordings show patterns similar to the representative unit, with heavy-tailed burst-size and merging-number distributions across timescales.

    Section III, paragraph 5fig2-other-neurons-similar
    From the paper

    Other sequences in the datasets show similar patterns.

    Criterion

    The paper names no set of neurons and shows none of them. The registry reads the set as (a) the pyramidal units of session Achilles-10252013, or (b) the pyramidal units of every session in the dandiset. Under either, only units whose relevant range holds at least three grid points are used, with all spikes of the session (O2) and the fit of O3. A unit shows the pattern when the power law is supported (R > 0, p < 0.05) for burst sizes at more than half of its relevant timescales, and for merging numbers at more than half of its pairs (ratios 3.3, 5.9 and 11). Reproduced when more than half of the units show the pattern under one reading. These thresholds are the registry's reading of "similar patterns". The distribution of each unit's median beta is reported.

  • Event sequences generated by the incremental dynamic algorithm, with linear, quadratic or exponential timescale series, show heavy-tailed burst-size and merging-number distributions over a wide range of timescales, with stable exponents.

    Section IV, paragraphs 9 and 10; Figure 4fig4-model-heavy-tails
    From the paper

    For all of the three timescale series, the generated ... show heavy-tailed distributions of burst sizes b for a wide range of timescales δ ... numbers k are also heavy-tailed across different pairs of timescales δ and δ′, even though these timescales do not coincide with the preset series of timescales δl ... the power-law exponent values of the heavy tails in Pδ(b) and Pδδ′(k) are stable across timescales

    Criterion

    Not testable on the dandiset. Algorithm 2 is run with n = 10^6, a zeta merging distribution with gamma = 2.5 (memory function of Eq. 16), delta_0 = 1 and uniform IETs (Eq. 10). The timescales are linear with w = 100, quadratic with w = 100, or exponential with w = 2 (Section IV). Bursts, the fit and the test are as for Figure 2. The pairs are those of the Figure 4 legends, delta = 1.8, 2.4 and 3.3 delta' (linear), 3.3, 5.9 and 11 delta' (quadratic), and 5.3, 12 and 28 delta' (exponential). Reproduced when, for each series, the power law is supported (R > 0, p < 0.05) at more than half of the relevant timescales and pairs, and the beta and gamma estimates each have an interquartile range below 0.5. "More than half" and the 0.5 band are the registry's readings of "wide range" and "stable", as in the Figure 2 claims.
