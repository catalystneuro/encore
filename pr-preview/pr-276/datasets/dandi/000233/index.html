    Results, SPW-Rs phase reset glucose oscillations, paragraph 1; Figure 2a-f

    Over the 18 rats with CA1 SPW-Rs and glucose recorded together, 3 of the 16 combinations of readings match both expected values. The first the criterion lists (raw delta current, Pearson, one correlogram per rat, lags -100 to +100 min) gives a mean peak R of -0.176 at a mean lag of 14.2 min; the high-pass delta gives -0.180 at 14.4 min, and Spearman with one correlogram per session and the high-pass delta gives -0.184 at 8.8 min. Mean R is within tolerance under all 16 combinations (-0.159 to -0.216) but the mean lag is not under the other 13 (15.8 to 33.1 min). Single-rat lags spread with an s.d. of 26 to 42 min, and leaving out rats with less than 6 h of data leaves 1 matching combination of 16. The peak R is negative in every rat; the median correlogram has its minimum, -0.108, at +5 min. CGM1, the only rat labelled as the ripple and glucose cohort (reading b), peaks at +5 min (R -0.101 Pearson, -0.194 Spearman). The outcome rests on the alignment of the SPW-R count with the glucose change (see deviations); with the count over the 5 min after each reading no per-rat combination matches (mean lags 19.2 to 37.8 min), and with the change to the next reading 4 of 8 match. With all stored events (no 5 s.d. threshold) the Spearman per-rat reading gives R -0.212 at 16.1 min and the Pearson one -0.175 at 14.2 min. The paper's n is 30 rats; 18 is all the dandiset allows. The match is fragile. With at least 6 h of both per rat only the high-pass, Spearman, per-session, -100 to +100 min combination matches. The median of the per-rat lags is 5 to 15 min in every combination, but only 7 to 10 rats peak between +5 and +15 min; in the raw, Pearson, per-rat combination single-rat lags run from -100 to +80 min, and the mean falls in tolerance because peaks at -100 to -20 min offset peaks at +55 to +80 min. R negative in most rats holds by construction, as R is the minimum over 41 offsets.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • 18 rats where the paper has 30. The dandiset holds 25 subjects; 7 have no SPW-R table or no glucose recorded on the days of the electrophysiology.
    • Assumed that the SPW-R count of a 5-min interval is paired with the glucose change over the same interval (the reading at its end minus the one at its start). The paper says only that SPW-R rate and glucose change are correlated at 5-min offsets; this pairing makes offset 0 the same interval for both. The two alignments one reading apart are shown and change the outcome.
    • SPW-Rs with a stored peak normed power below 5 s.d. are left out, following the Methods (SPW-R detection, "The peak power threshold was set to five s.d."); the stored tables of nine subjects hold events down to 4 s.d. All stored events are shown as a variant.
    • Glucose timestamps of twelve subjects were repaired by swapping day and month in dates whose day is 12 or less (see findings). Without the repair 1139 h of overlap remain of 1717.
    • The high-pass reading subtracts a centred 24-h running mean, a standard choice for a cutoff the paper does not give; a session is one file with at least 6 h of bins, a standard choice. Differencing already removes slow drift, so this reading changes delta little and nearly repeats the raw one.
    • Reading (a) of the rats leaves out CNO days and the stimulation periods of the optogenetic rats. The files label neither, so every day of every rat is used.

    SPW-R rate and glucose show power-power and phase-power coupling at periods of 15 to 75 min, with the faster SPW-R rhythm (about 40 min) modulating the slower glucose rhythm (about 60 min).

    We observed significant power-power and phase-power relationships in the 15-75-min range ... which suggests that a faster SPW-R frequency (about 40 min) modulates a slower (about 60 min) glucose rhythm

    Results, SPW-Rs phase reset glucose oscillations, paragraph 2; Extended Data Fig. 5d-e

    What the paper reports

    • power-power comodulogram against circularly shuffled data: Student's t-test, p < 10⁻²⁴ (n = 30 rats)
    • phase of glucose against SPW-R rate amplitude: Rayleigh test for non-uniformity, p < 10⁻¹⁸

    Over stretches of at least 12 h with both signals (16 rats), power-power coupling over 15 to 60 min exceeds 100 circular shuffles per rat (mean R 0.064 against -0.007, paired t = 2.57, p = 0.021), and the rate-weighted glucose phases are non-uniform (Rayleigh p = 3.2e-09, resultant length 0.033, above all 200 circular shifts of SPW-R rate, whose 95th percentile is 0.013; p < 0.05 in 10 of 16 rats singly). The comodulogram peak (R 0.216) is on the diagonal at 79.0 min for both signals, so the third condition, a peak at a longer glucose period, fails; the mean correlation where the glucose period is longer than the SPW-R period is 0.072 against 0.047 where it is shorter. The paper's p bounds (1e-24, 1e-18) are not reached.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Periods 12 to 120 min in place of 10 to 120, since 10 min is the Nyquist period of 5-min readings; band edges at the period divided and multiplied by 1.25 (a standard choice).

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Metadata errorIn twelve subjects (CGM30, CGM31, CGM39, CGM40, CGM41, CGM46, CGM48, CGM51, CGM57, CGM58, bruce, flex1) the GlucoseLevel timestamps carry dates with day and month swapped whenever the day is 12 or less, so a reading of 4 March 2021 is stored as 3 April 2021 and the series jumps by about a month at each midnight. The session reference times of some of these subjects are swapped the same way (CGM46 2021-04-03 for files of 4 March). Swapping back joins the readings into runs of 5-min steps and raises the hours of glucose overlapping the electrophysiology from 1139 to 1717. In CGM32, 86 readings are dated up to 2021-12-02, more than 30 days from the subject's other readings, which looks like the same swap for 1 to 12 February; swapping all of CGM32's dates does not reduce its breaks, so they were left as stored (no CGM32 recording falls on those days).
  • Other27 recordings of CGM1, CGM3, CGM30, CGM31 and CGM32 are deposited twice, under file names with dashes and with underscores, with the same LFP start, length and SPW-R events.
  • Description missingIn the SPW-R tables `start_time` and `stop_time` are on the session clock but `peak` is seconds from the start of the file; the column descriptions do not say so. Seven table names (Ripples, Ca1Ripples, Nonoise_Ca1Ripples, _Remove_Ca1Ripples, Dorsalripples, Ventralripples, Globalripples) are not described.
  • OtherThe stored SPW-R tables of nine subjects hold events with a peak normed power down to 4 s.d. (7 to 29% of their events), and CGM30's down to 0.23, where the Methods give a 5 s.d. peak threshold. Applying 5 s.d. changes the mean SPW-R rate from 11.07 to 10.19 per minute.
  • Metadata errorSession start times hold local wall-clock time but are labelled UTC (file-name times equal the reference plus the LFP start). In CGM2 and CGM3 files of one subject carry two different reference times, and in CGM1, CGM2 and CGM3 one file stores the glucose series in a different order or relative to its own reference; on the wall clock all files hold the same readings.
  • Description missingNothing links the 25 subjects (CGM1 to CGM60, bruce, dt15, flex1, ros, Vanessa) to the rats 1 to 45 of Supplementary Table 1 or to the cohorts of the paper (ripple and glucose, optogenetic CA1, CA3 or PPC, DREADD lateral or medial septum, CNO controls, dorsal and ventral). Only the files of CGM1 state an experiment; the others point to Supplementary Table 1.
  • Data missingThe dandiset holds no spike units, no optogenetic stimulation times, no CNO or vehicle labels or injection times, and no meal times. Sixteen of the paper's findings (Figures 3 and 4, Extended Data Figs 3e, 4d-f, 7, 8 and 10) and the meal part of Extended Data Fig. 1f cannot be tested.
  • OtherIn CGM37, CGM60, dt15 and flex1 the glucose readings and the electrophysiology cover different days, and CGM36, bruce and ros have no SPW-R table, so 7 of 25 subjects cannot contribute to the SPW-R and glucose analyses. Vanessa's glucose current has a median of 0.25 nA, against about 30 nA in the other subjects.
  • OtherNo interval between stored SPW-R peaks is shorter than 28.8 ms in any rat, and the gap from one event's stop to the next one's start is never below 20.0 ms (15.2 ms in CGM1, CGM2 and CGM3), with its minimum at exactly 20.0 ms in 19 of 22 rats. This matches a rule in the detection that merges events closer than 20 ms, and the 20-ms refractory period of Extended Data Fig. 2a may be this rule.
  • Methods not availableThe Methods say the input parameters of the SPW-R detection are stored with each session's ripple events. The NWB SPW-R tables carry only their column names and a description, so the thresholds and the merging interval used for each subject are not in the dandiset.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. How were the 5-min SPW-R counts aligned with the glucose readings for the cross-correlograms (the count of the interval ending at a reading paired with the change to that reading, or another pairing), which correlation was used (Pearson or Spearman), and over which offsets was the peak lag searched? With 18 rats the mean lag of 10.7 min is matched only under some combinations, and moving the alignment by one reading moves the mean lag by 5 min.

Reproduction SPW-R rate and interstitial glucose in rats (Tingley et al. 2021)

Tests the findings of Tingley, McClain, Kaya, Carpenter and Buzsaki (2021) on dandiset 000233, which holds CA1 LFP, detected SPW-Rs, the glucose sensor current, a head accelerometer and, for some rats, sleep states, from 25 rats. It tests the correlation of SPW-R rate with later glucose changes, its dependence on SPW-R features, glucose and SPW-R rhythms, and the SPW-R statistics. The optogenetic, DREADD, fasting and unit experiments cannot be tested, as the dandiset holds no stimulation, injection, meal or unit data and no cohort labels. All files

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • 18 rats where the paper has 30. The dandiset holds 25 subjects; 7 have no SPW-R table or no glucose recorded on the days of the electrophysiology.fig2-ripple-rate-predicts-glucose
  • Assumed that the SPW-R count of a 5-min interval is paired with the glucose change over the same interval (the reading at its end minus the one at its start). The paper says only that SPW-R rate and glucose change are correlated at 5-min offsets; this pairing makes offset 0 the same interval for both. The two alignments one reading apart are shown and change the outcome.fig2-ripple-rate-predicts-glucose
  • SPW-Rs with a stored peak normed power below 5 s.d. are left out, following the Methods (SPW-R detection, "The peak power threshold was set to five s.d."); the stored tables of nine subjects hold events down to 4 s.d. All stored events are shown as a variant.fig2-ripple-rate-predicts-glucose
  • Glucose timestamps of twelve subjects were repaired by swapping day and month in dates whose day is 12 or less (see findings). Without the repair 1139 h of overlap remain of 1717.fig2-ripple-rate-predicts-glucose
  • The high-pass reading subtracts a centred 24-h running mean, a standard choice for a cutoff the paper does not give; a session is one file with at least 6 h of bins, a standard choice. Differencing already removes slow drift, so this reading changes delta little and nearly repeats the raw one.fig2-ripple-rate-predicts-glucose
  • Reading (a) of the rats leaves out CNO days and the stimulation periods of the optogenetic rats. The files label neither, so every day of every rat is used.fig2-ripple-rate-predicts-glucose
  • 18 rats where the paper has 30; alignment and 5 s.d. threshold as in fig2-ripple-rate-predicts-glucose.fig2ef-correlation-beyond-shuffle
  • Epochs pooled over 18 rats; the paper does not say whether it pools.fig2g-clustered-ripples
  • Assumed, as in fig2-ripple-rate-predicts-glucose, that the SPW-R count of an epoch is paired with the glucose change over the same interval; the outcome changes under one of the two alignments one reading apart (shown).fig2g-clustered-ripples
  • Assumed, as in fig2-ripple-rate-predicts-glucose, that the 5-min interval holding a SPW-R is paired with the glucose change over the same interval; the outcome changes under one of the two alignments one reading apart (shown).edfig4-ripple-features

  • Band-pass filtering within runs of at least 24 h of readings (second-order Butterworth, zero phase), a standard choice.fig2h-phase-reset
