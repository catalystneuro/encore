Wirth et al. 2017 · PLOS Biology

Gaze-informed, task-situated representation of space in primate hippocampus during virtual navigation

Sylvia Wirth, Pierre Baraduc, Aurélie Planté, Serge Pinède, Jean‐René Duhamel

Outcomes of 28 claims

  • ✓5 reproduced
  • ◐2 partially reproduced

  • ⊘16 blocked

Some central findings on hippocampal cells of monkeys navigating a virtual maze held up in this data, several did not, and those involving gaze could not be tested. Maps in the task's state space carried more information than position and direction maps and were sparser than direction maps. Fewer cells were significant in state space (57 against the paper's 84), fewer coded distance to landmarks, and selectivity for the rotation at the maze centre failed under an assumed surrogate method the paper leaves open. Gaze maps, foveation-aligned responses and the direction tuning comparisons need a calibrated eye signal, which the files lack. Of the minor claims, gaze anticipation and learning speed reproduced and probe-session transfer did not.

    What the paper reports

    • Wilcoxon test, p < 0.001

    Before right turns (code 1) the horizontal eye signal, standardized within session, lies on the negative side of that before left turns (code 2) in each monkey, in the 500 and the 300 ms windows, for each eye and both eyes, successful or all trials, with rank-sum p below 1e-100. 500 ms, both eyes, successful trials, K median difference -0.77 session SD (p = 4.0e-148, 39 of 40 sessions, signed-rank across sessions p = 1.3e-11), S -1.00 (p = 4.3e-146, 20 of 20 sessions, p = 1.9e-6). Both monkeys, each window and the pooled test agree, so the readings of the criterion give one outcome. In the 500 ms before the code the view already rotates slowly toward the turn (median 0.49 to 0.75 degrees, in that direction in 95% to 99% of turns), and in K the eye velocity there differs between right and left turns in the direction of the slow phases (0.250 against -0.189 session SD, 38 of 40 sessions, p = 2.3e-9); in S it does not (9 of 20, p = 0.40). The S2B control (second of two turns) was not run.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The eye signal has no calibration, units or sign in the files, and is on a +-5000 scale in 36 sessions and a +-5 scale in 26. Values are standardized within each session (minus the session median, over its SD) before sessions are pooled; per-session differences are also counted and tested across sessions.
    • Assumed that a negative horizontal value is rightward, from the slow phases. During a turn the scene flows across the screen opposite to the turn and the slow phases of optokinetic nystagmus and pursuit follow it; the median eye velocity from 0 to 600 ms after the code is positive during right turns and negative during left turns in 40 of 40 sessions of K and 20 of 20 of S (signed-rank p = 1.8e-12 and 1.9e-6). This uses the eye velocity only, not the position the claim tests. The mean position during the turn moves toward the turn, as it does during optokinetic stimulation.
    • S1 Appendix A2, which holds the paper's analysis, was not available; the test is the rank-sum of the claims file.

  • openasked 2026-11-13about gaze-anticipates-landmarks

    The claim says that on being relocated the monkeys gazed proactively at where the landmarks would appear. The criterion compares the mean horizontal eye position over the whole passive return, turning right against left. During any rotation of the view the mean eye position shifts toward the side the view turns to, so this measure cannot tell anticipation from the eye position that goes with the rotation itself. Should the criterion restrict the window to the part of the return before the landmarks enter the field of view, or compare against a reference that rotates without landmarks to come, so that it tests the proactive part of the claim? Or does the S2C legend, which shows the mean over the whole return, fix the measure as the claims file has it?

    Passage. Results, Animals use direction and compute trajectory, paragraph 2, "Similarly, when being relocated to a new entry, monkeys proactively gazed at the location at which the landmarks would appear (see S2 Fig and S1 Appendix A2) (Wilcoxon test, p < 0.001)"; S2C legend, "Mean horizontal eye position during the passive return journey towards the right or the left".

    Why it matters. Under the criterion as written the claim is reproduced in both monkeys (K -0.56 session SD, p = 6.2e-58; S -0.56, p = 6.4e-30), and the same shift appears during the turns at the centre, where no landmark is anticipated in this sense. A window limited to the time before the landmarks appear could give a smaller effect or none, and would need the landmark positions, which the files do not give (they could be computed from the paper's geometry). The verifier of round 2 reported this point.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • The eye signal has no calibration, units or sign in the files, and is on a +-5000 scale in 36 sessions and a +-5 scale in 26. Values are standardized within each session (minus the session median, over its SD) before sessions are pooled; per-session differences are also counted and tested across sessions.gaze-anticipates-action
  • Assumed that a negative horizontal value is rightward, from the slow phases. During a turn the scene flows across the screen opposite to the turn and the slow phases of optokinetic nystagmus and pursuit follow it; the median eye velocity from 0 to 600 ms after the code is positive during right turns and negative during left turns in 40 of 40 sessions of K and 20 of 20 of S (signed-rank p = 1.8e-12 and 1.9e-6). This uses the eye velocity only, not the position the claim tests. The mean position during the turn moves toward the turn, as it does during optokinetic stimulation.gaze-anticipates-action
  • S1 Appendix A2, which holds the paper's analysis, was not available; the test is the rank-sum of the claims file.gaze-anticipates-action
  • The same standardization and sign as gaze-anticipates-action.gaze-anticipates-landmarks
  • The side of a return is taken from the change of camera orientation over it (median absolute change 102.5 degrees); the files do not say where the landmarks appear.gaze-anticipates-landmarks
  • The curves are the files' own; the method of the paper's reference 60 is not named in the text read. The columns are read as probability, lower and upper bound, as the values show, where the descriptor puts the probability in the middle column.learning-trials
  • The denominator is not stated; the fraction is given over the sessions reaching both criteria and over all sessions, and both match.learning-to-90
  • Probe sessions are found from the start arms (the first 10 trials use one or two entries and later ones others), as the files do not mark them. The introduction is the first trial from an entry not used in the first 10 (S3C legend); the variants are shown.probe-transfer
  • The measured values are monkey K's, after and at the start; monkey S has no probe session in the data.probe-transfer
  • Map resolution steps (square bins for position and state space, as in the dataset's scripts, equal angular bins for direction), two readings of the surrogate shifting and three of the trial window, as in the claims file (Method W). The start to reward window with the returns is almost the full one, as the trial ends at the reward (median 0 s from code 11 to 23).state-space-most-responsive
  • The shift reading rotates each 5 s chunk within itself by its own random offset.   • Reading (b), all responsive cells, cannot be formed without the gaze space; the three-space responsive cells, which it contains, stand in for it, an assumption.direction-center-vs-periphery

  • The three rotational moves are not named in the paper.
