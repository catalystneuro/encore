  • fig3-field-simulation

    In the simulations, 1 kV applied to one electrode gives about 1.8 kV/cm in the center of the small array and 0.8 kV/cm in the center of the large array, and about 4-fold more near the energized electrode (7 and 3.5 kV/cm per kV). Energizing one diagonal gives no field in the center.

    For example, applying 1 kV to one electrode produced the electric field of approximately 1.8 kV/cm and 0.8 kV/cm in center of the small and large arrays ... The electric field strength was about 4-fold higher near the energized electrode (7 kV/cm and 3.5 kV/cm per 1 kV applied, for the small and large arrays, respectively) Note that energizing either electrode diagonal produced no electric field in the center of the array

    Methods 2.3, paragraph 4; Figure 3

    How it will be judged

    The values are outputs of the paper's Sim4Life model (Methods 2.3, built as in its reference 15), which the paper does not deposit. The claim is tested only if a dataset holds the simulated field maps; a field model built by the author is not the paper's and is not a test. With the maps, reproduced when the four values are within tolerance and the center field with a diagonal energized is zero, read as below 1% of the single-electrode center field (a standard choice for numerical zero; the paper gives no threshold). How far from the electrode "near" is measured is not given; readings (a) the highest value at the needle surface; (b) the value at the edge of the area Methods 2.4 excludes next to the electrode (5% of the diagonal). Without the maps, blocked as data_missing.

  • fig4-sham-no-uptake

    Sham-exposed cells gain no YP fluorescence over the 10 min of imaging, while cells given pulses show a gradual rise in YP signal from the first image after exposure.

    Sham-exposed cells showed no gain in YP fluorescence during the 10 min of observation. In contrast, cells treated by electric pulses displayed gradual increase in YP signal, starting from the image frame taken immediately after nsEP exposure.

    Results 3.1, paragraph 2; Figure 4

    How it will be judged

    Per cell F-F0 (Methods 2.2). A claim of absence for sham, for which the paper reports no test; it is judged with the test Methods 2.4 names, a two-sided Student's t-test at p < 0.05, here one-sample against zero at the end of recording (O10, both readings). Reproduced when sham F-F0 does not differ from zero, and, for every pulsed group, the mean at the first image after 57 s exceeds zero by the same test and the mean end value exceeds the mean first post-pulse value (the paper's "gradual increase", for which it gives no test, so means are compared). Not reproduced when sham differs from zero. The sham effect size is given either way.

  • fig4-unipolar-strongest

    A single 600-ns pulse at 15 kV/cm causes by far the strongest YP uptake, 126 +/- 14 a.u. (n = 25 cells) by the end of recording.

    A single 600-ns pulse at 15 kV/cm caused by far the strongest YP uptake, reaching 126±14 a.u. (n=25) by the end of recording.

    Results 3.1, paragraph 2; Figure 4

    How it will be judged

    Tolerance is the paper's s.e. Per cell F-F0 (Methods 2.2), mean over cells, at the end of recording (O10). Reproduced when, under one reading, the mean is within tolerance and exceeds every other group's mean. The group size is reported against n = 25.

  • fig4-bipolar-weaker

    Bipolar signals that start with the same pulse cause significantly weaker uptake (p < 0.001). By the end of recording the 4-phasic signal reaches 20.8 +/- 2.0 a.u., the 8-phasic 6.9 +/- 1.6 a.u., and the 8-phasic with 5-us delays 25.4 +/- 2.8 a.u.

    Bipolar signals started with exactly the same pulse (600 ns, 15 kV/cm) but caused significantly weaker effects (p<0.001). By the end of recording, YP signal reached only 20.8±2.0 a.u. for the 4-phasic signal; 6.9±1.6 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal; and 25.4±2.8 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal with 5-μs delays between phases.

    Results 3.1, paragraph 2; Figure 4

    How it will be judged

    Tolerances are the paper's s.e. Per cell F-F0 at the end of recording (O10). Each bipolar group is compared with the unipolar group by an unpaired two-sided Student's t-test (Methods 2.4, applied to single cells as a reading; Welch's form is a variant). Reproduced when all three means are within tolerance under one reading of O10 and all three tests give p < 0.001, the test outcome taken by the opening comment's rule for tests. Partially reproduced when the tests hold but a mean is outside tolerance.

  • fig4-twenty-fold-cancellation

    The 8-phasic signal with no delays causes about 20-fold weaker electroporation than a single unipolar pulse and is the most efficient at cancellation; the 4-phasic signal and the delayed 8-phasic signal cancel 4 to 5-fold.

    identified the 8-phasic bipolar signal as the most efficient at cancellation (~20-fold) ... the other two types of bipolar signals which were less efficient at cancellation (4–5-fold, Fig. 4 ) ... Note that applying the 8-phasic bipolar signal with no inter-phase delays caused about 20-fold weaker electroporation than a single unipolar pulse

    Figure 4 legend; Results 3.1, paragraph 2; Results 3.4, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Ratios of group means at the end of recording (O10). Results 3.4 gives the other two signals as "4-5-fold, Fig. 4"; the paper's own means give 18.3 for the 8-phasic signal, 6.1 for the 4-phasic and 5.0 for the delayed 8-phasic, so its 4-phasic value is outside its own range. Reproduced when, under one reading, the 8-phasic ratio is within 18 to 22, is the largest of the three, and the other two ratios are within 3.6 to 5.5. Partially reproduced when the 8-phasic ratio is within tolerance and the largest but another ratio is outside 3.6 to 5.5; the paper's 6.1 is reported beside the result.
