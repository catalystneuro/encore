Findings about the data 25

What the attempts that used this dataset found about the data themselves, for its owner and the archive. The claims and their outcomes are on each paper’s page.

  • Paper inconsistentThe text gives 449 MTL units for the screening task. The screening files hold 421 MTL units (159 hippocampus, 262 amygdala), which agrees with the paper's own screening percentages (138 is 32.78% of 421); 449 is the Sternberg MTL count.
    Found by the attempt on Kyzar et al. 2024 (kyzar-2024-01, 2026-10-30)
  • Paper inconsistentThe persistent activity values in the text (4.76 plus or minus 4.71 against 2.67 plus or minus 3.66 Hz, preferred against non-preferred trials "during the maintenance period") are reproduced exactly by the rates 200 to 1000 ms after Encoding 1 onset of all 119 concept cells of the code's parametric test, split by whether the Encoding 1 image is the preferred one. In the maintenance window the same cells fire at 3.05 against 2.70 Hz. The released code computes these Encoding 1 rates under the names hzPref and hzNonPref, with a mask that, as written, selects every trial as preferred (per the claims file's reading of NWB_calcSelective_SB.m).
    Found by the attempt on Kyzar et al. 2024 (kyzar-2024-01, 2026-10-30)
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. The Methods name permutation tests for the concept, maintenance and probe cells, while the released code runs parametric tests (anovan, paired t-tests) and keeps the permutation versions in comments; the parametric tests reproduce every published count exactly here (138, 26, 94, 144, 122). Were the published counts computed with the parametric tests? If permutation tests were used, how many permutations, and were the probe and maintenance comparisons paired by trial (sign flipping) or unpaired (label shuffling)? For the probe cells the choice decides whether the count matches (118 or 111 paired, 109 or 104 unpaired, against 122), and the encoding rate (all encoding periods shown, or Encoding 1 only) is also not stated.
    Found by the attempt on Kyzar et al. 2024 (kyzar-2024-01, 2026-10-30)
  • Selection not documentedThe reported ISI violation spread (0.41 plus or minus 0.50%) is matched only after leaving out units with 4% or more violations, as NWB_QA_graphs.m does (one unit, at 11.74%); over all units it is 0.43 plus or minus 0.63%. The reaction time ANOVA uses correct trials only, as the code does; neither filter is stated in the paper.
    Found by the attempt on Kyzar et al. 2024 (kyzar-2024-01, 2026-10-30)
  • File format errorFour units have spike times out of order, each with one backward step. Screening files of subject 11 (units 26 and 32, steps of 9.8 and 12.4 ms) and subject 5 (unit 18, 0.9 ms), and the Sternberg file of subject 2 (unit 22, 0.3 ms). NWB expects spike times in ascending order.
    Found by the attempt on Kyzar et al. 2024 (kyzar-2024-01, 2026-10-30)
  • Metadata errorColumn descriptions in the units and trials tables are wrong. `waveforms_mean_proj_dist` is described as "Cluster Isolation distance, computed using all waveforms in the cluster" (the description of `waveforms_isolation_distance`), and `loadsEnc3_PicIDs` as "Picture ID for Enc1 loads". Every column of the electrodes table but `id` is described as "my description".
    Found by the attempt on Kyzar et al. 2024 (kyzar-2024-01, 2026-10-30)
  • Description missingSubjects 13 and 14 have the native IDs P39CS and P39CS_2, the same age (26), sex and recording year, and Table 1 gives the same diagnosis. Neither the files nor the paper say whether they are the same patient, so the count of 21 patients may be 20.
    Found by the attempt on Kyzar et al. 2024 (kyzar-2024-01, 2026-10-30)
  • OtherTwo Sternberg trials have implausible timestamps. In one trial of subject 9 (load 1) Encoding 1 lasts 2.5 s and the maintenance and probe onsets fall at the same time (529.288 s), so it has no maintenance period. In another trial of subject 9 (load 3) the fixation cross and Encoding 1 onsets are 0.03 ms apart (1189.469 s), so the baseline window falls on the image. In one trial of subject 21 the fixation cross lasts 14.07 s, against the 0.9 to 1 s the paper describes (the 1st and 99th percentiles over trials are 0.93 and 1.20 s). All three are included in the analysis, as the code includes them; leaving them out does not change the persistent activity result.
    Found by the attempt on Kyzar et al. 2024 (kyzar-2024-01, 2026-10-30)
  • Published code disagreesThe paper's GLM code (01_task/main_1e,f.py:9 and 113) uses subject 1's reaction times as the predictor for every neuron of every participant. The RT-modulation percentages of Fig. 1f (AMY 5.79%, preSMA 8.00%, vmPFC 6.25%) are reproduced exactly only with that predictor; with each trial's own reaction time preSMA, AMY and dACC have significant proportions of RT-modulated neurons after correction.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Selection not documentedThe matched trial sets of the Methods (96 load-matched and 75 identity-matched trials per participant) exist only as the code's tables data/new_trial_info.xlsx and data/new_trial_final.xlsx; no script builds them and the paper does not say how they were chosen. Pseudo-population decoding accuracies depend on which trials enter (random draws of six trials per image give 56.7 to 86.7% at 1000 ms against 76.67% with the code's table).
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • OtherThe paper states that sixteen participants completed 135 trials and five completed 108; the dandiset has fifteen sessions of 135 trials and six of 108 (subjects 6, 7, 8, 9, 16 and 19).
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Metadata errorIn sub-9's Sternberg file one trial (row 29) has timestamps_Maintenance equal to timestamps_Probe, a maintenance of 0 s, against 2.53 to 2.84 s in every other trial.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • OtherThe duration of the first encoding image differs by session, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5 or 2.5 s (from timestamps_Encoding1 to timestamps_Encoding1_end), which the paper does not state; its analyses use a 1 s encoding window. Fixation lasts about 0.91 to 1.2 s in most sessions, with single longer fixations up to 14.07 s, where the paper gives 0.9 to 1.0 s.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Published code disagreesThe code's epoch burst analysis (04_bursting/main_4f-k,6c,d.py:216-219) assigns maintenance bursts to M1, M2 and M3 by thirds of the whole concatenated train, that is by thirds of the trials, while the Methods define three consecutive 1 s windows from maintenance onset. The burst counts per window differ between the two.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Published code disagreesThe column the code reports as CV2 for Fig. 5h (02_trial_info.py:42-46) is the plain coefficient of variation of the interspike intervals, not Holt's local CV2 that the Methods cite; the paper's values (PY 1.673, IN 1.319) match the plain CV.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors: which peak rule detects population bursts, a smoothed value above the 90th percentile (Methods) or a prominence at or above it (Results and code), and what are the threshold of the surrogates and the minimum spacing (140 ms in the Methods, 70 ms in the Results)? Fig. 4c is reproduced only under the Methods' height rule with the Methods' groups, with a spacing of 70 ms (1 bin) or 140 ms (2 bins; low ACG BH p 0.0483, and 0.0601 under one rounding-level variant of the ACG fit), and under the code's prominence rule concept cells also differ from the null.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors: what is the 'label-shuffled null' of Fig. 6a,b, whose legend names rate-matched Poisson surrogates? Under the Poisson null interneurons exceed the null in encoding, and under a shuffle of cell-type labels within participant they fall below it.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors: which decay time constants are compared between preferred and nonpreferred trials in Extended Data Fig. 5g,h? The code reads them from a shipped table (decay_constant_change.xlsx) that no script computes; ACG tau_decay per trial type shows no effect here.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors: how is the 'temporal extent' of a cross-temporal cluster measured for the 1,000-shuffle cluster-duration null, and is the binomial test one- or two-sided (Methods, Population decoding, says two-sided; Statistical analyses and the code, one-sided)? Here the shuffled maps had no cluster, so the choice did not matter.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • OtherThe ACG features of the code's shipped Cell_metrics.xlsx (firing rate, normalised mean ACG, burst index, ISI CV) are recomputed from the dandiset's spike times to within 1% for 99% of units, using every spike of the unit; the fitted R2 and time constants come from MATLAB's optimizer and differ for part of the units in a scipy port, which gives 317 well-fit units in place of 294. The triple-exponential fit is sensitive at the level of rounding: changing its start point by a factor 1 +/- 1e-9 or the ACG by 1 + 1e-12 moves R2 by more than 1e-3 for 37 to 40 units and gives 315 to 317 well-fit units, so results built on it (cell types, tau decay groups) can differ between machines.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Published code disagreesThe code's maintenance burst test (04_bursting/main_4c,d,6a,b.py, TRIAL_WIN = 2.8 at line 13, surrogate times uniform on [0, TRIAL_WIN] at lines 288-289) concatenates maintenance trials with a 2.8 s stride, while the maintenance in the data lasts 2.53 to 2.84 s and the real spikes stop at the probe. The surrogates fill the silent tail, and homogeneous Poisson trains confined to each trial's maintenance then exceed the null for all neurons in 17 to 20 of 20 participants (BH p 0.0005 to 0.0025). The maintenance results of Fig. 4d and Fig. 6a,b against the Poisson null can arise from the window alone.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Published code disagrees08_dpca_matrices.py builds the encoding tensor from the trials whose new_trial_id is among the drawn ids (line 105) but the maintenance tensor from the trials whose trial_id is among them (line 192). In 4 of the 14 sessions with concept cells (subjects 7, 8, 9 and 16) these are other trials, only 9 to 11 of 30 shared and 13 of 30 at load 1. The paper's Fig. 3d statistics are matched most closely with the code as written.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Published code disagrees03_dpca/main_3h,supp_2c,d,l.py fits the early half of maintenance with protect ['t'] and the late half with protect [] (lines 115 and 124). With these settings the early half separates the stimuli less than the late half in all 100 splits, the reverse of Fig. 3g,h; the paper's result is obtained with protect [] for the early half.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors: over which window are the Poisson surrogates of the maintenance burst test drawn? The code draws them on [0, 2.8 s] for every trial (main_4c,d,6a,b.py:13 and 288-289) while each trial's maintenance ends at the probe, 2.53 to 2.84 s after onset, and burst-free trains then exceed the null. Here Fig. 4d is reproduced over each trial's own maintenance with the Methods' groups and the prominence rule, and not with the code's groups.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
  • OtherIn the paper's GLM test (01_task/main_1e,f.py:172-205) a neuron is significant when |b1| is at or above the 95th percentile of its permutation null. With load (three levels) as the predictor, the slope depends on the data only through sum(x * y), so many permutations give exactly the real slope, and 8 of the 902 neurons have |b1| equal to their threshold; floating-point rounding then decides them. Counting them in or out moves preSMA between 47 and 51 significant neurons and vmPFC between 6 and 7 with the code's shared permutation.
    Found by the attempt on Kussovska et al. 2025 (kussovska-2025-01, 2026-11-10)
