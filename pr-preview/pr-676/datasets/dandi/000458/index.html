Cortico-thalamo-cortical interactions modulate electrically evoked EEG responses in mice: Most depth and state effects held in this data: superficial stimulation gave smaller ERPs and lower PCIst than deep, and PCIst was higher awake than under isoflurane at 30 of 30 sites. The late ERP peak was not absent with superficial stimulation (0 to 2 of 12 sites lacked it), and spiking outside the stimulated area was not limited to deep stimulation. Thalamus led cortex in the rebound by far less than reported, and burst correlations with cortical rate were weaker. Laminar CSD and stimulation depth could not be tested because the files hold no layers or electrode depth. Figure 4 unit counts are one third of the legend's, probably because it counts each unit once per current.

10.1016/j.neuron.2023.08.005: The paper's central patch-clamp findings, that synaptic GABAA receptor reversal is more depolarized and inhibition shunting in awake cortex and that blocking local network activity undoes this, could not be tested, as those recordings are not deposited. Its Neuropixels finding that neurons are less synchronous and more variable in spike timing awake than under isoflurane held up in direction and test, with means slightly off. Exact numbers come out only with the firing rate the paper's code computes, not the Methods' rate (144 units excluded against 203). The network model's shunting inhibition gave lower synchrony, higher entropy and better decoding in every run, two means just outside tight tolerances. Higher awake firing and the rate-regressed control also held.
