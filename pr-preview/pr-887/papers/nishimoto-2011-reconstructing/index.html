Reconstructing Visual Experiences from Brain Activity Evoked by Natural Movies

Shinji Nishimoto, An T. Vu, Thomas Naselaris, Yuval Benjamini, Bin Yu, Jack L. Gallant

Outcomes of 10 claims

Summary: The paper's encoding findings held up in this data, while its movie reconstructions could not be tested. Motion-energy models predicted visual cortex responses better than a static model, with a small gain from direction, V1 best predicted, retinotopic receptive fields and faster preferred speeds in the periphery. The model identified the test second behind 88% of one subject's responses, against the paper's 95%. Both results rest on ridge regression in place of the paper's L1 fit, whose penalty choice the paper does not give, and hold only with penalties chosen by cross-validation. Reconstruction and identification among a million clips need a library of internet movies that is not public.

    Deviations from the paper’s method
    • Ridge regression replaces the paper's L1-regularized least squares (Supplemental, Model Fitting, "L1-regularized least squares regression procedure was used to obtain the linear weights", refs. 15 and 16). This is an assumption. The paper gives neither the L1 penalty nor the stopping rule of its coordinate descent, and ridge with a penalty per voxel chosen on held-out data is the standard fit of voxel-wise encoding models of this kind. As a check, the static and directional models are fit by an approximate L1 (accelerated proximal gradient along seven penalties from 0.5 to 0.01 of the largest useful one, 60 iterations each, penalty chosen per voxel on the held-out 10%) on 120 voxels; the paper gives no stopping rule, and at the smallest penalties the objective still changes by about 1% over the last iterations. The penalty is chosen per voxel from 19 values (1 to 1e9, half decades) under both readings of the claims file, (a) a held-out 10% of the training data in 50 s blocks and (b) five-fold cross-validation over such blocks (whole decades only, for time), and the model is refit on all training data.
    • The data set's test responses are not on the scale of the z-scored training responses (s.d. 0.10 for rv and 0.20 for rva, against 0.99 for rt). Each test repeat is z-scored per voxel before averaging, following Data Preprocessing ("For each 10 minute run and each individual voxel ... signals were then normalized to mean 0.0 and standard deviation 1.0"). Correlations with the mean signal hardly depend on this.
    • Voxels are those of the data set's V1, V2, V3, V3A and V3B masks (4,183, 4,668 and 4,410 per subject, against the paper's about 4,500); 179, 46 and 29 voxels with missing values are dropped.
    • The motion-energy bank is pymoten's pyramid with spatial frequencies 0 to 32 cycles/image on 96 x 96 frames at 15 Hz, which gives exactly 6,555 filters (spatial envelope 0.6 wavelengths, 10-frame window), the reading the claims file asks to reproduce that count. Its 855 zero-spatial-frequency filters are one per position of each nonzero frequency's grid, with that frequency's envelope, at each temporal frequency, and have no direction ("zero spatial frequency filters occur only once (no orientation)", Gabor Wavelet Basis Set); the nondirectional model keeps them as single features, so it has 4,275 features. Quadrature pairs are combined by the square root of the summed squares before the log, which after the z-score differs from the paper's squared sum only through the log offset of 1e-5. The log is pymoten's log(x + 1e-5), which is the claims file's log(x) reading, not its standard choice log(1 + x); the claims file judges this unlikely to change the outcome, and log(1 + x) was not run. Envelope variants (half and double) were not run, for time.
    • The held-out 10% and the five folds are drawn once and used for all three subjects, so that one eigendecomposition of the kernel serves the three (standard choice); the held-out blocks are laid on the concatenated kept samples, and hold 728 samples.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Methods not availableThe paper fits each voxel by L1-regularized least squares by coordinate descent (refs. 15 and 16) but does not say how the penalty or the stopping point was chosen, and publishes no code. Here ridge is used, with the penalty chosen per voxel on held-out training data, and the static model's mean accuracy is within tolerance under five-fold cross-validation (0.233) but not under a single held-out 10% (0.211); an approximate L1 fit on 120 voxels predicts better than ridge by 0.005 to 0.025. Was the penalty chosen per voxel on held-out training data, and on which split?#
  • Methods not availableThe paper does not say whether identification and prediction accuracy use the mean of the ten test repeats or single repeats, nor how the Tikhonov penalty of the noise covariance was chosen. Here the repeat mean with a Tikhonov penalty chosen on held-out training data gives 408 of 486 with encoding penalties chosen on one held-out 10% and 426 with penalties chosen by five-fold cross-validation, below the reported 464; were these the choices, and how were the encoding penalties chosen for identification?#

Reproduction Motion-energy encoding of natural movies in human visual cortex (Nishimoto et al. 2011)

Tests the encoding-model findings of Nishimoto, Vu, Naselaris, Benjamini, Yu and Gallant (2011) on CRCNS vim-2, which holds the BOLD responses of three subjects to 7,200 s of training movies and 540 s of test movies repeated ten times, with the movies themselves. It fits static, nondirectional and directional motion-energy models to the voxels of V1 to V3B, compares their prediction accuracy by subject and area, maps receptive fields and speed tuning with simulated probes, identifies the test second that evoked each BOLD pattern in subject S1, and tests a model with color channels. The sampled movie prior and the 1,000,000-clip set used for reconstruction are not public. All files

Environment: Python 3.13, 15 packages pinned with their hashes in requirements.lock.txt; CI runs each notebook from a new environment built from it.

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • Probes follow Selectivity Estimation (17 x 17 grid of dynamic Gaussian white noise on uniform gray; full-field drifting gratings at the basis set's six spatial frequencies, three temporal frequencies and eight directions; response minus the response to gray). The paper does not give the noise or grating contrast, the gray level or the probe duration; the standard choices are L* 50 gray, noise s.d. 25 L*, grating amplitude 40 L*, 40 frames of which the middle 20 are scored. The predicted response to a sustained probe is its features times the directional weights (penalty reading a) summed over the four delays. The Gaussian is isotropic, fit by a search over centers (half-cell steps, also outside the display) and widths with amplitude and offset by least squares.fig2fgh-retinotopic-receptive-fields fig2j-speed-eccentricity
  • The fits of fig2de-prediction-accuracy (ridge in place of L1), under penalty reading a.fig2fgh-retinotopic-receptive-fields fig2j-speed-eccentricity
  • Ridge regression replaces the paper's L1-regularized least squares (Supplemental, Model Fitting, "L1-regularized least squares regression procedure was used to obtain the linear weights", refs. 15 and 16). This is an assumption. The paper gives neither the L1 penalty nor the stopping rule of its coordinate descent, and ridge with a penalty per voxel chosen on held-out data is the standard fit of voxel-wise encoding models of this kind. As a check, the static and directional models are fit by an approximate L1 (accelerated proximal gradient along seven penalties from 0.5 to 0.01 of the largest useful one, 60 iterations each, penalty chosen per voxel on the held-out 10%) on 120 voxels; the paper gives no stopping rule, and at the smallest penalties the objective still changes by about 1% over the last iterations. The penalty is chosen per voxel from 19 values (1 to 1e9, half decades) under both readings of the claims file, (a) a held-out 10% of the training data in 50 s blocks and (b) five-fold cross-validation over such blocks (whole decades only, for time), and the model is refit on all training data.fig2de-prediction-accuracy
  • The data set's test responses are not on the scale of the z-scored training responses (s.d. 0.10 for rv and 0.20 for rva, against 0.99 for rt). Each test repeat is z-scored per voxel before averaging, following Data Preprocessing ("For each 10 minute run and each individual voxel ... signals were then normalized to mean 0.0 and standard deviation 1.0"). Correlations with the mean signal hardly depend on this.fig2de-prediction-accuracy
  • Voxels are those of the data set's V1, V2, V3, V3A and V3B masks (4,183, 4,668 and 4,410 per subject, against the paper's about 4,500); 179, 46 and 29 voxels with missing values are dropped.fig2de-prediction-accuracy
  • The motion-energy bank is pymoten's pyramid with spatial frequencies 0 to 32 cycles/image on 96 x 96 frames at 15 Hz, which gives exactly 6,555 filters (spatial envelope 0.6 wavelengths, 10-frame window), the reading the claims file asks to reproduce that count. Its 855 zero-spatial-frequency filters are one per position of each nonzero frequency's grid, with that frequency's envelope, at each temporal frequency, and have no direction ("zero spatial frequency filters occur only once (no orientation)", Gabor Wavelet Basis Set); the nondirectional model keeps them as single features, so it has 4,275 features. Quadrature pairs are combined by the square root of the summed squares before the log, which after the z-score differs from the paper's squared sum only through the log offset of 1e-5. The log is pymoten's log(x + 1e-5), which is the claims file's log(x) reading, not its standard choice log(1 + x); the claims file judges this unlikely to change the outcome, and log(1 + x) was not run. Envelope variants (half and double) were not run, for time.fig2de-prediction-accuracy
  • The held-out 10% and the five folds are drawn once and used for all three subjects, so that one eigendecomposition of the kernel serves the three (standard choice); the held-out blocks are laid on the concatenated kept samples, and hold 728 samples.fig2de-prediction-accuracy

  • The rank-sum p-values are given one-sided (V1 greater, as the claim is that V1 is best) and two-sided; both give the same counts.figs1a-motion-better-every-area
  • Judged on the claims file's proxy test (fits inside the display, contralateral V1, eccentricity rising forward along V1); the comparison with multifocal maps is not run, since those maps are not public.fig2fgh-retinotopic-receptive-fields
  • The fits use ridge regression in place of L1 (see fig2de-prediction-accuracy), under penalty readings a and b; the outcome rests on reading b, under which the 90% fit for voxel selection and the Tikhonov choice uses the reading b penalties.fig3ab-identification-s1
  • The Tikhonov inverse is inv(S + lambda * mean(diag S) * I), lambda from 0.1 to 100 in quarter decades, chosen by identification of the held-out 10% of the training data with the residual covariance of the 90% fit (claims file, Likelihood). The scaling by the mean residual variance is a standard choice.fig3ab-identification-s1
