Problems that change results 1

The data are wrong in a way the files do not show, so an analysis that takes them as given comes out wrong. Each was checked against the notebook’s outputs by someone other than the attempt’s author.

  • File format errorChanges resultsIn 50 of the 99 ecephys files the units table repeats a unit id (72 extra rows, for example ids 3, 4, 4, 5, 5), and in every case the repeated rows hold the identical spike train. A unit is stored twice, so an analysis that takes each row as a unit counts it twice. In this attempt the duplicates change which sessions reach the paper's 18-unit filter (9 or 10 mice and 15 to 18 sessions depending on whether they are counted once). In the population-coupling analysis each duplicated unit forms a pair at r = 1 with its copy and its coupling rises, as the copy is part of the summed activity of the other neurons; 12 of the 14 duplicated units in the sessions selected with every row kept fall in the top coupling bin. With every row kept the high-coupler outcome is partially reproduced under seven of eight readings, and with units counted once not reproduced under seven of eight. Whether another unit was lost in place of the copy cannot be told from the files.#
    Checked: The outputs show the core statement: cell 5 prints 50 of 99 files, 72 extra rows, all with identical spike trains, and the example ids 3, 4, 4, 5, 5; cell 15 shows the selection effect (9 against 10 mice, 15 to 16 against 17 to 18 sessions); cell 22 shows 12 of 14 duplicated units in the top bin at r = 1. The causal clause about bin 5 is not shown. Critical is right: one unit deposited as two rows is one of the rubric's examples, the repeated id is the only trace, and an analysis that reads the table as stored comes out wrong, as this attempt's adopted reading did.
    Found by the attempt on Gable et al. 2026 (gable-2026-01, 2026-11-22)

They look like placeholders for removed trials filled with default values. Taken as given they raise performance and bias trial counts towards lick right. 1,502 of them are in the 75 sessions with tagging light, 754 (49%) in the first or last tenth of a session, and 7 in the Ai32 mouse 218693, so they are not the photoactivation trials.#

  • Metadata errorThe imaging mice are given the genotype 'Rosa-lsl-tdtomato' (one) or none (three); the Methods use C57Bl/6Crl mice. The ROI labels include 'Pyr' (23) and 'interneuron' (9), which the paper does not describe, and the `included` flag is described only as 'whether to include this roi into later analyses', without its rule; 424 included ROIs are not 5% brighter than their neuropil by the median ratio.#
    Found by the attempt on Li et al. 2015 (li-2015-01, 2026-10-21)
  • Description missingThe `lick_trace` series (10 kHz) is described only as the tongue movement when licking; nothing says it is the photodiode signal used for reaction times or how a reaction time is read from it.     Found by the attempt on Li et al. 2015 (li-2015-01, 2026-10-21)
  • Other66 extracellular trials without an early lick have a go cue that is not 2.6 s after the sample event (65 sooner, down to 0.03 s, and one later, 2.68 s), against 2.6 s in all others, and 75 have no go cue; they are dropped here.#
    Found by the attempt on Li et al. 2015 (li-2015-01, 2026-10-21)
  • OtherSpikes are stored almost only within 5.5 s trial windows (from 0.57 s before the sample event); in 97 of 99 sessions at least 99% of the spikes fall in a window. In two sessions (mice 214429 and 219031) 24% and 30% fall between trials, in gaps between the windows. So baselines before the trial and inter-trial activity cannot be measured in most sessions, and in early-lick trials, where the go cue comes later, part of the response epoch is not stored.#
