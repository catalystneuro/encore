  • ✓1 reproduced

The main finding holds: MSTd noise correlations are lower in trained than in naive monkeys (0.024 against 0.119), and training lowers the relation between noise and signal correlation without changing its slope, though the intercept intervals overlap. The population simulations also hold, with correlated noise limiting sensitivity and the trained reduction changing it little, but narrowing preferences gives a smaller gain than reported, under tuning parameters the paper does not state. Smaller choice probabilities in fore-aft neurons did not reproduce, on 129 units from two trained monkeys that may not be the paper's sample. Time courses, Fano factors and naive against trained single-neuron tuning could not be tested, as the data set holds no spike times or naive single units.

    • factorial ANOVA, main effect of preference group, p < 0.001 (n = 992 visual and 556 vestibular neurons)

    On 258 values from 129 units of two trained monkeys, lateral units have lower median thresholds than fore-aft ones under every reading, in both conditions. Under one of the 8 readings of the claims file the difference reaches p < 0.001, as the criterion requires ("under one reading"), with vector-sum preference, spline threshold, log10, condition as a factor, p = 1.9e-07. Per condition that reading gives visual p = 3.4e-07 and vestibular p = 0.040, so the per-condition reading fails in the vestibular condition. In the other 7 readings the two-factor p is 0.013 to 0.77, and with spline-peak preferences no reading reaches p < 0.05. The neurometric thresholds of experiment 2, a variant, give two-factor p = 7.3e-07 (vector sum, log10) and 0.0011 (spline peak, log10). Per monkey, the visual difference is significant in both (p = 0.00024 and 0.00017) and the vestibular one in neither (p = 0.082 and 0.185). The outcome rests on the one passing reading. In it the preference by condition interaction is significant (p = 0.026), the difference being larger in the visual condition. The two-factor ANOVA counts each unit twice; a mixed model with a random intercept per unit gives p = 2.7e-07 in that reading and no other reading below 0.001. With a proxy of the paper's tuning selection, 252 of 258 values are kept and the reading gives p = 1.2e-07 (mixed model 1.8e-07); with a stricter proxy, 169 are kept and it gives p = 9.6e-08. Under both, no other reading reaches p < 0.001 (smallest 0.012), and the smallest two-factor p with spline-peak preferences is 0.051. Tested after the round-1 verification found that the claim had been blocked wrongly.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • A subset of the paper's data. The experiment 1 tuning curves hold 129 units of two trained monkeys (Gu et al. 2008), against the paper's 992 visual and 556 vestibular neurons of naive and trained animals, so the ANOVA has no training factor. The two-factor reading has preference group and condition.
    • For the linear interpolation the derivative at 0, a sampled heading, is the slope between -22.5 and 22.5 degrees, a choice made here as a piecewise-linear curve has no derivative at a knot. Two values whose curve is flat at 0 have infinite thresholds and are left out of that reading.
    • The tuning curves in spikes/s are read as counts over the middle 1 s (Experimental Procedures, Noise and Signal Correlations). The first term of equation 3 scales with the window and the trace term does not, so another window does not scale all thresholds alike; with 0.5 s or 2 s the passing reading gives p = 1.9e-07, as with 1 s.
    • The Figure 4 legend includes only neurons with significant heading tuning (p < 0.05, ANOVA). That selection needs trial responses, which the data set does not hold, so the readings include every unit. As a check, a proxy selection computes the one-way ANOVA from each mean curve with variance equal to the mean (equation 4) and five repetitions ("typically repeated five times", Experimental Protocol and Task), and a stricter one with three repetitions and variance twice the mean; the outcome is the same under both.
    • The two-factor reading counts each unit once per condition; a mixed model with a random intercept per unit is shown and agrees.
    • The vector sum weights each response by the angle it covers, as in fig8ab.
