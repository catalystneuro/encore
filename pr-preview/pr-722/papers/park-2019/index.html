  • cell-counts

    The paper analyses 115 dorsal MEC head-direction cells from 13 rats, among 353 MEC cells forming 556 cell pairs (Table S1), out of 1594 MEC single units recorded.

    We recorded 115 dorsal MEC head-direction cells (Figure S1; Table S1) from 13 rats

    Results, Degraded Directional Tuning, paragraph 1; STAR Methods, Electrophysiology; Table S1

    How it will be judged

    Counts of units and simultaneously recorded pairs, matched across the three trials with FileNameList.xlsx. The dataset may hold only the cells of Table S1, or all recorded units; the readings are (a) count the units and pairs the dataset holds; (b) apply the paper's head-direction criteria (mean rate below 10 AP/s, significant Rayleigh test and significant Kuiper's test against sampled head directions in stable1, each at p < 0.05, a level the Methods do not state) and count the cells that pass. Spike width cannot be applied without waveforms. Reproduced when the counts are within tolerance under a reading; the per-rat counts of Table S1 are compared as well.

    Figure 1A legend; Results, Degraded Directional Tuning, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    The paper does not say which measure of avoidance is plotted or how many trials the curves span (the corrected numerator degrees of freedom imply at least five trials). Per condition, a repeated-measures ANOVA on trial (and in the rotating condition on trial and frame, room or arena zone) with Greenhouse-Geisser correction, as the legend and Statistics state. Reproduced when, under a reading, the trial effect has p < 0.05 in both conditions, performance improves over trials, and the trial by frame interaction has p ≥ 0.05. Rats R030 and R850 are left out (Table S1 footnote). Training sessions may not be in the dataset; whether they are is the author's to find.

Reproduced when, under a reading, the condition effect has p < 0.05, stable2 is lower than each other condition by Tukey-Kramer at p < 0.05, and the other three do not differ; partially reproduced when the effect holds with another order.

  • fig1d-preference-change

    The change in preferred direction differs across trial pairs (stable1-stable2 = stable1-rotating room frame > stable1-rotating arena frame), and is greater for every trial pair than between the two halves of stable1.

    The rotation-induced change in (left) directional preference differs across trial pairs

    Figure 1D, left, and its legend; Results, Degraded Directional Tuning, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Preferred direction is the bin of maximum rate in the 10 degree tuning curve; the change is the absolute circular difference (0 to 180 degrees) between the two curves, a standard choice the paper does not state. Pairs stable1-stable2, stable1-rotating room, stable1-rotating arena, and the first against the second 15 min of stable1. A repeated-measures ANOVA on the three trial pairs with Tukey-Kramer HSD, and a one-way ANOVA of the four groups with Dunnett's test against the halves (the degrees of freedom, 3 and 456, imply a between-groups test). Reproduced when both ANOVAs have p < 0.05, every trial pair exceeds the halves by Dunnett's test at p < 0.05, and the Tukey order matches the legend; partially reproduced when the Dunnett part holds but the order among trial pairs differs.

  • fig1d-instability

    Directional instability (the KLD between tuning curves) differs across trial pairs and is greater for every trial pair than between the two halves of stable1.

    the change in preferred direction of directional tuning and the overall change in directional tuning (''instability'') was greater across the two stable conditions and between the stable and rotating conditions than it was between the two halves of the first stable session (Figure 1D)

    Figure 1D, right, and its legend; Results, Degraded Directional Tuning, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    KLD as in Quantifying Head-direction Signals, Directional Stability, resistor average of the two Kullback-Leibler divergences between the 10 degree tuning curves made into probabilities with the Krichevsky-Trofimov estimate. The Methods write n_i, counts; the readings are (a) spike counts per bin; (b) the rates per bin. The logarithm base does not change the tests. The same trial pairs and tests as fig1d-preference-change. Reproduced when both ANOVAs have p < 0.05 and every trial pair exceeds the halves by Dunnett's test at p < 0.05.

  • fig2-decoding-stable-vs-rotating

    Head direction decoded from stable1 ensemble tuning is relatively accurate in stable2 and relatively inaccurate and abruptly changing in both frames during rotation, although for some ensembles room-frame decoding is similar to stable2; seven ensembles of at least five head-direction cells were analysed.

    Seven ensembles of R5 cells were analyzed ... Relatively accurate decoding of the stable2 recordings (Figure 2A) contrast with relatively inaccurate and abruptly changing decoding in both the room and arena spatial frames during rotation, although for some ensembles, decoding in the room frame was similar to decoding in the stable2 condition

    Results, Degraded Directional Tuning, paragraph 2; Figure 2A and 2B

    How it will be judged

    The decoder of HD_Max_likelihood.py (see the opening comment), trained on stable1 and applied to stable2 and to the rotating trial in room and in arena coordinates; error is the circular difference of decoded and actual direction. The window is 660 ms in the Methods (20 samples in the code) and 133 ms in the Figure 2 legend; 660 ms is the main reading and 133 ms a variant. The paper gives no summary measure (Figure 2 shows error histograms); the readings are (a) the median absolute circular error per ensemble and condition, (b) the mean absolute circular error. The paper names no count of ensembles for "relatively accurate" and "relatively inaccurate"; a majority (at least four of seven) is an assumption. Reproduced when there are seven ensembles of at least five simultaneously recorded head-direction cells (no tolerance, as this is a count of recordings), the stable2 error is below the arena-frame error in at least four ensembles, and below the room-frame error in at least four (the paper allows some ensembles with room-frame decoding similar to stable2, and one ensemble, b, with poor stable2 decoding).

  • figs2-frame-preference

    During rotation, frame-specific directional discharge alternated between signaling room and arena direction; four of seven ensembles showed an overall room preference and two of seven an arena preference.

    4/7 ensembles showed an overall preference to signal room directions over arena directions and 2/7 showed an overall preference to signal arena directions over room directions

    Results, Degraded Directional Tuning, paragraph 3; Figure S2A and S2B; Figure 2C legend

    How it will be judged

    Per cell and 133 ms interval of the rotating trial, Idir = |p(n|d) log2(p(n|d)/p(n))| for the observed spike count n and head direction d, separately with room and arena directions; ΔIdir is the room sum over cells minus the arena sum (Estimating momentary directional information). Open details and their readings. Direction bins, (a) 10 degrees as the tuning curves, (b) 45 degrees as the position by direction maps. The probabilities from (a) the rotating trial itself, (b) stable1. The SD of the 2 SD rule, (a) the SD of the ensemble's ΔIdir series, (b) the SD of stable1 Idir, given in Figure 2C for calibration. An ensemble prefers the room when p(ΔIdir > 2 SD) exceeds p(ΔIdir < -2 SD) and the arena in the reverse case (Figure 2C legend, Figure S2B); the margin for "no preference" is not stated, and is read as (a) the two probabilities within a factor of 2, (b) both below 0.05. Counts are of seven ensembles, so no tolerance. Reproduced when the counts are 4 and 2 under a reading; partially reproduced when room-preferring ensembles outnumber arena-preferring ones.

  • fig2c-proximity-anticorrelation

    The rat's relative proximity to one shock zone versus the other is anti-correlated with frame-specific directional information, so that near the room shock zone directional discharge favours the arena frame and the reverse; this holds for Idir in each frame (mean r = -0.141 ± 0.029, all p < 0.0001) and for ΔIdir (r = -0.18, p < 0.05, in the exemplar).

    This anticorrelation was observed for Idir defined for each spatial frame ... 0.141 ± 0.029; all p < 0.0001), as well as for their combination DIdir

    Results, Degraded Directional Tuning, paragraphs 3 and 4; Figure 2C and its legend; Figure S2C

    How it will be judged

    Idir and ΔIdir as in figs2-frame-preference, at 133 ms steps of the rotating trial, correlated (Pearson) with the relative proximity to the two shock zones at the same steps. The paper does not define relative proximity (it cites Kelemen and Fenton 2010); the readings are (a) (d_arena - d_room) / (d_arena + d_room) with d the distance to each zone, (b) d_arena - d_room, each signed so that a positive value means nearer the room zone, paired with the room-frame Idir and its mirror for the arena-frame Idir. The two averages the Results give (-0.141 ± 0.029 and -0.132 ± 0.024) are read as (a) one per frame-specific Idir correlation over 14 values, (b) the second over the ΔIdir correlations; either value may match. The tolerance is the paper's stated spread. Reproduced when every correlation is negative with p < 0.0001 and the mean is within tolerance of either value; partially reproduced when the mean is negative but not every correlation is significant.
