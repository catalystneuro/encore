Reproducing Luo, Jiang, Desai et al. (2024) from dandiset 001261¶

Paper. Luo F, Jiang L, Desai NS, Bai L, Watkins GV, Eldridge MAG, et al. (2024). Comparative physiology and morphology of BLA-projecting NBM/SI cholinergic neurons in mouse and macaque. J. Comp. Neurol. 532, e70001. doi:10.1002/cne.70001. The claims file was written from the version of record (open access, PMC11583843), with its supplement.

Dandiset. DANDI:001261, version 0.250917.2023. The paper cites no version (its Data Availability Statement gives only https://dandiarchive.org/). This is the only published version, so it is pinned. The draft holds the same 152 files (same checksums); it differs only in metadata (contributors, a related resource, and the like), none of which the analysis uses. Files are read over HTTP into memory, not to disk.

What the dandiset holds. One NWB file per neuron: whole-cell current-clamp responses to a family of current steps, at 10 kHz, from mice and macaques, with the group of the neuron (species, cholinergic or not) in session_description. It holds no morphology, no quality-control measures and no slice identifiers.

Code. The features follow the claims file, which takes them from Methods 2.5 and the paper's MATLAB code (github.com/nsdesai/patch_clamp_analysis, commit 17a3265, doi:10.5281/zenodo.10975327), re-implemented in Python in nbmsi.py; the UMAP is umap-learn in place of Meehan's MATLAB implementation, and the LDA is scikit-learn's in place of MATLAB's fitcdiscr.

In the plotted 200 pA sweep the analysed window holds 3 spikes, while the 500 ms from the first non-zero sample hold none. In the first round of this attempt the onset was the first non-zero sample, which put the window on the test pulse in these 8 files: they then seemed not to depolarise with positive steps and to fire no spike.

The stimulus values are in pA, not amperes as the files say. They run from -100 to 1200 pA: the largest step is 200 pA in 127 files, 150 pA in 2, 300 pA in 22 and 1200 pA in 1. The main reading of each feature is the Methods' (claims file, F1 to F18): input resistance and tau on the -20 pA step, latency to the threshold crossing, downstroke reading (a) (first peak to 20 ms after it), AHP on the rheobase step within 100 ms of threshold, adaptation and CV on the 200 pA step (reading a); the AHP width with its crossings searched within the 100 ms window of F12, as the claims file's F14 says ("Window as in F12"). The other readings carry a suffix: downstroke_code (F11 b), adaptation_most and cv_isi_most (F17 and F18 b, the step with the most spikes); ahp_width_to_end (F14 with the crossings searched to the end of the step); and the code's variants rin_code, tau_code, latency_peak, ahp_*_code, rate_200 (the 200 pA reading of F16) and max_rate_code (the code's maximum over every step, including those above 200 pA). How the analysis differs from the paper's. The features are re-implemented in Python from the claims file's definitions (Methods 2.5 and the paper's MATLAB code); the UMAP is umap-learn and the LDA scikit-learn's. The paper's quality control cannot be repeated, as its measures are not in the files.

The group labels in session_description give the paper's counts, but one mouse recording is deposited twice, once as cholinergic and once as noncholinergic, and one macaque recording twice.
