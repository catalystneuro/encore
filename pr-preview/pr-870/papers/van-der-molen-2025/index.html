Summary: The paper's central findings on human organoids held in this data: bursts are organised around backbone units that fire in every burst and correlate more across bursts and with each other than non-rigid units, as in mouse cortical slices. Hidden Markov models fit the bursts better than shuffled data, but states did not converge at the burst peak in most organoids, and state features did not classify units better than firing rate. Several cross-model comparisons in Fig. 7 failed for murine organoids, where the paper does not give the burst detection used and four of nine show no bursts under the stated settings. Minor rank-order claims could not be tested, as the paper gives two conflicting normalisations.
