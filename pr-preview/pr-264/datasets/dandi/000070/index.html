Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds the paper's two array datasets but not the sorted isolations the paper analysed (146 for J-array, 218 for N-array). Its Units table has 192 units per file, one per electrode. The seven single-electrode datasets, the EMG, the leech and the walking-monkey data are not in it either, so six of the paper's main findings cannot be tested.
  • File format errorIn nine of the ten files (all written by the first conversion) the first entry of units/spike_times_index is the true end of unit 0 modulo 65536. Unit 0 is cut short (in J-array 61030 of its 257638 spikes) and its remaining spikes open unit 1, whose times then jump from the end of the session back to its start.
  • File format errorIn five Nitschke files (ses-20090812, 20090819, 20090910, 20090920 and 20100923) the clock restarts at recording segments after the first, as the conversion offsets only one segment. Trials overlap in time (2094 overlapping pairs in N-array), and every unit's spike times go backwards at each restart, so spikes cannot be placed in trials by time; they can be placed by their storage order, which the notebook shows decodes reach direction in every segment.
  • Metadata errorHand position is stated in metres (with conversion NaN); its values span about -220 to +165 on the scale of the target positions (up to 145) and the median speed peaks at 600 to 730 per second during a reach, so they are in mm.
  • Description missingThe hand position ends a median 35 mm (Jenkins) and 52 to 53 mm (Nitschke) below the reached target in y, while the cursor lands on the target; the offset between hand and screen coordinates is not described in the files.
  • Description missingThe Units table carries only the default description ("Autogenerated by NWBFile") and does not say whether its units are sorted neurons, multi-unit activity or threshold crossings. Their number (one per electrode) and rates (8.8 to 300 spikes/s over trial time in the three sessions analysed) are consistent with channel-level activity.
  • Description missingThe dandiset does not say which of its ten sessions are the paper's datasets. Two match the J-array and N-array dates; the other eight (27-condition and two-condition maze sessions, and one 108-condition session) are not described.
  • OtherThe first conversion's code sets the year of every session date to 2009. The file sub-Nitschke ses-20090910 has 108 conditions with the same reached target as N-array (2010) for all of them, and is consistent with the N-9-10-2010 dataset of Supplementary Figure 5; if so, its date is a year early. The other 2009 Nitschke dates may be affected in the same way.

Reproduction Rotational dynamics of motor cortex during reaching (Churchland et al. 2012)

Tests the findings of Churchland, Cunningham, Kaufman, Foster, Nuyujukian, Ryu and Shenoy (2012) that can be tested on dandiset 000070, which holds the paper's two Utah array datasets (monkeys J and N, 108 maze conditions each) and eight further array sessions. It runs jPCA on condition-averaged rates around movement onset and tests the consistency of rotations across conditions, the variance the jPCA planes capture, the fit of rotational dynamics, the shuffle controls, the separate PMd and M1 arrays, and simulated kinematic populations. The single-electrode datasets, EMG, leech and walking-monkey data are not in the dandiset. All files
