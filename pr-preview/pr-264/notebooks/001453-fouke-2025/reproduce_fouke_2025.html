Paper. Fouke KE, He Z, Loring MD, Naumann EA (2025). Neural circuits underlying divergent visuomotor strategies of zebrafish and Danionella cerebrum. Current Biology. doi:10.1016/j.cub.2025.04.027. This attempt used the full text of the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC12218792); the version of record is not open access.

Dandiset. DANDI:001453, version 0.250518.1950. The paper cites no DANDI version (its data statement points to a GitLab repository that holds code only). This is the only published version. The draft holds the same 37 files (same blobs and sizes); it differs only in metadata added later (authors, keywords, and a relatedResource link to the paper), none of which the analysis uses.

What the dandiset holds. Freely swimming tracking of 25 zebrafish (ZF) and 12 D. cerebrum (DC) larvae during the optomotor protocol of Figure 1 and Figure S1. The two-photon imaging and the head-fixed tail tracking of Figures 2 to 4 are not in it.

Outside inputs. The stimulus codes in the files have no names. The names come from the paper's analysis notebook Fig1-S1.ipynb (GitLab ean26/danionella_omr_neuralcircuits2025, commit 33a5c29) and the protocol file of the stimulus software pandastim (GitHub Naumann-Lab/pandastim, commit 7d9d161). The spatial scale (10 pixels per mm) is the factor in Fig1-S1.ipynb. Claims marked "with inputs" use these and depend on the link claim.

No random numbers are drawn anywhere in this notebook.

# What f0_vtheta is: compare its sum over a trial with the trial's change of f0_theta.
rows = []
for s in sessions:
    ratio_sum, ratio_int, vx_frame, vx_sec = [], [], [], []
    for number, c, idx in omr.trials(s):
        if len(idx) < 50:
            continue
        th, vth, t = s["f0_theta"][idx], s["f0_vtheta"][idx], s["t"][idx]
        if abs(th[-1] - th[0]) > 0.5:
            ratio_sum.append(vth.sum() / (th[-1] - th[0]))
            ratio_int.append(np.trapezoid(vth, t) / (th[-1] - th[0]))
        dx = np.diff(s["f0_x"][idx])
        vx = s["f0_vx"][idx][1:]

f0_vtheta is stated in radians/s. Summed over a trial's frames it gives the trial's change of f0_theta (median ratio near 1 in most files), while its time integral gives about 1/frame rate of it. It is the heading change per frame, in radians. The paper's code uses it that way (it sums it to get the cumulative angle), so the analysis is unaffected, but the stated unit is wrong. f0_vx and f0_vy are stated in pixels/s; the ratio to the per-frame displacement, printed above, is the check: per frame it is 0.63 to 1.11, per second about the frame interval, so these are pixels per frame. The paper's code converts them with the frame interval, which treats them as per frame. They are not used below.

18 of 25 ZF files and 1 of 12 DC files are sampled at 32 to 35 Hz, against the Methods' "more than 150 Hz". The raw timestamp intervals show these are the rates of acquisition: in those files the median interval is 28.8 to 30.8 ms and 96% to 99.6% of intervals are within 10% of it, and their trials last a median 34.96 s (printed below), as the 35 s protocol has, so the timestamps are in seconds. The other files have median intervals of 5.9 to 7.8 ms (128 to 171 Hz), quantised in steps of about 1 ms, and only 11 of 37 files have a median interval below 6.67 ms (above 150 Hz). The largest intervals in DC files (minutes to hours) are the gaps between trials. The ratio of f0_vtheta's sum to the change of f0_theta is lower in most DC files (0.09 to 0.89) than in most ZF files, so in DC files the two series do not agree over a trial; the analysis uses f0_vtheta, as the paper's code does. The measures below are reported by frame-rate group where it could matter.

def turning_course(shift):
    out = {}
    for s in sessions:
        tr = list(omr.trials(s))
        for k, (number, c, idx) in enumerate(tr):
            label = tr[k - shift][1] if k - shift >= 0 else None
            if label not in (2, 3):
                continue
            t = s["t"][idx] - s["t"][idx][0]
            deg = np.degrees(s["f0_vtheta"][idx])
            h = np.histogram(t, bins, weights=deg)[0] / 0.5
            out.setdefault((omr.fish_key(s)[:2], label), []).append(h)
    return out

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The stimulus names come from the paper's code and the stimulus software, not from the dandiset. The trial processing follows the paper's notebook Fig1-S1.ipynb, except that DC files have no vel column, so the code's filter on it is not applied. The dandiset has 25 ZF, not 26. Angular acceleration follows the Methods' definition, on heading resampled to 30 Hz; the code's quantity is a variant. The test of "following the direction" and the measure of continuous swimming are choices made here, as the paper gives no test for the first and bases the second on bout files that are not in the dandiset.

What was found about the dandiset.

  • It holds only the freely swimming behaviour of Figure 1 and Figure S1. The imaging and head-fixed tail tracking of Figures 2 to 4 are not in it, and the GitLab repository the paper names for "all behavioral and imaging data" holds notebooks only.
  • The stimulus codes 0 to 20 have no names or description in the files or the metadata. The paper's code and the stimulus software disagree on which of codes 9 and 11 is lateral right.
  • Subject ids 0 to 11 are each used for one ZF and one DC; the archive's summary counts 25 subjects and lists only Danio rerio.
  • f0_vtheta is a heading change per frame, not radians/s as stated; in most DC files its sum does not match the change of f0_theta.
  • 18 of 25 ZF files and 1 DC file are at 32 to 35 Hz, not above 150 Hz as the Methods say; their timestamp intervals and trial lengths show these are the real acquisition rates.
  • No spatial scale is given; positions in pixels are consistent with the code's 10 pixels per mm, except one DC file whose positions reach 1490 pixels.
  • ZF and DC files were exported differently: ZF files keep untracked and between-trial frames, DC files hold only tracked trial frames and an extra dist series.
  • The paper's own Tukey output finds three DC pairs significant (static against backward left, backward right and forward left), as here, while the paper states that DC do not modulate speed.
  • The stored dist series of DC files does not agree with the positions. The code's speed check uses it, but the check from positions passes the same trials; 11 DC trials above 100 mm/s pass because the check covers 0 to 35 s, not the 3 to 35 s the speed is measured on.
  • The paper's code computes "angular acceleration" as heading change per frame divided by the file's mean frame interval, which the gaps between trials inflate in DC files; the Figure 1H result depends on it. With the Methods' definition, DC values are higher than ZF at every speed, including when the fish is still (72.4 against 34.9 deg/s² below 1 mm/s), so part of the DC values may be tracking noise; the outputs cannot say how much.
