Reproducing the IBL brain-wide map (2025) from dandiset 000409¶

Paper. International Brain Laboratory, Angelaki D, Benson B, et al. (2025). A brain-wide map of neural activity during complex behaviour. Nature 645, 177-191. doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09235-0. This attempt used the full text of the version of record, which is open access (CC BY 4.0).

Dandiset. DANDI:000409, version 0.260309.1324. The paper does not cite a DANDI version (its data statement points to the IBL's own data release). 0.260309.1324 is the only published version. The draft holds the same 2,048 files with the same paths and sizes; its metadata differ (the draft links the Nature paper, the published version the bioRxiv preprint), none of which the analysis uses.

What the dandiset holds. For each of 459 sessions a processed file (trials, spike-sorted units of all probes with their Allen CCF localisation, wheel, licks, and for most sessions video pose, pupil and motion energy), a raw electrophysiology file, and up to three videos. This notebook streams the processed files only.

Outside inputs. The paper's table of per-region results, brainwidemap/meta/region_info.csv in https://github.com/int-brain-lab/paper-brain-wide-map at commit 118fc36, and its table of inclusion counts meta/inclusion_info.csv, and its trajectory results table meta/Results/stim.pkl, read only as the paper's reported values. No measurement comes from outside the dandiset.

Scope. The neural analyses of the paper run over 454 sessions and 201 regions with 10 repeats of nested cross-validation and 200 null sessions each; that does not fit the 30-minute limit. The decoding, single-cell and trajectory claims are tested on a set of regions fixed in the claims file, each with all its sessions. The stimulus trajectories are computed for every canonical region. Four claims that need every region and heavier models were not attempted (blocked, other); the cost measured here is printed at the end.

rows, trial_tables = [], []
for s in scans:
    i, u = s["info"], s["units"]
    t = bwm.task_table(s["trials"])
    mask, n_kept = bwm.analysis_mask(t)
    t["analysed"] = mask
    t["eid"] = i["session_id"]
    t["subject"] = i["subject_id"]
    t["trial"] = np.arange(len(t))
    t["from_end"] = len(t) - t.trial
    trial_tables.append(t)
    rows.append({"eid": i["session_id"], "subject": i["subject_id"], "lab": i["lab"], "sex": i["sex"],

In 64 sessions, every one recorded with the protocol named _iblrig_tasks_ephysChoiceWorld without a version, probability_left alternates between 0.2 and 0.8 on every trial after the first 90, and the stimulus falls on the stated block's likely side on about half of the trials, as by chance; in the other 395 sessions it does so on 80% of trials, with blocks of 20 to 100 trials as the Methods state. The block column of those 64 sessions does not describe the blocks the mice experienced. Analyses that need the block (Figure 1g and 1h, the single-cell tests and the trajectory permutations) leave these sessions out; the decoding nulls are drawn from the generative process and do not use the recorded blocks.

The stimulus, choice and reward columns agree: a left stimulus is rewarded after a clockwise turn and a right stimulus after a counter-clockwise turn, as the table's description says (clockwise moves the stimulus right, towards the centre). The choice called right here is the counter-clockwise turn. The contrasts and block probabilities are those of the Methods, and the first block is the 90 unbiased trials. Two descriptions disagree with the data. The first movement time is described as "after the go cue", but in a part of the trials it precedes both the go cue and the stimulus onset (printed above); the paper's 80 ms threshold removes these. The reward is 3 uL in the Methods, while the files record the volumes printed above. Neither is used by an analysis that depends on it, other than through the paper's own trial mask. Two sessions are truncated to pass the 90% criterion on easy trials, to 457 and 460 trials, both above the 400-trial floor; no session fails the criterion at 400 trials, so none is excluded for it.

How the analysis differs from the paper's.

  • The decoding, single-cell and latency claims are tested on 14 regions fixed in advance (10 for decoding), each with all its canonical sessions, not on all 201 regions; the significance correction runs over the tested regions. The count of regions with stimulus trajectories uses all 201.
  • Decoding uses one run of the nested cross-validation (paper 10) and 100 null sessions (paper 200), and each null reuses the C chosen for the real labels; on three example sessions this null is narrower than the full nested null in 6 of 9 comparisons of the 95th percentiles. For VISp stimulus, over all its sessions, the conclusion is the same under both nulls.
  • The synthetic sessions for choice and feedback come from an action-kernel model fitted here, with a logistic choice policy, in place of the companion paper's model package.
  • The paper's trial mask uses saturation intervals and data revisions that are not in the dandiset; the mask here has the Methods' criteria and the code's truncation.
  • 64 sessions whose block column does not follow the stimuli are left out of the block analyses.
  • Trajectory permutations follow the Methods' classes (each block apart); the code merges the unbiased block with the 0.2 blocks, shown as a variant.

What was found about the dandiset.

  • It holds the sessions and units of the released dataset: 459 sessions, 139 mice, 12 labs, 699 insertions, 621,733 units, 75,708 well-isolated units, and every count of the paper's inclusion table. The receptive-field mapping and the saturation intervals of the trial mask are not in it.
  • In 64 sessions (protocol name _iblrig_tasks_ephysChoiceWorld without a version) probability_left, and so block_index and block_type, alternate on every trial after the first 90 and do not describe the blocks: the stimulus is on the stated likely side in 47.5% to 57% of trials, against 76% to 83% elsewhere.
  • No session holds the receptive-field mapping phase or its stimulus.
  • The quality score matches a noise cutoff below 5 for every unit; the Methods give 20 uV.
  • The first movement time is described as after the go cue but precedes it in 12% of trials; the reward volume is 1.5 uL on every rewarded trial, where the Methods give 3 uL.
  • Subject weight is one value per mouse, and the ages at recording (13.1 to 65.9 weeks) do not reach the paper's 178 weeks.
  • The spikes share the trials' clock in 448 of 454 canonical sessions; in the other 6 it could not be shown.
