Reproducing Churchland et al. (2010) from dandiset 000138¶

Paper. Churchland MM, Cunningham JP, Kaufman MT, Ryu SI, Shenoy KV (2010). Cortical preparatory activity: representation of movement or first cog in a dynamical machine? Neuron 68:387-400. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2010.09.015. The claims file (claims/000138/10.1016-j.neuron.2010.09.015.yaml) was written from the NIH author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC2991102) with its supplement; the main-text figure panels were not available to its reader.

Dandiset. DANDI:000138, version 0.220113.0407 (MC_Maze_Large, Neural Latents Benchmark). The paper (2010) predates the dandiset and cites no version. This is the only published version, and the draft holds the same two files (same blobs, checked below), so the most recent published version is pinned.

What it holds. One session of monkey J (2009-10-06) on the maze task: 500 successful trials over 27 conditions, 162 sorted units from two Utah arrays, hand, cursor and eye position, and hand velocity. The paper's J-array dataset had 108 conditions, 146 units and a mean 2166 trials per neuron; the dandiset is a 500-trial subset of a session, and nothing in it or the paper says whether this session is the one the paper used. The single-electrode datasets (monkeys A, B and J) and all EMG are not in it.

Readings. The claims file names several open method details and the paper leaves a few more open. Each is run where it could change an outcome; a main reading, the one best supported by the paper, decides an outcome when the readings disagree, and the outcome is then marked as resting on an assumption. Seeds are fixed per claim.

Clock check. If spikes share the trial table's clock, the reach target can be decoded from spike counts after target onset and not before it; if hand data share it, hand speed rises at the table's movement onset. Target (one of 9 mazes, trial_type) is decoded with a leave-one-trial-out nearest-centroid classifier on square-root spike counts (a standard choice), in 200 ms windows. The same is done with the windows shifted 1 s earlier, which moves every window before target onset.

def decode(t0_s, window):
    i0 = np.round((t0_s + window[0] / 1000) * 1000).astype(int)
    counts = np.zeros((len(trials), len(spikes)))
    for u, s in enumerate(spikes):
        ms = np.floor(s * 1000).astype(int)
        cs = np.r_[0, np.cumsum(np.bincount(ms, minlength=int(stops.max() * 1000) + 3000))]
        counts[:, u] = cs[i0 + window[1] - window[0]] - cs[i0]
    X = np.sqrt(counts)
    y = trials.trial_type.values
    labels = np.unique(y)
    sums = np.stack([X[y == l].sum(0) for l in labels])
    ns = np.array([(y == l).sum() for l in labels])
    correct = 0
    for i in range(len(y)):
        li = np.searchsorted(labels, y[i])
        cent = sums.copy()
        cent[li] -= X[i]
        nn = ns.copy()
        nn[li] -= 1
        cent = cent / nn[:, None]
        correct += labels[np.argmin(((cent - X[i]) ** 2).sum(1))] == y[i]
    return correct / len(y)

Readings¶

The claims file and the paper leave several method details open. Each is run. The first value listed is the main reading, chosen as best supported by the paper (the reasons are in the attempt record):

  • proto: proto-trial as the condition's mean (a) or elementwise median (b) velocity profile.
  • combine: the r > 0.9 test on the concatenated horizontal and vertical profile, or on each.
  • onset: movement onset from the trial table (a) or a speed threshold (b).
  • tuning: preparatory tuning as the range (a) or SD (b) of condition means.
  • t_units: all units (a) or only included units (b) in the peri-movement T-matrix.
  • fold: PCA space computed once (a) or recomputed without the left-out condition (b).
  • norm: performance per neuron, then averaged (a), or pooled (b).
  • kin_end: kinematic window ends at +800 ms (a) or at the end of the reach (b).
  • task_end: reach end by speed threshold (a) or at +800 ms (b), for task spaces.
  • split: halfpoint and segments by path length (a) or time (b).
  • kmax: dimensionalities 1 to 14 (a) or 1 to 12 (b).

def n_for_90(M):
    Mc = M - M.mean(0)
    s = np.linalg.svd(Mc, compute_uv=False) ** 2
    return int(np.searchsorted(np.cumsum(s) / s.sum(), 0.9) + 1), np.cumsum(s) / s.sum()

fig7c-anterior-posterior¶

The peri-movement space captures preparatory tuning at both anterior and posterior sites, with normalized performance only slightly different from that of random halves of the data, slightly better at anterior sites.

Anterior and posterior are the two arrays, from the unit id prefix (prefix 1 assumed PMd, anterior), as in results-ap-location-no-effect: with array membership as the AP location, the units at or above the median are one array and those at or below it the other. For each subset, the peri-movement space is built from that subset's units only (the unit being fit excluded) and performance computed at k = 6, normalized by the upper limit, the PCA space of the subset's preparatory rates, at k = 6 or at its best k. The Supplemental Experimental Procedures derive the upper-limit space "from the preparatory activity of all neurons (T was c×n with each row containing the mean preparatory activity for every neuron)" and state no exclusion, where the Methods state one for the peri-movement space ("all neurons except the neuron whose preparatory activity we were trying to capture"). The space "provides a rough upper limit on performance", and with the unit left out the random halves exceed it (below). The main reading therefore keeps the unit being fit in its upper-limit space. The reading with the unit left out is also run, and every row counts. Random halves: the units split at random into two halves, 20 draws (a standard choice), 40 halves; their spread is printed so that the 10% bound can be compared with sampling noise.

def subset_scores(ctx, members, inc, fold, t_units, self_in_upper):
    """Mean performance at k = 6 in the peri-movement space of `members`, and of the upper-limit
    space (k = 1..14), over included units of the subset."""
    fit = members & inc
    in_t = members if t_units == "all" else fit
    X = ctx["prep"][fit]
    Gp = ps.neuron_grams(ps.t_matrix(ctx["move"]))
    G = Gp[in_t].sum(0)
    err = ps.loo_pca_space(G, Gp[fit] * in_t[fit][:, None, None], X, 6, fold)
    peri6 = np.mean(ps.performance(err[:, 5], X))
    P = ctx["prep"][:, :, None]
    Pp = ps.neuron_grams(P)
    Gu = Pp[members].sum(0)
    parts = None if self_in_upper else Pp[fit]
    if self_in_upper:
        erru = ps.loo_pca_space(Gu, None, X, 14, fold)
    else:
        erru = ps.loo_pca_space(Gu, parts, X, 14, fold)
    upper = np.array([np.mean(ps.performance(erru[:, k], X)) for k in range(14)])
    return peri6, upper, int(fit.sum())

fig7d-pd-consistency¶

In all four datasets and for every comparison, the PD is more stable across random subsets of conditions when measured in the peri-movement space than in task, kinematic or EMG spaces.

Per unit, 25 conditions drawn with replacement, the unit-length PD fitted, another 25 drawn and the PD refitted; consistency is the dot product, averaged over 100 draws (standard choice) and units. Compared: endpoints + maximum speed (3-D) and segment directions (6-D) against the peri-movement space at 3 and 6 dimensions, and the kinematic space at its best k against the peri-movement space at that k. def consistency(S, X, rng, n_draws=100):
    c = S.shape[0]
    D = np.column_stack([np.ones(c), S])
    vals = np.zeros(X.shape[0])
    for _ in range(n_draws):
        i1, i2 = rng.integers(0, c, 25), rng.integers(0, c, 25)
        b1 = np.linalg.lstsq(D[i1], X[:, i1].T, rcond=None)[0][1:]
        b2 = np.linalg.lstsq(D[i2], X[:, i2].T, rcond=None)[0][1:]
        b1 /= np.linalg.norm(b1, axis=0, keepdims=True)
        b2 /= np.linalg.norm(b2, axis=0, keepdims=True)
        vals += (b1 * b2).sum(0) / n_draws
    return vals

How the analysis differs from the paper's. Only the array dataset of monkey J can be tested, on 27 of its 108 conditions and 500 trials of one session, so every J-array number rests on a quarter of the conditions and fewer trials per condition after selection (11 to 18). The paper leaves open the proto-trial, how the velocity correlation combines the two axes, the movement onset method, what "tuning" in spikes/s means, whether the PCA space is recomputed without the left-out condition, which units form the T-matrix, the end of the kinematic window and of the reach, how paths are split, and, for Figure 7, whether the upper-limit space includes the unit being fit and the scaling of axes. Each is run, and outcomes that differ between readings are marked as assumptions. The main reading takes the mean proto-trial, the concatenated velocity profile, the trial table's movement onset, tuning as the range of condition means, all units in the T-matrix, the space computed once from all conditions (the paper describes leaving out a condition only for the PD fit), performance averaged over units, and, for Figure 7C, an upper-limit space of the preparatory activity of all units of the subset, including the unit being fit. Figure 4E uses the movement-locked average, the part of the paper's spliced trace that is locked to movement onset. The anterior and posterior split uses the array of each unit, inferred from the unit id prefix.

What was found about the dataset.

  • The units' electrode references all point to the PMd group: the referenced electrode is the channel in the unit id minus one, for both id prefixes, so the 79 units of prefix 2 reference PMd electrodes. Which prefix is which array is not stated; electrode coordinates are NaN.
  • The dandiset holds a 500-trial, 27-condition subset of one session; the paper's J-array dataset had 108 conditions and 146 units (the file has 162), and there is no way to tell from the files whether the session is the paper's. The single-electrode datasets and EMG are not in it.
  • Hand data are at 1 kHz within trials only, where the paper tracked the hand at 60 Hz; the resampling is not described. Velocity agrees with the derivative of position.
  • The test file holds 100 unlabelled 700 ms segments of the held-in units, not usable for these claims; none of 8734 non-overlapping runs of five inter-spike intervals sampled from the segments of all 122 units occurs among the same unit's training intervals.
