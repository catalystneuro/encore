Routing of Hippocampal Ripples to Subcortical Structures via the Lateral Septum

David Tingley, György Buzsáki

Summary: Three main findings held: lateral septum (LS) high-frequency oscillations are shorter than CA1 ripples, LS neurons lock to their trough (though only 56% of neurons, against 92%), and pair co-activation during them is unchanged by behaviour. Raised LS firing at the events (one of six animals), slower frequency, the 6.4 ms lag after ripples and stronger coupling in NREM sleep did not reproduce. Travelling waves, optogenetic induction and depth within LS could not be tested, for lack of electrode coordinates and the optogenetic rats. The files hold no detected events, so they were found anew. A signal shared with the hippocampal channel probably affects the results.
