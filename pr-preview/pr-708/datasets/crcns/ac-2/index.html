Most of the paper's main findings held in this data: the split of variability into a shared part and a small private part (best private estimate near 1.5 mV), the total variability across neurons, the drop after LFP-based culling, and the negative link between spontaneous activity and the evoked response. The random-culling controls did not reproduce (3.82 mV against 4.5), most likely because the paper's handling of its 10-trial rule is unclear. PSP-based culling alone and the largest LFP-based reduction held only in part. The miniature EPSP size could not be pinned down, since the paper gives no detection method. Many outcomes depend on a spike-removal rule the paper does not describe, which the agent assumed.

Reproduction Shared and private variability in the auditory cortex (DeWeese and Zador 2004)

Tests the findings of DeWeese and Zador (2004) on CRCNS data set ac-2, which holds the paper's 33 in vivo whole-cell recordings from rat auditory cortex during pure tones, 29 of them with a simultaneous LFP, and six recordings after TTX. It measures the variability index of tone-evoked PSPs, partitions it into private and shared parts with the paper's LFP- and PSP-based culling and estimation procedures, and checks the controls, the large-response and CV analyses, the spontaneous veto, the anesthesia groups and the recording properties.
