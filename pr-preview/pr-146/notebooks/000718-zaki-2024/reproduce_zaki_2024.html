Reproducing Zaki et al. (2024) from dandiset 000718¶

Paper. Zaki Y, Pennington ZT, Morales-Rodriguez D, et al. (2024). Offline ensemble co-reactivation links memories across days. Nature 637, 145-155 (issue of January 2025; published online 6 November 2024). doi:10.1038/s41586-024-08168-4. This attempt used the full text, which is open access under CC BY (PMC11666460).

Dandiset. DANDI:000718, version 0.260825.1902. The paper cites no DANDI version (its data statement says the imaging data are available "upon reasonable request"). This is the only published version, so it is pinned; the draft holds the same 20 assets (same asset ids and sizes).

What the dandiset holds. Two of the nine mice of the calcium imaging and EEG/EMG experiment of Figure 5 and Extended Data Figures 9 and 10: Ca_EEG2-1 (high shock, 1.5 mA) and Ca_EEG3-4 (low shock, 0.25 mA). For each, the Miniscope recordings of encoding and recall sessions with Minian output, freezing from ezTrack, and the first 10 min offline recording after aversive encoding (of the 24 the Methods describe per offline period); for Ca_EEG3-4 also the first after neutral encoding. Ca_EEG2-1 has no neutral or novel recall session. Only Ca_EEG2-1 has cell registration (CellReg tables, in its Week file, with a week of telemetry). The mice of the one-hour offline experiments of Figures 2 to 4 and 6, and of the behaviour-only, inhibitory tagging and no-shock experiments, are not in it.

Outside inputs. One claim (source-data-link) compares the dandiset with the paper's per-mouse source data, from the public repository denisecailab/RetrospectiveMemoryLinkingData_2024 at commit a5081645d3d3e5743ade58ea632c28b003794345 (files ExtendedData9/ExtData9b.csv, ExtData9c.csv and Figure5/Fig_5c.csv). The few reference values used are typed in that section. The analysis code the paper names (github.com/denisecailab/RetrospectiveMemoryLinkingAnalysis_2024) returned 404 and could not be read; the conversion code (catalystneuro/cai-lab-to-nwb at commit a37dadafecaec507669c25baa705333460e0f48d) was read for the meaning of the files.

# The telemetry drops to zero while the mouse is out of its home cage (in a behaviour chamber).
def dropouts(t0, t1, step=10.0):
    x = ml.week_signal(t0, t1)
    n = int(step * week["eeg_rate"])
    z = (x[: len(x) // n * n].reshape(-1, n) == 0).mean(1) > 0.9
    edges = np.diff(np.concatenate([[0], z.astype(int), [0]]))
    return [(t0 + i * step, t0 + j * step) for i, j in zip(np.where(edges == 1)[0], np.where(edges == -1)[0])]

The Week file's epoch table has 27 rows: neutral exposure, FC, Recall1, five offline-day-1 recordings (the first four last 56 to 268 s) and 19 offline-day-2 recordings, against the 24 per offline period of the Methods. It puts the first offline recording after aversive encoding (OfflineDay2Session1, starting 527068 s) 47 min before the FC session (529885 s), and the session start times in the files say the same (09:24 against 10:11). The telemetry shows otherwise: the EEG of the offline file sits exactly at the epoch's start (527067.679 against 527067.677 s), and the telemetry is absent, as when the mouse is out of its home cage, from 526175 to 526955 s, which contains the FC epoch moved 1 h earlier and ends 113 s before that offline recording. The next offline recording starts 1800 s after it, and the others follow every 1800 s (10 min of recording and a 20 min break, as the Methods describe), so no offline-day-2 recording lies between the FC session, read 1 h earlier, and OfflineDay2Session1. The output above gives the same one-hour lead for the neutral exposure and Recall1 epochs. The data are consistent with the behaviour-session times of Ca_EEG2-1 being about 1 h late, and with the offline recording being the first after FC, as its name says; it is treated so here, and the start times are recorded as a finding.
