Reproducing Jeong, Taylor, Floeder et al. (2022) from dandiset 000351¶

Paper. Jeong H, Taylor A, Floeder JR, Lohmann M, Mihalas S, Wu B, Zhou M, Burke DA, Namboodiri VMK (2022). Mesolimbic dopamine release conveys causal associations. Science 378, eabq6740. doi:10.1126/science.abq6740. The claims file (claims/000351/10.1126-science.abq6740.yaml) was written from the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC9910357), its figure legends, and its supplement (Materials and Methods, Supplementary Notes, Table S1). Its criteria cite the Methods and the paper's code, github.com/namboodirilab/ANCCR at commit cff44144e60a4313a30ffbb4bb8a1f3dd398b525.

Dandiset. DANDI:000351, version 0.260828.1905. The paper cites the dandiset without a version (its code reads the draft). This is the only published version, so it is pinned. The draft has the same 428 assets and size; it is not used, since it can change.

What the dandiset holds. Photometry (dF/F of dLight1.3b in the nucleus accumbens core) and the event log (licks, rewards, cues, laser) of the 8 mice of Experiments 1 to 6, the 14 mice of Experiment 7, and the event logs (no photometry) of the 6 mice of Fig S8L. The simulations of the paper (Figs 2, 3C and F, 5B, 6H, S5, S6, S13) use no recorded data.

Outside inputs. The day lists (startday, endday, learnedday) that the paper's Matlab scripts use to select sessions are copied into the helper jeong.py from the ANCCR repository at the commit above, and the notebook reads the scripts at that commit to check the copies; they name sessions by the Day number in the dandiset's file names (claim session-day-link). The spreadsheet that the dandiset's description names ("Subject and session information", a Google spreadsheet) is not used: it has no fixed version to pin.

# dF/F scale: the files say %. The Methods define dF/F as a fraction, (470 - fitted 405) / fitted
# 405. Compare the stored dF/F with that fraction computed from the raw channels of one session
# per photometry system (the paper's baseline correction is not repeated, so only the scale and
# the correlation are checked).
import h5py, remfile
check = [a for a in assets if a["path"].endswith("sub-HJ-FP-M2_ses-Day1.nwb")
         or a["path"].endswith("sub-HJ-FP-WT-stGtACR-F3_ses-Day2-Pavlovian.nwb")]
for a in check:
    with h5py.File(remfile.File(a["url"]), "r") as f:
        raw = f["acquisition/photometry/data"][:]
        rt = f["acquisition/photometry/timestamps"][:]
        d = f["processing/photometry/dff/data"][:]
        dt = f["processing/photometry/dff/timestamps"][:]
    good = np.isfinite(rt) & np.isfinite(raw).all(1)
    best = None
    for sig, ref in [(0, 1), (1, 0)]:
        c = np.polyfit(raw[good, ref], raw[good, sig], 1)
        fit = np.polyval(c, raw[good, ref])
        frac = (raw[good, sig] - fit) / fit
        # dF/F timestamps are on the behaviour clock; the raw series starts at its own 0. Map the
        # raw samples onto dF/F time by their position in the recording.
        td = dt[np.isfinite(dt)]
        pos = (rt[good] - rt[good][0]) / (rt[good][-1] - rt[good][0])
        stored = np.interp(td[0] + pos * (td[-1] - td[0]), td, d[np.isfinite(dt)])
        ok = np.isfinite(stored)
        r = np.corrcoef(frac[ok], stored[ok])[0, 1]
        slope = np.polyfit(frac[ok], stored[ok], 1)[0]
        if best is None or r > best[1]:
            best = (sig, r, slope)
    print(f"{a['path']}: channel {best[0]} as the signal, r = {best[1]:.2f} with stored dF/F, "
          f"stored dF/F = {best[2]:.1f} x the fraction")

The stored dF/F follows the fraction computed from the raw channels and is about 100 times it, so it is in percent, as stated. The paper's Matlab scripts give their bounds for fitting dopamine responses (claim fig4hi-delay-change-dopamine-unchanged) in units of a fraction times milliseconds (aucsignal.m multiplies by the bin in ms); the helper divides dF/F by 100 for the Matlab-derived measures, so that their values are on the scale the Methods define (a fraction). Whether the code's bounds were set for this scale is not known: they bind on it as well as on percent (fig4hi-delay-change-dopamine-unchanged), and the analysis assumes the paper's fit was bounded as here. Ratios, correlations and t-tests do not depend on it. For the pyPhotometry session the raw samples are placed on the dF/F clock by their position in the recording, since the dF/F timestamps start about 15 s before the raw series' zero.

session-day-link¶

The subjects and "Day" numbers that the paper's analysis code names for each experiment are sessions of the dandiset, so that the code's selection of sessions can be applied to it.

Subjects are matched with _ read as -, and sessions by the number after "Day" in the file name (load_dandi_url). For each script's day list, the listed days (startday, endday, learnedday and the single days of omission.m) are checked to be sessions of the task, and every session in each range is checked for the condition: 2 s cue (3 s cue-reward delay) for Experiment 2, 8 s cue (9 s delay) for Experiment 3, background rewards for Experiment 5, no delivered reward for Experiment 4, the trial-less task (Poisson) for Experiment 6. The criterion's "every Day number from startday to endday" is also counted literally, every integer day in each range.

def cumsum_condition(m, c, drop_incomplete=False):
    # cumsum_pavlovian.m for condition c (0 initial learning, 1 cue duration, 2 extinction).
    # With drop_incomplete, trials with a NaN smoothed dF/F sample from -1 to 2 s are left out of
    # the series (a variant; the code keeps them with their nansum AUC).
    st, en = jeong.CUMSUM_START[m][c], jeong.CUMSUM_END[m][c]
    days = [d for d in sorted(pav[m]) if st - jeong.CUMSUM_BEFORE[c] <= d <= en]
    cut = jeong.LEARNED_DAY[m] if c == 0 else st
    out = {"days": days}
    keep = {d: np.ones(len(pav[m][d]["da"]), bool) for d in days}
    if c == 1:
        keep = {d: np.isfinite(pav[m][d]["lick_median_ms"]) for d in days}
        out["dropped_no_lick"] = sum(int((~keep[d]).sum()) for d in days)
    if drop_incomplete:
        keep = {d: keep[d] & ~pav[m][d]["da_incomplete"] for d in days}
    cat = lambda f: np.concatenate([f(pav[m][d])[keep[d]] for d in days])
    nb = sum(int(keep[d].sum()) for d in days if d < cut)
    da = cat(lambda v: v["da"])
    if c == 1:
        out["lick_series"] = {"median lick time": cat(lambda v: v["lick_median_ms"])}
    else:
        out["lick_series"] = {"Methods 0-2 s": cat(lambda v: v["lick_methods"]),
                              "code cue+trace": cat(lambda v: v["lick_code_cs"] - v["lick_code_base"])}
    nt = min(R(nb * jeong.CUMSUM_RATIO[c]), len(da))   # the cap binds only when trials are left out
    out.update(nb=nb, nt=nt, da=da, n_incomplete=int(cat(lambda v: v["da_incomplete"])[:nt].sum()))
    out["da_abrupt"], out["da_change"] = jeong.abruptness(np.nancumsum(da), nt)
    out["lick"] = {k: jeong.abruptness(np.cumsum(v), nt) for k, v in out["lick_series"].items()}
    return out

def bgd_vs_ext(start, end, before, P_key="da", strict=False):

        for c, name in enumerate(["bgd", "ext"]):
            st, en = start[m][c], end[m][c]
            days = [d for d in sorted(pav[m]) if st - before <= d <= en]
            n = sum(len(pav[m][d]["da"]) for d in days[:before])

            data = np.concatenate([pav[m][d]["da"] for d in days])[sel]
            inc = np.concatenate([pav[m][d]["da_incomplete"] for d in days])[sel]
            if strict:
                data = np.where(inc, np.nan, data)
            nc = np.nancumsum(data) / np.nansum(data[:50])

        rows.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The measures follow the paper's code at the commit the claims file cites, ported to Python; where the Methods and the code differ, both are shown. The Matlab curve fits start at random points; here the best of 20 seeded starts is kept. dF/F is divided by 100 for the Matlab-derived measures, as the files store percent and the Methods define dF/F as a fraction. The code's bounds on the dopamine model bind on either scale, so the dopamine model comparison (fig4hi-delay-change-dopamine-unchanged) cannot detect a change here; without the bounds it finds one in every animal. That outcome rests on the assumption that the paper's fit was bounded as here. As in the code, trials with NaN dF/F samples in their windows keep the nansum of the others (0 outside the coverage); they are 37 of 8721 CS+ trials, and leaving them out changes no outcome. In Experiment 7, where a trial with no dF/F sample in its window has no value and drops out of the session mean, 17 of the 50 CS1 trials of HJ-FP-WT-stGtACR-M2's last session are such trials; replacing that session changes no outcome. Seven integer days inside the code's day ranges have no session in the dandiset, and the analysis runs on the sessions present.

What was found about the dandiset.

  • Event times are in seconds, though the files state milliseconds.
  • Seven integer days within the code's day ranges have no session in the dandiset (M2 Day23, M3 Day16 and Day31, M4 Day30 and Day38, F2 Day16, M6 Day49). M4 Day38 is a listed start day, its first background reward session, so that session is missing; the paper's session counts of Experiments 2, 4 and 5 equal the counts with all seven days, which suggests the others are missing sessions too.
  • In the large Experiment 7 files the raw photometry timestamps are in thousands of seconds, though stated in seconds.
  • The DAT-Cre mouse HJ-FP-datHT-stGtACR-M7 has genotype wt in its file.
  • Some sessions lose the start or the end of their photometry relative to the event log.
  • No recording is deposited twice; dF/F and the event log are consistent with one clock, except in 27 sessions with no clear response to the reference event, where the check cannot show it.
