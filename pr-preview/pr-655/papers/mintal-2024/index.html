The Volebny Kompas Datasets on Slovak Voter and Party Positions

Most of the paper's main findings hold in this data: the 134,699 responses, 11 parties, Table 2 demographics, 11 factors in the parallel analysis and the party clustering of Figure 7 all reproduced. The party placements of Figures 2 and 3 match except for PS on the economic axis, where the deposited positions give 47.7 against 54.5 in the figure. The coder agreement of 0.84 reproduces only under one assumed weighting that the paper does not state. Minor checks hold in part, such as 432 responses dated after election day. The election-result and website-view claims could not be tested because the outside data has no citable public source.

2026-10-27 · on FIGSHARE:24949470

  • ✓Reproduced The User Responses Dataset holds 134,699 voter responses, each with answers to the 39 statements, along with demographics, the three most salient statements and the parties the respondent would and would never vote for, for most responses.
  • ◐Partially reproduced The responses were gathered during the 26 days leading up to and including election day, from the launch on 5 September 2023 to the election on 30 September 2023.
  • ✓Reproduced The Party Positions Dataset lists the 39 statements in Slovak, Hungarian and English and holds 429 party positions, both party self-placements and expert-coded positions, with sources, snippets and links.
  • ✓Reproduced Eleven parties are positioned on the 39 statements.
  • ✓Reproduced Four of the eleven parties returned the self-placement questionnaire.
  • ✓Reproduced Agreement between the two expert coders of party positions was high, with a weighted Cohen's kappa of 0.84.
  • ◐Partially reproduced The platform placed each party on five uni-dimensions, using 18 of the 39 statements, and on a two-dimensional socio-economic and socio-cultural landscape from 0 to 100, using 16 statements; the placements (Figs. 2 and 3) conform to the anticipated placements of Slovak parties.
  • ✓Reproduced Hierarchical clustering of the parties' recoded positions, by Manhattan distance and average linkage, groups the parties largely as anticipated (Fig. 7).
  • ✓Reproduced Of the responses counted in Table 2, 41.61% are female (44,919), 57.08% male (61,626) and 1.32% other or prefer not to say (1,420).
  • ✓Reproduced Respondents are skewed toward younger ages; 32.32% are 25 to 34 (34,894) and 25.79% are 35 to 44 (27,847), with 2,716 aged 65 to 74 and 683 aged 75 or older (Table 2).
  • ✓Reproduced Respondents are skewed toward the well educated; 64.41% have a university degree or academic higher education (69,542), with 990 reporting no education and 1,665 only primary education (Table 2).
  • ◐Partially reproduced The multivariate check found 40 respondents under 18 and 400 aged 18 to 20 reporting university-level education, which is not congruent with their age.
  • ✓Reproduced Opinions on S2 (higher earners should face a higher income tax rate) and S3 (the retirement age should rise with life expectancy) span a wide range, with means of 43 and 42, standard deviations of 33 and medians of 25.
  • ✓Reproduced S4 (more personal responsibility in health care) and S5 (heavier taxes on banks and big corporations) show stronger consensus, with medians of 75 (means 70 and 69, standard deviations 26 and 29), though more than 17,435 respondents for S4 and 23,344 for S5 disagree with scores of 0 or 25.
  • ✓Reproduced Parallel analyses on both the polychoric and the Pearson correlation matrices of S1 to S39 indicated that 11 factors should be extracted.
  • ◐Partially reproduced With 11 factors and promax rotation, maximum likelihood and principal axis factoring gave consistent results, and S1 (real estate tax raised to lower labour taxes) and S3 (retirement age) loaded strongly.
  • ⊘Blocked The 11 selected parties were the top 11 vote recipients in the 2023 election; each received at least 2% of the vote and no excluded party reached 1%.
  • ⊘Blocked The platform registered 270,836 website views in the 26 days leading up to the election.
