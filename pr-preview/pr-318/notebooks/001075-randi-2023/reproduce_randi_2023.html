Reproducing Randi et al. (2023) from dandiset 001075¶

Paper. Randi F, Sharma AK, Dvali S, Leifer AM (2023). Neural signal propagation atlas of Caenorhabditis elegans. Nature 623, 406-414. doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06683-4. This attempt used the full text (open access, PubMed Central PMC10632145), its Methods, figure legends and the Source Data files of the article.

Dandiset. DANDI:001075, version 0.240930.1859. The paper cites no DANDI version (its data statement points to OSF). There are two published versions. They list the same 223 files; every file was uploaded again, the imaging files with the same sizes and the segmentation files with new content. The later version adds, to every segmentation file, the column target_pumpprobe_id (the manually located target of a stimulation) and NaN-interpolated copies of the traces. The stimulated neuron cannot be named without the target, so the later version, which is also the most recent, is pinned. The difference is shown in the data check.

What the dandiset holds. The 113 wild-type-background (WT) recordings of the paper: per animal, the raw volumes (about 40 GB) and, for 110 animals, a small segmentation file with the green (GCaMP6s) and red (tagRFP-T) intensity of every tracked neuron, NeuroPAL labels, and the table of optogenetic stimulations with their target positions. It does not hold the 18 unc-31 recordings, the spontaneous recordings of Fig. 6, or the unstimulated control recordings that the paper's q values compare against.

Outside inputs. The list of the 188 head neurons, from the paper's code (GitHub leiferlab/pumpprobe, commit 1dbc5e0). The paper's Source Data (Extended Data Figs. 3a, 4a, 4b), for the per-pair link claim and the q < 0.05 selection of one claim. The paper's exported recordings on OSF (doi 10.17605/OSF.IO/E2SYT, raw_extracted_data, file version 1), for the recording link claim, and its unc-31 export (exported_data_unc31.tar.gz) for fig4-rid-unc31.

Method. Traces are pre-processed as the Methods state; where the Methods leave a choice open the paper's code settles it (see the docstring of sigprop.py). The neuron stimulated in each event is the manually located ROI where the file gives one, otherwise the ROI nearest the target position. The photobleaching fit is checked against a full-resolution fit on recordings drawn with a fixed seed; nothing else is random.

How the analysis differs from the paper's.

  • The stimulated neuron is the manually located ROI where the file gives one (half of the stimulations), otherwise the ROI centroid nearest the target in the reference volume. The paper finds the nearest neuron in the volume at the stimulation and maps it through tracking matches that are not in the dandiset. For automatically placed targets the two agree in 66% of cases.
  • The paper's pipeline excludes some stimulations (marked -2 in its export, 950 of them) and substitutes "matchless" traces for some dim neurons; neither is in the dandiset, so all stimulations and only the regular traces are used.
  • <dF/F0>_t follows the Methods (F0 the mean of the 30 s before); the code's correction of decaying traces is shown as a variant.
  • The photobleaching fit is the code's, run on about 500 evenly spaced volumes per trace for speed; on 12 recordings the full fit gives <dF/F0>_t values with a median correlation of 0.997 and the same classifier call for 99.5% of stimulation-ROI pairs.
  • F0 is used as it is; the 3,173 rows with F0 <= 0 and the traces without photobleaching correction are kept in the main results and left out in variants, which change no outcome.
  • For the RID claims, as in the Methods for Fig. 4, no autoresponse is required and values beyond +-2 are excluded. Only RID stimulations whose target is an ROI labelled RID can be found, and the matchless traces the paper uses for dim neurons are missing, so ADL is not observed in WT. The unc-31 values come from the paper's OSF export, processed with the same functions.
  • No threshold on label confidence is applied, as in the code.
  • The kernel fits of Extended Data Fig. 6 were not run: the paper's package builds only with numpy's headers added by hand, and three packages it imports do not build (shown above).
  • Runs on different machines have printed counts that differ by one (3,172 or 3,173 rows with F0 <= 0, 58 or 59 failed fits) and correlations that differ by 0.001 (0.616 or 0.617), presumably from floating-point differences in the photobleaching fits; no conclusion depends on them.

What was found about the dandiset.

  • Three of the 113 subjects (20, 23, 33) have only the raw imaging file, without traces or a stimulation table.
  • The unc-31 recordings, the spontaneous recordings of Fig. 6 and the unstimulated control recordings are not in it. The control recordings, which every q value needs, are not public anywhere the paper names.
  • The files do not say which neuron was stimulated, except for the manually located targets; for the rest only a target position is given. RID stimulations can be recognised only where RID was segmented and labelled (8 stimulations in 5 animals).
  • Labels differ from the paper's export in six recordings: the dandiset has almost no labels in subjects 64, 76 and 77, places shared names on other ROIs in subjects 86 and 91, and has 161 ROIs against 200 in subject 11, for which the export has no labels.
  • The stimulation durations and powers are constants from the paper, the target depth_in_um is not in micrometres, the red channel's indicator is given as GCaMP6s, two subjects have strain "none", the age is P1D for every animal, neuropal_ids holds text such as "merge", some ROIs are labelled "target", one confidence is 11, and nine recordings hold large negative intensities.
  • With the files and the paper's own code, the per-pair counts and means of the paper's Source Data are recovered only in part (Spearman 0.77 for counts, Pearson 0.55 for means).
