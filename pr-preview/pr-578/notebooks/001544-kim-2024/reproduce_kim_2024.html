Paper. Kim R, Ananth MR, Desai NS, Role LW, Talmage DA (2024). Distinct subpopulations of ventral pallidal cholinergic projection neurons encode valence of olfactory stimuli. Cell Reports 43(4), 114009. doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2024.114009. The claims file was written from the NIH author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC11080946), main text, STAR Methods and figure legends; this attempt also read the supplement (Figures S1 to S14 and their legends), which is now in the paper's text. After a question from this attempt, a reader read the Figure S12 legend and amended figs12-other-features-shared and added figs12-app-differs-from-all; both are tested here as amended.

Dandiset. DANDI:001544, version 0.260827.1410. The paper cites no DANDI version (the dandiset was made after publication). This is the only published version, and the most recent; the draft has the same 44 assets and size, so nothing in the draft is missing from it.

What the dandiset holds. Whole-cell current-clamp recordings of 44 VP cholinergic neurons, one per file, each a family of 500 ms current steps: 12 neurons labelled as activated by the appetitive (APP) odor, 8 by the aversive (AV) odor, and 24 from home-cage ChAT-tau-eGFP mice (ALL). These are the data of Figure 6F to 6H and Figure S12. The behaviour, histology, fiber photometry, chirp currents and neuronal morphology of the paper are not in it.

Code. Features follow the STAR Methods, with the paper's code (nsdesai/patch_clamp_analysis at tag v1.0.0, commit 4ee1125) where the Methods are silent, as the claims file says; each parameter's source is in the docstring of vpchat.py.

  • Units. The stimulus series are stated in amperes with conversion 1, but hold values from -60 to 500: they are picoamperes, the step amplitudes of the Methods. The voltage, stated in volts, ranges over values that are millivolts after conversion (resting near -65 mV, spike peaks near +25 mV), so its unit is right.
  • Sampling. Every series is at 10 kHz. In 40 files a sweep lasts 2 s and the step starts at 200 ms; in the three files whose identifier ends in _FL a sweep lasts 5 s and the step starts at 1000 ms. Every step lasts 500 ms, as the Methods say, and every sweep carries a -20 pA, 100 ms test pulse after the step.
  • Every sweep's starting_time is 0, so the 10 s between steps of the Methods cannot be checked.
  • bias_current is 1e-11 A (10 pA) and bridge_balance 2e7 Ohm in every file. A single DC current cannot hold 44 cells at -65 mV, so these look like placeholders; the holding current is not recorded, and no segment of any sweep is free of it. The Methods' membrane potential "measured in the absence of a current injection" (reading (b) of the claims file) cannot be measured; only the pre-step potential under the holding current (reading (a)) can.
  • The step families differ between cells: most run from -60 to 200 pA, some to 150 or 70 pA, and some ALL cells go to 300, 400 or 500 pA, with repeated families in three files. Only steps from -60 to 200 pA are used (Methods).
  • Three sweeps end early: their voltage is NaN for the last 0.1 to 0.6 s, after the step.
  • The voltage floor. Five files reach exactly -105.6 mV and stay there for 10 ms or more during the -60 pA step (see the sag claim): the recording saturates there, which truncates the response of cells with high input resistance.

No cell breaks the input resistance or resting potential rules. Under the 20% change rule as read here (the test pulse, first sweep against last), 2 APP, 2 AV and 10 ALL cells would go. That the APP and AV counts in the dandiset already equal the paper's (12 and 8) suggests the deposited cells are the ones the paper kept, and that the paper did not apply the change rule this way. The analysis is therefore run on three cell sets, and each claim reports all three:

  • all: every cell in the dandiset (24 ALL, 12 APP, 8 AV), the main set;
  • no_FL: without the three _FL files, which leaves 21 ALL cells, the paper's count (from 10 mice, not 8); that these are the cells the paper left out is a guess;
  • change20: without the cells whose test-pulse input resistance changed by more than 20%.

The single-sweep test pulse is noisy (the per-sweep table above): read as the largest departure from the first sweep, the 20% rule would remove 30 of 44 cells, which cannot be the paper's rule. The first-against-last reading is kept for change20, and stays a variant.

Each set is tested by the two readings of the claims file: (a) a cell-level one-way ANOVA and uncorrected two-tailed Student t-tests for each pair, and (b) the nested design the Methods name (cells nested in mice nested in group), fitted as a linear mixed model with a random intercept per mouse, with Wald tests for the group effect and each pair, and the classical nested ANOVA F (group mean square over mouse-within-group mean square) beside it. In the tables, a pair "X-Y" is Y minus X, and t is positive when Y is larger. The tables also give (b) p_t, the same Wald contrasts referred to t on the number of mice minus 3 degrees of freedom ((b) df_t), since the normal reference is anticonservative with 4 to 11 mice per group. The STAR Methods (Quantification and statistical analysis) say "Shapiro-Wilk and Smirnov-Kolmogorov tests were used to assess normality of the data. Non-parametric tests were conducted if the data failed these tests." The claims file's readings do not include this rule (a question for a reader, questions/001544.yaml), so the outcomes follow readings (a) and (b), and the tables give beside them the smallest Shapiro-Wilk p over groups, the Kruskal-Wallis p and two-sided Mann-Whitney U p for each pair (MW p). Each conclusion says whether the non-parametric tests agree.

How the analysis differs from the paper's: The features follow the STAR Methods, with the paper's code (v1.0.0) where the Methods are silent; the code's own definitions of threshold (10 mV/ms), latency (to the peak) and AHP amplitude are shown as variants. The paper analysed 21 ALL cells from 8 mice; the dandiset holds 24 from 11 and does not say which were left out, so all 24 are used, with the 21 cells left when the _FL files are set aside as a variant. The exclusion rule on a 20% change could only be read through the test pulse, and is a variant. The nested ANOVA of the Methods is fitted as a mixed model with a random intercept per mouse, with Wald tests against the normal and against t on the number of mice minus 3 df. The Methods' switch to non-parametric tests when normality fails is not in the claims file's readings; Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney results are shown beside them; every outcome follows the claims file's readings, and the rank tests would change none of them. Membrane potential can only be read under the DC holding current.

What was found about the dataset: The stimulus is in picoamperes though stated in amperes. bias_current and bridge_balance hold the same value in every file, and the holding current is not recorded. The ALL group has 24 cells from 11 mice where the paper has 21 from 8, and five of its mice are younger than the 8 to 12 weeks of the Methods. The APP mice are 4 male and 2 female where the legend says 3 and 3. Five recordings saturate at -105.6 mV during the -60 pA step, and the code's saturation test cannot detect it. Every sweep starts at time 0, so the interval between steps is not recorded. Only the patch-clamp data are in the dandiset; the paper deposits none of its other data.
