  • ✕Not reproducedFigure 2d and 2e; Extended Data Figure 6e; Results, Co-reactivation occurs more during wake, paragraph 1

    Offline population bursts coincide with the mouse briefly slowing down about 1 s before the burst and resuming its movement about 1 to 2 s later.

    Mice showed a robust and brief slowing down around 1 s before each burst event, before increasing locomotion back up around 2 s later.

    Figure 2d and 2e; Extended Data Figure 6e; Results, Co-reactivation occurs more during wake, paragraph 1

    Telemetry movement in the second before a burst is not lower than 3 to 5 s before it in any of the three recordings under either reading of time zero (peak, one-sided Wilcoxon p = 0.76, 0.89, 0.39; onset, p = 0.88, 0.94, 0.41); 30 to 60% of bursts have lower movement before them. Movement 1 to 2 s after the peak is lower than in the second before it in two of three recordings (0.155 against 0.225, and 0.129 against 0.257). The telemetry activity signal is a coarse proxy whose relation to calcium activity is weak (movement decoded at r = 0.06 to 0.24 at zero shift; Spearman r with the population trace 0.07 to 0.32 in 1 s bins). The files describe it as motion of the probe relative to the receiver and a proxy for locomotion; its mean magnitude is 4 to 15 times higher in wake than in quiet wake epochs. The test is not calibrated, giving p = 0.016 at 300 random times in Ca_EEG2-1 (0.9999 and 0.92 in the others). The outcome says this proxy does not show the pause, and is weak evidence that the pause is absent.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Movement is the magnitude of the HD-X02 telemetry activity signal; the paper tracked locomotion with a webcam, whose video is not deposited.
    • Tested on the EEG/EMG experiment's offline recordings; the paper shows the effect for the single-experience cohort (Figure 2d and 2e) and the inhibitory tagging cohort (Extended Data Figure 6e).
  • ✕Not reproducedFigure 5c (left); Methods, "Ensemble co-participation analysis"

    After a strong (high-shock) aversive experience, during wake in the offline period the overlap ensemble co-bursts more with the neutral ensemble than with the aversive ensemble (t4 = 4.94, P = 0.016).

    Left, wake high-shock mice had higher co-bursting of overlap × neutral than overlap × aversive

    Figure 5c (left); Methods, "Ensemble co-participation analysis"

    What the paper reports

    • t4 = 4.94, p = 0.016 (n = 5 high-shock mice)

    In Ca_EEG2-1's first offline recording after aversive encoding, under reading (a), wake and quiet wake together (all 87 bursts), OxN is below OxA (26.4 against 35.6% inclusive, 8.0 against 17.2% exclusive; 7 against 15 discordant bursts, McNemar p = 0.13). Reading (a) is the one the Methods imply. Their scoring (6 s epochs, two-state Gaussian mixture on EMG) applied to this mouse's log EMG RMS over the 12 h offline period puts 94.1% of quiet-wake frames and 98.5% of wake frames in wake (97.9% of quiet wake when fitted on the first 6 h), and all 87 bursts in wake. Under reading (b), wake alone, OxN exceeds OxA (32.0 against 28.0% of 25 bursts; 8.0 against 4.0%) on 2 against 1 discordant bursts (McNemar p = 1.0). Under the Methods' wake the order holds at thresholds 1.5 (34.9 against 41.1%) and 2.5 (21.7 against 30.0%), and no difference reaches p < 0.05. Outside bursts both are at most 1.02% of 0.5 s windows. The neutral, aversive and overlap ensembles each have more of their cells in bursts than the remaining cells (median differences 0.005 to 0.010, Wilcoxon p = 0.041 to 0.0003). The paper's statistic (t4 = 4.94, P = 0.016, across five mice) cannot be tested. The evidence against the paper is weak; one 10 min recording of 24 for one mouse of five, with ensemble traces z-scored within it, so levels are not comparable with the paper's source data for this mouse over all 24 recordings (10.37% OxN against 8.39% OxA in wake). Counted inclusively, co-participation here (26 to 36%) is 2.5 to 4 times those values; with the third ensemble excluded (8.0 and 17.2%) it is on their scale, with OxN the lower.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • One high-shock mouse of five, and its first offline recording of 24 (10 min of 4 h).
    • The Methods z-score the ensemble traces over all 24 offline recordings concatenated; here only one is deposited, so they are z-scored within it.
    • The criterion fixed four readings (quiet wake in or out of wake; co-participation with or without the third ensemble) and would count the claim under any of them. After the first verification the outcome is judged on wake including quiet wake, as the Methods' own EMG scoring puts quiet wake in wake; under wake alone OxN exceeds OxA without a significant difference, which the criterion would have called partially reproduced.
    • The EMG feature of the Methods' scoring (log RMS per 6 s epoch) is chosen here, after the data were seen; the Methods do not name one. Without the log the mixture does not separate sleep from wake.
    • Bursts are assigned the sleep state of their peak frame, a choice made here.

  • ⊘BlockedResults, Neutral ensemble recruited into population bursts, paragraph 1; Extended Data Figure 3l and 3m· Methods underspecifiedverifier disagreed at first

    During offline periods, hippocampal population bursts occur more often than expected from shuffled neuronal activity, so CA1 neurons fire in a more coordinated way than chance.

    True burst frequencies were greater than shuffled burst frequencies in every mouse ... suggesting that during the offline period, hippocampal CA1 neurons fire in a more coordinated manner than would be expected from shuffled neuronal activities

    Results, Neutral ensemble recruited into population bursts, paragraph 1; Extended Data Figure 3l and 3m

    What the paper reports

    • t7 = 6.159, p = 4.6 × 10⁻⁴ (n = 8 mice)

    The analysis ran. Under the criterion's procedure (surrogates z-scored and thresholded at z = 2 as the data) the true burst rate is below the surrogate mean in all three offline recordings (5.30, 6.40 and 8.71 per min against 8.48 to 10.94; true over surrogate mean 0.63 to 0.80), which would be not reproduced. The paper's chance level for this test (source data for Extended Data Figure 3m, another cohort) is 3.38 to 4.39 surrogate bursts per min against 4.51 to 4.92 true, surrogate over true 0.72 to 0.96; this procedure gives 1.26 to 1.60, far from it, so the procedures differ and the claim is blocked as the protocol requires. The Methods do not say how bursts are counted in a surrogate. Variants disagree; merging events closer than 1 s leaves the true rate below the surrogates (4.40 to 7.31 against 6.56 to 8.52 per min), and thresholding surrogates at the true trace's own mean + 2 SD puts the true rate far above them (5.30 to 8.71 against at most 0.50 per min). At z = 2.5 the true rate is above the surrogate mean in 2 of 3 recordings and above the 95th percentile in none. On synthetic data with 6 coordinated events per minute, the criterion's test gives 5.6 true bursts per min against a surrogate mean of 34.98, and with independent cells 34.8 against 37.36, so it cannot detect coordination; the fixed-threshold variant separates the two (5.6 against 0.00, 34.8 against 38.37), but its chance level in the data (surrogate means at most 0.10 per min) is far below the paper's.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Run on the three 10 min offline recordings of the EEG/EMG experiment at 15 Hz; the paper shows the test for the one-hour recordings at 30 Hz of the single-experience cohort (Extended Data Figure 3), which is not deposited.
    • Burst events taken as maximal runs of frames above threshold, as the Methods do not define an event further.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds two of the nine mice of the calcium imaging and EEG/EMG experiment (Ca_EEG2-1, high shock; Ca_EEG3-4, low shock) and, of each offline period, only the first 10 min recording of the 24 the Methods describe. Ca_EEG2-1 has no offline-day-1 recording and no neutral or novel recall session. The mice of the one-hour imaging and offline experiments (Figures 1d to 1g, 2 to 4 and 6, Extended Data Figures 3, 4 and 7), the behaviour-only experiments, and the inhibitory tagging and no-shock experiments are not deposited. Twenty of the 26 claims cannot be tested for this reason.
  • Data missingCa_EEG3-4 has no cell registration in the dandiset, so its ensembles cannot be formed. The Week file of Ca_EEG2-1 holds 19 registration tables for offline-day-2 recordings, of which only the first recording is deposited, and none for offline day 1.
  • Selection not documentedThe dandiset does not say why these two mice, and only the first offline recording of each period, were deposited, nor that the paper analysed more.
  • Metadata errorThe session start times of the Ca_EEG2-1 behaviour sessions (NeutralExposure, FC, Recall1), and the Week epochs built from them, are about 1 h later than the telemetry and Miniscope clock. As deposited, OfflineDay2Session1 starts 47 min before the FC session it follows. The telemetry is absent (mouse out of its home cage) in windows that contain each of these epochs moved 1 h earlier, and the offline file's EEG sits exactly at its own epoch in the week recording.
  • File format errorThe OnePhotonSeries timestamps of three files (Ca_EEG2-1 NeutralExposure, Ca_EEG3-4 OfflineDay1Session1, Ca_EEG3-4 Recall2) run at 16.7 ms and end at half the recording's length, while their frame counts fit 30 Hz over the full length given by the deconvolved timestamps.
  • Description missingThe sleep states include `quiet wake`, which the paper neither uses nor maps to its wake, NREM and REM states, so it is not known which state the paper assigned these epochs to. The Methods' scoring, applied to the EMG of Ca_EEG2-1, puts 94% of them in wake. The three offline recordings hold no NREM (`sws`) or REM epochs.
  • OtherThe telemetry (EEG, EMG, activity) is sampled at 500 Hz in the offline files and the Week file; the Methods say the HD-X02 implants recorded at 100 Hz.
  • Metadata errorThe session description of the offline recordings says that mouse behaviour and calcium were recorded for an hour with a webcam above the home cage. The deposited offline recordings last 10 min, hold telemetry and no video, and the Methods describe this experiment's offline recordings as 10 min in the telemetry home cage.
  • Description missingThe fluorescence series carry placeholder descriptions ("description of deconvolved traces") and unit "n.a.", the FC session descriptions give the shock amplitude without a unit, and the Week file has an empty session description.
  • Methods not availableThe analysis code repository the paper names (github.com/denisecailab/RetrospectiveMemoryLinkingAnalysis_2024) returns 404, so how bursts are counted, in the data and in shuffled surrogates, could not be checked; the surrogate burst rates here differ from the paper's chance level.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • Only four per-mouse values can be compared, as the dandiset lacks the neutral and novel recall sessions of Ca_EEG2-1 and the other seven mice.source-data-link
  • One mouse per group (the paper has 5 high-shock and 4 low-shock mice); the test is within sessions, over 30 s bins, chosen here.ed9b-aversive-freezing-high-vs-low
  • Run on the three 10 min offline recordings of the EEG/EMG experiment at 15 Hz; the paper shows the test for the one-hour recordings at 30 Hz of the single-experience cohort (Extended Data Figure 3), which is not deposited.offline-bursts-above-shuffle
  • Burst events taken as maximal runs of frames above threshold, as the Methods do not define an event further.offline-bursts-above-shuffle
  • Run on the EEG/EMG experiment's three 10 min offline recordings; the paper shows the test for another cohort (Extended Data Figure 3o), which is not deposited.offline-skew-above-shuffle
  • Movement is the magnitude of the HD-X02 telemetry activity signal; the paper tracked locomotion with a webcam, whose video is not deposited.burst-locomotion-pause
  • Tested on the EEG/EMG experiment's offline recordings; the paper shows the effect for the single-experience cohort (Figure 2d and 2e) and the inhibitory tagging cohort (Extended Data Figure 6e).burst-locomotion-pause
  • One high-shock mouse of five, and its first offline recording of 24 (10 min of 4 h).fig5c-wake-overlap-neutral-coburst
  • The Methods z-score the ensemble traces over all 24 offline recordings concatenated; here only one is deposited, so they are z-scored within it.fig5c-wake-overlap-neutral-coburst
  • The criterion fixed four readings (quiet wake in or out of wake; co-participation with or without the third ensemble) and would count the claim under any of them. After the first verification the outcome is judged on wake including quiet wake, as the Methods' own EMG scoring puts quiet wake in wake; under wake alone OxN exceeds OxA without a significant difference, which the criterion would have called partially reproduced.fig5c-wake-overlap-neutral-coburst
  • The EMG feature of the Methods' scoring (log RMS per 6 s epoch) is chosen here, after the data were seen; the Methods do not name one. Without the log the mixture does not separate sleep from wake.fig5c-wake-overlap-neutral-coburst
  • Bursts are assigned the sleep state of their peak frame, a choice made here.fig5c-wake-overlap-neutral-coburst
