  • ✕Not reproducedAbstract

    The two PT types differ in when they carry information about the movement; the thalamus-projecting type carries early preparatory activity, the medulla-projecting type mainly late preparatory activity and motor commands.

    (a) Sample epoch selectivity is 0.614 for PTupper and 0.482 for PTlower (one-sided Fisher p = 0.134). (b) Response epoch selectivity is 0.643 for PTlower and 0.841 for PTupper, the reverse of the claim (p = 0.99; p = 0.022 for the reverse). Over animals neither holds (Mann-Whitney p = 0.30 and 0.93). The code's coding-direction variant points the abstract's way but is not significant. The sample to late delay similarity is 0.473 +/- 0.214 for PTupper and 0.216 +/- 0.204 for PTlower, with PTupper higher in only 83 of 100 bootstraps. Without the bootstrap, PTupper's similarity is above its shuffle null (p = 0.007) and PTlower's is not (0.177, p = 0.43). On the paper's 130 units from its processed data, (a) is 0.656 against 0.507 (p = 0.062) and (b) is reversed (0.551 against 0.770, p = 0.007 for the reverse); with one session per animal, (a) p = 0.28 and (b) is reversed (0.438 against 0.800). The paper's measure of early information (Extended Data Fig. 9, single-unit decoding accuracy in the sample epoch, at least 70%) goes the abstract's way, with 7 of 44 PTupper against 1 of 56 PTlower units here (Fisher p = 0.012) and 12 of 61 against 1 of 69 on the paper's units (p = 0.0005), where the legend gives 15 of 61 against 3 of 69; the means match the legend (0.638 and 0.586 against 0.644 and 0.589), and the smaller counts are not explained. Part (a) is thus supported under the paper's measure but not under the criterion's. Part (b), selectivity for lick direction in the response epoch, is reversed in every variant. Under the variant reading of "motor commands" as a response epoch rate that differs from baseline, PTlower is ahead, 53 of 56 against 37 of 44, without reaching significance (one-sided Fisher p = 0.080). The reversal thus shows that PTlower units are less often direction-selective in the response epoch, not that they lack movement-related activity.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Claims written from the abstract only; the comparison of fractions between types is this attempt's reading of the contrast in the abstract, and the paper may compare the types differently.
    • Each animal holds one type (8 PTupper and 4 PTlower animals), so type and animal are confounded; the PTlower recordings include sessions in both hemispheres.
    • The dandiset lacks 17 of the paper's 61 PTupper and 13 of its 69 PTlower units; the variant on the paper's units gives the same outcome.

  • ✓ReproducedAbstract (stated as background to the study)

    Descending PT neurons in layer 5 of motor cortex carry preparatory activity, which predicts the upcoming movement before it is initiated.

    72 of 100 included tagged units are selective for lick direction in the delay epoch (rank-sum p < 0.01); the label-shuffle null has a 99th percentile of 0.040 (p = 0.001). PTupper 29 of 44, PTlower 43 of 56; every animal 25% to 85%. 76 units at p < 0.05 and 67 at p < 0.001. The selective units lie at nominal depths of 430 to 1600 um; the files give no layer per unit, so the statement's "layer 5" is not checked. With one session per animal 26 of 36 units are selective (p = 0.001), and among the paper's 130 units from its processed data 89 are (p = 0.001).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Claims written from the abstract only; the criterion and the shuffle test of chance are this attempt's.
    • Epoch rates are spike counts on the file's clock; the paper's code averaged a causally smoothed PSTH of spikes already assigned to trials. Leaving out the spikes that overlap the next trial, as far as the stored order shows them, gives 73 of 100.
    • Units recorded on different days in one animal are counted separately, as in the code; they may be the same neurons (44 of 100 lie within 20 um of a unit of the same animal on another day, and the files hold no waveforms). A variant keeps one session per animal.
    • The dandiset holds 44 of the paper's 61 PTupper and 56 of its 69 PTlower units; the code's inclusion rule gives the paper's counts on its processed data and the same decision for every unit the dandiset holds.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dandiset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missing11 sessions of the paper's processed data (Figshare doi:10.25378/janelia.7007846) that hold tagged units are not in the dandiset. They hold 17 of the 61 PTupper and 13 of the 69 PTlower units the paper analysed, so the dandiset holds 44 and 56. Every tagged unit the dandiset does hold is in the processed data with the same label and the same inclusion decision under the paper's code. The dandiset's description says some files were found to be broken and were left out, without naming them.
  • Data missingThe dandiset holds the electrophysiology only. The transcriptomic and anatomical data behind four of the abstract's findings (the two PT types and their projection targets) are not in it; they are at GEO (GSE119182) and on Figshare (doi:10.25378/janelia.7007846).
  • File format errorSpike times are not sorted in 1120 of 1149 units. They were written trial by trial as trial start + go cue time + the spike time from the go cue. Each trial's spikes cover 6.4 s from about 0.97 s before the pole comes in, and trial starts are a median 6.4 s apart (5.4 to 7.9 s from the 1st to the 99th percentile), so consecutive trials overlap on the file's clock and some spikes of one trial lie in the next trial's early epochs (11.7% of the spikes counted in the baseline epoch, under 2% in later epochs, are detectably out of order). Trial membership of a spike cannot be recovered in full from the files.
  • File format errorThe trial table's stop_time is NaN in every file, and pole_in_time, pole_out_time and cue_start_time are relative to the trial start without saying so. On the file's clock the go cue of a trial often falls after the next trial's start_time.
  • Description missingThe tagged unit labels PTupper and PTlower are not described in the files or the dandiset metadata, and the projection targets (thalamus and medulla) that the paper uses to name the types are stated only in the paper's code.
  • Description missingUnit quality mixes two vocabularies (single, and Excellent, Good, Fair, Poor) and has undescribed empty values ([], 0, blank). Sex is U and genotype empty for every mouse, the electrode positions are all zero, and every electrode group gives layer 5 whatever the depth.
  • OtherIn the paper's processed data, in every one of the 53 sessions the dandiset holds, the spike windows of the first ten trials start later relative to the pole than those of the other trials (by about 2.7 s in the session examined in detail); the paper's code leaves out the first ten trials of each unit, which hides this. The files carry the same shift, as their spike times equal the processed ones in every unit.

Reproduction Two types of pyramidal tract neuron in mouse motor cortex (Economo et al. 2018)

Tests the findings in the abstract of Economo et al. (2018) that dandiset 000006 can test. The dandiset holds silicon probe recordings from the anterior lateral motor cortex of 12 mice in a delayed response task, with tagged PTupper and PTlower units. The notebook measures the selectivity for lick direction of the tagged units in the sample, delay and response epochs, and compares the two types. The transcriptomic and anatomical findings are not testable on it. All files
