Bech et al. 2026 · eLife

Retrosplenial cortex enables context-dependent goal-directed sensorimotor transformation

doi:10.7554/elife.109717 · paper key bech-2026

Datasets

  • DANDI:001847

Most of the paper's behavioural findings held up in this data, and its optogenetic maps could not be tested. Licking to the whisker stimulus, the first-trial switch at context changes, and the modelled behaviour all reproduced, as did most of the widefield and photoactivation-control findings, though the expert-session count did not (318 sessions in 49 mice against 201 in 41) because the paper's set is a fixed list. The seven optogenetic map claims are blocked because the methods give a different shuffle null in different places and no threshold for an affected site. The retrosplenial context-discrimination ranking is also blocked, because the paper states two baselines that give different results, and the retrosplenial tracing is untestable because the images are not in the dataset. The Figure 4 trajectory finding did not reproduce, and the Figure 4 connectivity claims were never run.

  • ✓Reproduced Catch-trial (spontaneous) lick rates are low but slightly higher in W+ blocks, 13 ± 7% against 4 ± 2% in W-.
  • ✓Reproduced The context modulation of whisker licking holds whichever background noise (pink or brown) is associated with each context.
  • ✓Reproduced When the two context noises are replaced by constant white noise, keeping the block reward structure, mice no longer discriminate between the context blocks in their whisker responses.
  • ✓Reproduced Mice change their whisker response at the first whisker trial after a context change, from 26 ± 8% to 73 ± 13% from W- to W+, and from 87 ± 7% to 57 ± 13% from W+ to W-.
  • ✓Reproduced After the first whisker trial of a new block, whisker responses adapt slightly further over the following whisker trials, the second whisker trial already closer to the new context than the first.
  • ✓Reproduced The lick probability to the first whisker trial of a block rises with the time elapsed since a transition to W+ and falls with it after a transition to W-, while the last whisker trial before a transition shows no sign of anticipating the switch.
  • ✓Reproduced Whisker-rewarded context blocks last longer than whisker-non-rewarded blocks (196 ± 13 against 185 ± 4 s).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Spout contact after the stimulus is slightly faster in W+ than in W-, for auditory (370 ± 9 against 400 ± 9 ms) and whisker trials (392 ± 9 against 418 ± 9 ms).
  • ✓Reproduced Before the whisker stimulus, pupil area is larger in W+ than in W- (0.52 ± 0.13 against 0.50 ± 0.12 mm2), while whisker angle and jaw opening show no general effect of context.
  • ✓Reproduced Independently of context, before trials in which the mouse then licks to the whisker stimulus the jaw rests more open (0.27 ± 0.05 against 0.23 ± 0.04 mm) and the whisker is more posterior (97.3 ± 13 against 98.6 ± 12 degrees).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Pre-stimulus jaw speed is higher in the rewarded context (3.0 ± 1.0 against 2.5 ± 0.8 mm/s), and also before lick than before no-lick trials, while whisking speed shows no effect of context or lick.
  • ✓Reproduced A gradient-boosted tree model of the whisker-trial lick choice, from task, history and orofacial features, reaches a cross-validated accuracy of 0.78 on held-out test trials.
  • ✓Reproduced Among the model's features, context gives the largest drop in accuracy when permuted, followed by whether the previous whisker trial was rewarded, the cumulative number of rewards and the index of the whisker trial in the block.
