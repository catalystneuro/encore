Control of the Electroporation Efficiency of Nanosecond Pulses by Swinging the Electric Field Vector Direction

DANDI:000631: Most of the paper's Figure 2 findings held: paired pulses cancelled dye uptake at large field-direction angles, enhanced it at small angles, and cancelled more at 833 kHz than at 1 Hz. The two cancellation sizes the paper reports never matched together in one ROI layout, and the facilitation plateau appeared at 833 kHz but not at 1 Hz. The 60-fold co- versus counter-directional difference matched only at the single lowest-uptake region, so confidence is low. The stronger uptake near the apex electrode with paired pulses held. The square-array and voltage-imaging findings could not be tested because their data are not in the dataset.

DANDI:000632: The no-difference findings held in this data, the 770 kHz enhancement did not, and the Figure 2, 4 and 5 findings could not be tested. Co- and cross-directional pulses gave similar dye uptake at 1 Hz, cross-directional pulses at 770 kHz matched 1 Hz, and uptake did not depend on membrane area. Co-directional pulses gained 1.96-fold at 770 kHz on average but not significantly (p = 0.16), and did not significantly beat cross-directional ones. With few dishes the tests had little power, so they show only that no difference was found. The field-angle and voltage-imaging findings sit in other dandisets outside this attempt's scope.

DANDI:000633: Only the three findings from Figure 4 could be tested, and all three held up, two of them on assumptions. Single-pulse dye uptake followed the computed field (Spearman rho 0.67), paired pulses concentrated uptake at the apex electrode, and a comet-shaped region appeared once bright debris was left out, though the half-maximum pass depends on how reach is measured. The nine findings on Figures 2, 3 and 5, including the 20-fold cancellation at 180 degrees, could not be tested because their data are not in the dataset. The single-pulse file's description names a different experiment, and which electrode was pulsed is assumed.
