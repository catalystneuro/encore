Mannion et al. 2015 · Other journals

Sensitivity to the visual field origin of natural image patches in human low-level visual cortex

doi:10.7717/peerj.1038 · paper key mannion-2015

Datasets

  • CRCNS:vim-3

None of the paper's main findings could be tested in this data. These are the interaction between where a natural image patch is shown and where it was taken from, the larger lower-field responses to patches from below fixation, and the links between patch statistics such as mean luminance and the field preference. All rest on responses in V1, V2 and V3, and the dataset holds the raw functional runs without the anatomy or area definitions. The image statistics also need the monitor's colour calibration, which is not public. Of the minor claims, equal task performance across conditions held up, while the residual and radial-bias checks could not be decided.

  • ⊘Blocked The localiser masks for upper visual field presentation lie largely in ventral subregions of V1 to V3 and those for lower visual field presentation in dorsal subregions.
  • ⊘Blocked Response amplitude shows a significant interaction between presentation location (upper, lower visual field) and source location (above, below fixation), F(1,6) = 11.19, p = 0.016.
  • ⊘Blocked The presentation by source interaction does not depend on visual area (three-way interaction F(1.9,11.4) = 1.52, p = 0.260), which justifies combining V1, V2 and V3.
  • ⊘Blocked For presentation in the lower visual field, combined over V1 to V3, patches sourced from below fixation evoke larger responses than patches from above (0.80 against 0.72 psc), a significant difference (t(6) = 5.17, p = 0.002).
  • ⊘Blocked For presentation in the upper visual field, patches sourced from below and above fixation evoke comparable responses (0.84 against 0.85 psc; t(6) = 0.38, p = 0.719), so the predicted preference for above-sourced patches in the upper field was not found.
  • ⊘Blocked Ranking the 60 image patch pairs by the difference between upper and lower field responses, four of the five most upper-preferred pairs are sourced from above fixation and four of the five most lower-preferred pairs from below fixation.
  • ⊘Blocked The upper-preferred patch pairs are regions of largely uniform luminance and mostly depict sky; the lower-preferred pairs are more variegated and mostly depict the ground.
  • ⊘Blocked Higher mean luminance of a patch pair goes with a larger upper minus lower response difference (Spearman r = 0.31, p = 0.016).
  • ⊘Blocked Neither the mean pixel value of either chromatic channel nor the standard deviation of pixel values in any channel is significantly related to the upper minus lower response difference.
  • ⊘Blocked Higher output of luminance Gabor filters at 1 and 2 cycles per degree goes with a larger upper minus lower response difference (Spearman r = 0.32, p = 0.013 and r = 0.26, p = 0.049).
  • ⊘Blocked Higher output of luminance Gabor filters at 45, 90 and 135 degrees goes with a larger upper minus lower response difference (Spearman r = 0.34, 0.25 and 0.39; p = 0.008, 0.050 and 0.002).
  • ⊘Blocked No other luminance spatial frequency (4, 8, 16 cpd) or orientation (0 degrees), and no spatial frequency or orientation in the chromatic channels, is significantly related to the upper minus lower response difference.
  • ⊘Blocked The output of Gabor filters oriented at 45 and 135 degrees does not differ significantly, so the patches show no reliable radial bias.
  • ✓Reproduced Performance on the central fixation task does not differ across the presentation and source location conditions.
  • ⊘Blocked The residual error of the GLM after event onset does not differ across source location conditions within each presentation location.
  • ⊘Blocked The residual error of the GLM is larger for upper than for lower visual field presentation.
