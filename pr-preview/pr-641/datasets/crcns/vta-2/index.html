  • fig1b-latency-scales-with-reward-rate

    Rats were more motivated when rewards were more likely; their latency from Light-On to Center-In scaled inversely with reward rate.

    This was apparent in their “latency” – the time between Light-On and Center-In - which was sensitive to the outcome of the preceding few trials ( Extended Data Fig.1 ) and thereby scaled inversely with reward rate ( Fig.1b ).

    Results, Dopamine tracks motivation in key loci, paragraph 1; Figure 1b

    How it will be judged

    Per session, reward rate from the leaky integrator with the session's best tau (Methods, Behavior, paragraph 2) and log(latency) per trial, correlated with Pearson's r (the Methods' "correlation"). Which sessions Fig. 1b uses is in its legend, not read; the readings are every bandit session of the electrophysiology rats, and of the photometry and microdialysis rats where those are available. Reproduced when the correlation is negative in more than half of the sessions and its mean over sessions is negative with a one-sample t-test p < 0.05 (a standard choice, the paper's test for Fig. 1b not being read). Note that tau is chosen to make this correlation negative, so the test is of its presence, not of an independent prediction.
