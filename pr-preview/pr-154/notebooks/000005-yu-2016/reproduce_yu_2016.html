Reproducing Yu et al. (2016) from dandiset 000005¶

Paper. Yu J, Gutnisky DA, Hires SA, Svoboda K (2016). Layer 4 fast-spiking interneurons filter thalamocortical signals during active somatosensation. Nature Neuroscience 19:1647-1657. doi:10.1038/nn.4412. The full text is not open access, and no open copy was found (PubMed Central, Europe PMC, Unpaywall, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar). This attempt used the abstract, the eight figure titles, and the supplementary information, which is public on the publisher's site: the legends of Supplementary Figures 1 to 14 and Supplementary Tables 1 and 2 (the mouse line of each mouse, and the cells per line, recording mode and manipulation). The claims were written from the abstract and figure titles before the supplement was read (in the first verification round). The supplement settles some things: FS cells are defined by spike waveform among light-driven cells in VGAT-ChR2 mice; spike-phase modulation is judged by the r² of a sinusoid fit (cut-off 0.25); the PV-Cre mice express eNpHR in PV cells; the VPM light is a 100 Hz train of 5 ms pulses; and Figure 7 tests the sensory origin of FS whisking responses by transecting the infraorbital nerve (ION). It gives no windows, thresholds or waveform boundaries, so the measures here remain choices made in this attempt. No number from the paper's main text is tested; the supplement's cell counts are compared with the files in the data check.

Dandiset. DANDI:000005, version 0.220126.1853. The paper predates the archive and cites no version. This is the only published version; the draft lists the same 148 assets and sizes, so the most recent published version was pinned.

What the dandiset holds. 18 sessions of silicon probe recordings in VPM (6 mice, sorted units), and 130 sessions with one L4 neuron each, recorded whole-cell or juxtacellularly (49 mice), all during a pole localization task with whisker tracking, and most L4 sessions with optogenetic stimulation. No file states the cell type of its L4 neuron, or whether the mouse had its ION transected, so FS interneurons and excitatory neurons are identified here from the data (see the data check).

Code. Neither the paper nor the dandiset links analysis code; none was found.

Outside input. Supplementary Table 1, read at run time from the supplement PDF (part of doi:10.1038/nn.4412), gives the mouse line of each mouse. It is used only in the data check.

# Long light epochs at the vS1 site in PV-Cre mice: firing inside the epoch, light trials against
# trials without light, every PV-Cre cell with at least 20 spikes in these trials.
def light_effect(r, site):
    w = yu2016.light_windows(r, site)
    if len(w) == 0:
        return None
    lit = np.zeros(len(r["trials"]), bool)
    lit[w.trial.to_numpy(int)] = True
    dark = ~yu2016.light_trials(r)
    win = (round(float(np.median(w.rel_start)), 3), round(float(np.median(w.rel_stop)), 3))
    rl, nl = yu2016.window_rate(r, lit, win)
    rd, nd = yu2016.window_rate(r, dark, win)
    starts = r["trials"]["start"].to_numpy()
    sp = np.sort(r["spikes"])
    per_trial = lambda m: np.array([np.searchsorted(sp, s + win[1]) - np.searchsorted(sp, s + win[0]) for s in starts[m]])
    p = stats.mannwhitneyu(per_trial(lit), per_trial(dark)).pvalue if lit.sum() and dark.sum() else np.nan
    return {"window": win, "lit_trials": int(lit.sum()), "dark_trials": int(dark.sum()), "rate_light": rl,
            "rate_dark": rd, "spikes": nl + nd, "p": p}

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The full text was not available; the claims are those of the abstract and figure titles, and the supplement was read only after the first run. No number of the paper's main text is tested. Every measure is a choice made here: the touch window (25 ms), the whisking band and threshold, the phase modulation and its null (the paper's sinusoid r² is shown as a variant), the touch selectivity index and the inclusion rules. FS cells are light-tagged GABAergic cells in VGAT-ChR2 mice, not cells inside the paper's waveform boundary, and excitatory cells are broad-spiking untagged whole-cell cells; the paper's excitatory groups also hold cell-attached cells. Cells from ION-transected mice, which the paper keeps apart, may be in both groups because the files do not mark them; the 4 mice inferred here to be transected contribute 3 excitatory and 4 included FS cells. Leaving them out is shown as a variant. Under it the selectivity test of exc-mainly-touch passes (p = 0.024 and 0.038), but the outcome stays not reproduced, because the flag is drawn from touch responses and favours the claim. The whisking test of the claims file (Rayleigh) was replaced after the first run, when its null control failed; both are shown. Membrane potential, the subject of Figures 2 to 4, is not analysed except to find cells with a touch input.

What was found about the dandiset.

  • No file names the cell type of its L4 neuron, the opsin, or what each light condition does; photo_stim_mode has no description, its labels do not map to one kind of light, and photo_stim_power is empty in every L4 file.
  • No file marks the ION-transected mice of Figure 7 (9 VGAT-ChR2 mice in Supplementary Table 2); 4 mice whose cells have no touch input are probably among them.
