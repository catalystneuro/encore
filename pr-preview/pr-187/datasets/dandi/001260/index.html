  • fig2e-category-stability

    About 57% of ww units keep their category from one block of about 50 spontaneous epochs to the next, far above the 18% expected by chance from the marginal distributions.

    Consistently, around 57% of units remained in the category they had in the previous block, which is considerably above the 18% expected from chance (marginal distributions; Fig. 2e )

    Figure 2e; Results, Stable classification of single-unit firing patterns, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Blocks as in Methods (Stability analyses), the recording's own blocks of 50 or 100 epochs (100 split in two) where the setting has them, else blocks of about 50 epochs; blocks with fewer than 12 epochs of at least 6 spikes dropped for that unit (Methods; the code uses at least 3 valid epochs per block and 5 per transition, shown as a variant). Block metrics standardized to the full-data ww SOM and assigned their best matching node's category. Stability per transition t is the trace of the summed transition matrix M_t over its total, and chance is the same for the matrix expected from the marginals of M_t (Methods, Assessment of overall stability per transition; Fig. 2e plots it per transition). The paper does not say how "around 57%" summarizes the transitions; readings (a) the mean over transitions, (b) the pooled matrix summed over transitions. Both are shown with the per-transition values, and the value holds under either. The dataset may not mark the recordings' inherent block structure; if it does not, blocks of about 50 consecutive epochs are used and this is a deviation.
