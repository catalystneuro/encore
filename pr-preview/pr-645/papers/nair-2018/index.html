Nair et al. 2018 · Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences

Basal forebrain contributes to default mode network regulation

doi:10.1073/pnas.1712431115 · paper key nair-2018

Datasets

  • CRCNS:bf-1

The main finding, that basal forebrain gamma is lower in the arena than in the home cage, held in direction and significance, but the drop was 48% against the paper's 35%. Gamma rising with movement in the home cage did not reproduce under the fixed test, which cannot detect a within-rat effect in so few rats, though gamma rose in every rat. Gamma rising with time in the arena held in sign but not in size, and the brief excursions to the home-cage state did not match in count or duration. The cortical, single-unit, behavior and object findings could not be tested because the data set holds only basal forebrain LFP.
