Dandiset. DANDI:001678, version 0.260227.1824 (doi:10.48324/dandi.001678/0.260227.1824). The paper cites no DANDI version: its data statement points to Science Data Bank (doi:10.57760/sciencedb.32375), whose record answers "PERMISSION NO ACCESS" without a login. 0.260227.1824 is the only published version, so it is pinned; the draft holds the same 22 files (checked below).

What the dandiset holds (shown in the data check). The 9 files of the 1-minute paradigm hold one channel per trial, and that channel is the stimulation command (a sinusoid at the trial's frequency whose peak is the current in uA, zero outside stimulation). They hold no recording channel: no multi-unit activity, no LFP and no stimulation monitor. The 13 files of the 20-minute paradigm hold a 16-channel stream at 12207 Hz that carries both the LFP and spikes (the multi-unit band is not filtered out), but no sorted units. The sham recording is not in the dandiset. Every claim on the 1-minute recordings or the sham recording is blocked for missing data. The claims on 20-minute spikes need the recordings sorted: one rat is sorted below over its whole 50-minute window, and at its measured speed sorting the 13 rats exceeds the notebook's 60-minute limit, so these claims, with the LFP claims that select channels by their neurons, are blocked as too large. The claim tested is the electric field of the 20-minute experiments, from the stimulation artifact.

name: Data for parametrizing alternating current stimulation for neuromodulation (Bell Vila, M., et al, 2026)
description: Data collected using a 4x4 multielectrode array (Microprobes) in propofol anesthetized rats receiving subcutaneous alternating current stimulation.

Recording electrodes had platinum iridium shanks with a tip width of 125μm, an impedance of 0.4 MOhm, and were spaced 0.5mm apart in the lateral plane. An additional shank with 10 kOhm impedance served as a reference. Electrodes were inserted ~300μm into the craniotomy exposed sensorimotor cortex (L2/3). Data was acquired with a sampling rate of 24 kHz (Tucker-Davis Technologies).

Stimulation electrodes were adhered directly to the skull, medial and lateral the cranial window.



There are two data files.

The first contains experiments where 9 three-month old Sprague-Dawley rats received a randomized order of stimulation parameters (frequencies: 5, 10, 20, 40, and 140 Hz, amplitudes: 50, 100, 200, and 400 μA). Each dated folder contains recordings for a single rat, with subfolders for each stimulation parameter delivery (1-min baseline + 1-min sACS + 1-min baseline).

The second data file contains experiments where 13 rats received a single 20-min delivery of 40 Hz, 100 μA stimulation. Each dated folder contains experiments from a single rat. Baseline activity was recorded for 20-min before stimulation onset and subsequently following sACS for 10 minutes. Additional subfolders contain further 20-min of post stimulation baseline activity, collected to an hour following stimulation offset.

Spike sorting of the 20-minute recordings: how long it takes¶

units-20min and every claim built on its neurons need the 20-minute recordings sorted, as the paper did with SpyKING CIRCUS (Methods 2.6). The claims use 20 minutes of baseline, the 20 minutes of stimulation and the 10 minutes after offset, so a sort needs 50 minutes per rat (less in the four rats whose first series ends sooner). Here one rat is sorted over its whole 50-minute window, as every rat would be: the multi-unit band (500 to 3000 Hz, Methods 2.3) is streamed and filtered, the artifact is removed during stimulation with a causal uniform comb filter over the 10 preceding periods (Methods 2.5, ARtACS; the paper's MUAartrem.m uses 10 periods), and SpyKING CIRCUS 2 sorts it at the paper's detection threshold of 6. The time of this rat, per minute of recording, gives the time for all 13 rats. The earlier version of this notebook extrapolated from 5 and 15 minutes of the same rat, and its estimate moved widely between runs; the full window measures it directly. The time depends on the machine, and the numbers printed below are those of this run.

def estimates(order=None):
    out = []
    for s, v in zip(subjects, pk):
        g = acs.grid(v, order)
        F = acs.fields(g)
        node = np.hypot(F["ex"][:3, :3], F["ey"][:3, :3]).ravel()
        out.append({"subject": s, "cell": F["cells"], "node": node, "node16": F["nodes_all"],
                    "maximal": np.array([F["maximal"]])})
    return out


def summarise(est, kind, grubbs):
    pooled = np.concatenate([e[kind] for e in est])
    if grubbs == "pooled":
        kept, removed = acs.grubbs(pooled)
        keep_sets = []
        for e in est:
            keep_sets.append(np.array([x for x in e[kind] if not np.any(np.isclose(x, removed))]))
    elif grubbs == "per rat":
        keep_sets = [acs.grubbs(e[kind])[0] if len(e[kind]) > 2 else e[kind] for e in est]
        removed = np.array([0] * (len(pooled) - sum(len(k) for k in keep_sets)))
    else:
        keep_sets = [e[kind] for e in est]
        removed = np.array([])
    allkept = np.concatenate(keep_sets)
    per_rat = np.array([k.mean() for k in keep_sets if len(k)])
    return {"estimates": len(pooled), "removed": len(removed),
            "(a) median of rat means": np.median(per_rat), "(b) median of all estimates": np.median(allkept),
            "rat means min": per_rat.min(), "rat means max": per_rat.max(),
            "IQR of all (b)": np.subtract(*np.percentile(allkept, [75, 25])), "SD of rat means": per_rat.std(ddof=1)}

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The electric field is computed from the stored 16-channel stream of the 20-minute files, decimated to 1.2 kHz, with a +/- 1 Hz Butterworth band at 40 Hz (the paper names no filter). The 4 x 4 layout of the channels is assumed (row by row), as neither the files nor the paper give it. The stimulation period is found from the artifact, as the files have no epochs. How the two orthogonal differences are paired for the vector addition is not stated; three pairings are shown. The spike sorting, timed on one rat, uses SpyKING CIRCUS 2 in place of SpyKING CIRCUS 1, after a comb filter in place of ARtACS.

What was found about the dataset.

  • The 1-minute files hold only the stimulation command channel ("S1r 1", the stream uS1r), one series per trial: the multi-unit recordings, the LFP and the stimulation monitor that the paper analysed are not there. The command gives each trial's frequency and amplitude.
  • The command channel is labelled volts with a conversion of 1e-6, but its values are the current in uA; the 20-minute streams are labelled the same way, but their values are volts.
  • The 20-minute files hold one 16-channel stream at 12207 Hz that is neither low-passed at 300 Hz nor notch-filtered, and carries spikes. No sorted units, trial or epoch tables, stimulation times or stimulation waveform are stored.
  • The electrodes table has no positions, so the 4 x 4 layout of the array is not documented.
  • The 1-minute rats have 15 to 23 trials (the paper says 20), three rats lack some of the 16 combinations, and one trial at 100 Hz and 20 uA is outside the paper's design.
  • The 20-minute subjects are 6 male and 7 female; the paper says 7 male and 6 female.
  • The sham recording is not in the dandiset.
