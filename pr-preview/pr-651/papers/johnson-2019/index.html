Johnson et al. 2019 · Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience

Spectral Imprints of Working Memory for Everyday Associations in the Frontoparietal Network

doi:10.3389/fnsys.2018.00065 · paper key johnson-2019

Datasets

  • CRCNS:fcx-3

In this intracranial dataset the paper's behavioural results held up: identity trials were slower than spatial and temporal ones, and accuracy did not differ by condition. Its per-subject power differences after the test cue held in part, with similar subject counts but smaller shares of electrodes, and sat at low frequencies. The frontoparietal phase-slope findings did not reproduce: with the null the Methods describe, 78 of 1135 pairs were directional in delta-theta, about what trial-shuffled data give, and the paper's counts appear only with the deposited code's null, which flags shuffled data as well. The absence of ERP differences failed in one subject, under a neighbour rule the paper does not state.

  • ◐Partially reproduced The analysed data comprise 192 bipolar virtual electrodes in seven subjects, 4 to 54 (mean 19) in PFC and 2 to 16 (mean 8) in parietal cortex per subject, as listed per subject in Table 1, forming 1129 same-hemisphere PFC-parietal pairs.

  • ✓Reproduced All subjects were proficient at the task (accuracy 0.63 to 0.98, chance 0.5), and accuracy did not differ between conditions.
  • ✓Reproduced Response times differed by condition, being longer on identity than on spatial and temporal trials, with no difference between spatial and temporal trials.
  • ?Not reproduced ERPs over the processing interval did not differ between identity and spatial or temporal trials in any subject.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In the identity against spatial contrast of processing-interval power, 5 of 7 subjects showed spatial > identity effects (54 +- 22% of electrodes per subject) and 2 of 7 showed identity > spatial effects (36 +- 12%).
  • ◐Partially reproduced In the identity against temporal contrast of processing-interval power, 4 of 7 subjects showed temporal > identity effects (68 +- 30% of electrodes per subject) and 2 of 7 showed identity > temporal effects (37 +- 10%).
  • ✓Reproduced The subjects with identity > spatial or temporal power effects were the same subjects who had both longer RTs and lower accuracy on identity trials.
  • ✓Reproduced Significant processing-interval power effects were most prominent at low frequencies, in distinct delta-theta (2 to 7 Hz) and alpha-beta (9 to 24 Hz) ranges, whatever the contrast.
  • ?Not reproduced During maintenance, PFC-parietal connectivity was unidirectional, parietal to PFC, in both the delta-theta (z < -40.03 +- 8.73) and alpha-beta (z < -30.37 +- 2.60) ranges.
  • ?Not reproduced After the test cue, delta-theta directionality shifted from a unidirectional parietal-led network to a bidirectional frontoparietal one, with net PFC-led connectivity above zero in 6 of 7 subjects during processing (z > 6.01 +- 6.42).

  • ?Not reproduced During processing, 1008 pairs were significantly directional in the delta-theta range and 984 in the alpha-beta range.
  • ?Not reproduced Time-resolved delta-theta PSI differed between conditions in sustained epochs at 0 to 120 ms and 470 to 610 ms (identity against spatial) and at 0 to 170 ms and 660 to 860 ms (identity against temporal) after the test cue, the spatial late epoch ending about 50 ms before the temporal one begins.
  • ?Not reproduced In identity trials the PFC led parietal sites early in processing (z > 2.62 and z > 2.35 in the two contrasts' early epochs) and the parietal cortex led later (z < -3.04 and z < -3.57).
