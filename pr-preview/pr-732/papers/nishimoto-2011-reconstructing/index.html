  • fig2de-prediction-accuracy

    Averaged over subjects and voxels in V1, V2, V3, V3A and V3B, the prediction accuracy on the test data is 0.24 for the static model, 0.39 for the nondirectional motion-energy model and 0.40 for the directional motion-energy model, and the differences are significant (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p < 0.0001).

    The averaged accuracy across subjects and voxels in early visual areas (V1, V2, V3, V3A, and V3B) was 0.24, 0.39, and 0.40 for the static, nondirectional, and directional encoding models, respectively ... difference in prediction accuracy was significant (p < 0.0001, Wilcoxon signed-rank test)

    Results, paragraph 6; Figures 2D and 2E with legend

    How it will be judged

    The three models are built and fit as in Common methods (Motion-energy features, Static and nondirectional models, Encoding model fit) and scored as in Prediction accuracy. Voxels are those of the stimulated portions of V1, V2, V3, V3A and V3B (about 4,500, Voxel Selection paragraph 1), taken as the voxels of the data set's masks for these areas; the 2,000-voxel selection is not used, as the paper applies it to reconstruction and identification only. Open, and able to change the outcome, how subjects are combined, (i) all voxels pooled, (ii) the mean of the three subject means. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is paired over voxels; the paper does not say which pairs "this difference" covers, so the two comparisons the Figure 2 legend names (directional against static, directional against nondirectional) are both run. Reproduced when, under one reading, the three means are within tolerance, their order is static < nondirectional < directional, and both tests give p < 0.0001. Partially reproduced when the order and tests hold but a mean falls outside its tolerance.

  • fig2e-directional-gain-small

    The directional model predicts better than the nondirectional model, but the difference is small, which suggests that BOLD signals carry spatially localized but mostly nondirectional motion information.

    Note that the difference in prediction accuracy between the directional and nondirectional motion models, though significant, was small

    Results, paragraph 6; Figure 2E with legend; Figure S1A

    How it will be judged

    From the fits of fig2de-prediction-accuracy, under the same readings. Reproduced when the directional model's mean accuracy exceeds the nondirectional model's, the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives p < 0.0001, and the difference is less than a tenth of the gain of the nondirectional model over the static model (the paper's values give 0.01 against 0.15; the bound of a tenth is set here, as the paper gives no number for small).

  • figs1a-motion-better-every-area

    In each of the five visual areas and each of the three subjects, both motion models predict better than the static model, and the best predictions are in V1 for all three subjects (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p < 0.0001).

    The directional and non-directional motion models perform better than the static model in every case. The best overall predictions for all three subjects (p < 0.0001, Wilcoxon rank-sum test) are obtained in area V1.

    Figure S1A legend

    How it will be judged

    From the fits of fig2de-prediction-accuracy, mean accuracy per model, area and subject (15 area and subject cells). Open, what V1 is compared with in the rank-sum test. Readings, (a) V1 voxels against the voxels of each other area in turn; (b) V1 voxels against the voxels of the other four areas pooled. Also open, which model's accuracy "best overall predictions" refers to. Readings, (i) the directional model, the model the main text calls best; (ii) each of the three models. Reproduced when both motion models beat the static model in all 15 cells and, under one reading, V1 has the highest mean with p < 0.0001 in all three subjects. Partially reproduced when the motion models beat the static model in at least 12 of 15 cells or V1 is best in two subjects.

  • fig2fgh-retinotopic-receptive-fields

    Voxel receptive fields estimated from the motion-energy model are spatially localized and retinotopically organized; their angle maps are similar to those from conventional multifocal mapping, and their eccentricity maps are similar except in the far periphery, where the multifocal mapping stimulus was coarse.

    The spatial receptive fields of individual voxels were spatially localized (Figures 2F and 2G, left) and were organized retinotopically (Figures 2H and 2I)

    Results, paragraph 7; Figures 2F to 2I with legends

    How it will be judged

    Receptive fields from the directional model as in Selectivity Estimation (Supplemental Experimental Procedures) - dynamic Gaussian white noise in each cell of a 17 x 17 grid, predicted response minus the response to uniform gray, and a 2D Gaussian fit whose center gives angle and eccentricity; voxels with prediction accuracy p > 0.01 left out. The paper compares the maps with multifocal mapping data that the data set does not hold and the paper does not deposit. Open, the p-value of prediction accuracy. Readings, (a) the test of a correlation on the 486 test samples; (b) the same on the held-out 10% of the training data. Without the mapping data the claim is judged on the retinotopy the dataset's ROI masks imply. Reproduced when, in each subject, more than half of the retained voxels have a Gaussian fit inside the 20 degree display, the centers of left-hemisphere V1 voxels lie mostly (over 75%) in the right visual field and the reverse for the right hemisphere, and eccentricity rises along V1 from the occipital pole forward (positive correlation, p < 0.05). The thresholds are set here, since the paper reports the maps visually. With the multifocal maps, a circular correlation of angle and a correlation of eccentricity are reported as well.

  • fig2j-speed-eccentricity

    Optimal speed (peak temporal frequency over peak spatial frequency) correlates positively with eccentricity, so peripheral voxels prefer higher speeds, in V1, V2 and V3 of all three subjects (t test for correlation coefficient, p < 0.0001).

    Projecting the optimal speed of the voxels onto a flattened map of the cortical surface (Figure 2J) revealed a significant positive correlation between eccentricity and optimal speed ... This pattern was observed in areas V1, V2, and V3 and for all three subjects (p < 0.0001, t test for correlation coefficient

    Results, paragraph 8; Figure 2J; Figure S1B legend

    How it will be judged

    Eccentricity from the receptive field fit of fig2fgh-retinotopic-receptive-fields. Spatial and temporal frequency tuning from the directional model's predicted responses to full-field drifting gratings at the basis set's directions, spatial and temporal frequencies, averaged over directions, minus the response to uniform gray (Selectivity Estimation). Spatial frequency in cycles per degree (cycles per image over 20 degrees). Voxels with prediction accuracy p > 0.01, or with optimal spatial frequency 0, left out (Figure S1B legend); the p-value readings are those of fig2fgh-retinotopic-receptive-fields. Open, how the peaks are taken. Readings, (a) the spatial and temporal frequency of the maximum of the joint tuning map; (b) the maximum of each marginal tuning curve. The Pearson correlation of eccentricity with optimal speed is taken over voxels per area and subject (the 2 degree bins of Figure S1B are for display). Reproduced when, under one reading, all nine area and subject correlations are positive with p < 0.0001. Partially reproduced when all nine are positive and at least six reach p < 0.0001.

  • fig3ab-identification-s1

    For subject S1 the motion-energy model identifies the test second that evoked an observed BOLD pattern within one volume (1 s) 95% of the time (464 of 486 volumes), far above chance (under 1%, 3 of 486).

    Our motion-energy encoding model could identify the specific movie stimulus that evoked an observed BOLD signal 95% of the time (464 of 486 volumes) within 6 one volume (1 s; subject S1; Figures 3A and 3B)

    Results, paragraph 9; Figures 3A and 3B with legends

    How it will be judged

    Voxel selection and the Likelihood of Common methods; for each observed test volume, the log-likelihood under the predicted signal of each of the 486 test seconds, and the second of maximum likelihood is chosen (Figure 3A legend); correct when it lies within one volume of the true second. Open, which encoding model gives the predictions. Readings, (a) the directional model with four delays; (b) the single 4 s delay refit used for reconstruction. Also open, which 486 samples are used, as the paper discards the first 6 s of each 1 min test chunk; the author takes the dataset's test samples that match this. Also open, and able to change the outcome, whether the observed test signal is the mean of the ten repeats or each repeat. Readings, (i) the repeat-averaged signal; (ii) each repeat identified separately, the count scaled to 486. Reproduced when, under one reading, S1's count is within tolerance (at least 418 of 486); the chance level of 3 of 486 is reported.

  • fig3c-identification-large-set

    Identification accuracy within one volume stays above 75% for all three subjects even when the candidate set holds 1,000,000 clips drawn at random from the internet.

    Identification accuracy (within 6 one volume) was >75% for all three subjects even when the set of possible natural movie clips included 1,000,000 separate clips chosen at random from the internet

    Results, paragraph 9; Figure 3C with legend

    How it will be judged

    As fig3ab-identification-s1, for each subject, with the candidate set enlarged by 1 s clips from a natural movie database not used in the experiment. The paper gives neither the database nor where it is; the author decides whether any public collection of natural movies can stand in, as an assumption. Reproduced when, at a set of 1,000,000 clips, each subject is above 75% correct within one volume.

  • fig4-reconstructions

    Bayesian decoding with a sampled prior of about 18,000,000 s of internet movies reconstructs the viewed movies; the maximum a posteriori (MAP) clip is good when the prior holds similar clips and poor otherwise, and the average of the 100 highest posterior clips (AHP) captures the spatiotemporal structure even of unique clips.

    When the sampled natural movie prior contained clips similar to the viewed clip, the MAP reconstructions were good ... However, when the prior contained no clips similar to the viewed clip, the reconstructions are poor ... The AHP reconstruction captures the spatiotemporal structure within a viewed clip even when it is completely unique

    Results, paragraphs 10 and 11; Figure 4A to 4C with legend; Figure S2

    How it will be judged

    The paper shows this with example frames only, and judges it by the measure of fig4d-reconstruction-accuracy, on which this claim is judged. It needs the sampled prior, which the data set does not hold and the paper does not deposit.

  • fig4d-reconstruction-accuracy

    For all three subjects both MAP and AHP reconstructions correlate with the viewed movies in motion-energy space above chance (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p < 0.0001), and AHP reconstructions are better than MAP (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p < 0.0001), though still crude (motion-energy correlation about 0.3).

    In both cases, reconstruction accuracy was significantly higher than chance (p < 0.0001, Wilcoxon rank-sum test; see Supplemental Experimental Procedures). Furthermore, AHP reconstructions were significantly better than MAP reconstructions (p < 0.0001, Wilcoxon signed-rank test).

    Results, paragraph 12; Figure 4D with legend; Supplemental Experimental Procedures, Evaluating the Accuracy of Reconstructions

    How it will be judged

    Posterior over the sampled prior from the Likelihood of Common methods with the single 4 s delay model, using BOLD 4 s after each 1 s clip; the MAP is the clip of highest posterior; the AHP is the average of the 100 highest, each clip's pixels scaled to unit s.d. before averaging and the average rescaled so that its mean and s.d. equal those of the average of the top 100 clips (Figure S2D legend), with the five seconds after a chosen clip of the same movie excluded from selection (Additional Notes on Reconstruction). Accuracy per 1 s clip is the correlation of motion-energy vectors (filter bank up to downsampling, no hemodynamics) of the viewed and reconstructed clip. Chance is the correlation with clips drawn at random from the prior; the dashed line is its 99th percentile. Open, and able to change the rank-sum p-value, how many random clips form the null distribution; reading, one per test clip, with ten per test clip as a variant. Open, which value "about 0.3" describes. Readings, (a) the AHP mean in each subject; (b) the AHP mean over the three subjects. Reproduced when, in each subject, both tests give p < 0.0001 with AHP above MAP, and under one reading the values are within tolerance. The prior is not in the data set and has no given location; the author decides whether a stand-in prior is defensible, as an assumption, or blocks the claim.

  • discussion-color-no-gain

    In preliminary work, encoding models with explicit color information did not predict or identify better than models using luminance alone.

    However, we found that color information did not improve the accuracy of predictions or identification beyond what could be achieved with models that include only luminance information.

    Discussion, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged

    The paper shows no data and does not describe its color models. Reading, the directional model of fig2de-prediction-accuracy with the same filter bank applied as well to the A* and B* channels of CIE L*A*B*, fit and scored the same way. A claim of absence, for which the paper names no test; the test is set here, the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over voxels of fig2de-prediction-accuracy at p < 0.0001. Reproduced when the color model's mean accuracy is not higher by that test; the difference is reported either way, and the outcome rests on the reading of the color model. The claim covers identification as well; the color model's identification count, run as in fig3ab-identification-s1, is reported beside the luminance model's, but the paper gives no test for it, so it does not decide the outcome.
