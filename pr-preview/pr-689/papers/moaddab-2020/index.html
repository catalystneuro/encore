Moaddab et al. 2020 · Scientific Reports

Early adolescent adversity alters periaqueductal gray/dorsal raphe threat responding in ad

doi:10.1038/s41598-020-74457-3 · paper key moaddab-2020

Datasets

  • CRCNS:pag-2

Most of the paper's main findings held up, one did not, and the weight-gain result could not be tested. EAA rats had more cue-responsive vlPAG neurons (133/203 against 80/151), inflated cue responses, and stronger fear output signaling in cue-inhibited neurons. Reduced threat probability signaling and decoupled firing in cue-excited neurons held only in part, the first at p = 0.055 and the second only with the authors' script normalization. The link between firing and fear output in cue-inhibited neurons did not reproduce, likely because the paper's figure used a different cue window than its legend gives. Body weights and pre-surgery learning sessions are absent from the dataset, and the behavioral and baseline controls held.

  • ✓Reproduced During recording sessions both groups showed high fear to danger, less to uncertainty and least to safety, with a main effect of cue, no group by cue interaction, and each cue pair differing in each group.

  • ✓Reproduced A higher proportion of EAA neurons than Control neurons were cue-responsive (133/203, 65.5%, against 80/151, 53.0%).
  • ✓Reproduced Baseline firing rates did not differ between Control and EAA neurons of any functional type, while across groups baseline firing differed by functional type and was lowest for cue-excited neurons.

  • ✓Reproduced In the first 1 s of the cue, EAA cue-excited neurons fired more than Control neurons (main effect of group), with a significant group difference to safety; both groups still discriminated danger from uncertainty and uncertainty from safety.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Control and EAA cue-inhibited neurons differed in the temporal pattern of their cue firing (a group by cue by bin interaction that held with bregma level as a factor). Control neurons decreased firing equally to danger and uncertainty with little change to safety, sustained over the cue; EAA neurons decreased most to danger, less to uncertainty, mostly in the first half of the cue.
  • ✓Reproduced In the first 5 s of the cue, EAA cue-inhibited neurons decreased firing more than Control neurons (main effect of group), with a greater decrease to danger; the group difference to uncertainty fell short of the corrected level.
  • ✓Reproduced In the first half of the cue, EAA cue-inhibited neurons discriminated danger from uncertainty, while Control cue-inhibited neurons did not.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Control cue-excited neurons signaled threat probability (positive betas from cue onset, rising over the cue) with fear output betas near zero; EAA cue-excited neurons showed lower threat probability betas and similar fear output betas, a group by regressor interaction.
  • ✓Reproduced The threat tuning curve of cue-excited neurons peaked at an uncertainty assignment of 0.500 in both groups; the EAA curve had the same shape but was lower across all assignments (main effect of assignment and of group, no interaction).
