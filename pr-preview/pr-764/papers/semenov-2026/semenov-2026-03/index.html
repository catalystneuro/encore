    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Field strengths are matched in order to the generator volts the files give (25 V as 43 V/cm, 50 as 150, 70 as 230, 100 as 360, 150 as 570, 200 as 780, 250 as 1000, 300 as 1200, 350 as 1400), an assumption. The paper names nine field strengths for its 1-us escalations and the files nine amplitudes, but the two are not proportional, and below electroporation the data fit a proportional response about as well on the volts as on the paper's V/cm. The first step supports the matching: from 25 to 50 V the mean pole |dF| triples (FluoVolt 3.05, Di-8-ANEPPS 2.90), against 3.49 for the paper's 43 to 150 V/cm and 2.00 for proportional volts. A variant with the field proportional to the volts (4 V/cm per V) is shown for every claim that uses the field strength as a number.
    • Background is subtracted from ROIs 30 to 36 pixels (8 to 10 um) outside the membrane, 20 degrees wide, near each pole (the far reading), an assumption. The paper does not state a background method for the laser images; its Methods follow reference [20] ('Most methods were identical to those described in detail recently [20]'), which places background ROIs 'near the poles outside of the cell' with no distance. The data check shows the membrane's glow is still a third of its excess 2 um out and a few percent 8 um out. The near (2 to 4 um) and no-background readings are shown and change dF by up to a factor of three. After verification round 1 every claim also shows a sweep of 6-pixel bands from 8 to 44 pixels out and a per-pole (local) background.
    • The perimeter trace (6 pixels wide, Methods 2.3) is found automatically as the peak of intensity on 360 rays of each set's baseline image, with polar angles from the cell centre; the paper drew it by hand in MetaMorph and resampled by perimeter length. Sets whose outline radius departs from the cell's median by more than 15% are traced again within 8 pixels of the cell's median centre and 4 pixels of its median radius; this re-traced FluoVolt c2 at 300 V (outline on a neighbouring cell) and Di-8-ANEPPS c7 at 300 and 350 V (outline on a body inside the cell), found in the outline overlays added after verification round 1.
    • Frames without a laser flash, which the files do not flag (frame 0 of 137 of 141 sets and 290 others), are left out of every average; the paper does not mention them. Keeping them is shown as a variant.
    • The field direction is not stored. The cathode-facing pole is placed where dF is positive (Methods 2.3, emission increased in the cathode-facing hemisphere), on one axis for all sets estimated from the 50 to 100 V sets (per-set median deviation 1.6 degrees).
    • All eight Di-8-ANEPPS escalation cells (c7 to c14) are used where the paper reports n = 5 without naming them; the criterion is also run on each of the 56 five-cell subsets.
    • Di-8-ANEPPS c13 has two 150 V sets; the first, in escalation order, is used.

    Should the criterion of fig2a-fluovolt-stable allow for the number of tests it makes? Each start/end reading runs 24 paired t-tests at p < 0.05 with n = 4 (6 pulse comparisons at 1000 to 1400 V/cm and 18 baseline comparisons), and the claim fails if any one of them is significant. With no change at all, the chance that at least one of 24 independent tests falls below 0.05 is about 70% (1 - 0.95^24), so the criterion fails a stable recording more often than not. A reading that corrects for the number of tests (for example Bonferroni or Holm over the 24), or that asks for a change larger than some size in % dF, would test what the paper says.
