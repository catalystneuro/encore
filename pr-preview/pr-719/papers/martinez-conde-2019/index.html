  • neuron-counts

    Of 118 V1 neurons recorded from two monkeys (102 from monkey J, 16 from monkey A), 60 were discarded for technical problems and 58 analysed; 7 were tested in all three conditions, 11 in two and 40 in one, giving 25 neurons for Square and Edge, 30 for Star and 28 for Corners.

    Thus, we present data from 58 neurons total. ... we were able to test all three conditions in 7 neurons, two conditions in 11 neurons, and a single condition in 40 neurons out of the 58.

    Results, paragraphs 1 and 3 to 5

    How it will be judged

    Counts of neurons are exact, so the tolerance is zero. Neurons are identified by their neuron code across conditions. Reproduced when every count matches; partially reproduced when the per-condition counts match but the overlap counts do not, or the reverse. The 118 recorded and 102 and 16 per monkey are reported where the data say, but the 60 discarded neurons are not expected in a dataset of analysed neurons.

  • spikes-per-condition

    Each experimental condition required at least 10,000 spikes, and the mean number of spikes per condition was 35,998 (STD 25,981).

    The minimum number of spikes required to complete each experimental condition was 10,000 (the mean and STD of number of spikes per condition was 35,998 ± 25,981).

    Methods, Guided-viewing task, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    The number of spikes in a neuron and condition is the total of its spike count map (Fig 3B legend). Reproduced when every neuron and condition has at least 10,000 spikes and the mean and STD are within tolerance (10% of each). Open is whether the STD is the sample or population STD; both are reported (the difference is negligible at these n). Also open is which spikes are counted, since the paper does not say; readings (a) the total of the spike count map; (b) all spikes recorded in the condition, including those with gaze off the map or outside fixation. If the dataset holds only the analysed neurons and the paper's count covers the same set, the comparison is direct; otherwise the difference in set is reported.

  • s4-baseline-no-difference

    Absolute baseline activity did not differ significantly across the three visual stimuli (Square and Edge, Star, Corners).

    Importantly, we found no significant differences in absolute baseline activity across the different visual stimuli presented ( S4 Fig ).

    Results, Strength of neuronal responses to corner-folds varies parametrically with fold angle, paragraph 1; S4 Fig

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. Absolute baseline per neuron and condition is the mean of the spike rate map in the four (Square and Edge, Star) or two (Corners) 16 x 16 DU 0% luminance squares (Methods, Visual stimuli). The paper does not name the test or threshold. Readings, each at alpha 0.05 (the alpha the paper states for Fig 6); (a) pairwise Wilcoxon rank sum tests between the three conditions corrected with Benjamini and Hochberg, the test of Fig 4B; (b) a Kruskal-Wallis test across the three conditions; (c) a one-way ANOVA. Reproduced when the readings find no difference; not reproduced when they find one. If they disagree, the author adopts reading (a) as the paper's own test elsewhere, as an assumption. The medians and quartiles of each condition (the S4 Fig box plots) are reported either way.

  • fig4a-above-baseline

    For every fold (P180, S90, S52, S40), most neurons' midpoint fold responses lie above their absolute baseline activity, and the mass center of each fold's points lies above the line of unit slope, with the mass center's distance from that line increasing as the fold angle decreases.

    For all folds, most data points and all mass centers fall above the line, indicating a predominance of elevation of midpoint fold response above absolute baseline activity.

    Figure 4A legend; Results, Strength of neuronal responses, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Per neuron and fold, midpoint fold response against absolute baseline (Methods; Fig 4A legend), with P180 and S90 from Square and Edge (N = 25), S52 from Star (N = 30), S40 from Corners (N = 28). Reproduced when, for each fold, more than half of the neurons have response above baseline, the mass center lies above the unit line, and the vertical distance of the mass center from the line is ordered P180 < S90 < S52 < S40. The legend says the distance indicates the average spike rate difference but the axes are logarithmic, so the mass center is open; readings (a) the mean of the raw values, whose vertical distance is the mean spike rate difference; (b) the mean of the log values, whose distance is a log ratio. The Corners midpoint is open as in the opening comment.

  • fig4b-spike-rate-difference

    The mean spike rate difference at the fold midpoint decreases with increasing fold angle, from S40 (25.60 spikes/s) through S52 (18.10) and S90 (10.69) to P180 (4.87).

    The distances in Fig 4B show the clear trend of diminished spike rate difference with increasing corner angle.

    Figure 4B; Table 1, Midpoint column

    How it will be judged

    Values are mean over neurons of the midpoint spike rate difference (Table 1, mean ± SEM); the tolerance is the paper's SEM. Reproduced when the four means are ordered S40 > S52 > S90 > P180 and each is within tolerance; partially reproduced when the order holds but a value is outside tolerance. The Corners midpoint is open; readings (a) the average of the two S40 fold midpoints, as for Square and Star; (b) the single S40 fold midpoint at the ROI centre. Whether the spike rate map is the smoothed one is fixed by the Fig 3B legend (smoothed after dividing by eye time); the unsmoothed map is shown as a variant.

  • fig4b-pairwise-significance

    The midpoint spike rate difference for S40 is significantly larger than for S90 and for P180, and that for S52 is significantly larger than for P180.

    The spike rate difference for S40° was significantly larger than that for fold S90° and P180°, and the spike rate difference for S52° was significantly larger than that for P180°.

    Results, Strength of neuronal responses, paragraph 1; Figure 4B legend

    How it will be judged

    Wilcoxon rank sum tests on per-neuron midpoint spike rate differences, corrected with Benjamini and Hochberg, at p < 0.05 (Fig 4B legend). Reproduced when the three named pairs are significant in the stated direction. The family of comparisons is open (exact p-values are in S1 Table, which is missing); readings (a) all six pairs of the four folds; (b) the three pairs named. The other pairs (S40 against S52, S52 against S90, S90 against P180) are reported; the text does not claim them either way. The Corners midpoint is open as in fig4b-spike-rate-difference. The rank sum test is applied to P180 against S90 though the neurons are the same, as the paper writes it.

  • fig5-average-maps

    The population-average spike rate difference maps show neural activity increasing as the corner angle decreases.

    The maps also show that neural activity increased with decreasing corner angles.

    Figure 5; Results, Strength of neuronal responses, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    Average over neurons of the spike rate difference maps, aligned in each condition's ROI (Methods, Spike rate and spike rate difference map; Fig 5 legend). The paper gives no measure for the maps beyond the midpoints marked by arrows; the reading taken is the value of the average map in the 8 x 8 DU midpoint squares of each fold, and as a variant the mean of the average map over the ten fold blocks of the Table 1 method. Both are assumptions; since the map average is linear, the midpoint reading gives the means of fig4b-spike-rate-difference and is not an independent test. Reproduced when the order is S40 > S52 > S90 > P180 under the midpoint reading.

The Edge midpoint is the 8 x 8 DU square at 50% luminance on the Edge. Reproduced when, under one reading, all five values are within tolerance; partially reproduced when at least three are.

  • fig6-midpoint-representative

    For each fold and the Edge, the spike rate difference at the midpoint does not differ significantly from the mean or the median spike rate difference along the fold or Edge, so the midpoint response represents the whole fold.

    We found that the spike rate difference at the corner-fold midpoint was not significantly different from the mean and median spike rate difference along the corner-fold or Edge (Wilcoxon rank sum test, α = 0.05; Fig 6 )

    Results, Responses averaged along the entire fold are comparable to those at fold midpoint, paragraph 1; Figure 6

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence, judged on the paper's test. For each of Edge, P180, S90, S52 and S40, a Wilcoxon rank sum test of the per-neuron midpoint spike rate differences against the per-neuron means of the ten blocks, and another against the per-neuron medians, at alpha 0.05 with no correction (none is stated). Reproduced when none of the ten tests is significant; not reproduced when any is. The median difference and p-value of each are reported. Block and midpoint readings are those of the Table 1 claims; a signed rank test (the values are paired) is shown as a variant.

  • fig7a-rms-contrast-fit

    Local RMS contrast along the Edge rises from 0.61 to 0.943 with luminance and is fit by an exponential model, f(l) = -0.34 exp(-0.05 l) + 0.95, with R2 = 0.9983.

    This resulted in smooth contrasts ranging from 0.61 to 0.943. In order to acquire neural responses to the Edge at equally spaced RMS contrast intervals, we fit the RMS contrast with an exponential model ( R 2 = 0.9983; Fig 7A ).

    Results, Neuron responses to corner-folds vs. different contrasts along edge, paragraph 1; Figure 7A

    How it will be judged

    Computed from the Square and Edge stimulus image. Local RMS contrast in a 16 x 16 DU square centred on the Edge, moved from 0% to 100% luminance in 1.5% steps (Results, paragraph 1 of the section), fit with f(l) = a exp(-b l) + c by Levenberg-Marquardt with least absolute residuals (Methods, Edge RMS contrast model). The R2 tolerance is set so the fit must exceed 0.9966, since 10% of a value near 1 is not informative. The definition of RMS contrast is open (reference [21] is not in the text read); readings (a) the standard deviation of pixel luminance divided by its mean; (b) the standard deviation of luminance normalised to 0 to 1. Reproduced when, under one reading, all values are within tolerance.

  • fig7c-fold-edge-equivalence

    Neural responses to P180 are roughly equivalent to those to a 50% contrast Edge, while responses to S40 are close to those to a 90% contrast Edge, so sharper corners produce responses equivalent to higher contrasts along a straight edge.

    Fig 7C shows that neural responses to P180° are roughly equivalent to neural responses to a 50% contrast Edge, while neural responses to S40° are close to neural responses to a 90% contrast Edge.

    Results, Neuron responses to corner-folds vs. different contrasts along edge, paragraph 3; Figure 7C

    How it will be judged

    Edge responses are the mean spike rate difference in a 16 x 16 DU square centred on the Edge at the luminance points that correspond, by the inverse of the exponential fit, to ten RMS contrasts from 0.610 to 0.943 in steps of 0.037 (Results, paragraph 1 of the section; the paper's fitted parameters, with a refit as variant). Fold responses are the midpoint means of fig4b-spike-rate-difference. The scale of "contrast" in percent is open; readings (a) Edge luminance level in percent, at the 10% luminance intervals of Fig 7B; (b) RMS contrast times 100, at the ten RMS levels (S2 Table is described by RMS contrast, but 50 lies below its range, so under (b) P180 is matched when it is nearest the lowest level). Reproduced when, under one reading, the Edge level whose mean response is nearest the P180 mean lies within one step of 50% and that nearest the S40 mean within one step of 90%. The one-step margin for "roughly equivalent" and "close", and the rule for P180 under (b), are assumptions; the paper gives no margin.

  • fig7c-fold-edge-significance

    Responses to P180 are significantly lower than responses to a 94% contrast Edge, and responses to S40 are significantly higher than responses to an Edge of less than 68% contrast.

    In addition, the neural responses to P180° are significantly lower than the responses to a 94% contrast Edge, and the neural responses to S40° are significantly higher than the responses to an Edge of less than 68% contrast (two sample t -test corrected; S2 Table ).

    Results, Neuron responses to corner-folds vs. different contrasts along edge, paragraph 3; S2 Table

    How it will be judged

    Two-sample t-tests of per-neuron fold midpoint spike rate differences against per-neuron Edge responses at each of the ten RMS contrast levels (S2 Table legend), as the paper writes it though P180 and Edge come from the same neurons. S2 Table is missing, so the correction, its family and the alpha are open; readings (a) Benjamini and Hochberg, as in Fig 4B, over all fold and contrast pairs (40 tests); (b) the same over the ten levels per fold; (c) Bonferroni over each of these families; alpha 0.05 in all, the paper's alpha elsewhere. Reproduced when, under one reading, P180 is significantly lower than the Edge at 0.943 and S40 is significantly higher than the Edge at 0.610 and 0.647 (the levels below 0.68). Which other levels are significant, including 0.684, is reported.

  • fig7d-nonlinear

    The relationship between corner angle and V1 spike rate difference is parametric but apparently not linear; the paper says it cannot be certain of this, given the few angles tested.

    In contrast with the previous perceptual data, however, we did not find a linear relationship between corner sharpness and the strength of the neural response.

    Figure 7D; Discussion, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    The paper reports no test, and itself says it cannot be certain of the non-linearity (Discussion, paragraph 5), so the measure is open. Reading taken, a straight line fitted by least squares to the four midpoint means against fold angle (40, 52, 90, 180 degrees), judged non-linear when at least one mean lies off the line by more than its SEM (this rule is an assumption, not the paper's); variant, the slopes between successive angles (40 to 52, 52 to 90, 90 to 180) compared with each other, judged non-linear when they differ by more than their propagated SEMs. Reproduced when the monotonic order of fig4b-spike-rate-difference holds and the reading finds the relation non-linear; the variant is reported.

    Figure 7D; Results, Center-surround model vs. V1 neuron responses, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged
