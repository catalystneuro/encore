  • ⊘4 blocked

The central result, identifying which new natural image a person saw from fMRI activity with a receptive-field model, holds up for both subjects (90.8% and 72.5% correct), but only under a field-of-view rule the paper does not describe. The other main findings could not be tested: single-trial identification needs a refit of every voxel's time series too large for the run, and the larger image sets and later sessions are not in the dataset. The example voxel of Figure 2 reproduces in part. Among minor claims, voxel counts, the fall in predictive power from V1 to V3 and errors on similar images reproduce; the band-pass V1 tuning, a classification control and the V1 share of selected voxels do not.

    For this subject, 92% (110/120) of the images were identified correctly (subject S1), whereas chance performance is just 0.8% (1/120)

    Main text, paragraph 6 ("Identification performance for one subject is illustrated in Fig. 3"); Figure 3 and its legend; Figure 4a

    Under O1 (a) with O5 (a), 109 of 120 S1 images are identified (90.8%, paper 110 of 120), binomial p against chance 1/120 of 2.5e-212; O1 (b) with O5 (a) gives 110 (91.7%). With the final model over the best initial model's field of view (O5 b) it is 91 of 120 (75.8%) under both readings of O1, outside tolerance, and the best initial models alone give 96. Ten bootstrap fits give 90 under O5 (b). The value holds under the O5 (a) readings, as the criterion allows.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Each model is fitted once by gradient descent with early stopping; the paper's final parameters are the mean of 100 bootstrap fits (SM 6, "Estimation of variance"), not run for time. Ten bootstrap fits, run as a variant under O5 (b), change the result by one image (S1) and three (S2).
    • One stopping set (20% of the 1,750 responses) is drawn per fit and shared by the voxels fitted together, a standard choice for a random draw (SM 6 draws one per fit).
    • The initial fields of view are 10.0 and 5.12 degrees, from the paper's resolution list min(500, round(2^(9 - x/8))) (SM 6); the paper states 10.1 and 5.2 degrees (SM 5), which the list does not give.
    • The Gaussian SD of each wavelet follows from a 1-octave spatial frequency bandwidth (full width at half maximum), which gives an orientation bandwidth of 37 degrees where SM 6 states 41 (O3, standard choice). Wavelet centres at the grid cell centres (O3).
    • Stimulus values are taken as linear in luminance (O4); the goggles' response is not given.
    • Stimuli are resampled by area averaging; SM 6 does not say how.
    • An isotropic Gaussian with a constant offset is fitted to each initial model's envelope at 32 x 32; SM 5 says only "an isotropic two-dimensional Gaussian".
    • The significance of predictive power is by 1,000 bootstrap resamples of the 120 image pairs (O2, standard choice).
    • The responses are the data set's estimated repeated-trial responses, z-scored per voxel by the data set; the paper standardized per session (SM 3). Voxels with NaN responses are left out (S1 345, S2 214 of V1 to V3).
    • The p-values that pick the O1 (a) candidate voxels use all 120 identification images, the one being identified included, where P5 leaves that image out; O1 (b), which has no such step, gives the same S1 count and one image fewer for S2.
    • The stopping rule is read as stopping at the first iteration whose stopping-set error does not decrease, so a fit whose first step raises that error keeps the zero kernel and the voxel has no model (about a third of the initial fits, 29% to 38% across the six).

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Methods not availableThe final model's field of view is "specifically tailored to the estimated RF location" (SM 5), with no rule given. Identification depends on it here; with the square of +/-2 SD of the fitted Gaussian the paper's 92% and 72% are reproduced (90.8% and 72.5%), with the best initial model's field of view the result is 75.8% and 46.7%. Which rule, and at which of the 25 resolutions, did the paper use?
  • Methods not availableSM 6 does not say whether the grating tuning curves include the DC offset, whether the gratings were restricted to the stimulus aperture, or how ROI-averaged curves combine voxels whose fields of view, and so whose spatial frequencies in cycles per degree, differ. Here the V1 average is high-pass under every choice tried, where Supplementary Figure 7 shows it band-pass.
  • Methods not availableSupplementary Figure 11 omits voxels whose RF was "not completely within the stimulus bounds"; whether the bounds are the 20 degree square or the circular aperture changes whether V1 < V2 < V3 holds in most eccentricity bins here.
  • Paper inconsistentSM 5 gives the initial fields of view as 10.1 and 5.2 degrees, but at 128 px the resolution list of SM 6 gives 10.0 (256 px) and 5.12 (500 px) degrees; and a 1-octave bandwidth with an isotropic Gaussian gives an orientation bandwidth of about 37 degrees, not the 41 stated.

Questions

Where the author or a verifier believed the claims file misread the paper, they asked, citing the paper, and a reader who had not seen the data answered from the paper alone, amending the claims file where it was wrong. The author then ran the claim as answered. Attempts before protocol pilot-11 also asked here for method details the claims file did not give.

  • openasked about fig4a-noise-ceiling

    The criterion gives the available trials per image as 13, or 14 for an image among the first 6 trials of a run. In the data set's presentation sequences (seqVal), the 120 identification images have 13, 14 or 15 trials over the 10 runs (70 images 13, 40 images 14, 10 images 15), counting the 6 trials repeated at the end of each run. Should the sample size be the number of trials each image has, whatever it is, or exactly 13 or 14?

    Passage. SM 1, "Each identification run used 12 distinct images presented 13 times each ... During stimulus presentation the first 6 trials were repeated at the end of the 168-trial sequence ... In the single-trial analysis, all data were used."

    Why it matters. The sample size of the repeated-trial ceiling sets how far its mean patterns average out the noise; an image with 15 trials gets a less noisy pattern than one with 13. The difference is small, but the criterion's wording does not cover 10 of the 120 images.

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked about fig4a-noise-ceiling

    The criterion's readings draw the predicted pattern of each candidate image with the same sample size as the measured one, so the single-trial ceiling compares one trial with one trial. SM 7 says the predicted patterns stand in for model predictions whose quality is "limited only by intrinsic measurement variability, not by the predictive power of receptive-field models", which fits predicted patterns averaged over all of an image's trials (a bootstrap of the available trials) and a single-trial measured pattern. Should the single-trial ceiling use one measured trial against predicted patterns averaged over all trials?

    Passage. SM 7, "Estimation of the noise ceiling", "In each simulation, the first step was to generate a measured voxel activity pattern for the correct image. This was accomplished by taking the mean of a random sample drawn from the single-trial activity patterns evoked by the correct image. The next step was to generate a predicted voxel activity pattern for each potential image ... by taking the mean of a random sample drawn from the single-trial activity patterns evoked by each potential image. (The intuition here is that the quality of the predicted voxel activity patterns is limited only by intrinsic measurement variability, not by the predictive power of receptive-field models.)"

    Why it matters. A ceiling computed from two single trials is noisier on both sides and can fall below the single-trial identification performance of the models themselves, which a ceiling should not. The paper's single-trial ceilings (about 80% and 64%) sit above its single-trial performance (51% and 32%); which reading gives values near them decides the outcome once single-trial responses are computed.

    No reader has answered it yet.

Reproduction Identifying natural images from human brain activity (Kay et al. 2008)

Tests the findings of Kay, Naselaris, Prenger and Gallant (2008) on CRCNS data set vim-1, which holds the estimated fMRI responses of two subjects to 1,750 model estimation images and 120 identification images, with ROI labels, the stimuli and the raw time series. It fits the Gabor wavelet pyramid receptive-field models of the paper, identifies the 120 images from the responses, and tests the retinotopy-only model, voxel counts and predictive power by area, receptive-field size, tuning, and the classification extension. The 999-image library, the later sessions of S1 and the retinotopic mapping scans are not in the data set. All files

Environment: Python 3.13, 28 packages pinned with their hashes in requirements.lock.txt; CI runs each notebook from a new environment built from it.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • Each model is fitted once by gradient descent with early stopping; the paper's final parameters are the mean of 100 bootstrap fits (SM 6, "Estimation of variance"), not run for time. Ten bootstrap fits, run as a variant under O5 (b), change the result by one image (S1) and three (S2).fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • One stopping set (20% of the 1,750 responses) is drawn per fit and shared by the voxels fitted together, a standard choice for a random draw (SM 6 draws one per fit).fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • The initial fields of view are 10.0 and 5.12 degrees, from the paper's resolution list min(500, round(2^(9 - x/8))) (SM 6); the paper states 10.1 and 5.2 degrees (SM 5), which the list does not give.fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • The Gaussian SD of each wavelet follows from a 1-octave spatial frequency bandwidth (full width at half maximum), which gives an orientation bandwidth of 37 degrees where SM 6 states 41 (O3, standard choice). Wavelet centres at the grid cell centres (O3).fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • Stimulus values are taken as linear in luminance (O4); the goggles' response is not given.fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • Stimuli are resampled by area averaging; SM 6 does not say how.fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • An isotropic Gaussian with a constant offset is fitted to each initial model's envelope at 32 x 32; SM 5 says only "an isotropic two-dimensional Gaussian".fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • The significance of predictive power is by 1,000 bootstrap resamples of the 120 image pairs (O2, standard choice).fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • The responses are the data set's estimated repeated-trial responses, z-scored per voxel by the data set; the paper standardized per session (SM 3). Voxels with NaN responses are left out (S1 345, S2 214 of V1 to V3).fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • The p-values that pick the O1 (a) candidate voxels use all 120 identification images, the one being identified included, where P5 leaves that image out; O1 (b), which has no such step, gives the same S1 count and one image fewer for S2.fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • The stopping rule is read as stopping at the first iteration whose stopping-set error does not decrease, so a fit whose first step raises that error keeps the zero kernel and the voxel has no model (about a third of the initial fits, 29% to 38% across the six).fig3-s1-repeated-120
  • As fig3-s1-repeated-120 (one fit in place of 100 bootstrap fits, shared stopping set, field of view sizes, wavelet SD, linear luminance, resampling, Gaussian with offset, NaN voxels left out).fig4a-s2-repeated-120
  • The contrast channel is read as the square root of the weighted variance (the SM 8 formula is garbled in the text); fitted by the gradient descent of SM 6 with the deviations of fig3-s1-repeated-120.suppm8-retinotopy-only-120
  • The luminance channel is the absolute weighted mean of the stimulus values minus the mean luminance of all stimuli (SM 8, "absolute deviation from mean luminance"; SM 6 subtracts the same mean). The reading of the first version, relative to the grey background, is shown as a variant.suppm8-retinotopy-only-120
  • The initial models carry the fitting deviations of fig3-s1-repeated-120.supptable1-predictive-power-by-area
  • Assumed, voxels with NaN responses in the data set (S1 345, S2 214 of V1 to V3) count in the denominators as not significant. Supplementary Table 1 gives the fractions as percentages of the area totals, and those totals equal the data set's label counts including the NaN voxels; the data set gives no estimates for them, so they cannot have significant predictive power here. Whether the paper had estimates for them is not known.supptable1-predictive-power-by-area
  • The readings differ in outcome. O5 (a) is adopted as an assumption, as the paper best supports it; its 5.45 degree field of view gives a highest frequency of 2.94 cycles/deg, the end of the spatial frequency axis of Figure 2b (2.9), where the O5 (b) model's ends at 0.80, and only O5 (a) reproduces the identification results.fig2-representative-voxel
  • Tuning curves are taken without the DC offset; SM 6 does not say whether the tuning curves include it, and with it the shape of the curves depends on the grating amplitude, which the paper does not give, so only the curves without it can be compared with the rule.fig2-representative-voxel
  • The paper does not say how it numbers voxels; voxIdx is assumed to be its numbering.fig2-representative-voxel
  • The curves are averaged over each model's five spatial frequency levels in cycles per field of view, as the fields of view differ between voxels; the paper does not say how the curves of voxels with different fields of view are combined.suppfig7-roi-tuning
  • Grating amplitude 0.5, a standard choice that scales the curves without the DC offset but does not change their shape.suppfig7-roi-tuning
  • The legend's "estimated RF location was not completely within the stimulus bounds" is read as the RF, the +/-2 SD square of SM 5, inside the circular aperture of 10 degrees radius (the 20 degree stimulus), as an assumption; Figure 2's legend calls the white circle "the bounds of the stimulus". The mask's own radius (9.5 degrees, from its area) and the disc filter of the first version are shown as variants and give the same outcome. The first version of this record adopted the 20 degree square, under which the order holds in most 1 degree bins.suppfig11-rf-size-eccentricity
  • For the relative contrast reading, stimulus values are shifted to start at 0, as the absolute luminance is not given. Image similarity uses the full 500 px images, blocks of 15 px (0.6 degrees).suppdisc1-classification-extension
