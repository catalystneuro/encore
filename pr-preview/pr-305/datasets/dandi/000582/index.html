    Reading O3 sets the gridness circle by "the distance from the centre to the outermost peak's centre plus that peak's radius", and removes the central peak "as a disc of the radius of that peak". Is a peak's centre the pixel of its maximum correlation or the centroid of its pixels, and is its radius that of a disc of the same area, or the largest distance from its centre to one of its pixels?     Passage. SOM, Autocorrelation of rate maps, "peaks defined as 100 or more contiguous pixels of 1.5 x 1.5 cm2 above a fixed threshold"; "the circle was fitted around the outermost peak". The SOM gives no rule for a peak's position or size.

Which one do the paper's means (55.9, 52.2 and 58.2 degrees) use?

    Passage. SOM, Analysis of head-direction cells, "The breadth of directional tuning was expressed by the angular standard deviation of the mean vector"; fig. S2 and S4 legends, "Angular deviation refers to the circular standard deviation as defined in the Supplementary Materials and Methods".

    Why it matters.     The reader’s answer. The paper does not settle it. The SOM says "the angular standard deviation of the mean vector" and cites Batschelet (S8); the fig.
