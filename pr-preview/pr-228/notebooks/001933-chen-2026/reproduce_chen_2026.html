Dandiset: DANDI:001933, version 0.260825.1457. The paper does not name the dandiset or a version (its data statement points to Zenodo, PRIDE and the BioImage Archive). This is the only published version, and it holds the same 27 files as the draft (checked in the data check), so it is pinned.

What the dandiset holds: one session per mouse of GRAB-DA3m fiber photometry in the dorsal striatum during optogenetic stimulation of SNc dopamine neurons, 15 Anxa1-iCre mice (dorsolateral striatum) and 12 Calb1-Cre mice (dorsomedial striatum), LRRK2-WT and LRRK2-G2019S (GS). This is the data of Figure 6 and Supplementary Figures 7 and 8. The immunostaining, proteomics, western blots and super-resolution imaging of Figures 1 to 5 are not in it.

Outside input: the paper's source data file (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.19930567, Source data.xlsx, md5 checked below), used only in the claim source-data-link to test whether the dandiset's recordings are those behind the published values. Every other claim runs on the dandiset alone. The epochs table and the photometry share a clock.

The response grows with the labelled power in every Calb1 file (Spearman 0.76 to 0.94) but not in many Anxa1 files (median 0.06). def rank_match(records):
    """Pair each source record with the dandiset epoch of the same mouse and power and the same rank."""
    seen, out = {}, []

        out.append((d, s, idx[r], idx))

def group_test(df):
    gs, wt = df[df.group == "GS"].value.dropna(), df[df.group == "WT"].value.dropna()
    t, p = lrrk2.one_tailed_less(gs, wt)
    return {"n_GS": len(gs), "n_WT": len(wt), "mean_GS": gs.mean(), "mean_WT": wt.mean(),
            "t": t, "p_one_tailed": p, "cohens_d": lrrk2.cohens_d(wt, gs)}


def per_mouse_tests(df, alpha):
    wt = df[df.group == "WT"].value.dropna()
    rows = []
    for m, g in df[df.group == "GS"].groupby("mouse"):
        v = g.value.dropna()
        row = {"mouse": m, "n": len(v), "mean": v.mean(), "WT_mean": wt.mean()}
        for name, equal_var, tails in (("a_welch_1t", False, 1), ("b_welch_2t", False, 2),
                                       ("c_student_2t", True, 2), ("d_student_1t", True, 1)):
            p = (lrrk2.one_tailed_less(v, wt, equal_var)[1] if tails == 1 else lrrk2.two_tailed(v, wt, equal_var)[1])
            row[f"p_{name}"] = p
            row[f"sig_{name}"] = bool(p < alpha and v.mean() < wt.mean())
        rows.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

The published integrals give 2.06 and 0.765 (see source-data-link), so the paper's values follow from its own data, and the Calb1 difference is consistent with the Calb1 dF/F in the dandiset not being the one the paper analysed. The Anxa1 effect is larger than the Calb1 effect at every power under the Methods, code and fixed windows (ratio 1.29 to 1.74).

How the analysis differs from the paper's:

  • The evoked integral is taken over the light-on period, as the Methods state; the paper's numbers come from the code's window, 0.1 s before to 1.0 s after onset (its published integrals equal that measure times 0.0001). Both are shown.
  • Spontaneous transients are measured by their integral, as the Methods state; the paper's published values are 0.005 times the mean dF/F over each transient. Both are shown.
  • Per-mouse tests are shown under four readings, as the Methods (Welch), the legends (one-tailed) and the code (Student, two-tailed) disagree.
  • The window chunking_index.m computes as written starts 1 s after onset; the source data show the paper used the intended window starting 1 s before onset. The literal window is shown as a variant only.

What was found about the dandiset:

  • The Anxa1 recordings are those behind Figure 6 and Supplementary Figure 7: the matched published traces equal the dandiset's dF/F to within 5.7e-13, and the evoked integrals and transient counts match, except one stimulation of mouse 4398 that the processed series cuts off 0.8 s after onset.
  • The Calb1 dF/F in the dandiset is not the trace behind Supplementary Figure 8. None of the 384 published traces or integrals match it, no epoch of any power matches a published trace, and recomputing from the raw signal with any single background fraction does not close the gap. The raw signal is there, but the processing the paper used for Calb1 is not, so the Calb1 results here are of the dandiset's processing, not the paper's.
  • The epochs table describes one kind of train and four powers; the files hold two kinds of train and eight powers, and two Anxa1 GS mice received the paper's powers as 0.33 s trains of 9 ms pulses; in these two mice the response does not follow the labelled power.
  • The dF/F unit is given as a.u. for a series in percent; two trains have a pulse length of 0; the four excluded mice are left out without a note; and the paper does not cite the dandiset.
