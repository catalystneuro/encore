  • fig6a-excitatory-on-plane

    Excitatory spectral receptive fields of MT neurons tend to lie on a single plane in the three-dimensional frequency domain; their average on-plane ratio is 0.59, significantly above the null model's 0.36.

    Figure 6A summarizes the onplane ratio for the excitatory receptive fields of all 52 area MT neurons in our sample. The average ratio is 0.59

    Results, Excitatory spectral receptive fields lie on a plane, paragraphs 1 and 2; Figure 6A

    How it will be judged

    From the compressive and divisive model fit of each neuron. The optimal velocity plane passes through the origin and is set by azimuth (direction) and elevation (speed); it maximizes the summed positive V1 filter weights whose temporal frequency lies within 1 octave or 5 Hz of the plane, whichever is larger (maximum coverage), together with the symmetry constraint, the negative of the difference of the weights summed on the two sides of the plane's azimuth (Materials and Methods, Optimal velocity plane). The on-plane ratio is the positive weights near the plane over the total positive weights (Figure 6 legend). Open details, with readings. The balance of the two constraints was set by hand; readings are (a) coverage only, with symmetry breaking ties; (b) coverage and symmetry each scaled by the total positive weight and summed with equal weight. The symmetry term is read as the negative of the absolute difference of the two sides (a signed difference would favour one side of every plane); the paper says only "subtracting these quantities and taking the negative". What is summed, (a) the fitted weights, as the Methods and Figure 6 legend say; (b) the weights times each filter's spectral amplitude, as "proportion of the spectral receptive field" in the Results suggests. Sign split, (a) per weight at each delay; (b) after summing each filter's weights over delays. Reproduced when, under one combination, the mean is within tolerance and a Wilcoxon rank-sum test against the null model ratios gives p < 0.01 with the real ratios higher.

  • fig7a-partial-ring

    The horizontal-vertical ratio is distributed continuously; few neurons form a full ring (ratio near 1) or a single spatiotemporal frequency peak (ratio near 0), and most form a partial ring in the optimal plane that avoids low temporal frequencies.

    However, the receptive fields of the large majority of MT neurons lie between these extremes, forming a partial ring in the optimal velocity plane and avoiding the region near zero temporal frequency

    Results, Excitatory spectral receptive fields of most MT neurons form a partial ring in the plane, paragraphs 1 and 2; Figure 7A; Figures 4 and 5

    How it will be judged

    The optimal plane of fig6a-excitatory-on-plane is divided into four quadrants, two centred on the vertical axis (along the optimal direction) and two on the horizontal axis (in the zero temporal frequency plane); the excitatory spectral amplitudes are integrated in the horizontal and in the vertical quadrants and the ratio horizontal over vertical is taken (Results, same section, paragraph 1). Open details, with readings. Quadrant borders at 45 degrees from each axis (the only symmetric choice, taken as standard). Amplitudes on the plane are those within the near-plane band of fig6a-excitatory-on-plane (a), or all excitatory amplitude projected onto the plane (b). "Extremes" are not bounded. The ring end has no upper bound, as ratios in Figure 7A run to about 1.7, and the ring neuron of Figure 4A sits at about 1.35. Readings are (a) ratios below 0.2 and at or above 0.8, or (b) below 0.1 and at or above 0.9. Reproduced when, under one combination, more than half of the neurons fall between the extremes and fewer than a quarter fall in each extreme (this file's reading of "only a small fraction", Figure 7 legend).

  • fig7b-pattern-index

    How it will be judged

Open details, with readings. Grating and plaid spatial and temporal frequency, (a) at each neuron's peak excitatory spectral response, (b) one frequency pair for all neurons near the population peak. Reproduced when, under one combination, more than half of the neurons have indices from -6.6 to 2.2 (the range widened by 10%) and fewer than 10% exceed 2.2 (rare is not quantified; 10% is this file's reading, and the count above 1.28, the pattern-cell bound of Smith et al., 2005, is reported as well).
