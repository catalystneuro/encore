Fujimoto et al. 2026 · Nature Neuroscience

Deep brain stimulation induces white matter remodeling and functional changes to brain-wide networks

doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02301-4 · paper key fujimoto-2026

Datasets

  • DANDI:001773
  • DANDI:001785

DANDI:001773: Most main findings hold up in this data: stimulation raised fractional anisotropy in the mid-cingulate bundle, increased myelinating oligodendrocytes with thicker myelin, lowered cortical and raised subcortical connectivity, and reduced subgenual cingulate connectivity with default mode, limbic and executive networks. In the implanted but unstimulated monkey, the opposite fractional anisotropy decrease also reproduced. The nonsurgical controls' lack of change, the local increase at the implant site and the network-sorted connectivity plot could not be tested, because whole-brain maps, control seed data and ROI names are missing. The connectivity results assume the deposited maps are those the paper analysed. One minor claim failed: that monkey's foraging fell, and the paper reports no test for it.

DANDI:001785: Most main findings hold in this data: stimulation raised fractional anisotropy in the stimulated cingulum bundle, lowered connectivity of the subcallosal seed with default mode, limbic and central executive hubs, and raised the share of myelinating oligodendrocytes in the mid-cingulate. The whole-brain connectivity matrices and the myelin g-ratio could not be tested, because the dataset has no fMRI time series and no hand axon measurements. The "no change" finding in the anterior cingulum did not reproduce, but it rests on the agent's own cell counting, since the paper does not describe its method. The white matter and connectivity results reuse the paper's own voxel selections, so they show the dataset holds the data, not that the regions would be found again. Among minor claims, the no-stimulation animal's foraging fell, unlike the reported null.
