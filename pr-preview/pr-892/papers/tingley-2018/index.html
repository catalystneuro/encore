Summary: The paper's central claim, that lateral septum neurons encode position better in the theta phase of their spikes than in their firing rate, did not hold up in this data, nor did the equivalence of rate and phase codes in hippocampal place fields. The likely reason is a step the paper does not describe: how position bins with no nearby spike, about 70% of samples, enter phase decoding. As reported, the septal phase code was more reliable across the population than hippocampal codes, and theta coherence and coupling with CA3 rose along the run. The depth gradient held under one analysis reading only, hippocampal-septal assemblies did not match the paper's, and the spiking model was not run.
