From the paper AxonDeepSeg: automatic axon and myelin segmentation from microscopy data using convolutional neural networks · claims file · checked 2026-10-21: ready

  • table3-tem-mice-accuracy

    The TEM model trained on mouse images segments the mouse test images with a mean three-class pixel-wise accuracy of 95% (0.9451 in Table 3, mean over 24 test images).

    The TEM model trained on mice microscopy achieved a pixel-wise accuracy of 95% on mice samples

    Results, Segmentation, paragraph 1; Table 3, row TEM, Mice

    How it will be judged

    Segment each mouse TEM image of the dandiset with default_TEM_model_v1 at commit 7f60132, by segment.py with its defaults (0.01 um working pixel size, overlap 25, the input contrast inverted once as segment.py does for this model, see the opening comment), using the pixel size the dandiset gives for each image (Table 1 gives 0.002 um). The result without the inversion is shown as a variant. Pixel-wise accuracy is, per image, the fraction of pixels whose class (background, myelin, axon) in the prediction equals the class in the dandiset's segmentation, at the native pixel size (Methods, Pipeline overview and Pixel-wise accuracy); the value is the mean over images (Table 3 legend). The test set is open, as the paper names neither the mice nor their group. The readings are (a) every mouse image in the dandiset, which also holds the model's training images; (b) the images of the control mice only, following the Discussion's statement that the models were trained and tested on healthy tissues. Neither reading is the paper's test set (3 of the 20 mice, disjoint from the 17 training mice, Table 1), so both may hold training images and a match is weaker evidence than a test on held-out mice. When a mouse has more than 8 images, all its images are used (the paper does not say which 8 it took). Reproduced when the mean is within tolerance under both readings; when the readings differ in outcome the claim is blocked as methods_underspecified. The per-mouse means, and the cuprizone mice apart, are reported.

  • table3-tem-mice-dice-detection

    On the mouse test images the TEM model reaches a mean axon Dice of 0.9493, a mean myelin Dice of 0.8552, a mean axon detection sensitivity of 0.9597 and a mean precision of 0.9647.

    How it will be judged

    Segmentations and test-set readings as in table3-tem-mice-accuracy. Dice per image is 2|A and B| / (|A| + |B|) between the predicted and the ground-truth masks, for axon and for myelin apart (Methods, Equation 1; pw_dice in segmentation_scoring.py). Sensitivity and precision per image are TP / (TP + FN) and TP / (TP + FP) over axon objects matched by centroids (Methods, Detection metrics), computed with score_analysis as the opening comment states. Each value is the mean over images. Reproduced when all four means are within tolerance under both readings; partially reproduced when two or three are under both.

  • fig7-corticospinal-tract

    In the corticospinal tract of the rat cervical spinal cord the segmentation gives small axon diameters (around 1 um), very high axon density (around 200,000 axons per mm2) and g-ratios around 0.6.

    In the corticospinal tract (tract

    Results, Morphometrics extraction, paragraph 1; Figure 7

    How it will be judged

    Not testable on this dandiset, which holds no rat spinal cord. Per the Methods (Morphometrics extraction), with AxonSeg (ref. 16) as the paper used, the segmentation of the full slice is aggregated into 50 x 50 um2 cells, within a white matter mask; axon diameter is the equivalent diameter sqrt(4 Area / pi) of each axon object, density is axons per mm2, and g-ratio is sqrt(1 / (1 + MVF / AVF)). The paper gives the tract only as a schematic diagram (Figure 7, refs. 34 to 36), so the cells that form the tract are open.
