The ten-finger decoding findings held up in this data, the share of finger-selective neurons did not, and the contralateral-task and online interface findings could not be tested. Ten fingers were decoded well above chance, with most errors on adjacent or matching fingers, and right and left finger representations generalized across hands. Right-left finger pairs were closer than other pairs, but the size of that effect depends on an assumed noise scaling. Only 48% of neurons in the first array were finger selective, against the paper's 66%, and the paper does not say which window or test it used. The other sessions are missing from the dataset.

    Results, Compositional representation of finger and laterality, paragraph 6; Supplementary Figure 10e

    What the paper reports

    • permutation test, p < 0.001 (n = pseudo-population of J-PPC neurons pooled over 2 sessions)

    J-PPC finger-type CCGP is 0.290 to 0.380 over the readings (shuffle mean 0.20), far below the within-hand accuracy on the pseudo-population (0.828 to 0.890). The permutation p is below 0.001 in one of eight readings (O1 b, O6 a, linear SVM, no null value of 1001 reaching the CCGP of 0.380, p = 1/1002, within-hand 0.850); in the others 1 to 37 null values reach it (p 0.002 to 0.038), so the paper's p below 0.001 holds only in that reading, at the floor of the test. There the CCGP is within tolerance of 36% and the within-hand accuracy is outside the tolerance of 65%, with CCGP below 0.9 of it, which the criterion calls partially reproduced. With the within-hand accuracy computed per session, run under every O1 x O7, it is 0.680 to 0.690, within tolerance in all eight readings, and the same reading (O1 b, O6 a, SVM) is reproduced. The two populations give different outcomes; the pseudo-population is kept for the reasons in the deviation below, a judgement call, as the round-2 verifier notes.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Assumed that the standard (within-hand) accuracy is computed on the same pseudo-population as CCGP, since the Methods (Compositionality of finger representations) call it an upper bound on CCGP, which holds only for the same neurons, and the CCGP literature it cites (ref 60) computes both on one pseudo-population; with the per-session accuracy N-PPC finger CCGP exceeds the bound (ratio up to 1.31). The Methods' offline classification trains a separate classifier per session, and that reading, now run under every O1 x O7 next to each O6 row, gives 0.680 to 0.690, near the paper's 65%, and would change the outcome to reproduced.
    • The shuffle scheme of the permutation test is not stated; condition labels are permuted across pseudo-trials. The J-PPC p-values lie near the 0.001 bound, so the scheme can matter. For O6 (b) the observed CCGP is a mean over 100 matchings and each null value uses one matching, which makes p conservative; it does not decide the outcome, which rests on an O6 (a) reading.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds only the ten-finger press task (10 sessions of N, 2 of J). The alternating-cues task (Figures 1, 4 and 5, Supplementary Figures 2 to 5) and the online BMI sessions of J (Figure 6b, Supplementary Figures 6 and 7) are not in it, though the paper's Data Availability names only this dandiset. N's BMI sessions of ref 46 appear to be in DANDI:000147.
  • File format errorIn 1509 of 1692 units the spike times are not stored in increasing time order, which NWB expects. Counts in windows are unaffected once sorted, as this attempt does.
  • Description missingThe files name the participants P1 (the paper's N) and N1 (the paper's J); no mapping to the paper's labels is given, and N1 is not participant N. The mapping follows from the sex and electrode locations.
  • Description missingThe trial columns Run, TrialNumber, cue_on_time, cue_off_time and go_on_time have no description. The Delay screen onset, which defines the paper's Delay window, is implied only by cue_off_time.
  • OtherThe per-session unit counts the paper reports match the units in the files before the 0.5 Hz exclusion that the Methods apply to all analyses; the J-MC session of 2019-02-12 has 139 units against the paper's stated maximum of 130.
  • Methods not availableFor the authors. How was the noise precision passed to rsatoolbox for the cross-validated Mahalanobis distance (Methods, Neural distance between fingers)? With two partitions and each partition's single-trial precision the differences are about a fifth of the paper's (N-PPC 0.28 against 1.56); with the precision of the partition mean, as the toolbox documents for a list of noises, they match (1.42). This attempt assumed the latter.
  • Methods not availableFor the authors. Was the standard (within-condition) accuracy compared with CCGP computed on the pseudo-population or per session (Methods, Compositionality of finger representations)? For J-PPC within-hand accuracy is 0.83 to 0.89 on the pseudo-population and 0.68 to 0.69 per session, against the paper's 65%. This attempt assumed the pseudo-population.
  • Methods not availableFor the authors. What did the 1001 label shuffles of the CCGP permutation test permute, and how were trials of different sessions matched in the pseudo-population? With condition labels permuted across pseudo-trials, J-PPC finger-type CCGP has p from 0.001 to 0.038 over the readings, against the paper's p below 0.001.
  • Methods not availableFor the authors. With the Methods' one-way ANOVA on Go-window rates and FDR over pooled neurons, 48%, 38% and 65% of N-PPC, J-PPC and J-MC neurons are discriminative in the ten-finger task, against 66%, 57% and 78%. A 1 s window from the Go cue gives 61%, 57% and 83%. Which window, exclusion and correction gave the paper's values?

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • The counts that match are those before the 0.5 Hz exclusion, which the Methods (Feature Extraction) say applies to all analyses; the criterion names this count among its readings.tenfinger-recording-counts
  • As for tenfinger-recording-counts, the matching count is the one before the exclusion.tenfinger-pooled-npcc-neurons
  • The Go window is used, as claims-file O11 says the text supports no other.tenfinger-discriminative-fraction
  • Units with the same rate in every trial (5 N-PPC and 2 J-MC before the exclusion, none after it) have no ANOVA p; they are counted in the FDR's m and as not discriminative.tenfinger-discriminative-fraction
  • The variance floor of the diagonal LDA (1e-9 of the largest variance, as scikit-learn's GaussianNB) is a standard choice that only prevents division by zero.tenfinger-decoding-accuracy
  • Assumed that the per-partition noise passed to rsatoolbox is the precision of the partition mean, the single-trial precision times the 5 trials per condition in each partition. The Methods say the toolbox (ref 57) incorporates the noise covariances of both folds, which is its list-of-noises branch, and the toolbox's calc_rdm_crossnobis documents that this list must hold the precision of the mean of each fold. The paper does not say how it computed the noise, and the scaling changes the values about fivefold; the unscaled result is shown.fig8b-matching-closer
  • Units with zero residual variance in a partition make the noise covariance singular and are left out of that session's distance (one N-PPC unit); a standard choice.fig8b-matching-closer
  • The MDS of the figure is drawn by classical MDS of the clipped squared distances and aligned by Generalized Procrustes with scaling; it is shown only and does not decide the outcome.fig8a-npcc-index-distinct
  • The shuffle permutes the ten condition labels across pseudo-trials and recomputes CCGP; the paper says only that labels were shuffled 1001 times. For O6 (b) each shuffle uses a new random matching of trials.fig8e-hand-ccgp
  • Standard accuracy is computed on the same pseudo-population (assumption, see jppc-finger-ccgp-low); the per-session accuracy, run under every O1 x O7, does not change this outcome, as hand CCGP is at least 0.940 of it.fig8e-hand-ccgp
  • As for fig8e-hand-ccgp, the shuffle scheme and the population of the standard accuracy are choices the paper does not state.fig8e-finger-ccgp
  • Assumed that the standard (within-hand) accuracy is computed on the same pseudo-population as CCGP, since the Methods (Compositionality of finger representations) call it an upper bound on CCGP, which holds only for the same neurons, and the CCGP literature it cites (ref 60) computes both on one pseudo-population; with the per-session accuracy N-PPC finger CCGP exceeds the bound (ratio up to 1.31). The Methods' offline classification trains a separate classifier per session, and that reading, now run under every O1 x O7 next to each O6 row, gives 0.680 to 0.690, near the paper's 65%, and would change the outcome to reproduced.jppc-finger-ccgp-low
  • The shuffle scheme of the permutation test is not stated; condition labels are permuted across pseudo-trials. The J-PPC p-values lie near the 0.001 bound, so the scheme can matter. For O6 (b) the observed CCGP is a mean over 100 matchings and each null value uses one matching, which makes p conservative; it does not decide the outcome, which rests on an O6 (a) reading.jppc-finger-ccgp-low
