  • fig3f-oracle-reliability

    Neurons differ in how reliably they respond to the repeated oracle clips; units with high oracle scores give repeat-to-repeat responses that are consistent, and units with low scores do not.

    Raster of deconvolved calcium activity for three neurons to repeated stimulus trials (oracle trials; ten repeats of six sequential clips, with each repeat normalized independently). Rasters for high (top), medium (middle) and low (bottom) oracle scores

    Fig. 3f; Results, Behavioural tracking and visual stimulation, paragraph 2; Methods, Oracle score

    How it will be judged

    The oracle score is, per unit, the jackknife mean of correlations between each oracle trial and the mean of the remaining trials (Methods, Oracle score), on the deconvolved trace interpolated at the scan's frame rate onto the oracle frames, with the unit's ms delay added to its frame times (code, Fig 3/panel_f/get_data.ipynb). The paper's code reads the score from a precomputed table, so the unit of a trial is open. The readings are (a) one trial is one repeat of the 6 clips, concatenated (10 trials); (b) each clip is scored apart over its 10 repeats, and the 6 scores are averaged. Units at the 99th, 79th and 34th percentiles of the score (the code's choices for the figure) are selected and their rasters shown. The paper's measure of repeat-to-repeat consistency is the oracle score itself (Results, paragraph 2; Methods, Oracle score), and it gives no other measure or test. Reproduced when, under both readings, the score can be computed for the units of the 14 scans and the scores of the three selected units are increasing from the 34th to the 99th percentile with the 99th percentile unit's score above 0, so that consistent and inconsistent units both occur. The mean pairwise Pearson correlation between single repeats of the 100 units nearest each percentile is shown as a variant; that measure is set here and is not the basis of the outcome.

  • fig3g-direction-tuning

    Monet2 drives direction-tuned responses; neurons can be found preferring each of the 16 directions, and their trial-averaged responses peak at their preferred direction.

    Trial-averaged raster (central 500 ms of trial-average raster for each direction, out of 937 ms) of deconvolved calcium activity for 80 neurons in 40 Monet2 trials (16 randomly ordered directions) grouped by preferred direction (5 neurons per direction

    Fig. 3g; Results, Behavioural tracking and visual stimulation, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    From the code (Fig 3/panel_g/get_data.ipynb). The readings are (a) the preferred direction is the direction with the highest mean response over the central window; (b) the peak of a von Mises function fitted to the 16 mean responses. Reproduced when, under both readings, every one of the 16 directions is preferred by at least 5 units (the figure's 5 per direction). The paper gives no measure or test of tuning strength. Two choices set here, not from the paper, that do not decide the outcome, are the following. The 5 units shown per direction are those with the highest ratio of the response at the preferred direction to the mean over all directions; and a split-half check (preferred direction from the first 20 trials, response from the last 20) of whether their response at the preferred direction exceeds the mean at the other 15 is shown as a variant.
