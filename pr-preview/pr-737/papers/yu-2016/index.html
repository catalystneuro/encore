On DANDI:000005: Some main findings held, some did not, and some could not be tested. Phase coupling to whisking in VPM and fast-spiking cells held, and so did the larger effect of touch than of whisking on excitatory cell membrane potential. VPM touch responses were weaker than reported (0.40 against 0.6 spikes per touch). Excitatory cells fired more during whisking, where the paper found no change. Suppressing PV cells raised excitatory firing as reported, though at higher rates. The nerve-cut experiments could not be tested because no file marks the cut mice. The depolarized-inhibition and thalamic multiunit recordings are not in the dataset. Thalamic drive of fast-spiking cells could not be tested because only 5 cells qualified.

On CRCNS:ssc-7: Most of the paper's main findings held up in this data. VPM and fast-spiking cells rose with whisking (VPM 4.3 to 11.9 spikes/s), their spikes were phase-locked to whisking, and cutting the infraorbital nerve removed fast-spiking touch and whisking responses. Thalamic photoinhibition lowered fast-spiking rates in direction, but the percentages differed from the paper's. Layer 4 excitatory cells did not show the reported lack of a whisking effect (p about 0.02), and the eNpHR touch-detection findings did not reproduce, although one of them flips with how passive first touches are handled. The inhibition seen at depolarized potentials, the thalamic multiunit photoinhibition and the rabies tracing could not be tested because those recordings are not in the dataset. Several latency and amplitude values were off, and the files do not say which tracked whisker is the principal one.
