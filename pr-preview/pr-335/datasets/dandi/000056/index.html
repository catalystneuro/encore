    Results, second paragraph; Figure 2e,f; Abstract ("accelerated tenfold")

    Over 1509 pairs (64 of 1573 dropped for a fit at the edge of the speed range), the median ratio of SWS to waking angular velocity is 8.33 (quartiles 5.91 to 11.31), below 9 to 11; SWS is faster in 1420 pairs (sign test p = 4.1e-309). Median speeds are 36.9 deg/s in waking, 33.5 in REM and 324.4 in SWS. By mouse the ratio is 7.89 to 13.52. The Bayesian decoder variant gives a median of 11.53 over 28 sessions. Under reading (b) the median is 8.02 over 2129 pairs; with pairs whose fits reach R2 of 0.25 or 0.5 in every state, 8.46 (1063 pairs) and 8.56 (748 pairs). Median R2 is 0.84 in waking, 0.55 in REM and 0.78 in SWS. The waking estimates are 0.60 times the measured mean angular speed of the head (median over sessions) and correlate with it at r = 0.20 across 33 sessions. On Poisson surrogates driven by the measured head direction and the waking tuning curves, the estimator returns 0.37 of the true mean speed (median 37.0 deg/s, as on the data, against a true 99.9), and for the same trajectory played ten times faster it gives a median ratio of 4.99 (quartiles 2.76 to 7.02), not 10. Scaled by that compression the data's 8.33 becomes 16.69, so the data are consistent with a speed-up below, at or above tenfold. The estimator shows that SWS is faster but does not determine the fold; how far its bias in SWS matches that of the surrogates is not known.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper does not say how the expected and observed cross-correlograms are put on one scale; a free scale and offset are fitted here.
    • The lag range (4 s) and speed grid are chosen here.
    • The expectation over the chi-squared speed distribution is computed exactly from its characteristic function.
    • Pairs within and across structures are pooled; the paper shows ADn-ADn and PoS-PoS pairs.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Description missingThe units tables hold only spike times. No unit has an electrode, shank, brain region, waveform or quality measure, and every electrode's location is "unknown". Units cannot be assigned to the antero-dorsal thalamus or the post-subiculum, which blocks 13 of the paper's 21 claims. The electrode groups and ripple annotations are consistent with Mouse24, Mouse25 and Mouse28 being the three dual-site mice, but the files do not say so.
  • Selection not documentedThe files hold 1959 unit rows (103 with no spikes) in 40 sessions, against the paper's 1077 recorded cells in 42 sessions. Mouse12-120809 alone has 292 units, 167 of them firing below 0.5 Hz. Unit ids run from 0 to N-1 and carry no shank or cluster number, the units table has no quality measure, and 1526 rows fire at 0.5 Hz or more, so the paper's 1077 cells cannot be recovered. Nothing marks which units or sessions the paper analysed (its Methods discard some sessions).
  • Description missingThe open-field exploration (the paper's waking epoch) is not marked. The "Awake" state also covers waking in the home cage, and some sessions hold a second exploration or a larger second environment. The epoch had to be found from position.
  • File format errorIn 36 of 40 files one unit's spike times are not sorted. Each such unit holds exactly two sorted runs, and in all 36 the first run ends at an index of the form k x 65536 - 1. The second run starts within 36 s of the start of the recording and the first between 24 and 13704 s, and the two end within 15 s of each other. In 26 units no time of the second run appears in the first; in 10, 821 to 22281 times do. This suggests an error in the conversion.
  • OtherIn Mouse12-120808 the position and the spikes do not share a clock. Head-direction tuning is best with position shifted by -1.5 s at the start of the exploration and -3.4 s at its end, and no unit passes the HD criteria as deposited; with position realigned by the best shift of each quarter, 13 units do. In all other sessions the best shift is -0.2 to 0 s.
  • Metadata errorThe raw LED positions in `acquisition` are stated in meters with a conversion of NaN but hold camera pixels (values in the hundreds).
  • Data missingMouse32-140820 has no sleep-state table and no processed position, and Mouse17-130201 has no processed position; both have raw LED series only. In three Mouse20 sessions the position series ends 11 to 109 minutes before the recording.
  • Description missingThe annotation series `dow`, `hdD` and `rip` (labels such as "DOWN ON 5", "HD UP ON", "Ripple peak 1") have no description.
  • Metadata errorThe dandiset's related resource for the paper gives its name and identifier as "nternally organized mechanisms of the head direction sense" (first letter missing, and the title in place of a DOI); the URL is correct.
  • Methods not availableNeither the paper nor the dandiset links analysis code, so the von Mises fit, the Rayleigh test, the scaling in the cross-correlogram estimate of angular velocity and the coherence measure had to be chosen or could not be repeated.

Reproduction Head-direction cells across waking and sleep (Peyrache et al. 2015)

Tests the findings of Peyrache, Lacroix, Petersen and Buzsaki (2015) on dandiset 000056, which holds spike times, LED positions and sleep states from 40 sessions of 7 mice recorded in the anterior thalamus and post-subiculum. It classifies head-direction cells, compares their rates and pairwise correlations across waking, REM and slow-wave sleep, estimates the speed of the internal head-direction signal from cross-correlograms, and tests noise correlations and decoding. The units carry no brain region, so the comparisons between the two structures cannot be made. All files
