  • fig1-free-heading-fraction

    In head-fixed mice moving a free floating chamber with a single cue, 14.0% of RSC neurons (373 of 2665) are heading selective, with no head movement.

    revealing that a proportion of neurons in RSC faithfully represent heading (14.0%, 373/2665 neurons

    Results, Neurons in the RSC represent heading in the absence of physical head movements, paragraph 2; Figure 1D, E

    How it will be judged

    The 4 free-chamber recordings over 3 mice. Method from the Methods (Analysis of calcium data, paragraph 2) and code FreelyMoving.m. The Rayleigh vector length of each neuron's tuning curve (inferred spikes, 6 degree bins, 15 degree smoothing) is compared with 1000 values from the spike data circularly shifted in time by a random amount; a neuron is selective when its RVL meets or exceeds the 99th percentile (Methods). The code keeps only frames with chamber speed above 2 (FreelyMoving.checkIsMoving), which the Methods do not mention; and its default threshold is the 95th percentile. Readings, run in turn, (a) all frames, 99th percentile; (b) moving frames only, 99th percentile; (c) the code's 95th percentile on moving frames, as a variant. Reproduced when (a) or (b) gives a percentage within tolerance; (c) is reported.

  • fig1g-rotation-heading-fraction

    With the chamber rotated by a motor (single cue), 14.2% of RSC neurons (452 of 3179, 16 recordings over 5 mice) are heading selective by the paper's reliability and Gaussian-fit test, a percentage similar to the free-chamber experiments.

    we found a similar percentage heading neurons as in the experiments without controlled rotation, comprising 14.2% (452/3179 neurons) of the total number of cells recorded (n = 16 recordings over 5 mice

    Results, Neurons in the RSC represent heading in the absence of physical head movements, paragraph 3; Figure 1G

    How it will be judged

    The heading-selection test of the opening comment (Methods; code ControlledRotation.calculateHeadDirection) with a single-term Gaussian, on inferred spikes of every neuron of the 16 single-cue controlled-rotation recordings. Reproduced when the percentage is within tolerance. The test uses random splits and shuffles; the random seed is a standard choice that should not change the count beyond a few cells.

  • fig1h-preferred-headings-tile

    The preferred headings of heading-selective cells tile the whole circumference of the chamber, including headings with the cue outside the visual field, with a moderate overrepresentation near 0 degrees, where the cue is centered in front of the mouse.

    showing that heading-responsive cells tile the entire circumference of the chamber, even when the cue card is outside of the visual field of the mouse; though we note there is a moderate overrepresentation of responses when the cue is centered in front of the mouse near 0°

    Results, Neurons in the RSC represent heading in the absence of physical head movements, paragraph 3; Figure 1H

    How it will be judged

    The cells selected in fig1g-rotation-heading-fraction; the preferred heading of each is the peak bin of its trial-mean tuning curve, cross-validated as in the Methods (sorted by "cross-validated angle of peak response", Figure 1H legend). Heading 0 is the cue centered in front of the mouse (Results); how the dataset's heading maps to the cue is the author's to find. The paper gives no measure of tiling or of overrepresentation, so both are open, with these readings, (a) every one of the 60 bins of 6 degrees holds at least one preferred heading; (b) every 90 degree quadrant, including the one centered on 180 degrees, holds at least 10% of the preferred headings. For the overrepresentation, the share of preferred headings within 45 degrees of 0 exceeds 25% (its share under uniformity). Reproduced when the overrepresentation holds and tiling holds under (a) or (b).

  • fig5-three-classes

    Clustering the Gaussian-fit coefficients of RSC somatic tuning curves in light-on and light-off conditions gives an optimum of three functional classes (heading, landmark and alignment cells), though the responses span a continuum.

    We found that the data were optimally clustered into three functional classes ... although we note that the responses span a continuum

    Results, A functional class of RSC neurons coregisters visual and heading signals, paragraph 1; Figure 5B; Methods, Clustering of RSC soma responses

    How it will be judged

    The 36 dual-cue RSC soma recordings over 6 mice; heading-selective cells only (two- term Gaussian test of the opening comment), as the reviewer code clusters is_head_direction cells and the Results speak of tuned cells. For each cell, the light-on and light-off tuning curves are aligned (cross-validated, light-on shifts), rescaled together to [0, 1] (code rowrescale on the concatenation) and each fitted with the two-term Gaussian (Methods, eq. 4; code LightDarkExperiment.calculateTuningFits2); the features are a1, a2, c1, c2 of both fits (8 values; Methods). Silhouette evaluation of Gaussian mixture models (Methods) for k = 2, 3 and 4. The cells clustered are open, with readings, (a) heading-selective cells only, as above (the Results give class shares "of the total number of tuned cells"); (b) all cells (Methods, "all cells were fit with a sum of Gaussians"). Both are run. Reproduced when the silhouette value at k = 3 exceeds those at k = 2 and k = 4 (a local maximum, Methods). The reviewer code's fit (calculateTuningFits, on unaligned curves centered on their peak) is a variant and is reported.

  • fig5-class-proportions

    12.1% of RSC neurons are tuned in the dual-cue experiment; of these, landmark cells are the plurality (33%), followed by alignment cells (28%) and heading cells (22%).

    Of the total number of tuned cells (12.1 ± 1.1%), landmark cells made up the plurality of cells (33 ± 3.5%), followed by alignment cells (28 ± 1.9%, mean ± s.e.m.), and heading (22 ± 2.3%) cells

    Results, A functional class of RSC neurons coregisters visual and heading signals, paragraph 3; Figure 5C to E

    How it will be judged

    Tolerances are the paper's s.e.m. Classes from a 3-component GMM on the features of fig5-three-classes (the reviewer code fits it with 20 replicates and up to 1000 iterations, followed). Then, per the Methods, cells whose minimum Mahalanobis distance to the component centroids is "significantly greater" go to a null cluster; archetypal light-on and light-off curves are built from each component's mean coefficients (eq. 4, with b1 and b2 at the code's pi/2 and 3pi/2); each cell goes to the class whose archetypes have the smallest summed cosine distance to its two curves. Classes are named by their archetypes as in the Results (heading, unimodal in light and dark; landmark, bimodal in light and absent in dark; alignment, bimodal in light and unimodal in dark). Open, with readings, the outlier rule, (a) squared Mahalanobis distance beyond the 95th percentile of a chi-square with 8 degrees of freedom, (b) no outlier step (the reviewer code); the averaging, (i) mean over recordings, (ii) mean over mice, (iii) pooled cells. The F-test degrees of freedom of Figure 5F to H imply 332 heading, 522 landmark and 409 alignment cells (2n - 2), and 332 is the smallest class in the decoding Methods; those counts are reported for comparison. The heat-map axis labels of Figure 5, as extracted from the PDF text (the figure could not be viewed), read 332, 409 and 552 cells for the three classes and 1569 for Figure 5A; pooled, 332, 552 and 409 of 1569 are 21%, 35% and 26%, close to the reported shares, so 552 may be the landmark count, which disagrees with the 522 implied by F(1,1042). Reproduced when a reading puts all four values within tolerance with landmark > alignment > heading; partially reproduced when the order holds but a value falls outside.
