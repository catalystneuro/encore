From the paper Offline ensemble co-reactivation links memories across days · claims file · not yet checked

  • source-data-link

    The two deposited mice are the mice of the same names in the paper's per-mouse source data, with the same shock group, and the freezing computed from the dandiset's freezing intervals agrees with the per-mouse freezing the paper reports for them.

    How it will be judged

    Records are matched by the mouse name (subject_id in the files, Mouse in the CSV files). The session descriptions name the context of each recall session. Freezing is read in two ways, (a) the percentage of MotionSeries frames that fall inside a freezing interval and (b) the summed duration of the freezing intervals over the duration of the MotionSeries. Reproduced when both mice are found, their shock amplitude in the files matches the source data's group, and all four values are within tolerance under one reading; partially reproduced when two or three are; not reproduced otherwise.

  • ed9b-aversive-freezing-high-vs-low

    In the calcium imaging and EEG/EMG experiment, high-shock mice froze more during aversive recall than low-shock mice (t7 = 8.99, p = 0.020).

    High-shock mice froze more during Aversive recall than low-shock mice

    Extended Data Figure 9b

    How it will be judged

    The dandiset holds one mouse per group, so the paper's t-test cannot be run. Freezing as in source-data-link reading (a), in Recall1 (the aversive context). Reproduced when the high-shock mouse (Ca_EEG2-1) freezes more than the low-shock mouse (Ca_EEG3-4) and the per-30-s-bin freezing of the two sessions (10 bins each) differs by a two-sided Mann-Whitney test at p < 0.05; partially reproduced when only the direction holds; not reproduced otherwise.

  • offline-bursts-above-shuffle

    During offline periods, hippocampal population bursts occur more often than expected from shuffled neuronal activity, so CA1 neurons fire in a more coordinated way than chance.

    True burst frequencies were greater than shuffled burst frequencies in every mouse ... suggesting that during the offline period, hippocampal CA1 neurons fire in a more coordinated manner than would be expected from shuffled neuronal activities

    Results, Neutral ensemble recruited into population bursts, paragraph 1; Extended Data Figure 3l and 3m

    How it will be judged

    In each of the three deposited offline recordings, burst frequency (events per minute) of the population trace, compared with 1000 surrogates in which each cell's trace is circularly shifted by an independent random offset and the population trace is recomputed and z-scored as for the data. Reproduced when the true frequency exceeds the 95th percentile of the surrogates in all three recordings; partially reproduced when it exceeds the surrogate mean in all three but the 95th percentile in fewer; not reproduced otherwise.

  • offline-skew-above-shuffle

    The mean population activity during offline periods is more right-skewed than in shuffled data, showing brief burst-like activations that need coordinated activity of groups of neurons.

    The true skew was greater than the shuffled skew in every mouse ... supporting the idea that the mean population activity undergoes brief burst-like activations requiring the coordinated activity of groups of neurons

    Extended Data Figure 3n and 3o

    How it will be judged

    As offline-bursts-above-shuffle, with the skewness of the population trace (scipy.stats.skew) in place of the burst frequency, and the same thresholds for the three outcomes.

  • burst-locomotion-pause

    Offline population bursts coincide with the mouse briefly slowing down about 1 s before the burst and resuming its movement about 1 to 2 s later.

    Mice showed a robust and brief slowing down around 1 s before each burst event, before increasing locomotion back up around 2 s later.

    Figure 2d and 2e; Extended Data Figure 6e; Results, Co-reactivation occurs more during wake, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Movement is the magnitude of the telemetry ActivitySignal (the absolute value, averaged in 100 ms bins), the only movement measure in the offline files. Time zero of a burst is read two ways, (a) the frame of peak population activity and (b) the first frame of the event. For each burst with both windows inside the recording, the mean movement from 1 s before to time zero is compared with the mean from 5 s to 3 s before, by a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test (lower before the burst). Reproduced when p < 0.05 in all three offline recordings under one reading; partially reproduced when it holds in one or two; not reproduced otherwise. The mean movement trace from 5 s before to 5 s after time zero is shown, with the same windows around random times as a reference.

  • fig5c-wake-overlap-neutral-coburst

    After a strong (high-shock) aversive experience, during wake in the offline period the overlap ensemble co-bursts more with the neutral ensemble than with the aversive ensemble (t4 = 4.94, P = 0.016).

    Left, wake high-shock mice had higher co-bursting of overlap × neutral than overlap × aversive

    Figure 5c (left); Methods, "Ensemble co-participation analysis"

    How it will be judged

    Tested on the one deposited high-shock mouse (Ca_EEG2-1) and its one deposited offline recording after aversive encoding (the first of 24). A burst is assigned the sleep state of its peak frame. Two readings of wake, (a) wake and quiet wake together and (b) wake alone, and two readings of a co-participation, (i) both ensembles participate, whatever the third does, and (ii) both participate and the third does not. Over the wake bursts, the percentage with overlap and neutral co-participation (OxN) and with overlap and aversive co-participation (OxA). Reproduced when OxN > OxA under one reading and an exact McNemar test across those bursts gives p < 0.05; partially reproduced when OxN > OxA under one reading without that; not reproduced when OxN <= OxA under every reading.
