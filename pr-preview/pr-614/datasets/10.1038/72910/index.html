    In flight, after an initial period, hippocampal place cells on the Escher staircase showed normal, spatially specific firing, with spatial information per spike not different from the same rats' preflight cells.

    Nonetheless, place cells eventually demonstrated normal, spatially specific firing properties.

    Main text, paragraph beginning "During the Neurolab Space Shuttle mission", last sentence; supported by the paragraphs beginning "Spatial firing patterns of 16 active place cells" and "The mean spatial-information content for all 7" and Figures 2a, 2c and 3a; also the standfirst

  • si-comparable-to-preflight

    For rat 2, the spatial information per spike of the 16 active cells recorded on the Escher staircase on flight day 9 (1.13 +/- 0.09 bits) did not differ significantly from that of its 19 active preflight cells (1.12 +/- 0.08 bits), by Mann-Whitney test.

    An index of spatial tuning specificity ... did not significantly differ between cells recorded preflight and those recorded on FD9 (for active cells, defined as having a mean firing rate > 0.05 Hz on the track; mean information per spike ± s.e. preflight, 1.12 ± 0.08 bits, n = 19; FD9, 1.13 ± 0.09 bits, n = 16; not significant by Mann-Whitney)

    Main text, paragraph beginning "Spatial firing patterns of 16 active place cells", sentences 1 and 3; Figure 2a,b

    How it will be judged

    From the paper's text. Cells are the active cells of each session, mean firing rate above 0.05 Hz over the session on the track (main text), the FD9 cells from the Escher staircase session and the preflight cells from the rectangular track (Fig. 2b). The measure is Skaggs' information per spike (ref. 13), computed with off-track positions kept (Fig. 1 legend). The test is the paper's, a Mann-Whitney test of FD9 against preflight values; the level is not printed and is read as two-sided p < 0.05. Tolerances are the paper's standard errors, and 10% for the counts. Bin size, smoothing, minimum occupancy and which sessions are open; the readings M-a and M-b and S-a and S-b of the opening comment are each run. Answers of 2026-10-20, the paper gives none of these details. The paper compares one index computed the same way for both groups, so each comparison with preflight uses the same map reading on both sides; a reading that cannot be computed for one side is not run for that comparison, the outcome rests on the readings that can be run, and a test of M-b flight values against M-a preflight values is a variant, not judged. The paper names no preflight epoch (it does not mention ES or MC epochs), so under S-a each preflight epoch is a candidate session; under S-b each epoch of the recording day closest before launch is run on its own, the paper describing rat 2's preflight cells as recorded on one track, and neither epoch is preferred. A claim of absence, judged on the paper's test. Reproduced when, under every reading, the test does not find a difference and, under at least one reading, both means fall within tolerance; partially reproduced when the test finds no difference under every reading but no reading puts both means within tolerance; not reproduced when the test finds a difference under every reading; blocked as methods_underspecified when the readings disagree on the test. The difference of the means and the test's p-value are given under every reading.

  • fig3-stability-across-sessions

    For rat 3 on flight day 4, most of the 7 cells kept the same firing fields in a third session that followed the second immediately; the fourth cell lost its field, and a few other cells gained a field in session 3.

    Another recording session followed immediately, and most cells maintained the same firing fields in both sessions (Fig. 3b), demonstrating that the spatial tuning was stable across different exposures to the track.

    Main text, paragraph beginning "The mean spatial-information content for all 7", sentence 2; Figure 3a,b and legend

    How it will be judged

    The cells are the 7 active cells of session 2 (si-rat3-fd4-vs-preflight), followed into session 3 on the same track. The paper judges "the same firing fields" from the maps of Fig. 3 and gives no measure, so the measure is open; "most" is read as at least 4 of the 7. Readings of keeping a field, each run on the maps of reading M-a and of M-b (opening comment) (a) the Pearson correlation of a cell's session 2 and session 3 rate maps, over bins visited in both, exceeds the 95th percentile of the correlations between different cells across the two sessions; (b) the bin of peak rate in session 3 lies in the session 2 field, taken as the bins with at least 20% of the session 2 peak rate (a common field definition, not the paper's). "Most" means more than half of the cells shown, the active cells of session 2 (answer of 2026-10-20); with the paper's 7 cells this is at least 4, and with another count of active cells it is more than half of that count, not a count of 4. Cells are paired across sessions as the same unit ("the same seven cells", Fig. 3 legend); the paper says nothing on how the deposited maps are tied to units, so a pairing through the unit whose spike-count map a deposited map best matches is allowed for M-a when each map is tied to one unit and no unit to two maps, and is reported with the match; otherwise M-a is not run for this claim. Reproduced when more than half of the cells keep their field under every reading; not reproduced when half or fewer do under every reading; blocked as methods_underspecified when the readings disagree. Which cell lost its field, and the cells silent in session 2 that gained a field in session 3, are reported but not judged, as the figure's cell order is not given in the text.

  • direction-selectivity

    Some cells had place fields only when the rat moved counterclockwise through a location, showing the direction selectivity seen on such tracks on Earth.

    Some cells demonstrated place fields when the rat was moving only counterclockwise through a single location, thus demonstrating the direction selectivity that is seen on such tracks under normal terrestrial conditions

    Main text, paragraph beginning "The rats occasionally turned around on the track", sentences 1 and 2

    How it will be judged

    The paper names no session, cells or measure. Sessions are the in-flight Escher staircase sessions with position (FD4 and FD9), active cells as in si-comparable-to-preflight. Direction of travel is open; it is read as the sign of the rat's movement along the track's circuit, from successive positions, clockwise being the trained direction (main text). For each cell and position bin of the maps of reading M-b, rates are computed separately for clockwise and counterclockwise samples. A cell has a counterclockwise-only field when, at some bin with counterclockwise occupancy, its counterclockwise rate is at least 1 Hz and its clockwise spike count there is lower than expected from the counterclockwise rate by a one-sided Poisson test at p < 0.05 (the thresholds are standard choices, not the paper's). "Some cells" is read as at least two cells over all sessions. Reproduced when at least two cells have such a field; partially reproduced when one does; not reproduced when none does. The paper gives no method, no time base by direction and no track layout (answer of 2026-10-20); it allows no reading without time spent per bin in each direction and the direction of travel, so when the data lack these the claim is blocked as methods_underspecified.

  • theta-normal

    Hippocampal EEG showed normal theta rhythm during active locomotion, in all rats on flight days 4 and 9.

    Hippocampal EEG activity was also ... recorded during baseline and behavioral sessions in all rats on FD4 and FD9. Although the small number of subjects precluded a statistical analysis, they showed normal theta rhythm during active locomotion

    Main text, paragraph beginning "The rats occasionally turned around on the track", sentences 3 and 4

    How it will be judged

    Needs the hippocampal EEG of the FD4 and FD9 behavioral sessions. The paper reports no statistical analysis and no measure of "normal". Read as a peak in the EEG power spectrum within the theta band during locomotion that is absent or smaller during immobility. Locomotion is read as speed above the session median and the band as 6 to 10 Hz; both are standard choices, not given in the paper. Reproduced when every rat and flight day with EEG shows this; partially reproduced when some do; not reproduced when none does.

  • ripples-normal

    Hippocampal EEG showed normal sharp waves and ripples during quiet inactivity in the sleeping pouch, on both flight days.

    they showed normal theta rhythm during active locomotion and normal sharp waves and ripples15 during quiet inactivity in the sleeping pouch on both flight days.

    Main text, paragraph beginning "The rats occasionally turned around on the track", sentence 4

    How it will be judged

    Needs the hippocampal EEG of the sleeping pouch baseline sessions on FD4 and FD9. The paper reports no statistical analysis and no detection method; it cites Buzsaki 1986 (ref. 15) for the events. Read as ripple events in the 100 to 250 Hz band, detected where the band envelope exceeds its mean by 3 standard deviations during immobility, coinciding with a large negative sharp wave in the filtered 0.5 to 30 Hz signal. The band, the threshold and the sharp wave filter are standard choices, not given in the paper or by ref. 15 as cited. Reproduced when every rat and flight day with pouch EEG shows such events; partially reproduced when some do; not reproduced when none does.

Reproduced when this holds for both rats under every reading; partially reproduced when it holds for one rat; not reproduced when it holds for neither; blocked as methods_underspecified when readings disagree. That the patterns differed between the rats has no measure in the paper and is reported, not judged.
