  • array-dataset-composition

    The two array datasets, monkey J-array recorded 2009-09-18 and monkey N-array recorded 2010-09-23, each present 108 reach conditions and comprise 146 and 218 isolations (a mix of single and multi-unit isolations).

    For the monkey J-array and N-array datasets, 108 conditions were presented in the same recording session ... These array-based datasets involved, respectively, 146 and 218 single isolations (each a mix of single and multi-unit isolations)

    Methods, Recordings and task design, paragraphs 2 and 3; Figure 3e and 3f legend

    How it will be judged

    Conditions are the distinct maze_condition values above 0 among the trials the Methods select; isolations are the rows of the Units table. Reproduced when both sessions are in the dandiset with all four values within tolerance; partially reproduced when the sessions and condition counts match but a unit count does not; not reproduced otherwise.

  • fig3ef-same-direction

    In the first jPCA plane the neural state rotates in the same direction for all conditions of the J-array and N-array datasets, including reaches in opposite directions.

    Second, the neural state rotates in the same direction across conditions (different traces) ... Yet for each dataset the neural state rotates in the same direction across conditions

    Results, Quasi-rhythmic responses during reaching, paragraph 4; Figure 3e and 3f

    How it will be judged

    For each condition, the net change of the polar angle of its state in the oriented jPC plane from -50 to +150 ms. Reproduced when at least 90% of the 108 conditions rotate counter-clockwise (positive) in each dataset; partially reproduced when at least 75% do in each, or 90% in one only; not reproduced otherwise. The values under the shuffle controls of suppfig3-shuffle-controls are shown as reference.

  • fig3-independent-of-curvature

    The rotations do not relate directly to reach curvature; the neural state rotates the same way for clockwise-curving, counter-clockwise-curving and straight reaches.

    Finally, state-space rotations do not relate directly to reach curvature ... Yet for each dataset the neural state rotates in the same direction across conditions

    Results, Quasi-rhythmic responses during reaching, paragraph 4; Figure 3e and 3f

    How it will be judged

    Each condition's reach is classed by the net change of heading of its mean hand path from movement onset to the end of the window (+500 ms) as clockwise (below -20 degrees), counter-clockwise (above +20 degrees) or straight. Reproduced when, in each dataset, at least 90% of the conditions in each class with at least five conditions rotate counter-clockwise in the jPC plane; partially reproduced when this holds for every class at 75%; not reproduced otherwise. A logistic fit of rotation direction on curvature is reported as well.

  • fig3-phase-from-prep-state

    The rotation phase follows from the preparatory state; knowing the initial state in the jPC plane, the subsequent states are reasonably predictable.

    Third, the rotation phase follows naturally from the preparatory state ... If one knows the initial population-level preparatory state (fig. 3, circles) subsequent states are reasonably predictable

    Results, Quasi-rhythmic responses during reaching, paragraphs 4 and 5; Figure 3

    How it will be judged

    Writing each condition's state in the plane as a complex number, the state at +150 ms is predicted from the state at -50 ms by one complex gain (a rotation and a scaling) shared by all conditions, fitted leaving one condition out. Reproduced when the cross-validated R2 is at least 0.5 in each dataset and above the median R2 of shuffle 3 of suppfig3-shuffle-controls (which keeps preparatory states and reassigns the movement activity); partially reproduced when this holds in one dataset; not reproduced otherwise.

  • fig6b-skew-fit

  • rotation-frequency

    The rotations are brief and fast, up to about 2.5 Hz; the largest eigenvalues of M_skew in the monkey A and B datasets are 8.8 to 13.5 radians/s.

    Their brevity and high frequency (up to ~2.5 Hz) makes them easy to miss ... the largest eigenvalues of Mskew were actually slightly smaller for fast reaches (8.8 versus 9.8 radians/s for monkey A, 12.2 versus 13.5 radians/s for monkey B)

    Results, Quasi-rhythmic responses during reaching, paragraph 5, and Population-level quantification, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    Frequency is the magnitude of the largest eigenvalue of M_skew (radians/s) over 2 pi. Reproduced when it lies within 1.26 to 2.75 Hz in each dataset (the range of the reported eigenvalues, 1.40 to 2.15 Hz, and the stated maximum of 2.5 Hz, widened by 10% of the bounds); partially reproduced when in one; not reproduced otherwise.

  • suppfig3-shuffle-controls

    Three shuffle controls that keep preparatory activity and invert or reassign the peri-movement activity largely remove the rotational structure found by jPCA, so jPCA does not find rotations that are not there.

    Multiple ‘shuffle’ controls demonstrate that jPCA does not find rotational structure when such structure is not present ... largely lost following shuffling

    Supplementary Figures 2 and 3; Results, Controls regarding rotational structure, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    The dividing point is 50 ms after the go cue, taken per dataset as the median go-cue time relative to movement onset plus 50 ms. Shuffle 1 (half the conditions inverted per unit) and 3 (movement activity reassigned) are run 20 times with fixed seeds, shuffle 2 once. Rotation strength is R2(M_skew) in 6-D. Reproduced when, in each dataset and for each shuffle, every run is below the original and the median is at most 75% of it; partially reproduced when this holds for at least two shuffles in each dataset or all three in one; not reproduced otherwise. The fraction of conditions rotating counter-clockwise and the R2 ratio are shown as well.
