  • fig1b-accepting-escalates

    During mating the probability of accepting behaviours (lordosis, wiggle) increases gradually over the interaction, while resistance behaviours (dart, top up, kick, turn) first increase and then slowly decrease.

    The behaviours were dynamic, with the probability of accepting behaviours gradually increasing, whereas resistance behaviours initially increased and then slowly decreased (Fig. 1b and Extended Data Fig. 1a).

    Figure 1b, Extended Data Figure 1a, Results "Dynamics of female behaviours in mating", paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Per trial, the fraction of each 20-s bin occupied by each group, from male entry (into_male_cage) to 600 s after it, averaged over trials (bins with fewer than 5 trials are dropped). Accepting escalates if the Spearman correlation of the mean curve with time is positive with p below 0.05 and the mean of the last third of the bins exceeds that of the first third. Resistance rises then falls if the peak of the mean curve, after a 3-bin running mean, is later than the first bin and within the first two thirds, and the mean of the last third is below the peak. Trial sets read in turn are all trials with female annotations, and only receptive trials (any lordosis or wiggle), because the paper's 74 trials are not identified. Reproduced if both parts hold under a reading; partially if one part holds.

  • fig1c-responsive-dominates

    Females spend 83.9% of their mating behaviour time in male-responsive behaviours (accepting, resistance, staying) and 16.1% in self-initiated ones (approach, sniff, check genital area).

    Females spent six times longer displaying male-responsive (83.9%) versus self-initiated behaviours (16.1%; Fig. 1c).

    Figure 1c, Results "Dynamics of female behaviours in mating", paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    Responsive time is the summed duration of lordose, wiggle, stay, dart, top up, kick and turn; self-initiated is approach, sniff and check genital. Readings are the mean over trials of the per-trial fraction, and the pooled fraction over all trials, each on all annotated trials and on receptive trials. Reproduced if any reading is within the tolerance.

  • fig2c-few-tuned-cells

    Only a small percentage of VMHvl alpha-cells (approximately 2 to 13%) are tuned to any one mating behaviour by choice probability; most show mixed selectivity.

    a relatively small percentage of VMHvl α-cells (approximately 2–13%) were ‘tuned’ to specific mating behaviours (Fig. 2c and Extended Data Fig. 2a,b), whereas the majority exhibited ‘mixed selectivity’

    Figure 2c, Extended Data Figure 2a,b, Results "Tuning of female VMHvl neurons in mating", paragraph 2, Methods "Choice probability"

    How it will be judged

    In the 15 receptive sessions, 1-s bins of the z-scored trace; a bin belongs to a behaviour when the behaviour covers at least half of it. Choice probability is the ROC area for behaviour bins against the other bins of the interaction (male entry to end of annotation); a cell is tuned if it is above 0.7 or below 0.3 and outside 2 SD of 1000 label shuffles. Behaviours with at least 10 bins in at least 5 mice are analysed. Reproduced if the pooled percentage of tuned cells is between 1% and 15% for every behaviour analysed (the paper says "approximately"); partially if the median over behaviours is in that range.

Reproduced if all three values are within tolerance under a reading; partially if the ordering (female below male, both at least male) holds and all three are below 0.25, the paper's point being that behaviour explains little.
