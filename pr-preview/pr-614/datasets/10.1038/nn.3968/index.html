  • hd-cell-fraction

    Of the recorded cells, 242 of 720 in the anterior thalamus and 111 of 357 in the PoS were classified as HD cells, 353 of 1077 in all.

    242 out of 720 recorded cells (41% on average) in the anterior thalamus, and 111 out of 357 (31%) cells in the PoS were classified as HD cells.

    Methods, Head direction cells, paragraph 2; Results, first paragraph

    How it will be judged

    HD cells are counted by the rule above in every session with position. The regions cannot be separated, so the pooled count and fraction are tested. The denominator can be read as (a) the units with at least one spike in the waking epoch, or (b) every row of the units tables in the sessions with position. Reproduced when both values are within tolerance under one reading; partially reproduced when one of them is. The thalamic split (242 of 720) and the PoS split (111 of 357) are blocked for lack of region labels; the count in the mice that appear to be thalamus only is shown as a variant and does not decide the outcome.

    How it will be judged

  • fig2f-sws-tenfold-faster

    The angular velocity of the internal HD signal, estimated from the temporal profile of pairwise cross-correlograms, is about tenfold faster during SWS than during waking.

    The angular velocity of the internal HD signal, either estimated by a Bayesian decoder or from the temporal profile of pairwise cross-correlograms ... was approximately tenfold faster during SWS than during waking (Fig. 2e,f)

    Results, second paragraph; Figure 2e,f; Abstract ("accelerated tenfold")

    How it will be judged

    The method of the paper's Methods (Estimation of angular velocity). For each pair the angular correlation of the two waking tuning curves as a function of offset; angular speed drawn from a chi-squared distribution with 3 degrees of freedom scaled to a mean v, with both directions of turning equally likely; the expected cross-correlogram at lag tau as the mean over speeds of the angular correlation at speed times tau. The observed cross-correlogram (10 ms bins, lags to 4 s, ratio to the value expected for independent firing) is fitted by scale times expected plus offset, with v found by a coarse grid and then a Nelder-Mead search. The paper does not say how expected and observed are put on one scale, so the free scale and offset are chosen here. Pairs as in fig2b reading (a), and a pair is kept when its fit in each state does not end at the edge of the search range. Reproduced when the median over pairs of the SWS to waking ratio is within tolerance; partially reproduced when it is outside but above 2 with SWS faster than waking in most pairs (sign test p < 0.05); not reproduced otherwise. The ratio is also given per mouse.

  • fig4b-noise-correlations

    During waking, the observed correlations of HD cell pairs exceed those expected from independent rate-coded neurons for pairs with overlapping fields (0 plus or minus 15 degrees), and fall below them for pairs at about 60 degrees.

    However, correlations expected from independent rate-coded neurons could not fully account for the observed pairwise correlations of HD cells (Fig. 4a). ... were observed for neuron pairs with overlapping fields (0±15°), with a marked negative signal correlation at 60±15° offset (Fig. 4b,c)

    Results, fourth paragraph; Figure 4a,b

    How it will be judged

    Waking only, following the Methods' formula. For each cell an intensity function, its waking tuning curve read at the head direction in 10 ms bins (expected spikes per bin); for each pair the mean cross-product of the two binned spike trains and of the two intensity functions at each lag, and the noise correlation as their difference averaged over lags from -50 to +50 ms. Pairs as in fig2b reading (a). Reproduced when the noise correlation is above 0 for pairs at 0 plus or minus 15 degrees and below 0 for pairs at 60 plus or minus 15 degrees, each by a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test at p < 0.05; partially reproduced when one of the two holds. This criterion replaces one written before the first run, which put both cross-correlations on a Pearson scale (see the opening comment); that version is shown as a variant.

  • decoding-tracks-hd

    The HD signal decoded with a Bayesian decoder from the HD cells faithfully tracks the head direction of the waking mouse.

    These estimates faithfully tracked the head position in the waking mouse.

    Methods, Bayesian reconstruction of head-direction; Figure 1c

    How it will be judged

    In each session with at least 6 HD cells, a Poisson Bayesian decoder (10 ms bins, counts smoothed with a 300 ms s.d. Gaussian, flat prior, 6 degree angle bins) trained on tuning curves from one half of the waking epoch and tested on the other, both ways. The paper gives no number, so "faithfully" is fixed here as a median absolute error of 30 degrees or less. Reproduced when the pooled median error is 30 degrees or less and at least 80% of the sessions meet it; partially reproduced when the pooled median meets it and fewer sessions do. The paper decodes from one structure at a time; here the HD cells of a session are pooled.
