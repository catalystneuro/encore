    How it will be judged

    Putative pyramidal cells (baseline-epoch rate below 5 Hz), each session counted once.   • fig1i-swr-decrease

    Light-induced spike responses of individual pyramidal cells decreased during sharp-wave ripples (n = 485 neurons, P < 1e-55, Wilcoxon paired signed-rank test).

    light-induced spike responses in individual pyramidal cells decreased during SPW-Rs (ΔRate; Fig. 1, F to I).

    Main text, paragraph 3; Figure 1, F to I

    How it will be judged

    Light-responsive putative pyramidal cells of the two sessions, over the home-cage stimulation epochs (PreStim and PostStim). For each cell, the light rate in its light windows and the control rate in control windows, each split by whether the window overlaps a deposited ripple interval. Readings of the response are (a) the rate difference, light minus control (Hz), (b) the ratio light over control. Reproduced when, under one reading, the response inside ripples is lower than outside them by a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test at p < 0.05 with a negative median difference. The paper's p-value cannot be reached with the few cells of two sessions and is not part of the criterion; cells with no control spikes inside ripples are left out of reading (b).

  • figs10-swr-gain-vs-baseline-rate

    The firing-rate change of pyramidal neurons during SPW-Rs is negatively correlated with their baseline firing rate (rho = -0.19, P < 1e-7).

    the negative correlation between firing-rate change during SPW-R and baseline firing rates of neurons

    Main text, paragraph 3 (figure S10)

    How it will be judged

    Every putative pyramidal cell of every session with at least 50 deposited ripples in its first epoch. Baseline rate is the rate in the first epoch; the SPW-R change is measured in the same epoch, inside the deposited ripple intervals against outside them. Readings of the change are (a) log2 of the ratio of the rate inside to the rate outside (cells with no spikes inside ripples left out), (b) the difference inside minus outside (Hz). Reproduced when, under one reading, rho is within tolerance and p < 1e-7. Partially reproduced when rho is negative with p < 0.05 under one reading but no reading meets the full criterion. The tolerance is the half-width of the 95% interval of a Spearman rho at the paper's n of 822, since the paper gives no interval and 10% of 0.19 is below the sampling error.

  • fig2b-theta-trough-gain

    During track running, the light-induced rate gain is largest at the trough of the theta cycle (P < 1e-6, Wilcoxon test).

    We observed a gain at the trough of the theta cycle, the phase corresponding to the strongest synchrony of pyramidal neurons (Fig. 2A)

    Main text, paragraph 4; Figure 2, A and B

    How it will be judged

    Light-responsive putative pyramidal cells of the two sessions, Maze epoch, during laps. Theta phase from the LFP channel with the largest ripple power (6 to 10 Hz, Hilbert), taken at the middle of each light or control window. Gain is light rate minus control rate in the trough half of the cycle (phase beyond +-90 degrees from the peak) and in the peak half. Reproduced when the gain in the trough half is higher by a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test at p < 0.05. The gain in six 60-degree bins is shown as a variant.
