  • fig1c-psychometric

    Mice of both genotypes show sigmoidal psychometric curves, with the proportion of rightward choices increasing as the stimulus contrast increases on the right.

    Both WT (n = 11) and KO (n = 8) animals exhibited typical sigmoidal psychometric curves (Figure 1C), with an increasing likelihood of rightward choices as stimulus contrast increased in that direction

    Figure 1C; Results, Nrxn1α mice reliably leverage sensory evidence to guide decision-making, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    Over the nine signed contrasts of the session (left contrasts negative), the proportion of rightward choices has a Spearman correlation with signed contrast of at least 0.9, and the proportion at +100% exceeds that at -100% by at least 0.5. A lapse-and-erf psychometric fit (bias, slope, two lapses) is reported, with its slope.

  • fig1d-psychometric-params-genotype

    Psychometric bias, slope and lapse rates do not differ between WT and KO mice (bias p = 0.94, slope p = 0.95, left lapse p = 0.16, right lapse p = 0.22).

    Quantification of psychometric parameters (Figure 1D) showed no significant differences between WT and KO mice in bias (p = 0.94), slope (p = 0.95), left lapse (p = 0.16), or right lapse (p = 0.22)

    Figure 1D; Results, Nrxn1α mice reliably leverage sensory evidence to guide decision-making, paragraph 2
  • fig1e-rt-contrast

    Figure 1E

    How it will be judged

    Over choice trials with a wheel movement onset after the stimulus onset, the Spearman correlation between absolute contrast and reaction time is negative with p < 0.05, and the median reaction time at 0% contrast exceeds that at 100%.

  • fig2bc-prior-shift

    Mice use the block prior; rightward choices are more frequent in right-likely blocks than in left-likely blocks, most clearly when contrast is low, shifting the psychometric curve toward the likely side (shown for WT, and present but smaller in KO).

    This reliance on priors is demonstrated in WT animals by a pronounced shift in the change of rightward choices conditioned by block-type (right-likely - left-likely blocks) and in their psychometric curves towards the higher probability stimulus side ... Nrxn1α KO animals display a more modest shift

    Figure 2B and 2C; Results, Nrxn1α mice exhibit slower adaptation to shifting reward contingencies, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    At 0% contrast, the proportion of rightward choices in right-likely (20:80) blocks minus that in left-likely (80:20) blocks is greater than zero by a one-sided Fisher exact test at p < 0.05. The difference at every signed contrast and the bias of psychometric fits per block are reported.

  • fig2bd-ko-smaller-shift

    KO mice shift their choices less between biased blocks than WT mice, with a difference in psychometric bias and none in slope or lapses.

    Nrxn1α KO animals display a more modest shift (Figure 2B, C) ... Parameters of individual fits of the psychometric curves reveal specific changes in bias, with no changes in the other parameters

    Figure 2B to 2D; Results, Nrxn1α mice exhibit slower adaptation to shifting reward contingencies, paragraph 1
  • fig2ef-block-adaptation

    After a block switch, mice adapt their choices toward the newly likely side, and the proportion of choices toward the block side rises from an initial value A to an asymptote B with a time constant tau.

    Mean proportion of probability of choices towards block side (e.g. right choices during right likely block and left choices for left likely block). WT mice adapt more strongly and consistently than KO mice

    Figure 2E to 2G; Results, Nrxn1α mice exhibit slower adaptation to shifting reward contingencies, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Over the switches between biased blocks, the proportion of choices toward the new block side, by trial number after the switch (0 to 39), all contrasts. Fitted with B - (B - A) exp(-t / tau). Reproduced when the fit gives B > A with tau between 0 and 40 trials, and the proportion in trials 20 to 39 exceeds that in trials 0 to 4 by a one-sided Fisher exact test at p < 0.05. Trials at contrasts of 12.5% or less are shown as a variant.

  • fig2g-ko-slower-adaptation

    we fit a parametric curve to each animal’s performance trajectory, demonstrating significantly longer tau values (p = 0.004) and lower initial performance (p = 0.041) immediately after the switch in KO animals compared to WT littermates

    Figure 2G; Results, Nrxn1α mice exhibit slower adaptation to shifting reward contingencies, paragraph 1
  • fig3b-contralateral-visual

    Visual cortex (VISp) responds robustly to contralateral visual stimuli.

    Task-evoked responses in the visual cortex (VISp) showed robust activation following contralateral visual stimuli

    Figure 3B; Results, Widefield calcium imaging reveals altered cortical dynamics in Nrxn1α KO mice during task performance, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    The dandiset has no registration to the Allen atlas, so VISp and the hemispheres cannot be named; the lateralization is tested in image coordinates. The midline is the column of lowest mean fluorescence in the middle 40% of columns of the mean image. Evoked maps are the mean dF/F 0.1 to 0.4 s after onset minus -0.3 to 0 s, for trials at 25% and 100% contrast. Lateralized when the peak pixel of the right-minus-left stimulus map and the peak pixel of the left-minus-right map lie on opposite sides of the midline, and at each peak the trials of the stimulus that defines it respond more than those of the other side (one-sided Mann-Whitney p < 0.05). Partially reproduced when lateralized, since the area cannot be identified as VISp; not reproduced otherwise.

  • fig4c-full-cvr2

    The full linear encoding model explains more than half of single-trial cortical variance (cvR2 0.60 +/- 0.02 in KO and 0.54 +/- 0.02 in WT).

    Model performance, evaluated via cross-validated R2, was reassuringly high for both cohorts of mice, accounting for more than half of the total observed single trial variance

  • fig4cd-movement-dominates

    Movement predictors account for most of the explained variance; shuffling them reduces cvR2 by much more than shuffling task predictors (delta R2 for movement 0.40 +/- 0.02 in KO and 0.33 +/- 0.02 in WT; task shuffle beta -0.02).

    Shuffling movement predictors produced a large reduction in CVR2 ... whereas shuffling task predictors had little effect ... removing movement predictors reduced variance explained much more than removing task predictors overall

    Figure 4C and 4D; Results, An encoding model reveals unique contributions of movement variables to cortical activity in Nrxn1α KO mice, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    delta R2 is the full model's cvR2 minus that of the model with the group shuffled, under the same reading as fig4c-full-cvr2. The tolerance spans both group means widened by 10% of each bound (0.297 to 0.440). Reproduced when, under one reading, movement delta R2 is in that range and task delta R2 is below one fifth of it. Partially reproduced when movement delta R2 exceeds task delta R2 under every reading but the range is not met under any. Not reproduced when task delta R2 is at least the movement delta R2 under any reading.
