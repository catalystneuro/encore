  • spatial-plane-variance

    The spatial plane captures a large part of the variance of the condition-averaged activity for the ring, horizontal, vertical and 3-rings tasks; 66.8, 64.4, 47.7 and 68.2% for J and 33.7, 30.7, 31.0 and 30.1% for R (p < 1e-4).

    The spatial plane captures a large percentage of the overall variance of the trial-averaged neural responses for the ring, horizontal, vertical, and 3-rings tasks: 66.8%, 64.4%, 47.7%, and 68.2% (monkey J), 33.7%, 30.7%, 31.0%, 30.1% (monkey R)

    Results, Structure of neural activity during delay period, paragraph 2; Figure 2a

    How it will be judged

    For each task, the variance of its condition averages (centred within the task) that lies in the plane (orthonormalised), as a share of (a) their total variance over all PMd channels, or (b) their variance within the top PCs. R's horizontal and vertical tasks are taken (a) by the dates of the Methods, or (b) by the target geometry in the files. J horizontal is not in the dandiset. Reproduced when, under one pairing of readings, all R values and all J values are within tolerance; partially reproduced when all R values are, or when at least four of the seven are. The p-value is compared with planes fit to target positions shuffled across conditions (1000 shuffles).

  • plane-timecourse

    After target onset the neural state in the spatial plane starts at the center, diverges after 50 ms (R 75 ms) and converges to the steady delay state after 300 ms (R 400 ms).

    at target onset, the neural states projected onto this plane start at the center of this plane, start to diverge after 50 ms (R: 75 ms), and converge to the steady neural states after J: 300 ms, R: 400ms

    Results, Structure of neural activity during delay period, paragraph 2; Figure 2c

    How it will be judged

    Tolerance of 50 ms because the times are round values read from a figure and the thresholds are chosen here. Delay activity in 20 ms bins aligned on target onset, smoothed with a 3-bin boxcar, projected onto the plane; condition averages at each time use trials whose go cue comes after the bin. d(t) is the mean over conditions of the distance of the condition's state from its converged delay state (the 200 ms before the go cue). With d0 the mean of d over the 100 ms before target onset and dmin its minimum after onset, D(t) = (d(t) - dmin) / (d0 - dmin). Divergence is the first bin after onset with D below 0.9, convergence the first with D below 0.1. Reproduced when all four times are within tolerance; partially reproduced when the times of one monkey are, or both divergence times are.

    Both on-axis ... and off-axis ... deviations in the neural state were positively correlated with on-axis and off-axis deviations in the position of max-speed for both monkeys

Pearson correlation of neural with kinematic deviation, separately on and off axis, per monkey, over (a) all tasks pooled, or (b) the 3-rings task alone. Reproduced when under one reading all four correlations are positive with two-sided p < 0.05; partially reproduced when two or three are.

  • speed-axis-within-distance

    The speed axis of delay activity varies with maximum speed for reaches to the same target distance, for 11 of 12 distances in J and 11 of 12 in R (p < 0.05), so it encodes speed independently of distance.

    We then tested whether the neural state in this dimension varied with speed for reaches to the same target, and found that for almost all distances it did (J: 11/12 distances in the vertical and horizontal tasks, R: 11/12, p < 0.05)

    How it will be judged

Per distance, Pearson correlation of the speed-axis projection with maximum speed, counted when positive with p < 0.05. The axis is fit (a) on all trials of all tasks, or (b) on the horizontal and vertical trials. Reproduced when both counts are within tolerance under one reading; partially reproduced when one is. Projections cross-validated in 10 folds are shown as a variant. J has only vertical sessions here.

  • distance-after-speed

    After regressing out speed from the neural activity, a positive correlation with target distance remains (p < 1e-4 for J and R).

    we found that after regressing out speed from the neural activity, there is still a positive correlation

    Results, Structure of neural activity during delay period, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged

    Each channel's delay activity is regressed on maximum speed and the residuals kept. A linear regression from the residuals to target distance, over all trials of all tasks, is cross-validated in 10 folds (fixed seed); the Pearson correlation of the out-of-fold predictions with distance must be positive with p < 1e-4 in both monkeys for reproduced, in one for partially reproduced. The in-sample fit is shown as a variant.

    How it will be judged

Each axis is normalised to unit length and projected onto the top PCs (6 J, 5 R); the ratio is the norm of the distance projection over that of the speed projection. Reproduced when both ratios are within tolerance; partially reproduced when one is.

  • arc-error-distance-independent

    How it will be judged

The test restricted to errors within the range of the 4 cm ring is shown as a variant, with the size of the differences.

  • distance-error-and-plane-projection

    Mean distance-classification error is slightly but significantly lower than the arc-length error of direction classification, and projecting the activity onto the spatial plane before classification raises it (J 2.64 to 3.03 cm; R given as 2.78 and 3.27 cm), to the level of the direction errors.

    we found that the mean classification error for distance classification was significantly (albeit only slightly) lower than the errors for directional classification ... The resulting classification error rate (Fig 4d, blue bars) is significantly greater than classification using the full dimensional neural activity

    Results, Accuracy of endpoint prediction from delay activity, paragraph 4; Figure 4d

    How it will be judged

    The text gives R as "2.78 cm v.s. 3.27 cm" after J's "3.03 cm v.s. 2.64 cm" (projected first), so R can be read as (a) full 2.78 and projected 3.27, as the claim that projection raises the error implies, or (b) projected 2.78 and full 3.27. Distance errors are |true - predicted| in cm from the distance SVM. Reproduced when under one reading all four values are within tolerance and the projected error exceeds the full one by a Welch t-test over trials at p < 0.05 in both monkeys; partially reproduced when the increase holds in both but some values are outside tolerance, or when it holds in one monkey.
