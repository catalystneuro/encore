  • data-sessions-and-trials

    Thirty-seven receptor cells were recorded from 23 animals, giving 132 sessions, of which 86 met the firing-rate and stationarity conditions; each stimulus was repeated 98 to 503 times, 172 on average.

    Thirty-seven receptor cells were recorded, from 23 animals. Each cell was tested with two or more stimuli, resulting in 132 data sets in total ... There were 86 out of 132 data sets that fulfilled these two conditions ... N repetitions of each stimulus were presented, with N ranging between 98 and 503 (average 172)

    Materials and Methods, Electrophysiology and stimulus design, paragraphs 1, 4 and 5

    How it will be judged

    The selection keeps sessions whose trial-to-trial SD of the firing rate is below 35 Hz and whose mean rate is about 100 Hz (Methods, paragraph 4). The paper gives no bound for about 100 Hz, nor the window of the per-trial rate. Readings for the rate condition are (a) the SD condition alone; (b) the SD condition and a target rate of 100 Hz where the data record the target. Per-trial rates are taken over 200 to 1000 ms (the paper discards the first 200 ms in all results) and, as a variant, over the whole 1 s. The trial counts and the average SD of 6 Hz (paragraph 4, in parentheses) are read (a) over the 86 kept sessions, (b) over all sessions. Reproduced when every value is within tolerance under one reading.

    How it will be judged

    The model of Eq. 2 per session, as the Methods (Model neurons) give it. The stimulus s(t) is the AM signal in dB, thresholded at s_TH before filtering; h(tau) from the cross-correlation of the spike train with s*(t); t_ref the session's minimal ISI; spikes by an inhomogeneous Poisson process with rate r_s(t), zero for t_ref after each spike, at 0.1 ms steps, with as many trials as recorded. s_TH is measured (Methods, paragraph 2); if the data set does not hold it, the claim is not testable as the paper ran it. Open details are the filter length, which Eq. 2 writes as T, the symbol the Methods define as the duration of the AM signal, read as (a) T as defined there (the cross-correlation over all lags of the 200 to 1000 ms analysis window) and (b) truncated at 25 ms (the nBTA window before a burst, Methods, Estimation of burst-triggered averages), and the filter's scale, which the cross-correlation does not fix, read as (a) the cross-correlation divided by the variance of s*(t), (b) scaled so the model's mean rate equals the session's. Bursting classification and limiting ISI as for the data. Reproduced when both values are within tolerance under one reading. The linear model of Figure 3 (no thresholding) is shown with the same measures.

  • fig7ab-information-per-burst-increases
