  • localiser-masks-ventral-dorsal

    The localiser masks for upper visual field presentation lie largely in ventral subregions of V1 to V3 and those for lower visual field presentation in dorsal subregions.

    These upper and lower presentation masks were largely confined to ventral and dorsal subregions, respectively

    Materials & Methods, Localiser, paragraph 1; Figure 2B

    How it will be judged

    Masks as in the Methods, Localiser, and glm_prep.py (a GLM with one regressor for all image events of a presentation field; nodes of V1, V2 or V3 with t above 2.58). The paper gives no measure of "largely confined" and shows one example participant (Figure 2B), so the rule below is not from the paper and the outcome rests on it as an assumption. Reproduced when, for most participants, most mask nodes of the upper masks lie in the ventral half of each area and most of the lower masks in the dorsal half, the split into ventral and dorsal halves following the retinotopic definition of each area. The fraction of nodes in the expected half is reported per participant, area and mask. Needs the participants' cortical surfaces and V1 to V3 definitions.

  • lower-field-below-greater

    Responses to images sourced from below fixation were higher than those from above fixation when presented in the lower visual field ... 0.80 psc (SE = 0.02) and 0.72 psc (SE = 0.02), respectively. This difference was statistically significant ( t 6 = 5.17, p = 0.002)

    Results and Discussion, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    From group.py stats(). Per participant, the psc response averaged over images and over V1, V2 and V3, for each presentation and source; a paired t-test (scipy ttest_rel, two-sided) of above against below for lower field presentation. The means are taken over participants; the tolerance on each mean is the paper's SE. The SE in group.py is computed after removing each participant's mean over the four conditions and adding the grand mean (the normalisation the Figure 3 legend names), with ddof 1 over the square root of 7; it is reported, and that normalisation leaves the means and the t value unchanged. Reproduced when both means and t are within tolerance and p < 0.05 with below higher; partially reproduced when the test holds and a value is outside tolerance.

  • fig5a-mean-luminance

    Higher mean luminance of a patch pair goes with a larger upper minus lower response difference (Spearman r = 0.31, p = 0.016).

    higher levels of mean luminance were related to greater differences in the responses evoked by upper and lower visual field presentation (Spearman's r = 0.31, p = 0.016)

    Results and Discussion, paragraph 4; Figure 5A

    How it will be judged

    From the Methods, Image patch analysis, and imstats.py. Each patch is cut as a 353 by 353 pixel square from the stimulus screenshot at its aperture, converted to DKL space with the monitor's measured CIE coordinates; the mean is over pixels inside a circular mask of radius 0.8 of the square (run_hist); the left and right patches are averaged; Spearman correlation over the 60 image and source values with the difference term of fig4-top-five-matching-source. Reproduced when r is within tolerance and p < 0.05; partially reproduced when r is positive with p < 0.05 and outside tolerance. The monitor's CIE coordinates are not given in the paper or the code (they come from a local PsychoPy monitor calibration), nor is the DKL conversion function (monitors.conversions, not linked); the author decides whether a substitute can be used (author, step 4).

  • fig5bc-spatial-frequency

    Higher output of luminance Gabor filters at 1 and 2 cycles per degree goes with a larger upper minus lower response difference (Spearman r = 0.32, p = 0.013 and r = 0.26, p = 0.049).

    (Spearman's r = 0.32, p = 0.013; Spearman's r = 0.26, p = 0.049; for spatial frequencies of 1 and 2 cycles per degree of visual angle, respectively)

    Results and Discussion, paragraph 5; Figures 5B and 5C

    How it will be judged

    Patches as in fig5a-mean-luminance, multiplied by a circular mask of radius 0.9 (run_filter); filters as in the Methods and _get_filter_bank (odd-symmetric Gabors, one octave bandwidth, 1 to 16 cpd, 0 to 135 degrees); the response is the square root of the summed squared convolution output; averaged over orientation and over left and right patches. The filter wavelengths in pixels come from psychopy deg2pix with the local "BOLDscreen" monitor profile, which is not in the code; the paper's 1,920 pixels over 24.4 degrees gives about 78.7 pixels per degree, and its 128 pixel patches upsampled by 2.75 to fill a 4.5 degree aperture give about 78.2. The value is open; use about 78.7 and show the other. The paper names Spearman correlation; imstats.py compare_filt_with_data uses Pearson for spatial frequency. Spearman is the main test (Methods first) and Pearson is shown as a variant. Reproduced when both r are within tolerance and both p < 0.05 (p rounding to 0.049 or less for 2 cpd counts as significant); partially reproduced when one holds.
