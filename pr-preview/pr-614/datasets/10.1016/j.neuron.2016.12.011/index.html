From the paper Physiological Properties and Behavioral Correlates of Hippocampal Granule Cells and Mossy Cells · claims file · not yet checked

  • unit-counts

    The second-stage classification divided dentate excitatory neurons into 586 putative granule cells (GC) and 500 putative mossy cells (MC).

    K-means clustering on the three sets of features reliably segregated excitatory neurons as putative granule cells (n = 586) and putative mossy cells (n = 500)

    Results, Identification of granule cells and mossy cells in extracellular recordings, paragraph 2; Figure 1F

    How it will be judged

    Counted from the units' cell_type labels over all files, once per recording day. Reproduced when both counts are within tolerance; partially reproduced when one is.

  • first-stage-labels

    Narrow-waveform interneurons have a trough-to-peak latency below 0.425 ms, and among wide-waveform units the excitatory cells have a burst index above the threshold separating them from wide-waveform interneurons.

    Units with trough-to-peak latency < 0.425 ms were classified as narrow-waveform putative interneurons ... Wide-waveform units were grouped into putative excitatory cells (E cells, red; burst index>1.8) and wide-waveform putative interneurons

    Figure 1A; STAR Methods, Unit classification

    How it will be judged

    Trough-to-peak latency from the mean of each unit's first 200 stored waveforms, on the channel of largest amplitude, upsampled to 100 kHz; burst index from the spike times as the Methods define it. The threshold reads 1.2 in the Methods and 1.8 in the Figure 1A legend; both readings are tested. Reproduced when, under one reading, at least 90% of labelled narrow-waveform units are below 0.425 ms, at least 90% of GC and MC are at or above 0.425 ms with a burst index above the threshold, and at least 90% of wide-waveform interneurons are at or above 0.425 ms with a burst index at or below it. Partially reproduced when two of the three groups meet it.

  • interneuron-rates

    Narrow-waveform interneurons fire faster than wide-waveform interneurons (medians 7.97 and 2.11 Hz; Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.00001).

    The mean firing rates of narrow (median = 7.97 Hz) and wide-waveform (median = 2.11 Hz) interneurons were significantly different

    Results, Identification of granule cells and mossy cells in extracellular recordings, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    The rate is not defined in the paper; readings are (a) spikes over the span of the recording and (b) the rate in `awake` periods. The dandiset's interneuron labels include CA3 interneurons, which the paper counts apart. Reproduced when, under one reading, both medians are within tolerance and the Kruskal-Wallis test gives P < 0.00001; partially reproduced when only the test holds.

  • gc-narrower-waveform

    The waveform of granule cells is narrower than that of mossy cells.

    Overall, the waveform of granule cells was narrower

    Results, Identification of granule cells and mossy cells in extracellular recordings, paragraph 3; Figure 1E

    How it will be judged

    Trough-to-peak latency as in first-stage-labels. Reproduced when the GC median is below the MC median with a two-sided Mann-Whitney P < 0.05. The share of units whose peak falls at the end of the stored 1.6 ms is reported, as their latency is a lower bound.

  • optotag-drd2

    In DRD2-Cre::Ai35 mice, 29 of 107 putative mossy cells were suppressed by green light, against none of 202 putative granule cells, 8 of 68 wide-waveform and none of 69 narrow-waveform interneurons.

    29 out of 107 putative mossy cells were light responsive ... whereas only eight of 68 w-I interneurons and none of the putative granule cells (n = 202) or n-I interneurons (n = 69) were suppressed significantly by green light

    Results, Optogenetic validation of the physiology-based separation of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 1; Figure 1H; STAR Methods, Optogenetic tagging

    How it will be judged

    Units of DRD2-Cre::Ai35 sessions that hold the 20 ms light pulses; suppressed when the rate in +2 to +15 ms after pulse onset is at least 20% below the baseline (Methods). The baseline is read two ways (the 20 ms before the pulse; 50 to 200 ms after onset). Reproduced when, under one reading, the MC fraction is within tolerance and the GC fraction is below 0.05; partially reproduced when, under one reading, the MC fraction exceeds four times the GC fraction but the values are outside these bounds.

  • mc-more-active

    Mossy cells are more active than granule cells during both waking and NREM sleep (Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.0001 for each).

    Mossy cells were more active than granule cells during both waking and NREM sleep

    Results, Physiological properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 1; Figure 2A

    How it will be judged

    Per-unit rates in each state from the `states` table. Reproduced when, under one reading of wake, the MC median exceeds the GC median in wake and in NREM with Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.0001 for each; partially reproduced when it holds in one state.

  • gc-nrem-highest

    Granule cells fire most during NREM sleep and at similar rates during wake and REM.

    Granule cells had their highest firing rates during NREM sleep and similar rates during wake and REM

    Results, Physiological properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 1; Figure 2A

    How it will be judged

    The paper's test, a Kruskal-Wallis test across the three states followed by pairwise Mann-Whitney tests with Bonferroni correction (three comparisons). Reproduced when, under one reading of wake, NREM exceeds wake and REM (adjusted P < 0.05 for both) and wake against REM is not significant (adjusted P >= 0.05). Partially reproduced when the NREM part holds and wake and REM differ.

  • ca3-wake-highest

    CA3 pyramidal neurons are most active during wake and least active during REM.

    CA3 pyramidal neurons were most active during wake and least active during REM

    Results, Physiological properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 1; Figure 2A

    How it will be judged

    As in gc-nrem-highest. Reproduced when, under one reading of wake, the wake median is the highest and the REM median the lowest, with each pairwise test at adjusted P < 0.05; partially reproduced when the ordering holds but not every test is significant.

  • nrem-wake-ratio

    Granule cells increase their firing in NREM sleep compared with waking, while mossy cells fire at equivalent rates in the two states.

    Granule cells had cell bodies located near the depth of DS2 reversal, had increased firing in NREM compared to waking ... In contrast, mossy cells ... had equivalent firing rates in NREM and waking

    Figure 1D; Discussion, first paragraph

    How it will be judged

    Per-unit ratio of the NREM rate to the wake rate (units with a wake rate above 0). Reproduced when, under one reading of wake, the GC median ratio is above 1 and above the MC median ratio (two-sided Mann-Whitney P < 0.05). The MC median ratio and a Wilcoxon test of MC NREM against wake are reported.

  • theta-modulation

    Granule cells are more strongly phase-modulated by theta oscillations than mossy cells and fire at later theta phases (Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.0001 for both).

    Granule cells fired on earlier phases of gamma waves ... and later phases of theta oscillations ... Granule cells were more strongly phase-modulated by both gamma ... and theta

    Results, Physiological properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 2; Figure 2C

    How it will be judged

    Theta (4 to 10 Hz) phase from the file's theta-reference LFP channel during `awake` periods (up to an hour per file) where the amplitude exceeds median + 0.5 SD; units with Rayleigh P < 0.05 and kappa > 0.1. MRL compared by Kruskal-Wallis. Phases are compared on (a) (-pi, pi] with 0 at the peak of the filtered LFP, (b) [0, 2 pi). Reproduced when the GC MRL median is higher with P < 0.0001 and, under one phase reading, the GC circular mean phase is later than the MC one with Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.0001; partially reproduced when one part holds.

  • gamma-modulation

    Granule cells are more strongly phase-modulated by gamma oscillations than mossy cells and fire at earlier gamma phases (Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.0001 for both).

    Granule cells fired on earlier phases of gamma waves ... Granule cells were more strongly phase-modulated by both gamma

    Results, Physiological properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 2; Figure 2B

    How it will be judged

    As theta-modulation, for gamma (30 to 80 Hz, amplitude above median + 1 SD), with earlier in place of later.

  • gamma-rate-correlation

    Slow-firing neurons are more strongly coupled to gamma than fast-firing ones (GC r = -0.176, MC r = -0.184, P < 0.0001 for each).

    Interestingly, slow firing neurons had stronger phase coupling to gamma waves than fast firing cells

    Results, Physiological properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    Rate is spikes over the recording span. Readings are (a) all GC or MC with a gamma MRL, (b) only the units passing Rayleigh P < 0.05 and kappa > 0.1. Reproduced when, under one reading, both r are within tolerance with P < 0.0001; partially reproduced when both r are negative with P < 0.0001.

  • ds2-recruitment

    Granule cells are more effectively recruited by type 2 dentate spikes than mossy cells and fire earlier (Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.0001 for both).

    Granule cells were also more effectively recruited to LFP DS2 than mossy cells ... and fired earlier

    Results, Physiological properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 2; Figure 2D and 2E

    How it will be judged

    Histograms of spikes around the DS2 peaks stored in the file (2 ms bins, +-100 ms). Reproduced when the GC median peak ratio exceeds the MC median and the GC median peak time is earlier, each with Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.0001; partially reproduced when one holds.

  • monosyn-gc-to-mc

    5.5% of granule cells monosynaptically excite mossy cells (32 pairs), whereas no mossy cell significantly excites a granule cell.

    The spike transmission probability between excitatory neuron pairs determined that 5.5% of the granule cells monosynaptically excited mossy cells (n = 32 pairs), whereas no mossy cell significantly excited putative granule cells

    Results, Granule cell-mossy cell spike transmission, paragraph 1; Figure 3B and 3C

    How it will be judged

    The automatic test of the Methods on all GC-MC pairs recorded together; the paper's manual inspection is not repeated. The fraction is read three ways (a) significant GC to MC pairs over all GC, (b) GC with at least one MC target over all GC, (c) significant pairs over tested pairs. Reproduced when, under one reading, the fraction is within tolerance and no MC to GC pair is found; partially reproduced when GC to MC pairs outnumber MC to GC pairs at least four to one; not reproduced otherwise.

  • burst-facilitation

    Spike transmission probability from granule cells to mossy cells increases from the first to the second spike of a granule cell burst (P < 0.001).

    Spike transmission probability increased from first to second spike

    Results, Granule cell-mossy cell spike transmission, paragraph 2; Figure 3E to 3G

    How it will be judged

    For the detected GC to MC pairs, transmission probability (excess MC spikes at +1.5 to +5 ms over the CCG baseline, per GC spike) for first and second spikes of bursts (spikes at most 6 ms apart). Reproduced when the second spike's probability exceeds the first's by a paired Wilcoxon test at P < 0.001; partially reproduced at P < 0.05.

  • state-transmission

    GC to MC spike transmission is lower during NREM sleep than during waking (n = 31, P < 0.005).

    Granule cell to mossy cell and granule cell to interneuron comparisons showed reduced probability of spike transmission during NREM compared to wake

    Results, State-dependent changes in spike transmission probabilities, paragraph 1; Figure 4B

    How it will be judged

    Transmission probability as in burst-facilitation, from GC spikes in `awake` periods against GC spikes in `nrem` periods, per detected pair. Reproduced when NREM is lower by a paired Wilcoxon test at P < 0.005; partially reproduced at P < 0.05.

  • zero-lag-synchrony

    Synchronous co-activation within 1 ms is found in 23% of mossy cells and 9% of CA3 pyramidal cells, but in fewer than 0.2% of granule cell pairs.

    Only <0.2% of granule cell pairs showed significant peaks at < 1 ms while 23% of mossy cells and 9% of CA3 pyramidal cells showed synchronous (< 1 ms) co-activation

    Results, Granule cell-mossy cell spike transmission, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    The Methods' test at -1 to +1 ms on all same-type pairs recorded together. The MC and CA3 fractions are read as (a) fractions of pairs and (b) fractions of cells with at least one synchronous partner; the GC value as a fraction of pairs, where the claim holds for any value up to 0.0022. Reproduced when, under one reading, all three are within tolerance; partially reproduced when the MC and CA3 fractions exceed the GC pair fraction by at least ten times. Units on one shank cannot be told apart from units on different shanks, as the files do not say which shank a unit is on.

  • tmaze-central-active

    On the circular T-maze, the fraction of active neurons (above 1 Hz) is higher in the central arm than in the side arms, for granule cells, mossy cells and CA3 pyramidal cells (Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.00001 for each).

    The fraction of active neurons (> 1 Hz) was significantly higher in the central arm compared to the side arms

    Results, Spatial and memory properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 1; Figure 5B

    How it will be judged

    Fraction of each type's units above 1 Hz in each spatial bin of the linearised maze; central-arm bins against side-arm bins. Reproduced when the central median exceeds the side-arm median with Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.00001 for all three types; partially reproduced when it holds at P < 0.05 for all three or at P < 0.00001 for one or two.

  • tmaze-multiple-fields

    Few granule cells have two or more place fields on the T-maze (6.6%), against 57.8% of mossy cells and 36.1% of CA3 pyramidal cells (Kolmogorov-Smirnov P < 0.0001).

    only a small fraction (6.6% of recorded cells) had two or more. In contrast, 57.8% of mossy cells and 36.1% of CA3 pyramidal neurons had two or more place fields

    Results, Spatial and memory properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 1; Figure 5C and 5D

    How it will be judged

    Fields from linearised rate maps of every GC, MC and CA3 unit in sessions with T-maze runs. The fractions are read as (a) of all units, (b) of units with at least one field. Reproduced when, under one reading, all three fractions are within tolerance and the GC against MC Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on field counts gives P < 0.0001; partially reproduced when the GC fraction is the lowest and the test holds.

  • tmaze-peak-info

    Granule cells have the lowest within-field peak firing rates (GC against MC, Kolmogorov-Smirnov P < 0.003) and the lowest spatial information of the three types (P < 0.0005).

    Of the three neuron types, granule cells had the lowest within-place field peak firing rates ... and lowest spatial information

    Results, Spatial and memory properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 1; Figure 5E and 5F

    How it will be judged

    Within-field peak rate of units with a field; spatial information (bits/s) of all units. Reproduced when the GC median peak rate is below the MC median with KS P < 0.003, and the GC median information is below the MC and CA3 medians with KS P < 0.0005 for both; partially reproduced when the medians are ordered so but a test fails.

  • tmaze-splitters

    The fraction of splitter cells, whose central-arm firing predicts the coming turn, is comparable in granule cells, mossy cells and CA3 pyramidal cells.

    The fraction of splitter cells was comparable in the three principal cell groups

    Results, Spatial and memory properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. A splitter is a unit with a central-arm field whose per-run rates in the field's bins differ between left and right runs by a two-way ANOVA (position x choice, choice P < 0.001, Methods). The paper names no test for the comparison across types; a chi-square test of splitter counts across the three types is fixed here. Reproduced when P >= 0.05, not reproduced when P < 0.05; the fractions are reported either way.

  • remap-positive

    For neurons with stable place fields, the remapping index between open fields A and B is above zero in each group (Wilcoxon signed-rank; GC P < 0.05, MC and CA3 P < 0.00001).

    For neurons with significant place fields ... the remapping index was significantly higher than zero in each group

    Results, Spatial and memory properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 2; Figure 6C and 6D

    How it will be judged

    Remapping index r(AA') - mean(r(AB), r(BA')) from smoothed rate maps; place cells are units with, in at least one of A, B, A', first-half against second-half map correlation of at least 0.5 and a place field of at least 100 pixels with a peak of at least 1 Hz. Reproduced when the three Wilcoxon tests reach the paper's levels; partially reproduced when all three are positive at P < 0.05.

    only a minority of granule cells (36.7%, n = 11 out of n = 30 place cells) showed remapping, whereas the majority of both mossy cells (61.3%, n=68 out of n = 111 place cells) and CA3 pyramidal neurons (55.6%, n = 80 out of n = 144) did

  • pv-remap

    Population-vector remapping is smaller for granule cells than for mossy cells and CA3 pyramidal cells, for populations of 5, 10, 15 and 20 place cells.

    The magnitude of population remapping was significantly smaller for granule cells compared to mossy cells and CA3 pyramidal cells ... These results remained robust within a range of threshold values with different numbers of neurons comprising the population vector

    Results, Spatial and memory properties of granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 3; Figure 6E to 6G

    How it will be judged

    Place cells pooled across sessions per type; for each population size, 1000 random draws (fixed seed); population-vector correlation as the mean over pixels visited in both maps of the Pearson correlation of the stacked rates; index PV(AA') - mean(PV(AB), PV(BA')). Reproduced when, for every size, the GC median index is below the MC and CA3 medians with Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.00001; partially reproduced when it holds for some sizes.

  • inheritance-tmaze

    Mossy cells rarely inherit place fields from single presynaptic granule cells; of 10 place fields of connected granule cells, only 2 overlapped a field of the postsynaptic mossy cell.

    Across 10 place fields from granule cells and 48 place fields from mossy cells, only two place fields showed overlap between monosynaptically connected pairs

    Results, Transmission of place information between granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 1; Figure 7A and 7B

    How it will be judged

    Detected GC to MC pairs in sessions with T-maze runs; a GC field overlaps when it shares a bin with a field of the partner MC on the same arm. "Rarely" is taken as at most a third of the GC fields (the paper's 2 of 10 is inside it). Reproduced when at most a third of GC fields overlap; not reproduced otherwise. Fewer than 5 GC fields leaves it blocked.

  • inheritance-openfield

    In the open fields, the rate maps of connected granule cells and mossy cells are not correlated within the granule cell's place field (median near 0, n = 19, Wilcoxon P = 0.60).

    GC-MC map correlations of all comparisons in which the presynaptic GC had significant place fields. Note median correlation around 0

    Results, Transmission of place information between granule cells and mossy cells, paragraph 2; Figure 7J

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence, judged on the paper's test, a Wilcoxon signed-rank test of the correlations against 0. For detected GC to MC pairs, in each open-field session (A, B, A' and the curtain sessions) where the GC has a place field, the correlation of the GC and MC maps over the field's pixels. Reproduced when P >= 0.05; not reproduced when P < 0.05. Fewer than 5 comparisons leaves it blocked.

  • mc-deeper

    Mossy cells are located deeper than granule cells relative to the reversal of the type 2 dentate spike (DS2) (Mann-Whitney P < 0.001).

    MC were located significantly deeper than GC

    Figure 1G; Results, Identification of granule cells and mossy cells in extracellular recordings, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    The files give no coordinates for the recording sites, so depth is read from the DS2 amplitude at each unit's site, the feature the paper uses to place units (positive below the reversal, in the hilus, and negative above it). Only the 17 files whose units carry their shank (`electrode_group`) and waveforms can be used; the site is the channel where the unit's spike is largest in the wide-band signal (revised after verification round 1; the first version took the shank channel with the largest stored waveform through the electrodes table, now a variant). Reproduced when the MC median DS2 amplitude exceeds the GC median with a two-sided Mann-Whitney P < 0.001; partially reproduced at P < 0.05.

  • gc-mc-classification

    K-means clustering on the DS2 amplitude at the unit's site, the NREM to wake rate ratio and two principal components of the waveform's second derivative separates excitatory dentate units into granule cells and mossy cells.

    Based on the bimodality of the distribution, K-means clustering over the four parameters was performed to classify E units into putative GC (yellow) and MC (magenta)

    Results, Identification of granule cells and mossy cells in extracellular recordings, paragraph 2; Figure 1D to 1F; STAR Methods, Unit classification

    How it will be judged

    On the GC and MC of the 17 files where the site is known (see mc-deeper). Features as in the Methods, with the NREM to wake rate ratio itself and the unfiltered wide-band waveform at the unit's site (normalised to its trough, upsampled to 100 kHz, second derivative from the trough to 0.8 ms after it), wake read as `awake`; the four features standardised, then k-means with two clusters (fixed seed, 50 starts). Reproduced when the clusters agree with the labels for at least 90% of units (under the better of the two ways of naming the clusters); partially reproduced at 75% to 90%.
