  • fig8a-single-trial-dopamine

    In a single trial (trial 133 of the session), dopamine concentration changes at site c8ds fluctuate, especially after the value cue display.

    Single trial measurements of dopamine concentration changes (Δ[DA]) as a function of time relative to value cue display (V) at 0 s (trial 133 in the session). ... Δ[DA] fluctuations are especially visible after the value cue display.

    Figure 8A and its legend

    How it will be judged

    The trial's dopamine trace (the `da` column) is referenced to its value at V. The standard deviation over 0 to 5 s after V is compared with that over 5 s to 0 s before V. Reproduced when the ratio after to before exceeds 1. The trial can be read as trial id 133 (1-based, as in the paper's Zenodo lists; index 132) or as index 133; reproduced when either reading gives a ratio above 1. The ratio over all successful trials is reported as a reference.

  • fig8b-dopamine-large-vs-small

    Trial-averaged dopamine at site c8ds, aligned to the value cue, increases more for large than for small reward trials, especially between the value cue display and reward.

    Trial averaged Δ[DA] aligned to V at 0 s for large and small reward trials, showing increased dopamine release for large in comparison to small reward trials, especially between the value cue display and reward (RW) event.

    Figure 8B and its legend

    How it will be judged

    For each successful trial, the `da` trace is referenced to its value at V (the paper subtracts the background at V; the PCA estimate is linear in the current, so this is the same up to the model) and averaged from V to the trial's RW. Reproduced when the large trials' values exceed the small trials' (difference of means above 0) with a two-sided Mann-Whitney test p < 0.05. Not reproduced otherwise. Trials whose window holds only NaN are dropped and counted. Other windows (0 to 2 s, RW to RW + 3 s) and the two halves of the session are shown as variants.

  • fig9a-spikes-task-aligned

    Spikes recorded at site c5d on a chamber-implanted CFE can be shown as a raster and a peristimulus time histogram aligned to the value cue display, for large and small reward trials.

    Raster plot of spikes detected on each trial (y-axis) as a function of time relative to value cue display (V) at 0 s (site c5d). ... Peristimulus time histogram (PSTH) showing average spike firing rate (y-axis) for the large (red) and small (blue) reward conditions

    Figure 9A and its legend; Results, section 3.5, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged

    The thresholded spikes of the paper's file (the `thresholded_units` table, the unit with nearly all spikes) are used; the Plexon unit is a variant. Reproduced when (i) at least 90% of successful trials have a spike within 1 s before to 3 s after V, and (ii) the trial-averaged firing rate is modulated around V, by the variance across 100 ms bins from -1 to 3 s of the PSTH exceeding the 95th percentile of the same statistic over 200 random alignments (V replaced by a uniform time within the same trial's span). The large and small PSTHs are shown and their difference reported without deciding the outcome, as the paper states none.

  • fig9bc-unit-waveform-isi

    The spikes at site c5d have a consistent waveform (mean and standard deviation) and an interspike interval distribution (1 ms bins) consistent with a single unit.

    Average spike waveform + /− standard deviation. C. Interspike interval (ISI) histogram showing the distribution of the timing between spikes detected. Bin widths are 1 ms.

    Figure 9B and 9C and their legends

    How it will be judged

    Same unit as fig9a-spikes-task-aligned. Reproduced when fewer than 1% of interspike intervals are shorter than 1 ms (a common refractory criterion; the paper gives none) and the largest absolute value of the mean waveform is more than twice the standard deviation across spikes at that sample. Partially reproduced when only one holds.

  • fig9d-beta-suppression-large

    Trial-averaged beta-band LFP power at c7b referenced to c5c is suppressed more in the brief window after the value cue for large than for small reward cues.

    Trial averaged beta-band LFP power showing increased suppression during the brief time window immediately following the cue (V) for large as compared to small reward cues (site c7b-c5c).

    Figure 9D and its legend

    How it will be judged

    The stored bipolar LFP is band-passed to 13 to 30 Hz (a common beta band; the paper gives none) with a 4th order zero-phase Butterworth filter over V - 3 s to V + 3 s, and power is the squared Hilbert envelope. Per successful trial, the measure is log10 of mean power over 0 to 0.5 s after V divided by mean power over 0.5 s before V. Reproduced when large trials are lower than small (difference of medians below 0) with a two-sided Mann-Whitney test p < 0.05. Partially reproduced when both conditions have a median below 0 and large is lower but p >= 0.05. Not reproduced otherwise. Trials with NaN in the window are dropped and counted. Windows of 0 to 0.3 s and 0 to 1 s, a 1 s baseline, and bands of 15 to 30 and 12 to 25 Hz are variants.
