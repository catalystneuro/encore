  • stimulus-code-link

    The dandiset's stimulus codes can be linked to the stimuli the paper names, using the paper's analysis code and the protocol file of its stimulus software, and the values computed through that link agree with those the paper's code printed.

    How it will be judged

    Records are matched by the integer stimulus code. The link holds (reproduced) when every code the analysis uses (0 to 7, 9, 10, 11, 13 and 20) occurs in the dandiset, and the species' median speeds for the nine whole-field stimuli computed here are within 10% of the values printed in Fig1-S1.ipynb for at least 14 of the 18 species and stimulus pairs. It is partially reproduced for 9 to 13 pairs and not reproduced below 9. The fish cannot be matched one to one, as no identifier links the dandiset's subjects to the code's folders; the counts per species are compared instead. The sign of the zebrafish turning response to codes 9 and 11 is reported, as the code and the protocol file disagree on which of them is lateral right.

  • fig1d-dc-faster-all-codes

    Danionella cerebrum (DC) swim at a higher median velocity than zebrafish (ZF) across the visual stimuli.

    DC show a significantly higher median velocity across all stimuli than ZF

    Results, Larval DC exhibit higher average swimming speeds, paragraph 1; Figure 1D

    How it will be judged

    Tested without stimulus names, over all 21 stimulus codes. For each fish and code the median over trials of the trial speed in pixels per second; a two-way ANOVA (type II) of these values on species, code and their interaction. Reproduced when the species effect has p < 0.05 and the DC median over all fish and code values exceeds the ZF median.

  • fig1d-median-velocity

    Across the whole-field stimuli DC have a higher median velocity than ZF (9.8 against 6.4 mm/s), with a significant main effect of species in a two-way ANOVA.

    In contrast, DC show a significantly higher median velocity across all stimuli than ZF (DC = 9.8 mm/s, ZF = 6.4 mm/s

    Results, Larval DC exhibit higher average swimming speeds, paragraph 1; Figure 1D

    How it will be judged

    Over the nine whole-field stimuli (static, and the eight directions), the paper does not say how the overall median is taken. The readings are (a) the median of the nine per-stimulus species medians; (b) the median over all fish and stimulus values pooled; (c) the median over fish of each fish's median across stimuli; (d) the median over all passing trials pooled. Reproduced when, under one reading, both values are within tolerance and the species effect of a two-way ANOVA (type II) on fish-by-stimulus values has p < 0.05 with DC higher. Partially reproduced when the ANOVA holds but no reading puts both values within tolerance.

    Results, Larval DC exhibit higher average swimming speeds, paragraph 1; Figure 1D legend

    How it will be judged

Reproduced when no pair of DC stimuli differs significantly; not reproduced when any does. The range of DC per-stimulus medians and the ZF pairs that differ are reported either way.

  • fig1d-dc-faster-oblique

    DC swim at a significantly higher velocity than ZF for oblique stimuli.

    DC swims at a statistically higher velocity than ZF for oblique stimuli

    Figure 1D legend

    How it will be judged

    Per stimulus, ZF against DC fish-by-stimulus median speeds with Tukey's HSD on the two groups, at the Bonferroni level 0.05/9, as in Fig1-S1.ipynb. "Oblique" can be read as (a) the intercardinal directions forward right, forward left, backward right and backward left, or (b) the motion of 90 degrees or more used for turning in the Results (right, left, backward right, backward left). Reproduced when, under one reading, at least two of its four stimuli show DC significantly faster than ZF.

  • fig1g-follow-direction

    Both species follow the direction of optic flow, turning right (positive angular velocity) to rightward motion and left to leftward motion.

    Median angular velocity shows that both species follow the direction of optic flow, with positive and negative angular velocity indicating rightward and leftward turning, respectively

    Figure 1G; Results, Optic flow induces angular matching and turns in DC, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    For each fish, the mean of its median angular velocities for the rightward stimuli (right, forward right, backward right) minus the mean for the leftward stimuli (left, forward left, backward left). Reproduced when this difference is greater than zero in each species by a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test at p < 0.05; partially reproduced when it holds in one species only. The per-stimulus species medians are reported as well.

  • fig1h-zf-higher-angular-acceleration

    Across directional stimuli, ZF maintained higher angular acceleration than DC

    Results, Optic flow induces angular matching and turns in DC, paragraph 2; Figure 1H (Kruskal-Wallis test with Bonferroni correction)

    How it will be judged

    Angular acceleration as the Methods define it (degrees per second squared), from the heading resampled to 30 Hz so that files with different frame rates are treated alike, smoothed with a 7-sample rolling median before and after each derivative as the code does; absolute values between 10 and 1000 degrees per second squared kept, frames 0 to 35 s into each passing trial, as in Fig1-S1.ipynb. Per stimulus, a Kruskal-Wallis test of ZF against DC frames at p < 0.05/9. Reproduced when, for all nine whole-field stimuli, the test is significant and the ZF median is higher; partially reproduced when that holds for five to eight; not reproduced otherwise. The quantity Fig1-S1.ipynb computes under this name (heading change per frame divided by the file's mean frame interval) is shown as a variant, as is a test on per-fish medians.

  • fig1c-continuous-vs-bouts

    ZF swim in discrete bouts separated by glides, while DC swim continuously at a relatively constant velocity.

    These visually evoked bouts in ZF consist of sharp velocity increases ... followed by interbout glides, resulting in characteristic periodic patterns. While DC also match the optic flow direction, they swim continuously, maintaining a relatively constant velocity

    Results, Larval DC exhibit higher average swimming speeds, paragraph 1; Figure 1C and Figure S1D

    How it will be judged

    The paper shows this with representative traces and a bout analysis whose bout files are not in the dandiset, so a measure is fixed here. Per trial, positions resampled to 30 Hz between 3 and 35 s, instantaneous speed, and the ratio of its median to its mean (near 1 for steady swimming, low for bursts); per fish, the median over all trials of all stimulus codes. Reproduced when the ZF fish have lower ratios than the DC fish by a two-sided Mann-Whitney test at p < 0.05.
