  • fig1h-epoch-durations

    In prolonged open loop, fish cycle between passive epochs lasting 24.4 +/- 2.5 s and active epochs lasting 36.1 +/- 4.2 s (mean +/- SEM, n = 74 fish), swimming 29 +/- 6 times per active epoch.

    Passive states (epochs when the animal did not swim for at least 5 s; Figures S1A and S1B) lasted 24.4 ± 2.5 s (mean ± SEM), and active states lasted 36.1 ± 4.2 s (n = 74; Figure 1H). ... the fish swam 29 ± 6 times (n = 74) during each epoch.

    Figure 1H; Results, Withholding Visual Feedback during Swimming Induces Passivity, paragraph 3; STAR Methods, Futility-induced passivity behavioral analysis

    How it will be judged

    The mean over fish of each fish's mean, in open loop. Readings, listed before the analysis; (a) epochs built from SwimIntervals by the Methods' rule; (b) the active and passive rows of the dandiset's ActivityStates table, over the whole recording, as the table does not say which condition they belong to; (c) as (a), with all epochs pooled over fish. Reproduced when, under one reading, all three values are within tolerance; partially reproduced when two are. The 12 fish here are a subset of the paper's 74.

  • methods-isi

    In closed loop fish swim in discrete bouts about once per second (inter-swim interval 1.2 +/- 0.05 s, n = 74), and within active epochs of open loop the interval is 1.19 +/- 0.047 s against 24.4 s in passive epochs.

    Paralyzed fish in closed loop in our VR setup typically swam in discrete bouts, about once per second (1.2 ± 0.05 s, n = 74). ... (1.19 ± 0.047 s inter-swim-interval in active epochs versus 24.4 ± 2.5 s in passive epochs, n = 74, Figure S1B).

    STAR Methods, Futility-induced passivity behavioral analysis

    How it will be judged

    Per fish the mean interval between successive bouts, then the mean over fish. The interval can be read as (a) start to start of successive bouts, or (b) end of one bout to the start of the next; and in closed loop as (i) intervals under 5 s, or (ii) all intervals. Reproduced when both values are within tolerance under one reading; partially reproduced when one is.

  • fig1j-open-loop-passivity

    Fish spend much more time in the passive state when swimming produces no visual feedback (open loop) than in closed loop with a naturalistic gain, where passivity is rare.

    Prevalence of the passive behavioral state depended on the motosensory gain ... As the gain approached 0 (i.e., open loop), fish gradually spent more and more time in the passive state.

    Figure 1J; Figure 1C to E; Results, Withholding Visual Feedback during Swimming Induces Passivity, paragraphs 3 and 4

    How it will be judged

    Per fish, the fraction of time in passive epochs (gaps of 5 s or more between bouts) in closed loop and in open loop. Reproduced when the open-loop fraction is higher by a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test over fish at p < 0.05 and the median closed-loop fraction is below 10%; partially reproduced when only the test holds.

  • fig1c-vigor-open-loop-onset

    When switched from closed to open loop, fish first swim with higher vigor, then stop swimming.

    In open loop, fish first swam with higher vigor, then stopped swimming for several to tens of seconds.

    Figure 1C; Results, Withholding Visual Feedback during Swimming Induces Passivity, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    For each switch from closed to open loop, the mean power (SwimIntervals) of the bouts from the switch to the first passive epoch, over the mean power of the bouts in the 30 s of closed loop before the switch. Per fish the median log ratio over switches. Reproduced when the log ratio is above 0 by a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test over fish at p < 0.05. Switches with fewer than 3 bouts on either side are left out.

  • fig1i-isi-before-passivity

    Before the switch to passivity the inter-swim interval increases progressively, and fish swim more vigorously and less frequently in the seconds before passivity.

    Several seconds prior to passivity, fish swam more vigorously and less frequently and attempted to make larger amplitude turns (Figures 1I and S1C). ... Before the switch to passivity, inter-swim interval progressively increased, n = 61 fish

    Figure 1I (n = 61 fish); Results, Withholding Visual Feedback during Swimming Induces Passivity, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged

    For each passivity onset whose active epoch holds at least 10 bouts, the start-to-start intervals numbered back from the last one (k = 1 is the interval ending at the last bout) and the power of the bouts numbered back from the last. Per fish the mean at each k. Reproduced when the interval at k = 1 is longer than at k = 7 (one-sided Wilcoxon over fish, p < 0.05), the Spearman correlation between k and the mean over fish for k = 1 to 7 is negative, and the mean power of the last 3 bouts is higher than that of bouts 8 to 10 before onset (one-sided Wilcoxon over fish, p < 0.05). Partially reproduced when the interval part holds and the power part does not, or the reverse.

  • fig2bc-neurons-lower-in-passivity

    Whole-brain average neuronal activity is higher during active behavior than during passivity, and decays after passivity onset.

    When animals switched from the active to the passive behavioral state, whole-brain average neuronal activity decreased ... Average neuronal Ca2+ decayed after passivity onset

    Figure 2B and 2C; Results, Correlates of Passivity in Neuronal and Astrocytic Activity, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    In the 8 files with processed neuronal traces, per fish the mean of the whole-brain neuronal signal over open-loop passive epochs minus that over open-loop active epochs. Reproduced when it is below 0 by a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test over fish at p < 0.05. The change after onset (5 to 10 s after, minus 10 to 5 s before) is reported per fish.

  • fig2c-glia-rise-at-passivity

    Whole-brain average radial astrocyte calcium increases before passivity onset and peaks soon after it.

    Average neuronal Ca2+ decayed after passivity onset, while average glial Ca2+ increased before passivity onset and peaked soon after. ... This increase in glial Ca2+ started seconds before passivity onset

    Figure 2B and 2C (one example fish, 64 events); Figure 2G and 2I; Results, Correlates of Passivity in Neuronal and Astrocytic Activity, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    In the 4 files with processed glial traces, per passivity onset the glial signal averaged over 1 to 4 s after onset minus its mean over 10 to 5 s before (the paper's reliability windows), and the mean over 2 to 0 s before onset minus the same baseline. Per fish, one-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank tests over onsets. Reproduced when, in at least 3 of the 4 fish, both the after-onset increase and the before-onset increase have p < 0.05 and the peak of the onset-triggered average lies between 0 and 10 s after onset. Partially reproduced when only the after-onset increase holds in at least 3 fish. The number of onsets per fish is compared with the 64 events of the paper's example.

  • figs2a-open-loop-higher

    Whole-brain average neuronal and glial calcium are higher and fluctuate more strongly in open loop than in closed loop.

    Both neuronal and glial Ca2+, averaged across the brain, were higher and fluctuated more strongly in open loop than in closed loop (Figure S2A).

    Results, Correlates of Passivity in Neuronal and Astrocytic Activity, paragraph 2; Figure S2A

    How it will be judged

    Per fish, the mean and the standard deviation of the whole-brain signal over open-loop time and over closed-loop time, leaving out the first 30 s after each switch. Neurons (8 fish); one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests over fish for open above closed at p < 0.05. Glia (4 fish); open above closed in all 4 (a Wilcoxon test over 4 fish cannot reach p < 0.05). Reproduced when both measures hold for both cell types; partially reproduced when at least one measure holds for one cell type.

  • fig2h-glia-weaker-closed-loop-pauses

    When fish in closed loop stop swimming because the stimulus stops moving, glial calcium signals are weaker than during futility-induced passivity.

    When fish in a closed-loop protocol stopped swimming because the stimulus stopped moving, glial Ca2+ signals were weaker than during futility-induced passivity (Figure 2H).

    Figure 2H and 2M; Results, Correlates of Passivity in Neuronal and Astrocytic Activity, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    In the 4 files with processed glial traces, closed-loop pauses are gaps of 5 s or more between bouts that start in closed-loop time or in stationary-grating time with gain above 0. Response as in fig2c-glia-rise-at-passivity (1 to 4 s after onset minus 10 to 5 s before). Per fish, a one-sided Mann-Whitney test of closed-loop pause responses against open-loop passivity responses. Fish with fewer than 3 closed-loop pauses are left out; the claim is blocked (other) when fewer than 2 fish remain. Reproduced when the pause responses are smaller with p < 0.05 in every remaining fish; partially reproduced when they are smaller in median in every remaining fish.
