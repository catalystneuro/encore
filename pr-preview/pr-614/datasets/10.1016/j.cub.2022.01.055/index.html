  • fig1d-ri60-vs-rr20-shocks

    Mice trained on RI60 accept more shocks in the shock probes than mice trained on RR20 (main effect of schedule, F1,43 = 6.37, p < 0.05; schedule by time interaction, F1,43 = 4.54, p < 0.05), and RI60 mice accept more shocks on the late probe than on the early one (p < 0.01).

    We observed a significant main effect of schedule (RI60 vs RR20) on the number of shocks mice were willing to receive on the shock probes ... after extended RI60 training, mice increased the number of shocks they were willing to receive

    Results, A random interval schedule of reinforcement promotes punishment-resistant reward-seeking, paragraph 2; Figure 1D

    How it will be judged

    Mice of the fiber photometry experiment with both probes. A two-way mixed ANOVA (schedule between mice, probe within) on shock counts. Reproduced when the schedule main effect and the interaction both have p < 0.05 with RI60 higher, and a paired t-test of late against early in RI60 mice has Bonferroni-corrected (x2) p < 0.01. Partially reproduced when the RI60 increase holds but the schedule effect or the interaction does not. Not reproduced otherwise.

  • fig1e-omission-ri60-longer

    RI60-trained mice took longer to complete the omission probe than RR20-trained mice

    Results, A random interval schedule of reinforcement promotes punishment-resistant reward-seeking, paragraph 3; Figure 1E

    How it will be judged

    Omission completion time per mouse (the 50th reward, or 60 min). Reproduced when the RI60 mean exceeds the RR20 mean with an unpaired t-test p < 0.05; partially when the direction holds with p >= 0.05; not reproduced when RR20 is longer.

  • fig2a-phenotype-rule

    RI60 mice are split by their shock probe counts into 9 punishment resistant (PR), 9 delayed punishment resistant (DPR) and 18 punishment sensitive (PS) mice, by the rule in the Methods (DPR when the count rises by more than 85%; otherwise PR when the early count exceeds 13).

    Mice in the top quartile of changers (who increased the number of shocks received by greater than 85%) were classified as delayed punishment resistant (DPR; n=9) ... with mice receiving more than 13 shocks on the first probe classified as punishment resistant (PR; n=9) and those earning fewer as punishment sensitive

    Figure 2A; STAR Methods, Behavioral Analysis, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    The counts are those of the labels stored in the dandiset's RI60 fiber photometry mice. Reproduced when the counts match and the rule, applied to the dandiset's probe counts, gives the stored label for every mouse with both probes. Partially reproduced when the counts match and the rule agrees for at least 80% of those mice. Not reproduced otherwise.
