The sparse, non-columnar shape clusters and the widespread contrast-invariant tuning held up in this data. The exact counts of clustered neurons and the texture findings did not. Clustered neurons were a minority and levelled off in well-sampled penetrations (21.5% here), but no analysis choice matched the paper's counts, probably because its code is unpublished and its reliability rule is ambiguous. Texture clustering did not reproduce, with a higher clustered fraction than the paper reports (26% to 52%), and the records give no clear cause. The current-source, orientation, curvature and gyral-penetration findings could not be tested, because the files hold only spike times, with no shape images or grid coordinates.

    position-optimal-consistent

    721 shape-responsive neurons in 33 M2 penetrations (723 in the paper) enter the position test (penetrations with 4 or more). Isimilarity at the optimal position against the main shape test with dark shapes (reading b) gives Spearman's r = 0.297 (reliability a, n = 378) and 0.325 (b, n = 676), p below 1e-4. Against the position test's center (reading a), r is 0.447 to 0.474 with the inferred center, p below 1e-4, so with the inferred center every reading gives a positive r with p below 0.0001 and reading (b) is within tolerance. Reading (b) does not use the position labels. Under reliability (b) the five choices of center give r = 0.274 to 0.406, all p below 1e-4, within tolerance only with position 3 (0.329); under (a) 0.279 to 0.567, p below 1e-4 except position 4 (r = 0.279, p = 2.1e-4), which fails the criterion's p below 0.0001. The outcome therefore assumes that the center is not position 4.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The files code positions 0 to 4 without saying which is the center; the position with the largest mean response of the penetration's responsive neurons is taken as the center (position 1 in 12 files, 2 in 11, 4 in 5, 0 in 3, 3 in 3), and all five are shown. The aggregate RF center is where the stimuli were placed on the neurons' RFs, so the population response should peak there; this is assumed, since the files do not label it. With position 4 as the center under reliability (a), p = 2.1e-4 and the claim would not be reproduced.
    • Neurons are those responsive in the main shape test; neurons responsive in the position test are a variant (603 neurons, r = 0.298 and 0.348).
    • Penetrations enter with 4 or more neurons, since a regression line needs 3 other neurons with reliable similarities (Materials and Methods, Clusters of similarly tuned neurons); this follows from the line rule.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Positions are not labelled; reading (a) is evaluated for every choice of center.
    • The noise-corrected correlation was written from the Methods' description and Pospisil and Bair (2021) (Materials and Methods, Tuning similarity and contrast invariance); its bias terms are written for unequal repeats per stimulus (3 to 10 here), which reduce to the published terms with equal repeats. The paper's code is not public.
    • The bootstrap resamples each stimulus's repeats (500 times), with the same indices for all neurons of a session. Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds spike times of good units only. The LF signal behind the CSD analysis, the RF-mapping experiment, the stimulus images and the penetrations' grid coordinates are not in it, so the CSD, feature-model and gyral-penetration findings cannot be tested.
  • Description missingShapes are coded by stim_ID 0 to 14 and the position test's positions 0 to 4 without saying which shape each code is or which position is the aggregate RF center. The position with the largest population response is position 1 in 12 files, 2 in 11, 4 in 5, 0 in 3 and 3 in 3, so the center cannot be read from the data either.
  • Metadata errorThe trial tables of all 34 position-test files give the session as '...SL' (the shape test's code), while their units tables and file names give '...SP'.
  • OtherThe shape files hold a third luminance level, 8 cd/m2 (the background luminance), presented as often as the dark and bright shapes in 73 of 74 files; the paper and the dandiset description mention only dark and bright shapes. The texture files hold the spectral-noise version the description mentions and the paper does not use.
  • OtherUnit depth is the best channel's distance from the tip in 20 um steps, not the template center of mass the Methods use; it gives the paper's distances for the example pairs (260 and 80 um) and spans (2,340 and 2,580 um).
  • Paper inconsistentThe Methods say shape and texture tests were run in 34 M2 sessions; the dandiset has 34 M2 texture files of which 29 share a day and penetration label with a shape file, and 5 (p07, p17, p40, p42, p53) are texture-only days. The 2021-05-30 texture file is labelled p40, as is the 2021-05-29 shape file.
  • OtherThe position test has 34 files, where the paper reports 33 sessions; one (2021-05-29, p40) holds a single unit. The shape test of the same day is the penetration with one responsive neuron, where the paper reports at least 6 per penetration.
  • OtherThe texture files do not store the rotation of each presentation, which the Methods say was chosen at random from 8.
  • OtherSpikes and presentations share a clock in 152 of 159 files (units are tuned to the stimuli at the photodiode onsets and not at onsets moved by -1.3 s). It could not be established in five M1 texture files (2020-07-15, 07-19, 07-29, 07-30, 08-04), where 0% to 12% of units are tuned at the true onsets, nor in the two single-unit files of 2021-05-29. In the five texture files 9% to 47% of units fire more on stimuli than on blanks (median 41% across texture files), so the units respond to stimulus onset but not selectively among the textures, which points to stimulus labels that do not match the presentations.
  • Methods not availableFor the paper's authors. Is a noise-corrected similarity unreliable when the width of its 95% bootstrap interval exceeds 0.5, or its half-width, and what interval level is used? Under the width reading, 1,729 neurons have a regression line with dark shapes and 681 texture neurons with original textures; under the half-width reading 1,805 and 923; the paper has 1,848 and 931. The significant-Isimilarity counts change by 10% to 60% between them. How are unequal repeats per stimulus handled in the bias correction? And what does the estimator give when a neuron's bias-corrected variance is not positive? Here its similarities are undefined, and 62 of the 64 shape neurons without a line under the half-width reading lack one for that reason (the paper has 2).
  • Methods not availableFor the paper's authors. Does the noise-corrected similarity between two simultaneously recorded neurons correct for their noise correlation? Here noise correlations are small but positive and largest for near pairs (mean 0.072 within 100 um, 0.023 beyond 800 um); subtracting them lowers the number of neurons with significant positive Isimilarity from 400 to 274 (dark shapes, width reading) against the paper's 344.
