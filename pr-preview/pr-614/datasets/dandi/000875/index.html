    fig6a-more-packets-more-uptake

    Under the primary reading (both diagonals, raw counts, the two central ROIs, the mean of the two ends, the mean of the two side minima) the means for 4, 20 and 40 packets are 544, 3240 and 9712 counts at the periphery, 674, 2631 and 5172 at the center and 479, 1148 and 1880 at the least affected area; 40 exceeds 4 at each place by a two-sided Student's t-test (3 dishes each, p = 0.016, 0.00076 and 0.00079). The order holds in all 162 combinations of the claims file's readings; all three tests pass in 72 and at least one in the other 90. Only the periphery test fails (p up to 0.127), in the 54 combinations on diagonal A alone and the 36 that take the periphery from the negative end alone, so the full result needs diagonal B or both diagonals with the periphery from both ends or the positive end, not from the negative end alone. Saturation of up to 5% of an ROI near the 10Px4 electrodes lowers the 10Px4 periphery and works against the order. With the ROI extent taken from the footprints the primary reading gives p = 0.035, 0.0008 and 0.0008.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper's ROIs (MetaMorph) are not deposited; ROIs are placed by code along the diagonals of the detected electrode footprints, over +/- 1.65 mm from the array center with 92 ROIs, the small-array values of Methods 2.4 ("92 or 224 regions of interest (ROI), placed along one or both diagonals, and covering a distance of +/- 1.65 mm or +/- 3.65 mm"). The array is taken as the small one from the images, as the claims file asks.
    • The files give no pixel size. 0.65 um per pixel (the ORCA-Flash4 camera's 6.5 um pixel through the 10x objective, both named in the file's device description and Methods 2.4) is assumed. With it the small array's ROI extent fits between the footprints and the large array's does not, and the small array measures 3.73 to 4.05 mm between needle edges, against 3.80 mm from the paper's large array (8.4 mm diagonal, Section 3.5) and its ROI extents. The scale of about 0.53 um that would make the footprints 1.5 mm across leaves 2.77 to 3.03 mm between them, too short for the paper's 3.30 mm of ROIs. A variant that ends the ROIs 5% of the diagonal short of the footprints, using no pixel size, gives the same outcome.
    • 3 dishes per treatment, where the Fig. 6 legend gives n = 3 to 4.
    • The ROI layout, background, center, periphery and least-affected-area readings of the claims file header are all run; with an even number of ROIs two are equally near the center, and their mean is taken for reading c1, a standard choice. The files hold no dark frame, so the background reading b2 ("minus a cell-free or dark offset, where the data hold one") is run with two estimates, the 1st percentile of each image's nonzero 8 x 8 block means (73.5 to 87.1 counts, from blocks more than twice the center-to-footprint distance from the array center; the 1st percentile of the blocks away from the zero padding is within 1.4 counts of it) and the darkest pixel near the array (110 to 200 counts), both standard choices.

With the ROI extent from the footprints w1 keeps the order under all three smoothings.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • ROIs placed by code, pixel size assumed, dark offsets estimated and 3 dishes per treatment, as for fig6a-more-packets-more-uptake. Widths in mm rest on the assumed pixel size; their order does not.
    • LOESS is fitted with statsmodels lowess without robustness iterations, a standard choice; the paper names LOESS (Methods 2.4) without a span or implementation, and the claims file names spans 0.25 and 0.5.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • ROIs placed by code, pixel size assumed and 3 dishes per treatment, as for fig6a-more-packets-more-uptake.
    • The paper does not fix the layout, background, center, periphery or normalization order; the claims file names readings for each and a claim holds under any combination. The partial outcome rests on reading b2, a dark offset subtracted, which the Methods do not name. The files hold no dark frame, so the offset is estimated (assumed) as the 1st percentile of each image's nonzero block means, which lie far outside the array, or the darkest pixel near the array. A sweep of a common offset over 1Px4 alone puts it above 180% from 50 counts on diagonal A with c2, e1 and n2 (within 220% up to 175 counts, 222.3% at 200); it does not check the order against 5Px4 and 10Px4. The first round used the median inside the electrode footprints; full-resolution crops show a faint monolayer inside them (median 0.74 to 0.94 of the monolayer outside the array, 3.1 to 5.0 times the dark estimate), so it is not cell-free and is shown only as a variant outside the outcome (7 of 36 partial).

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds only the experiment of Fig. 6 (nine dishes, 1Px4, 5Px4 and 10Px4 at 3.2 kV, three each, against n = 3 to 4 in the legend). The single-cell time-lapse of Fig. 4 and the monolayer experiments of Figs. 5, 7, 8 and 9 are not in it, so 13 of the paper's 17 claims cannot be tested.
  • Description missingThe files give no pixel size or field of view, no electrode positions, no ROIs or measured profiles, no dark frame, and do not say which of the two arrays was used. At the camera scale (0.65 um per pixel) the geometry fits the small array and the paper's ROI extents; the dark electrode footprints are then 1.66 to 2.09 mm across, wider than the 1.5 mm needles of Methods 2.3, and they hold a faint monolayer, so they give no cell-free background.
  • Metadata errorThe subject species is NCBITaxon 9913 (Bos taurus, cattle) in every file and in the archive's summary, while the strain field and the paper say CHO cells (Chinese hamster). The age is "P1D", which has no meaning for a cell line, and the archive's summary counts 18 subjects for 9 files and 9 subject ids.
  • Metadata errorTwo pairs of dishes share a session start time to the second (dishes 20 and 22 of 5Px4; dish 09 of 5Px4 and dish 25 of 1Px4). The images differ (no two identical, array position different in each), so the start times are not per-dish acquisition times.
  • Description missingThe ImageSeries has unit "n.a." and a rate of 2 Hz for a single frame, and the "200ms" in every file and session name is not explained; the description says packets were given at 1 Hz.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. Methods 2.4 says the ROIs were "placed along one or both diagonals" but not which for Fig. 6, nor the ROI size, whether a background was subtracted, which ROI is the center, or whether dishes were normalized before averaging. The Fig. 6B normalized center depends on these (1Px4 is 121.7 to 301.3% over the readings, near 200% only on one diagonal with a dark offset subtracted). Which layout, ROI size and background were used for Fig. 6?
