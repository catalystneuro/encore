Most of the paper's main findings hold up in this data: the intrinsic excitability groups, the synaptic and gap-junction connection counts (802 of 7,280 pairs in one mouse, exactly as published), the subthreshold dynamics, the link from excitability to spontaneous firing, and the NPY subtype differences. The synaptic length constant came out much longer than published, and the spikes lost or gained through connections did not match. Traveling waves and the interaction kernel did not reproduce, where the paper gives few method details. A small NPY gap-junction subtype effect appeared where the paper reports none, and single-spike SNR was lower than published. The Figure 1 tool-development claims could not be tested because those recordings are not in the dataset.

    detection-limits

    The pairwise table holds only the calls, amplitudes, decays, distances and Hadamard spike times (its full listing is in the notebook), no per-pair statistic, p-value or null distribution. Rebuilding the paper's statistic (the residual sums of squares of the IPSP model with and without its double exponential) for 10,000 null draws per tested pair takes about 2,410 hours at the 59.5 ms per fit measured in the notebook's last run (14,576 tested pairs; the timing varies between runs), and the 100 pairs per mouse near the cutoff cannot be picked without every pair's p-value. The claims file's readings of the 50% point were therefore not run.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Identifier missingUnit ids are per session and nothing links the cells of a mouse's three sessions.
