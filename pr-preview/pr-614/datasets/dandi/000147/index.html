electrophysiological Human 80.0 MB 10 files

Most of the paper's main findings held up in this data, one did not, and several could not be tested. Decoding of the cued finger (86% online, 93% offline), the thumb-distinct finger structure, the better match to able-bodied fMRI than to an unstructured model, and no drift toward an unstructured representation all reproduced. The claim that cue location cannot be decoded did not hold: it was classified at 11.5% against 8.3% chance, a small but reliable effect. The muscle-then-somatotopy dynamics and the comparison with individual participants could not be tested, because those model RDMs are not in the dataset. The calibration and control tasks are also missing.

  • ✓Reproduced

    recording-counts

    The ten files hold 95.8 units per session (SD 6.7) and 408.0 trials per session (SD 40.8), 4080 trials in all, as reported.

  • ✓Reproduced

    fig1b-online-accuracy

    The recorded decoder outputs match the cued finger on 85.96% of the 4016 scored trials, with a trial-weighted SD across sessions of 3.6%. Shuffling the outputs gives 16.9%, the paper's chance level of 17%.

  • ✓Reproduced

    fig1b-errors-middle-ring-pinky

    Of the 379 online errors between two fingers, 69.7% confuse middle with ring or ring with pinky. Counting errors that involve No-Go, the fraction is 46.8% of 564 errors, or 59.2% of the 446 errors on finger trials.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper does not say which errors form the denominator. Only errors between two fingers, leaving out No-Go, give the paper's 70%.
  • ✓Reproduced

    fig1s4a-offline-accuracy

    Diagonal LDA with leave-one-out cross-validation within sessions gives 93.4% (weighted SD 1.6%) over 4080 trials. The classifier and eight stratified folds of the authors' code give 92.4% (SD 1.7%).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper's methods say leave-one-out; the authors' code uses eight folds, a mean-count cutoff of 0.5, z-scoring and LDA with identity covariance. Both are reported, and the measured value is the leave-one-out one.
  • ✓Reproduced

    fig1s5-tuned-fraction

    With FDR correction over all units and fingers pooled, 87.9% of 958 units are tuned to at least one finger. FDR within each session gives 87.5%, within each unit 83.9%, and no correction 91.7%. No unit fell below the 0.1 Hz cutoff, whether the rate is taken in the window over all trials (lowest 0.113 Hz) or over the whole recording (lowest 0.147 Hz).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The FDR family is not stated in the paper; the measured value pools all units and fingers.
    • Student's t-test with equal variances; the paper does not say which form of the t-test.
  • ✓Reproduced

    fig2d-rdm-structure

    In the session-averaged RDM, max-scaled, the distances among middle, ring and pinky are 0.21 to 0.48 and every distance involving the thumb is 0.64 or more. This holds in 9 of the 10 sessions on their own.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The noise covariance uses OAS shrinkage, as in the authors' code; the paper names Ledoit-Wolf.
  • ✓Reproduced

    fig2f-able-bodied-over-unstructured

    Mean WUC is 0.881 with the able-bodied PC-IP model and 0.781 with the unstructured model. The PC-IP model is higher in all 10 sessions; the bootstrap 95% interval of the difference is 0.074 to 0.127, and the bootstrap t-test gives p = 4.4e-5 (paper 5.7e-5).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The model RDMs are read from the authors' code repository at the commit archived with the paper, not from the dandiset.
    • The p-value comes from 1000 resamples of the sessions (mean difference over its bootstrap SD, nine degrees of freedom); the paper used rsatoolbox's bootstrap t-test.
  • ✓Reproduced

    fig2s5-mc-over-pcip

    Mean WUC with the MC model is 0.969, higher than with the PC-IP model in all 10 sessions (difference 0.088, bootstrap 95% interval 0.060 to 0.112, p = 7.9e-5; paper 1.9e-6). It is 0.015 below the lower noise ceiling of 0.984, and at or above its own session's ceiling in 1 of 10 sessions.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The model RDMs are read from the authors' code repository, as for fig2f-able-bodied-over-unstructured.
    • The criterion's margin of 0.05 below the noise ceiling is a choice; the paper says only "close to".
  • ✓Reproduced

    rdm-consistency

    The 45 pairs of session RDMs correlate at a mean Pearson r of 0.928 (SD 0.041, min 0.817, max 0.990), against 0.90 (SD 0.04, min 0.83, max 0.99). The mean is 0.028 above the paper's, inside the tolerance of 0.03.

  • ✓Reproduced

    fig3a-no-trend-to-unstructured

    The session by model interaction is t(16) = 1.47, one-tailed p = 0.080 toward the unstructured model (paper t(6) = 0.50, p = 0.32). The WUC with the PC-IP model alone declines across sessions (slope -0.009 per session, p = 0.038); the slopes for the unstructured and MC models are not significant (p = 0.20 and 0.23). In other specifications the trend is also not significant. The same t read with 6 degrees of freedom gives p = 0.096; the difference between the unstructured and PC-IP fits regressed on session gives t(8) = 1.30, p = 0.116; with the MC model in place of PC-IP the slope is negative (p = 0.84).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Ordinary least squares on the twenty session-by-model WUC values, with sixteen residual degrees of freedom. The paper's t(6) implies a different model or degrees of freedom, which it does not describe, and its code for this test is not in the repository.
    • Three specifications are shown, since the paper's t(6) is not explained.
    • No Bayes factor is computed.
  • ✓Reproduced

    fig3b-confused-pairs-no-increase

    For the middle-ring and ring-pinky distances of 85 per-run RDMs, the session term is t(8) = -3.56 (paper -4.5) and the run term t(83) = -1.05 (paper -0.45). In all six estimates the coefficients are negative and the one-tailed p for an increase is 0.73 or more.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper's code for this regression is not in its repository. The regression follows the methods' formula on per-run RDMs, with degrees of freedom of sessions minus two for the session term and runs minus two for the run term, as the paper's t(8) and t(82) suggest.
    • The dandiset has 85 runs; the paper's t(82) implies 84.
    • Per-run RDMs use five folds of eight trials each, so a fold's noise covariance rests on one or two trials per finger and inflates the distances (mean 5.06 against 0.68 for session RDMs). A variant with the session's noise covariance (mean 0.76) is reported.
    • No Bayes factors are computed.
  • ✓Reproduced

    fig3s1-all-pairs-small-decrease

    Over all ten pairs the session and run coefficients are negative in all six estimates, and Cohen's f2 is 0.006 for session and 0.012 for run in the main regression, and 0.017 and 0.013 with the session's noise covariance (paper 0.008 and 0.005). In the main regression the session decrease is t(8) = -2.28, p = 0.052, and the run decrease t(83) = -3.12, p = 0.0025. With the session's noise covariance they are t(8) = -3.76, p = 0.0055, and t(83) = -3.29, p = 0.0015, against the paper's t(8) = -4.0, p = 0.004, and t(82) = -2.4, p = 0.019.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The same regression and choices as fig3b-confused-pairs-no-increase.
    • The session decrease is significant at 0.05 only in the variant with the session's noise covariance; the pre-registered criterion asks for negative coefficients and small effect sizes, not significance. The per-run fold covariance inflates the distances of the main regression, so the variant with the session's covariance is the better estimate of the session term.

    fig1s6b-gaze-classification

    Diagonal LDA classifies the 12 cue locations at 11.5% against a chance level of 8.3% (binomial p = 1e-12), above all 500 label shuffles across trials (highest 10.0%) and all 500 shuffles within finger (highest 10.2%). The authors' classifier gives 11.2%, and removing each finger's mean activity first gives 11.7%. Five of the ten sessions are above chance at p < 0.05. With folds that never split a screen the accuracy is 11.1%, above all 200 shuffles of location within screens (highest 9.8%), and with folds that never split a run it is 11.3%. Every screen cues each location once; location and position within the screen are weakly associated over all trials (chi-square p = 0.037). The cued finger is classified at 93.4% from the same features. With folds that never split a run, 11.3% is above all 200 shuffles of location within runs (highest 9.8%). Position within the screen is itself decoded at 10.5%, above all 200 shuffles within screens (highest 9.5%), but after removing each position's mean activity location is still classified at 11.1%.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Eight stratified folds within each session; the paper does not say how this classifier was cross-validated, and the authors' code uses eight folds for its classifiers.
    • The paper shows a confusion matrix without an accuracy or a test, so the comparison is with its statement that the location could not be classified.

  • ⊘Blocked· Metadata insufficient

    fig1s6a-gaze-regression

    The trials table gives the cue location as an index from 1 to 12 on the grid of three rows and four columns, described only as a screen location index, with no map to the grid or to screen coordinates. As a check, the coordinates were regressed on spike counts under the two plain orders of the index. Ordinary least squares gives a cross-validated r2 below 0 for both coordinates in all ten sessions under both orders. Ridge regression gives r2 up to 0.097 for x and 0.104 for y, with both coordinates at or below 0.05 in 5 (row order) or 6 (column order) of the ten sessions. The result depends on the regression as well as on the unknown layout.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The check assumes the index runs along the rows or down the columns; neither is documented.
    • The paper does not say whether its regression was regularised, so both ordinary least squares and ridge regression are shown, each with eight unshuffled folds per session.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds only the main finger task. The calibration task (nine sessions) and the no-movement control task (six sessions) analysed in the paper are not included.
  • Data missingThe model RDMs that the paper's main comparisons rest on are not in the dandiset. The two able-bodied fMRI group RDMs and the unstructured model are in the authors' code repository; the muscle, hand-usage and somatotopy models and the individual fMRI participants' RDMs are in neither.
  • Data missingThe units have no waveforms or sorting quality metrics, so the paper's selection of well-isolated units by L-ratio cannot be repeated.
  • OtherSpike times are not in ascending order within units. In nearly every unit the spikes of the first seconds of each run are stored before the rest of the previous run (590 to 1016 backward jumps per file). No trial's analysed range meets an affected span, so sorted spike times give the same counts, but tools that assume ascending spike times could be misled near run boundaries.
  • OtherThe cue location is an index from 1 to 12, described as the screen location index of the cue, with no map to the grid of three rows and four columns or to screen coordinates. The trials table's Run and TrialNumber columns read "no description", and the start time has only the NWB default description. Decoding the finger over time is consistent with a start time at the cue onset, but does not pin it to the cue within a few hundred milliseconds.
  • OtherElectrode coordinates and impedances are missing (NaN), and the participant is identified as P1 in the files and as NS in the paper.
  • OtherThe paper says the first session had 84 No-Go trials. The file has 64, and the paper's own total of 4016 scored trials (4080 minus the first session's No-Go trials) implies 64.
  • Selection not documentedThe two published versions hold the same ten files. The earlier version, which the authors' download script pins, names the bioRxiv preprint; the later one names the eLife paper. Neither says which of the paper's tasks the data cover.

Reproduction Finger representations in human posterior parietal cortex after paralysis (Guan et al. 2022)

Tests the findings of Guan, Aflalo et al. (2022) that dandiset 000147 can support. It checks online and offline finger decoding, single-unit tuning and whether the cue location can be decoded, builds the cross-validated finger RDMs of the ten sessions, compares them with the able-bodied fMRI models from the authors' repository, and tests whether the representation changed across sessions and runs. The calibration and control tasks, the component model RDMs and sorting quality metrics are not in the dandiset, so the claims that need them are blocked. All files
