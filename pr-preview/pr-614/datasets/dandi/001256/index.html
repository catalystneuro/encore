Most of the paper's central findings hold up in this data, but several do not. The inverse-U relation between baseline pupil diameter and tone response, reliability and contrast gain holds. So does the baseline-by-contrast interaction. Evoked pupil responses to high contrast were never significantly larger over the whole sound, and cortical responses did differ by contrast where the paper found none. The zinc-blocker (ZX1) pupil effects partly held and the Figure 5 widefield findings could not be tested. Widefield imaging is missing from the dataset, and the paper's pupil cleaning is underspecified, so the agent's own cleaning choices (an assumption) drive many outcomes. The minor claims on pupil drift and the likelihood-ratio test mostly did not match.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Group A is the ten mice the files label pupilPTinContrast (four F, six M, one ZnT3-WT), where the paper gives eight C57BL6/J mice (four F, four M); CTRL trials of the CGC assay are used.     • Pupil cleaning (assumption). The last step was added after the first verification round, because runs of tracking failures in AA0319 and AA0334 set their maxima. This sets each animal's maximum PD, hence baseline PD in % of maximum and the 50% selection. The rule reads the raw trace, before the spike filter, so it drops trials in 15 mice, not only AA0319 and AA0334, including trials whose only fault is a spike the filter removes; counted after the spike filter it drops 39 trials in 6 mice and gives the same animal maxima. The round-1 cleaning, the single pass and the narrower step rule are shown as variants in the notebook.
    • The FRA-mapping reading of cell inclusion cannot be run; mapping trials are in 7 of 30 files and one group A mouse.
    • Peak and response windows are half-open frame windows at 5 Hz, frames timed from the trial's first frame minus stimDelay (Methods, 2PCI processing and analysis).
