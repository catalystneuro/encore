The central OFC findings held up in this data, but the causal-strength analyses could not be tested and the Eyes versus Mouth comparison did not reproduce. OFC stimulation brought gaze closer to the partner's eyes (p = 0.0002 over 27 days), mostly in the contralateral hemifield, and shortened the inter-looking interval and reciprocation latency, with no such effects for dmPFC or ACCg. The six causal-strength and slope findings were blocked because the decomposition needs at least 159 CPU hours. The Eyes versus Mouth bootstrap did not reproduce, and the paper does not say how its p-value is computed. Among the controls, the saccade results rest on an assumed detector.

    • Assumption. The detection here is taken to stand in for the paper's. The paper names the software (Key resources table, Otero-Millan et al. 2014) and says it "captured both canonical microsaccades ... and macrosaccades" (STAR Methods, Control analyses), but not whether the gaze was filtered first, and it reports no property of the detected saccades.     figs4a-causal-strength-no-stim-effect

    The relative causal strength needs the causal decomposition of Yang, Peng and Huang (2018), whose code runs 2 + 2 x (number of IMFs) ensemble EMDs per trial, each with 10 fixed sifts per IMF. Neither the noise level nor the ensemble size has a default in that code, and its example uses 1,000 ensembles, as does the claims file's reading (a), so both readings cost the same. In the run recorded here, one EEMD of one 150-bin series with 1,000 ensembles takes 15.9 s on one core with PyEMD (6.8 s on four processes; the round-2 run gave 13.1 and 5.5 s, so the timing varies between runs) and yields 7 IMFs, so a trial takes about 255 CPU s, and the 19,474 social Eyes trials about 1,380 CPU hours (about 591 hours of wall time on four processes). A compiled implementation at 10 microseconds per cubic-spline envelope would still need about 104 CPU hours. The notebook limit is one hour. The trials, distances and epochs the analysis would use are all in the dataset.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • OtherThe dataset holds 6.0 control (Dot) runs per day on average for monkey L (4 to 8; 14 of 45 days have 8), where the paper reports 5 on average, and 14 social runs on every Eyes day of monkey T, where the paper reports 15 on average (14 to 15). Either some runs were not analysed in the paper or the counts in the paper are off; the files do not say which runs the paper used. On the 14 monkey L days with 8 control runs, the runs are numbered 1 to 8, each 295.8 to 296.9 s long with 9 to 32 kept trials, so nothing in the files marks any of them as extra.
  • Published code disagreesThe Methods give the fixation counts of Figure S1E as the total per day, and the paper's code takes the mean per session. Because a day has about twice as many social runs as control runs, the Eyes against RDM comparison is significant for every area with day totals (corrected p 0.000025 to 0.00041) and comparable with means per run (0.40 to 0.52), as the paper reports.
  • Description missingNeither the files nor the paper give the padding of the Eyes ROI (the Methods text has the number missing), nor how fixation events were assigned to ROIs. 6% of the M1 Eyes events of social runs and 11% of those of Dot runs have a mean gaze position outside the Eyes rectangle of their file, and 3% and 10% of whole Face events outside the Face rectangle. The description of `laterality_label` does not name its reference; it matches the centre of `roi_m2_face` for 99.98% of M1 events.
  • Description missingThe Dot (control) runs do not hold `roi_m2_face`, the reference of their hemifield split, nor say that their `roi_m1_eyes` is the RDM stimulus; both are inferred, the first from the same day's social runs and the second from the Methods.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. For the inter-looking interval, does a whole Face event that starts on the trial sample itself (91 trials; likely the triggering fixation as the offline detection found it) count as the look back with latency 0? Counting it changes the OFC day-level p from 0.044 to 0.058. And is the inter-looking interval taken over M1-only events or M1-only and mutual events, as the Methods say (OFC p 0.0022 against 0.044)?
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. Is M2's reciprocation latency measured from the start of M1's look at M2 or from the trial time? Measured from M1's look, dmPFC gives p = 0.044; from the trial, no area is below 0.46. And how is the p-value of the Eyes against Mouth bootstrap computed (one- or two-sided, and which tail)?
  • OtherTrial times fall inside an offline M1 event in the triggering ROI on 71% of social Eyes trials and 62% of Dot Eyes trials, against 5 to 20% for times shifted by 0.5 to 2 s; this is consistent with a shared clock of trials and events, and shows that about 30% of online triggers do not coincide with an offline fixation in the ROI (65 of 954 social Eyes runs are below 50%).
  • Description missingThe fixation tables are named alike but cut the face differently. m1_neface (and m2_neface) is the face outside the Eyes and includes the Mouth (all 23,641 m1_mouth events are in it), while mutual_face is the face outside both the Eyes and the Mouth (mutual_wholeface equals mutual_eye plus mutual_face plus mutual_mouth). The table descriptions do not say so.
  • OtherThe files hold no saccade events. Run on M1's gaze as the paper's code calls it, the Otero-Millan et al. (2014) detector finds 386 saccades per social run with a median duration of 147 ms and a 95th percentile of 1,062 ms; the gaze velocity inside fixations (median about 20 deg/s in one run) is above the detector's 5 deg/s saccade limit for about 95% of samples, so many detected saccades span several fixations. In 202 of 1,179 social runs, the MATLAB code's merge of overlapping saccades yields unequal numbers of starts and ends, where that code would stop with an error. Whether the paper's gaze was filtered before detection is not stated.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. Was M1's gaze filtered before the Otero-Millan et al. (2014) detector was run, and what durations and amplitudes did the detected saccades have? Run on the files' gaze unfiltered, as script/detect_microsaccades.m passes it, the detector gives a median duration of 147 ms (95th percentile 1,062 ms), and the two saccade outcomes (OFC macrosaccade counts p = 0.044 against the paper's 0.055; 0 of 14 kinematics tests failing) rest on this detection standing in for the paper's. With the code's labelling of microsaccades the counts give no failing test and the kinematics 3 of 14.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • A session of the paper is taken to be one file (one run); the valid run length is the span of non-NaN samples of `t`.design-sessions-per-day
  • Benjamini-Hochberg correction from statsmodels in place of dsp3.fdr, which the claims file names; both implement the same procedure.fig1e-stim-counts-comparable
  • The events counted are not fixed by the claims file. M1's fixations in the ROI including the M1 side of mutual events are the main set, following the Methods' "total number of gaze fixations ... of the stimulated monkey" (STAR Methods, Frequency of microstimulations and regions of interest); M1-only events are shown and give the same outcome.figs1e-eyes-more-than-face
  • The non-eye Face is the files' m1_neface table, which includes the Mouth, with mutual_face and mutual_mouth for mutual events (mutual_face is the face outside the Eyes and the Mouth; the notebook shows that m1_wholeface equals m1_eye plus m1_neface and that mutual_wholeface equals mutual_eye plus mutual_face plus mutual_mouth).figs1e-eyes-more-than-face
  • The RDM fixations are the M1 events labelled Eyes in the Dot runs, whose Eyes ROI is the RDM location ("same location and size as Eyes ROI", STAR Methods, Closed-loop microstimulation protocol).figs1e-eyes-vs-rdm-comparable
  • Fixations are the Methods reading (M1-only and mutual events). The grid is centred on each run's Eyes ROI centre, and the ROI squares are those whose centres lie in the ROI rectangle, relative to the Eyes centre, of every social run of the day, a standard choice; the three recalibrated days, which have two ROI sets, are dmPFC and ACCg days. Pixels are converted with 27 cm at 50 cm over 768 pixels (claims file, opening comment).fig2a-density-map-ofc
  • The mutual events added in the Methods reading are the M1 side of mutual_wholeface, which holds every mutual event (mutual_eye, mutual_face and mutual_mouth are subsets of it, shown in the notebook); mutual events are defined only within the face, and m1_all holds no M1 side of a mutual event.fig2c-ofc-social-gaze-distance
  • Each run's own ROIs are used, since three days were recalibrated between runs (08142019, 12102019, 12132019).fig2c-ofc-social-gaze-distance
  • The hemifield is computed from the centre of `roi_m2_face` as the claims file says; the files' own laterality_label agrees for 99.98% of M1 events.fig2e-ofc-contralateral
  • The RDM centre is the centre of each Dot run's `roi_m1_eyes`; the Dot runs hold M1 events only, so the fixations are m1_all.fig2f-nonsocial-no-distance-effect
  • The Dot runs do not hold `roi_m2_face`; the hemifield reference is taken from the same day's social runs, where it never varies within a day.fig2gh-nonsocial-no-hemifield-effect
  • The Mouth centre is the centre of each run's `roi_m1_mouth`, and the non-social centre that of the Dot run's `roi_m1_mouth` (the RDM at the Mouth location); the files do not say whether the Mouth rectangle is the one used online.methods-mouth-stimulation-no-effect
  • Seed 959 is a standard choice; five other seeds are shown.methods-eyes-vs-mouth-bootstrap
  • An event "after" the trial must start later than the trial's own sample (criterion, "starts after it"); 91 events that start on the trial sample, likely the triggering fixation as the offline fixation detection saw it, are not counted. Whether the paper's code counts them is not known; this is asked of the paper's authors in the findings.fig3b-ofc-inter-looking-interval
  • The whole Face in the Dot runs is the files' m1_wholeface table of those runs.ili-nonsocial-no-effect
  • A mutual event enters both lists, at M1's and at M2's own start times; ties keep the M1 event first, a standard choice.figs3c-reciprocation-day-level-none
  • M2's distance is to the centre of `roi_m2_eyes` (M1's Eyes in M2's coordinates); the Methods reading adds the M2 side of mutual whole Face events to m2_all.partner-monkey-unchanged
  • Reading (a) of the M2 inter-looking interval, start of the second minus start of the first M2 whole Face event in the window, follows the Results' "the latency between the second and first whole Face events of M2"; reading (b), from the end of the first, is shown.partner-monkey-unchanged
  • The 188 M2 events whose gaze samples are all NaN have no position and are left out of the M2 distance.partner-monkey-unchanged
  • The triggering fixation is taken as the offline (EyeMMV) Eyes event that contains the trial time; the online fixation that triggered the trial is not in the files.fig5a-trigger-duration-unchanged
  • Pupil size is the raw file value (a.u.), averaged within 10-ms bins, without baseline correction, as the Methods describe none.fig5b-pupil-unchanged
  • Assumption. The detection here is taken to stand in for the paper's. The paper names the software (Key resources table, Otero-Millan et al. 2014) and says it "captured both canonical microsaccades ... and macrosaccades" (STAR Methods, Control analyses), but not whether the gaze was filtered first, and it reports no property of the detected saccades. The paper's code passes M1's gaze unfiltered (script/detect_microsaccades.m at cc0bf994), so unfiltered gaze is the best-supported choice, and the files' gaze is taken to be the gaze the paper passed. On these data the detector's output is not physiological (median duration 147 ms, 95th percentile 1,062 ms, velocity inside fixations under the 5 deg/s limit for 5.4% of samples in one run), which a filter would change; whether the paper's saccades looked the same is asked of the paper's authors in the findings.fig5cd-saccade-counts-unchanged
  • The dataset holds no saccade events. Saccades are detected with a Python port of the Otero-Millan et al. (2014) detector, from the authors' MATLAB code at a pinned commit (inputs), with that code's defaults, called as the paper's code calls it (script/run_saccade_detection.m and script/detect_microsaccades.m at cc0bf994; M1's gaze in degrees from the first valid time sample, passed as both eyes, NaN samples as blinks). The claim is with_inputs for that software, as the claims file says.fig5cd-saccade-counts-unchanged
  • The port uses scipy's k-means (Lloyd iterations from the same starting centres) in place of MATLAB's kmeans, and merges overlapping saccades as a union in the 202 runs where the MATLAB code would stop with an error.fig5cd-saccade-counts-unchanged
  • The fixations that define a microsaccade are the files' M1 fixations (M1-only and mutual), the EyeMMV events the claims file names; the paper's code detects its own fixations (is_fixation with 20, 10 and 0.03), and its labelling, reading (b), counts a saccade as a microsaccade when its start is at least 50 samples after a fixation's start and before its end (script/label_saccades.m).fig5cd-saccade-counts-unchanged
  • Assumption, as for fig5cd-saccade-counts-unchanged. The detection on the files' unfiltered gaze, as the paper's code passes it, is taken to stand in for the paper's, whose detected saccades are not described; the detector's output on these data is not physiological (median duration 147 ms), and the labelling of micro- and macrosaccades changes the count of failing tests (0 of 14 under reading a, 3 of 14 under b).fig5e-h-saccade-kinematics-unchanged
  • Saccades as in fig5cd-saccade-counts-unchanged (a Python port of the Otero-Millan et al. 2014 detector, with_inputs). Slopes are least-squares lines over at least 3 saccades per trial type and day; the permutation seed 5 is a standard choice. Hemifields of the saccade's start and end are taken against the centre of roi_m2_face, as for the fixations.fig5e-h-saccade-kinematics-unchanged
