The Figure 1 behavioural findings held up in this data: accepting behaviour rising over the interaction, female time mostly male-responsive, male-driven mating, the 13.7-s interbout interval and social behaviour within bouts. Two of the three central neural findings did not reproduce: the share of cells tuned to a behaviour (median 22% against 2 to 13%) and the share of persistent cells (20% against 30%); the paper does not say which mice or pairings it used. The finding that coupling explains activity held, and the behaviour-only model held only partly, on a design the records call weak. The central line-attractor results could not be tested, because the dandiset holds no fitted models and the rSLDS package would not build. The optogenetic perturbation sessions are also missing.

    fig1b-accepting-escalates

    Over the first 600 s after male entry the mean probability of accepting behaviour rises (Spearman 0.74, p = 3e-6, 69 annotated trials; 0.59, p = 6e-4, 58 receptive trials), from 0.11 in the first third of the 20-s bins to 0.19 in the last. Resistance peaks in the third bin (0.14) and falls to 0.07 in the last third. Later bins hold fewer trials (69 down to 11); with the same 63 trials in each of the first 15 bins the rise remains (Spearman 0.63, p = 0.012, 0.080 to 0.151). Within single mice the accepting correlation is positive in 12 of 23, and within single trials in 33 of 58 (median 0.01, sign test p = 0.36), so the rise is a property of the pooled trials.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • 69 distinct annotated trials from 23 mice, where the paper has 74 from 28; the paper's trials are not identified.
    • Time is counted from male entry and the curves are cut at 600 s; the paper's figure does not state either.

    Adding the instantaneous activity of the other cells gives a mean cvR2 of 0.477 over cells (0.458 over mouse means) with the cross-validated penalty, and 0.450 (0.434) with the last-fifth penalty, against 0.46.     ed6e-projection-unreceptive-days

    With the authors' weights, the normalised integration value during mounting in m541 is 0.61 on the receptive day against 0.23 and 0.19 on the unreceptive days (paper 0.69 against 0.09; the unreceptive values here are about twice the paper's). In mouse 543 it is 0.61 against 0.64 and 0.63, not higher. The ramp part of the claim holds in both mice; the projection rises by 2.5 and 2.1 receptive-day SDs on the receptive day and by -0.4 to 0.5 on unreceptive days. The outcome depends on which factor is taken as the integration factor. On the other factor the receptive day is higher in both mice (m541 factor 1, 0.62 against 0.48 and 0.49; 543 factor 2, 0.66 against 0.54 and 0.51), which would meet the criterion in both, though on 543's factor 2 the unreceptive days also ramp (1.07 and 1.03 SDs).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Two mice, where the paper has four; the weights of only two are deposited, and the file labelled m542 holds mouse 543's cells.
    • The authors' weights are applied to the stored traces of each day; the paper's projection procedure and normalisation are not described, and min-max over the three days is used. The cell order was checked after the first round of verification; 171 of 176 cells (m541) and 71 of 72 (543) best match the session cell of the same index. The per-mouse files' own traces were processed differently (same-index median correlation 0.86 and 0.80), but the projections from them and from the stored traces correlate at 0.94 to 0.96.
    • The integration factor is not labelled in the files; it is chosen by the longer ACHW of its receptive-day projection. Both factors reach the code's 50-s cap in m541, so the tie was broken with lags up to 300 s, a step not set out in the criterion. The outcome changes with the factor chosen.
    • The receptive day is the one matching the mouse's deposited receptive session.

    ed7-achw-across-cycle

    In both mice weighted cells have longer ACHWs on the receptive day, 28.4 against 17.2 s in m541 (44 cells, KS p = 2e-5) and 26.7 against 9.1 s in 543 (14 cells, KS p = 2e-4), against 25.2 and 16.1 s in the paper.     fig2c-few-tuned-cells

    In the 15 receptive sessions (1,335 cells), 6.9% (approach) to 37.4% (dart) of cells are tuned to a behaviour, median 22.0% over the 11 behaviours with enough bins, against about 2 to 13%. Eight of the 11 exceed 15%. The shuffle test alone passes 21% to 75% of cells. Comparing pairs of behaviours, as the Methods describe, tunes 15.4% to 57.6% over 20 pairs (median 39.3%), with none within 1 to 15%.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Each behaviour is compared with every other 1-s bin of the interaction. The Methods compare two behaviours without saying which pairs Figure 2c uses, and only this comparison was set out before the analysis. Pairs of behaviours are shown as a variant, added after the first round of verification.
    • A bin belongs to a behaviour when the behaviour covers at least half of it; the shuffle permutes bin labels. Neither is stated in the paper.

    fig2e-glm-behaviour-explains-little

    The outcome is the one both verification rounds agreed with. After the second round the author proposed partially_reproduced, from a penalty selection added then; that change has not been verified (protocol pilot-4, "Hand over for verification"). With tenfold contiguous cross-validation and the ridge penalty chosen per cell by fivefold cross-validation within each training set, the mean cvR2 over 1,335 cells is -0.041 from female behaviours, -0.005 from male behaviours and -0.004 from both (mouse means -0.026, 0.014 and 0.016), against 0.08, 0.14 and 0.15. The ordering holds, both against male by a margin of 0.0003 over cells and 0.0014 over mouse means, and all three are below 0.25, so the partial rule is met by a thin margin. With the penalty chosen on the last fifth of each training set, the design used in the first two rounds, the means are -0.077, -0.013 and -0.057 (mouse means -0.050, 0.008 and -0.017) and both scores below male, so the ordering failure that gave not_reproduced came from that selection. A model with only an intercept scores -0.086 under the same folds, so the negative values come from the fold design; with the cross-validated penalty the gain over it is 0.045, 0.081 and 0.082 (mouse means 0.054, 0.095 and 0.096), below the paper's values. With the last-fifth penalty, R2 within single folds is dominated by the same offsets (medians -0.61 to -0.71, intercept -0.70). With frames assigned to folds at random, a variant not named in the criterion, the means are 0.229, 0.207 and 0.322, with neighbouring frames in both training and test sets; the value from both behaviours is then above 0.25. The test is weak, since no design here separates behaviour from the slow drift of the traces.

    Proposed after the last verification, not yet verified: partially reproduced. With the ridge penalty chosen by fivefold cross-validation within each training set, a selection added after the second round, the ordering of the criterion holds (both above male by 0.0003 over cells) and all three values are below 0.25, which meets the partial rule.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Lags in 1-s steps over 0 to 10 s, not every frame, to keep the run short.
    • Contiguous folds, the ridge penalty chosen per cell by fivefold contiguous cross-validation within each training set, and R2 on pooled held-out predictions; the Methods state none of these. The cross-validated penalty selection was added after the second round of verification; the outcome would change with it, and that change is proposed, not verified.
    • Fit over the interaction (male entry to end of annotation) on z-scored, unsmoothed traces.

    fig2i-persistent-cells

    Over the whole recording, smoothed as in the authors' code, 20.0% of 1,335 cells have an ACHW above 25 s and the mean is 15.5 s, against 30% and 20 s. The first-crossing rule gives the same, and the other readings give 10 to 17% and 9.9 to 13.1 s. Per mouse the fraction runs from 0 to 0.48, and four mice reach 0.28 or more; the lower panel of Figure 2i has n = 4 mice, which are not identified. Ending the window at the last annotation, a variant that drops the unannotated imaging at the end of 7 sessions, gives 15.5 to 19.7% and 12.7 to 15.0 s. The mean ACHW is still longer than the mean interbout interval (14.0 s), as the paper says.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The ACHW follows the authors' code (the lag closest to 0.5, up to 50 s), where the Methods say the lag where the function reaches 0.5; both are shown.

  • ⊘Blocked· Other

    fig3a-rslds-fit

    Needs rSLDS fits. The ssm package is a Cython extension installed from GitHub that does not build in the registry's locked environment (it needs numpy at build time), and with the authors' settings one 10,000-frame session takes about 7.5 minutes to fit (10 of 150 iterations took 30 s in a separate environment), so the fits the blocked claims need would take hours against the 30-minute limit. A single linear dynamical system was tried as a stand-in and fitted rotations with two equal time constants in 14 of 15 receptive sessions, so it cannot separate an integration dimension and was not used. The Methods also leave the forward simulation interval open, and the authors' code computes this variance explained differently (a full free-running simulation). Neither timing nor stand-in is run in the notebook, so they cannot be checked from its outputs; the build failure alone blocks the fits here.

That gives a direction but no time constants, so it cannot test this claim, and using it for the ramp and persistence claims would assume the ramp they test; it is not used.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingNo rSLDS fits or latent factors are deposited apart from the per-cell weights of two mice, though the dandiset's description says it contains low-dimensional latent factors from dynamical models. The per-mouse files hold under analysis only a table of two weight columns, and under processing one trace series and the annotations; there are no latent time series or dynamics matrices. The paper's central line-attractor results can only be checked by refitting.
  • Data missingThe sessions with optogenetic inhibition of VMHvl during imaging (Figure 3h-k, Extended Data Figure 5) are not in the dandiset.
  • Metadata errorThe per-mouse file sub-m542/sub-m542_behavior.nwb is labelled m542 but its neural data are those of mouse 543 (72 cells and 10,527 frames, matching 543's receptive day cell by cell with a median correlation of 0.80); m542's own sessions have 129 cells.
  • Identifier missingOne mouse is labelled 543 in its natural-cycle files and m543 in its receptive session.
  • Metadata errorThe same recording is filed under two experiments five times. m764's natural-cycle day 1 has the same traces and annotations as its primed ovariectomy session; m776's primed session has the same traces as its male-inhibition session, with different annotations; and day 3 of the ovariectomy series of m438, m541 and m551 has the same traces as the mouse's primed session, with annotations that differ in m541 and m551 and an interaction of 290.7 against 274.6 s in m438.
  • OtherThe traces in the two per-mouse files, which carry the rSLDS weights, were processed differently from the session files of the same recordings (same-index median correlation 0.86 and 0.80, rising with smoothing of the session traces), and the files do not say how. The cell order matches the session files.
  • OtherWhich natural-cycle days were receptive is not recorded, and the female's behaviour is not annotated on most of those days, so the paper's definition of a receptive day cannot be applied. The receptive session of each mouse is deposited separately, without a link to the day it repeats.
  • Selection not documentedThe dandiset holds more animals or fewer trials than several analyses use, without saying which were used; 7 natural-cycle mice where Figure 5c has 4, 8 male-inhibition mice where Figure 4d has 4, 42 oestrogen-injected trials where Figure 5k has 50, and 69 annotated trials from 23 mice where Figure 1 has 74 from 28.
  • OtherIn 17 sessions imaging continues for up to 7 minutes after the last annotation, which may be the pencil-cup period of the Methods, but nothing marks it. The labels other and eat, and the two names for the male-inhibition epoch (laser_on and mating_inhibition), are not described.
  • Metadata errorEvery session file describes a two-photon microscope with 920-nm excitation, where the paper used a one-photon miniscope; the raw traces are labelled lumens but are arbitrary units with negative values; the traces described as z-scored are not z-scored per file (per-cell means -1.6 to 2.4); experimenter, lab, institution and session start time are placeholders; image masks are 1 by 1 pixel; and the file names say ecephys for imaging data. The per-mouse files describe their session as a resident-intruder assay.
  • Methods not availableThe linked code holds demonstration notebooks, not the code for the paper's figures, so the GLM, choice-probability, decoder and projection procedures cannot be checked against it.

Reproduction A hypothalamic line attractor for female mating (Liu, Nair et al. 2024)

Tests the findings of Liu, Nair et al. (2024) on dandiset 001097, which holds miniscope imaging of VMHvl alpha-cells in female mice during mating, with annotations of male and female behaviour. It tests the behavioural dynamics of mating, single-cell tuning, GLM fits and persistence, and, with the authors' deposited rSLDS weights for two mice, how the receptive-day integration dimension and its cells behave across the oestrus cycle. Claims that need new rSLDS fits are blocked, as the model cannot be built or fit within the registry's environment and time limit. All files
