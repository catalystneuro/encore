electrophysiological Norway rat 55.2 GB 22 files

Only one of this paper's main findings could be tested, and it held: the median electric field in the 20-minute 40 Hz experiments came out near the reported 1.65 mV/mm (1.83 and 1.53 under two readings), assuming the channels sit row by row in the 4 x 4 array. Everything else could not be tested. The 1-minute files hold only the stimulation command, with no recordings, so the firing-rate, entrainment and LFP findings and the sham control are untestable. The finite element model is not public. The 20-minute spike findings need re-sorting that runs about 85 minutes against a 60-minute limit.

    efield-20min-median

    Average peak per 40 Hz cycle per channel over the 20 minutes of stimulation of each of the 13 rats, neighbour differences over 0.5 mm, vector addition. With the channels laid out row by row and reading (i) taken per 2 x 2 cell, the median over rats of each rat's mean field (a) is 1.834 mV/mm and the median of all 117 estimates (b) is 1.525, both within 1.65 +/- 0.21. Per electrode on the 9 electrodes with a right and a lower neighbour, (b) gives 1.648 (within) and (a) 1.890 (outside); per electrode on all 16, using all 24 neighbour pairs, (a) gives 1.748 and (b) 1.650, both within. Reading (ii), the largest pair per axis, gives 3.698 (outside). Grubbs' test over the pooled cell estimates removes none. A serpentine layout gives 1.848 and 1.589 per cell. The criterion takes the claim as reproduced under one reading; (b) matches under every pairing and both layouts tested. The outcome depends on the layout. Over 2000 random layouts, (a) per cell is within tolerance in 18% and (b) in 43%, and over the smoothest 5% of them in 29% and 65%; that 5% is still much rougher than any layout in rows (summed roughness at least 22.4, against 13 for row by row and 13.8 for serpentine). A bootstrap over the 117 cell estimates gives a 95% interval of 1.310 to 1.777 for (b), half-width 0.234, close to the paper's 0.21; resampling rats gives half-widths of 0.313 for (b) and 0.522 for (a). The spread between rats is wider than the paper's 0.21, with rat means 0.810 to 3.052 mV/mm (SD 0.730). In 4 rats 14 of the 208 channels change their artifact by more than half between the two halves of the stimulation (the whole first row in two rats); dropping the cells that touch them gives 1.834 (a) and 1.695 (b). The detected onsets and offsets fall where the 40 Hz line ratio crosses from at most 310 to at least 2406 (and back to at most 810). The measured value is reading (a), (i) per cell, the first the criterion lists. The claims file's opening comment says a claim whose readings give different outcomes is blocked as methods_underspecified, while this criterion takes one matching reading; the criterion is followed, and whether reading (ii) is the paper's is asked of a reader (questions/001678.yaml).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The 4 x 4 layout of channels aw_1 to aw_16 is assumed to be row by row; the files have no electrode positions and the paper gives no map. The paper names the maker (Microprobes); its arrays are built to order, and neither its catalogue nor TDT's ZIF-Clip and channel-mapping manuals give the map of such an array (searched 2026-11-03). The artifact varies smoothly across the grid under this layout (at most 1.35% of 2000 random layouts are as smooth in any rat), which supports rows of four; the serpentine order gives the same outcome. Choosing a layout for its smoothness tends to lower the neighbour differences, so the random-layout results are shown with the outcome. This is the assumption the outcome rests on.
    • Methods 2.8 do not say how a difference along one axis is paired with one along the other for the vector addition. Three pairings are run, per 2 x 2 cell, per electrode on the 9 electrodes with a right and a lower neighbour, and per electrode on all 16 (the left or upper neighbour at the last column or row); under reading (b) all three match, under reading (a) the per-cell and the all-16 pairing.
    • Methods 2.8 do not say over which set Grubbs' test is applied; it is run over all estimates pooled, within each rat, and not at all, with the same outcome.
    • The band-pass filter is not named by the paper; a zero-phase order-2 Butterworth of 39 to 41 Hz after decimation to 1.2 kHz (Methods 2.5, LFP decimated to 1.2 kHz) is used, the criterion's standard choice. A band of 38 to 42 Hz changes the peaks by under 1% in the rat checked.
    • The stimulation period is found from the 40 Hz artifact to 1 s, as the files have no epochs; 1 s is dropped at each end.
    • The stored values are read as volts, not scaled by the files' conversion factor of 1e-6, which would give fields near 1.8e-6 mV/mm.
    • The field is computed from the stored 16-channel stream, which is not low-passed at 300 Hz and in 8 of 13 rats not notch-filtered at 60 Hz (Methods 2.3 describe the LFP stream as both); at 40 Hz this makes no difference to the artifact.

    units-20min

    The 20-minute files hold a 16-channel stream at 12207 Hz that carries spikes (negative 500 to 3000 Hz peaks with troughs of 50 to 180 uV, up to 44 per second on one channel), but no sorted units. One rat (sub-2023-04-28) was sorted over its whole window, 20 min of baseline, the 20 min of stimulation and the 9.3 min after offset that its first series holds (49.3 min). In the executed notebook of this record, streaming, filtering and removing the artifact took 89 s and SpyKING CIRCUS 2 (spikeinterface 0.103.0, printed) 302 s, 7.9 s per minute of recording. Scaled to the windows of the 13 rats (45.6 to 50 min), loading and sorting take about 85 minutes, 25 over the 60-minute limit of a notebook; with the 5 minutes the rest of the notebook took before this point, 90 minutes, before the permutation tests, the phase extraction and the LFP analyses. The time depends on the machine and varies between runs: the notebook handed to round 2 printed 68 minutes (6.4 s per minute), and the two runs of this revision 83 and 85. The block holds in every run, by a margin of 8 to 25 minutes for the sorting alone, which a faster machine could narrow. The comb filter brings the 40 Hz-locked part of the multi-unit band from 11 to 22 uV peak to peak to 0.8 to 1.6 uV, near the 0.4 to 2.0 uV of the baseline. The rat sorted gives 25 units (a median of 1 per channel), all passing the 0.25 Hz rule, against the paper's medians of 44 per rat and 4 per channel (24 to 27 units in the runs so far); one rat does not test the claim. SpyKING CIRCUS 1, which the paper used, was tried while preparing and needs an MPI library that is not available here; it is not in the locked environment, so the notebook does not show this.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The timing uses SpyKING CIRCUS 2 in place of SpyKING CIRCUS 1, with the paper's band (500 to 3000 Hz, Methods 2.3) and detection threshold (6, Methods 2.6), the sorter's whitening and default settings otherwise, on one rat's whole 49.3-minute window.
    • The artifact is removed during stimulation by subtracting the mean of the samples 1 to 10 stimulation periods earlier, a causal uniform comb filter as Methods 2.5 name (ARtACS; the paper's MUAartrem.m uses 10 periods), in place of the ARtACS software itself.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe 9 files of the 1-minute paradigm hold only the stimulation command channel ('S1r 1', the TDT stream uS1r), one series per trial. The multi-unit recordings, the LFP and the stimulation monitor that the paper analysed are not in them, so none of the paper's 1-minute findings (the parameter exploration, Tables 1 to 3, Figures 3 to 10) can be tested.
  • Data missingNo file holds sorted units, although the paper's results rest on SpyKING CIRCUS output. For the 20-minute paradigm the recordings could be sorted again, but at about 6.5 minutes per rat in this run, about 85 minutes for the 13 (68 to 85 in the runs so far); the published spike times would make the 20-minute findings testable.
  • Data missingThe sham recording (one F344 nTg female rat, 80 minutes under propofol) is not in the dandiset.
  • Metadata errorEvery series is labelled in volts with a conversion of 1e-6. In the 1-minute files the values are the stimulation current in uA (peaks of 49.98 to 399.87 for 50 to 400 uA); in the 20-minute files they are already in volts (median magnitude about 0.1 mV, spike troughs about 100 uV), so the conversion would make them a million times too small.
  • Description missingThe 20-minute stream (channels 'aw_ 1' to 'aw_ 16', 12207 Hz) matches neither stream of Methods 2.3. It has the LFP's rate but is not low-passed at 300 Hz, and it carries spikes. In 8 of 13 rats the 60 Hz line stands 10 to 1099 times above its neighbours, so these are not notch-filtered; in the other five the ratio is 1.0 to 9.6 (no line at all in sub-2023-05-04 and sub-2023-05-05), so a notch cannot be ruled out there. Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • The 4 x 4 layout of channels aw_1 to aw_16 is assumed to be row by row; the files have no electrode positions and the paper gives no map. The paper names the maker (Microprobes); its arrays are built to order, and neither its catalogue nor TDT's ZIF-Clip and channel-mapping manuals give the map of such an array (searched 2026-11-03). The artifact varies smoothly across the grid under this layout (at most 1.35% of 2000 random layouts are as smooth in any rat), which supports rows of four; the serpentine order gives the same outcome. Choosing a layout for its smoothness tends to lower the neighbour differences, so the random-layout results are shown with the outcome. This is the assumption the outcome rests on.efield-20min-median
  • Methods 2.8 do not say how a difference along one axis is paired with one along the other for the vector addition. Three pairings are run, per 2 x 2 cell, per electrode on the 9 electrodes with a right and a lower neighbour, and per electrode on all 16 (the left or upper neighbour at the last column or row); under reading (b) all three match, under reading (a) the per-cell and the all-16 pairing.efield-20min-median
  • Methods 2.8 do not say over which set Grubbs' test is applied; it is run over all estimates pooled, within each rat, and not at all, with the same outcome.efield-20min-median
  • The band-pass filter is not named by the paper; a zero-phase order-2 Butterworth of 39 to 41 Hz after decimation to 1.2 kHz (Methods 2.5, LFP decimated to 1.2 kHz) is used, the criterion's standard choice. A band of 38 to 42 Hz changes the peaks by under 1% in the rat checked.efield-20min-median
  • The stimulation period is found from the 40 Hz artifact to 1 s, as the files have no epochs; 1 s is dropped at each end.efield-20min-median
  • The stored values are read as volts, not scaled by the files' conversion factor of 1e-6, which would give fields near 1.8e-6 mV/mm.efield-20min-median
  • The field is computed from the stored 16-channel stream, which is not low-passed at 300 Hz and in 8 of 13 rats not notch-filtered at 60 Hz (Methods 2.3 describe the LFP stream as both); at 40 Hz this makes no difference to the artifact.efield-20min-median
  • The timing uses SpyKING CIRCUS 2 in place of SpyKING CIRCUS 1, with the paper's band (500 to 3000 Hz, Methods 2.3) and detection threshold (6, Methods 2.6), the sorter's whitening and default settings otherwise, on one rat's whole 49.3-minute window.units-20min
  • The artifact is removed during stimulation by subtracting the mean of the samples 1 to 10 stimulation periods earlier, a causal uniform comb filter as Methods 2.5 name (ARtACS; the paper's MUAartrem.m uses 10 periods), in place of the ARtACS software itself.units-20min
