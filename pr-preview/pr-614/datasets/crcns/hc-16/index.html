Fmr1-null place cells resembled wild-type ones on single-cell measures, as the paper reports, but most evidence for discoordinated network discharge did not hold up here. Weaker pass-by-pass covariance between Fmr1-null place cell pairs held, though not at the paper's values; weaker Kendall correlations at gamma and theta timescales, less recurrent network states and weaker ensemble coordination during arena rotation were not seen. Spatial firing quality differed by genotype, mostly in interneurons. No isolation measures or waveforms are given, so all sorted clusters stood in for the paper's well-isolated cells. Slice physiology, LFP phase coupling and the link to place avoidance could not be tested, as those recordings were not deposited.

    34 stationary recordings. Recordings as units, genotype F(1,32) = 0.24, p = 0.63, interaction F = 0.18, p = 0.67, half F = 22.8, p < 0.0001 (WT 6.63 to 5.86, Fmr1-null 6.31 to 5.66 cm/s). Within each environment alone the interaction is significant, in opposite directions; in the large box WT slows more (1.37 against 0.14 cm/s, p = 0.021, 15 mice), in the stationary arena Fmr1-null does (1.01 against 0.25 cm/s, p = 0.0076 with recordings, 0.024 with 10 mice). Which recordings Figure S5B uses is a question for the paper's authors (see findings). Linearity is not judged.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The spatial scale is assumed from the extent of the tracked positions and the enclosure sizes of the STAR Methods (large box 50 cm, arena 40 cm); the data set gives none.
    • Speed from room-frame positions smoothed over 1 s, a standard choice.
    • Assumption. The large box and stationary arena recordings are pooled, as the STAR Methods put all four environments under "Constant Conditions Open-Field" and the Figure S5B legend speaks of "the open field recordings". Within one kind of environment the interaction is significant, in opposite directions.
    • The recordings are all stationary sessions of the data set, which the paper does not list.

    interneuron-proportion

    Interneurons (rate above 2 AP/s) are 68 of 203 WT clusters (33.5%) and 106 of 238 Fmr1-null clusters (44.5%) in R1, and 35.4% and 48.5% in R2; with the classified cells as denominator 38.6% to 41.4% and 51.7% to 55.7%. Every percentage is far outside 12.4 +/- 1.24 and 29.3 +/- 2.93. The direction holds under every reading and is significant in the main ones (R1 z = -2.36, p = 0.018; R2 z = -3.33, p = 0.0009); with the refractory filter (clusters with at most 0.5% of ISIs below 2 ms) p = 0.092 in R1 and 0.023 in R2. The effect is present but the percentages are not the paper's, so partially reproduced. Only 70 of 774 clusters have more than 0.5% of ISIs below 2 ms and the filter changes the percentages by at most 4.9 points, so this filter does not remove the excess. It is a weak proxy for the isolation measures (interneuron rates are still 4.34 and 6.95 AP/s at the median in R1), so whether poorly isolated clusters explain the excess cannot be told from these data.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The data set has no isolation measures (IsoIBG, IsoINN) or waveforms, so every sorted cluster other than cluster 0 is used in place of the paper's well-isolated cells, and cells are classified by rate and bursting without waveform width.
    • The session that classifies a cell ('standard recordings') is open; R1 (each cell from its first stationary session) and R2 (each stationary session on its own) are both run, and a cluster name within a day's cut file is taken as one cell.
    • Rate alone and rate with bursting give the same interneurons, as the rate decides it.
    • A refractory filter (at most 0.5% of ISIs below 2 ms, a standard choice) is shown as a proxy for the isolation measures the data set lacks.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe data set holds part of the paper's data. It has 8 of 12 WT and 7 of 9 Fmr1-null mice, the large box and the stationary and rotating arena, but no small box or ring sessions, no sleep or quiet-wakefulness recordings, no LFP, no slice recordings and no avoidance sessions. Fourteen of the 32 claims cannot be tested, one of them (the mouse and cell counts) because the data set is a subset.
  • Description missingNo isolation measures (IsoIBG, IsoINN), waveforms or cell classes are given, and the files hold every sorted cluster, including cluster 0 (the unsorted spikes of each tetrode). The TS headers hold only the sampling rate, the first timestamp and a track-to-map scale of 1.00. The paper's 226 WT and 334 Fmr1-null well-isolated cells cannot be selected; Table S2 lists fewer cells per session than the files hold clusters (15 against 50 for M16 in stationary arena 1).
  • Description missingPositions are in tracker pixels (0 to 255) with no spatial scale in the data descriptor or the files. The tracked extent of the large box is about 186 pixels in most sessions, about 218 in three (M9, M10, M11) and 188 x 207 in M1, so the scale also differs between sessions.
  • Metadata errorThe header's SAMPLING_INTERVAL and FIRST_TIMESTAMP do not give the times of the position samples. Spikes lie up to 118 samples (about 4 s) after the nominal time of the sample they are filed under, as the stated rate drifts against the spike clock and frames are dropped. The data descriptor's example header gives 58.3 samples/s, where every file has 30.0.
  • File format errorSix spike trains have one backward step in their spike times, of 0.2 to 1.4 s (M34 041015 blackbox P0C2 and P2C2, M37 051115 stationary1 P2C2 and P8C2, M16 090414 rotating P1C1, M35 040215 stationary2 P0C2).
  • Identifier missingNothing identifies a cell across sessions. Sessions of a day mostly share a cut file and cluster names, which the attempt takes to be the same cells, but M29 stationary arena 1 and M34 stationary arena 2 have their own cut files, and box and arena sessions are on different days with different cut files.
  • OtherThe _0-15min and _15-30min files repeat the full-session files except one position sample at the split, which is in neither part (M16 090414 rotating, 26,981 + 27,007 samples against 53,989), so a spike filed under it is lost (one of five spike trains checked). The .times files repeat the TS spike times (107 of 107 checked).
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. Which recording classifies a cell as a place cell, interneuron or non-spatial cell ('standard recordings')? For the rotating-arena cells of Figures 5 and S6, are place cells those classified in the stationary session of the same day or in the rotating session's room frame? With same-day stationary classification room-frame Ipos is greater in Fmr1-null (p = 0.012); with rotating-session classification it is not (p = 0.25). For Figure S6 the environment effect on coherence is p = 0.0012 and 0.067. The attempt assumed the stationary session for Figure S6 and judged Figure 5B on both.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. Which recordings enter the locomotion test of Figure S5B (genotype x half ANOVA, 27 error degrees of freedom), and are environments pooled? Pooling the large box and stationary arena recordings gives no interaction (p = 0.67 with 34 recordings, 0.32 with 15 mice), but within each environment the interaction is significant in opposite directions (large box p = 0.021, stationary arena p = 0.0076). The attempt assumed pooling.
