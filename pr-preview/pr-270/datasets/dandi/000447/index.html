  • fig2e-ca1-error-familiar

    Decoding with N' rate maps, CA1 position error is higher in F than in N, and in F no better than with cell-ID shuffled templates.

    the error between actual and reconstructed positions in the familiar environment was significantly higher than that in the novel environment and no better than chance for CA1

    Figure 2E; Results, PFC spatial map generalizes, while CA1 completely remaps, across contexts, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged

    Decoder as the Methods give it. A memoryless Bayesian decoder with Poisson spiking and a uniform prior over position and trajectory type; templates are the N' linearized rate maps of cells with peak rate at least 3 Hz; spikes in N and F in 200-ms windows; run periods above 5 cm/s; positions within 15 cm of a reward well excluded; the decoded position is the maximum of the posterior, the error the linear distance from the actual position. The Methods say the window moves "with 50 ms overlap", a 150-ms step; the code (decoding_position_*_novelfamiliar_cross.m) steps by 50 ms and keeps only bins in its theta periods, which are variants. As the code does, a bin is decoded when its positions lie on one trajectory and at least one cell fires, and the error is measured along the four trajectories laid end to end, so a decode onto the wrong trajectory is far. Time bins pooled over the five rats. Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn's post hoc; the legend does not say which groups enter one test, so the readings are (a) CA1 N, CA1 F, PFC N and PFC F together, (b) N and F per region. The shuffle permutes cell identities of the N' rate maps (Methods). The paper does not say how many shuffles or what the Wilcoxon test pairs; readings are (a) each bin's error against its error under one shuffle, (b) against its median error over 100 shuffles (a standard number; the paper gives none). Reproduced when, under each grouping reading, Dunn's test puts CA1 F above CA1 N at p < 0.05 and, under each shuffle reading, the CA1 F errors do not differ from the shuffle at p < 0.05 (a claim of absence on the Wilcoxon paired test).
