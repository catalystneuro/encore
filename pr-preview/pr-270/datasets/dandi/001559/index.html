  • fig4d-steady-baseline

    Infusing DTZ at 25 uL/min through the tail vein gives a steady level of baseline bioluminescence during the recording.

    Infusion at 25 μL/min generated a steady level of baseline bioluminescence

    Results, In Vivo BLI of Ca2+ Dynamics in Live Mice, paragraph 1; Figure 4D

    How it will be judged

    The paper gives no measure of steadiness. As a reading of "steady" that the analysis can apply, for each recording the pre-stimulus baseline of each trial (the mean of the signal in the 5 s before stimulus onset; 5 s matches the stimulus length, a standard choice) is computed, and the change of baseline over the recording is compared with the mean evoked response at 4 mA in the same recording (response as in fig4d-evoked-increase). Reproduced when, in the mouse or mice tested, the baseline changes from the first to the last trial by less than the mean 4 mA evoked response, so responses stand out from any drift. The baseline over time is shown for every recording. This threshold is not the paper's; the outcome is to be read with that in mind and the drift reported as numbers.

  • fig4d-evoked-increase

    Whisker pad stimulation (5 Hz for 5 s) at 4 mA and at 2 mA, through each of the two electrodes, gives clear increases in cortical eBRIC bioluminescence.

    clear increases in bioluminescence were observed with stimulation (4 and 2 mA) at both electrodes on the whisker pad

    Results, In Vivo BLI of Ca2+ Dynamics in Live Mice, paragraph 1; Figure 4D

    How it will be judged

    From the Figure 4D legend, traces smoothed with a 1-s moving average and averaged over trials per electrode and current. The response window, the baseline window and the test are not given. The baseline is the 5 s before stimulus onset (a standard choice). The response window has two readings the text allows, as the Results describe a delayed peak for one electrode, (a) the 5-s stimulation period, (b) the stimulation period and the 5 s after it. Response per trial is the mean signal in the response window minus the baseline mean. The paper reports no test, so the test has two readings, both one-sided against zero on the trial responses, (1) a Wilcoxon signed-rank test, (2) a one-sample t-test; an increase is counted when the mean response over trials is positive and the test gives p < 0.05. With fewer than 5 trials in a condition, (1) cannot reach p < 0.05 and only (2) is applied. The windows (a, b) and tests (1, 2) are method details the paper leaves open, so the outcome is the one they all give, and methods_underspecified when they differ. Figure 4D shows one representative mouse that the paper does not identify, so there are two readings of which mice must show it, (i) at least one mouse, as in the figure, (ii) every mouse recorded with both currents and both electrodes; the claim is reproduced if it holds under either. Reproduced when all four conditions (two electrodes by two currents) show an increase; the notebook shows every combination.

  • fig4d-4ma-single-trials

    At 4 mA the response is strong enough to detect individual stimulation events, in single trials without averaging.

    with the 4 mA condition producing a sufficiently strong response to detect individual stimulation events

    Results, In Vivo BLI of Ca2+ Dynamics in Live Mice, paragraph 1; Figure 4D; Discussion, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged

    The paper gives no detection rule. "Individual stimulation events" has two readings, (a) single 5-s stimulation trials, as the Discussion puts it ("clear neural responses to single whisker pad shocks in individual trials"), (b) single pulses of the 5 Hz train. Reading (b) cannot be resolved in traces smoothed with the 1-s moving average of the legend; it is tested on the unsmoothed signal by averaging it at each pulse time within a trial (event-triggered on the 0.2-s pulse period) and is shown as a variant. For reading (a), with windows as in fig4d-evoked-increase, a trial is detected when its response exceeds k times the standard deviation of its smoothed baseline, with k = 2 or k = 3 named as the two readings of a threshold the paper does not give. The windows and the threshold are method details the paper leaves open, so under reading (a) the outcome is the one all windows and both thresholds give, and methods_underspecified when they differ. Reproduced when most 4 mA trials (more than half) in the mouse or mice tested, as in the two readings of fig4d-evoked-increase, are detected. The paper does not say that 2 mA trials fail to be detected, so the 2 mA fraction is reported and does not decide the claim. Every fraction is reported.

  • fig4d-electrode-timing

    The responses to the two electrode locations differ in timing; stimulation of region 1 gives a delayed peak, and region 2 an earlier rise after stimulation onset.

    Stimulation of region 1 resulted in a delayed peak response, while region 2 elicited an earlier rise in signal following stimulation onset

    Results, In Vivo BLI of Ca2+ Dynamics in Live Mice, paragraph 1; Figure 4D

    How it will be judged

    Per electrode and current, the trial-averaged trace smoothed with a 1-s moving average (Figure 4D legend), baseline the 5 s before onset. Time to peak is the time of the maximum within the stimulation period and the 5 s after it; time of rise is the first time after onset at which the averaged trace exceeds half its peak above baseline (a standard choice, not the paper's). The paper does not say which electrode is region 1, and the colours of Figure 4D could not be read without the PDF; if the dataset labels the electrodes 1 and 2, those labels are used, otherwise both assignments are shown and the claim is tested as (a) one electrode has both the later peak and the later rise. The paper does not say at which current; the readings are 4 mA, 2 mA, or both. Reproduced when region 1 has the later time to peak and region 2 the earlier time of rise at either current, in the representative mouse reading (at least one mouse) of fig4d-evoked-increase; the values for every mouse are reported.
