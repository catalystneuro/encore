From the paper Intracellular Features Predicted by Extracellular Recordings in the Hippocampus In Vivo · claims file · checked 2026-11-08: ready

  • sample-cells

    Of 33 neurons recorded both intracellularly and by the tetrode, 22 were kept for analysis (stable recording, resting potential more negative than -55 mV, satisfactory labelling), 21 pyramidal cells and one interneuron.

    we were able to obtain recordings from 33 neurons ... A subset of 22 neurons were selected for further analysis because they had stable intracellular recordings ... This subset consisted of 21 pyramidal cells and one interneuron

    Results, opening section, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Counts of distinct cells in the data set, with tetrode recordings (the five single-wire cases counted apart). Kept cells are those with a resting potential more negative than -55 mV (Methods and Results); labelling cannot be checked from recordings, so the count of kept cells is reported both with the potential criterion alone and with the data set's own cell type labels where it has them. The interneuron count is exact (one basket cell, Fig. 2). Reproduced when all four values are within tolerance; partially reproduced when the total and the kept count are.

  • fig1c-start-end-timing

    In the 11 somatic recordings, the start and the end of the average extracellular spike fall at almost the same times as those of the average intracellular action potential (0.01 +/- 0.02 ms and 0.02 +/- 0.14 ms earlier, mean +/- SD).

    For the 11 somatic intracellular recordings obtained for this study, the average extracellular spike started 0.01 ... ms earlier than the corresponding points on the average intracellular action potential

    Results, Temporal relationship between extracellularly and intracellularly recorded action potentials, paragraph 2; Figure 1C

    How it will be judged

    Per somatic pyramidal cell (11 in the paper), averaged waveforms as in the shared method; the tolerance is the SD the paper gives. Rests on O2, O3, O4 and O5; the readings are every combination of them. Reproduced when, under one reading, both means over cells are within tolerance.

  • ap-duration-match

    The action potential duration is the same whether measured from the intracellular waveform (1.28 +/- 0.18 ms) or from the extracellular one, start to late positive peak (1.27 +/- 0.19 ms).

    The action potential duration was calculated to be 1.28 ... from the intracellular and extracellular waveforms, respectively

    Results, Temporal relationship between extracellularly and intracellularly recorded action potentials, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    The same cells, waveforms and readings as fig1c-start-end-timing (O2 to O5); intracellular duration is start to return to threshold (or to baseline, the second reading of the end in O4), extracellular duration is start to the peak of the late positivity. The tolerance is the SD the paper gives. Reproduced when, under one reading, both means are within tolerance.

  • duration-correlation

    Across somatic cells, intracellular and extracellular spike durations are linearly related (R = 0.66, P < 0.03).

    As expected, there was a linear relationship between the intracellular and extracellular spike durations across individual cells

    Results, Temporal relationship between extracellularly and intracellularly recorded action potentials, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    The per-cell durations of ap-duration-match; a least-squares linear regression of one on the other, R its correlation coefficient and P the two-sided test of zero slope (the paper names a linear relationship and R, not the test). Readings as in ap-duration-match. Reproduced when, under one reading, R is positive with P < 0.03 and R within tolerance; partially reproduced when R is positive with P < 0.03 but outside tolerance.

  • neg-peak-precedes-intra-peak

    In somatic recordings the negative peak of the extracellular spike precedes the peak of the intracellular action potential by 0.20 +/- 0.08 ms.

    The negative peak of the extracellular spike preceded the peak of the intracellular somatic action potential by 0.20

    Results, Temporal relationship between extracellularly and intracellularly recorded action potentials, paragraph 2; Figure 3B and C (black squares)

    How it will be judged

    Per somatic pyramidal cell, the time from the minimum of the averaged extracellular spike to the maximum of the averaged intracellular action potential; the tolerance is the SD the paper gives. Rests on O2, O3 and O5. Reproduced when, under one reading, the mean over cells is within tolerance and positive.

  • fig1c-derivative-mismatch

    The extracellular spike resembles the negative first derivative of the intracellular action potential during the rising phase but not during the falling phase and afterhyperpolarization, where the derivative follows a much faster time course.

    Whereas the waveforms were similar during the ascending phase of the action potential ... they differed substantially during the descending phase and spike afterhyperpolarization ... We consistently observed that after the initial ascending phase ... the intracellular derivative followed a much faster trajectory than the extracellular spike

    Results, Temporal relationship between extracellularly and intracellularly recorded action potentials, paragraph 4; Figure 1C

    How it will be judged

    The paper shows this on traces and gives no measure, so the measure is open. Per somatic cell, the negative first derivative of the averaged intracellular action potential against the averaged extracellular spike (O2, O3, O5). Readings (a) after scaling the derivative to the extracellular negative peak, the Pearson correlation between the two over the rising phase (intracellular start to peak) exceeds that over the falling phase (intracellular peak to return to threshold); (b) the time from the negative peak to the positive peak is shorter for the negative derivative than for the extracellular spike. Reproduced when, under one reading, it holds in more than half of the somatic cells ("consistently" read as a majority).

  • notch-and-ahp-landmarks

    A notch on the ascending phase of the extracellular spike after its negative peak coincides with the peak of the intracellular action potential, and a change of slope after the late positive peak coincides with the peak of the afterhyperpolarization.

    This “notch” on the ascending phase corresponded in time to the peak of the intracellularly recorded action potential ... Often the late positive peak was followed by a clearly recognizable change in the slope ... corresponding to the peak of the intracellular spike afterhyperpolarization

    Results, Temporal relationship between extracellularly and intracellularly recorded action potentials, paragraph 3; Figures 1C, 2B and 4B

    How it will be judged

    Seen "in some cases" and "often", without a measure, so the detection and the agreement window are open. Per pyramidal cell (somatic and dendritic, the paper does not restrict it), on averaged waveforms (O2, O3, O5) - the notch is a local minimum of the extracellular first derivative between the negative peak and the late positive peak; the slope change is a local extreme of the second derivative within 3 ms after the late positive peak; the AHP peak is the minimum of the intracellular trace within 5 ms after the return to threshold. Agreement readings (a) within 0.1 ms, (b) within 0.2 ms. Reproduced when, under one reading, the notch agrees with the intracellular peak in more than half of the cells that show a notch, and the slope change agrees with the AHP peak in more than half of the cells that show one; partially reproduced when one of the two holds. The number of cells with each landmark is reported.

  • fig2-interneuron-narrow

    The basket interneuron has a short action potential with fast repolarization, and its extracellular spike is narrow as well, supporting extracellular spike width as a measure to separate pyramidal cells from interneurons.

    Note the short-duration and fast spike repolarization of the basket neuron ... these data further validate previous observations that suggest that the extracellular spike width is a useful measure for the separation of pyramidal cell and interneuron spikes

    Figure 2C legend; Discussion, Relationship between intracellular action potentials and extracellular spikes, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged

    Durations of the interneuron's averaged waveforms measured as in ap-duration-match. The paper gives no comparison rule; readings (a) the interneuron's intracellular and extracellular durations are both shorter than those of every somatic pyramidal cell, (b) both are more than 2 SD below the pyramidal means. Reproduced when, under one reading, both durations are shorter.

  • fig3b-latency-vs-amplitude

    The latency from the extracellular negative peak to the intracellular peak grows as the intracellular action potential amplitude falls, longer in dendritic than in somatic recordings, as expected if spikes start at the soma and propagate back into the dendrites.

    Figure 3B shows that the latency between the extracellular peak and intracellular peak for all 21 pyramidal cells also varies as a function of intracellular action potential amplitude

    Results, Temporal relationship between extracellularly and intracellularly recorded action potentials, paragraph beginning "Nine other pyramidal cell recordings" (8th or 9th); Figure 3B

    How it will be judged

    Per pyramidal cell (somatic and dendritic; 20 in the Fig. 3B legend, 21 in the text), the latency of neg-peak-precedes-intra-peak against the intracellular action potential amplitude (O6); dendritic and somatic as the Results define them. The paper reports no test; readings (a) Pearson, (b) Spearman correlation. Reproduced when, under one reading, the correlation is negative with two-sided P < 0.05 and the dendritic cells' median latency exceeds the somatic cells'; partially reproduced when only one of the two holds.

  • fig3a-example-pair

    In two cells impaled on the same track, a dendrite 240 um deeper than a soma, the extracellular peak led the intracellular peak by 0.55 ms in the dendritic recording and by 0.28 ms in the somatic one.

    For the dendritic recording, the peak of the extracellular spike occurred 0.55 ms before the intracellular peak, whereas for the somatic recording, the extracellular spike occurred 0.28 ms before the intracellular peak

    Results, Temporal relationship between extracellularly and intracellularly recorded action potentials, paragraph beginning "Nine other pyramidal cell recordings" (8th or 9th); Figure 3A

    How it will be judged

    The two cells are those recorded one after the other with the same intracellular electrode and the same tetrode, the first somatic, the second dendritic (Fig. 3A legend). Latencies as in neg-peak-precedes-intra-peak (O2, O3, O5). Reproduced when, under one reading, both are within tolerance; not testable if the data set does not let the pair be identified.

  • fig3c-backprop-velocity

    Converting intracellular action potential amplitude to distance from the soma (4.9 um/mV, from Kamondi et al. 1998), the latencies give an average back-propagation velocity of 1.61 m/s.

    The average conduction velocity of the back-propagating spike calculated from these data are 1.61 m/s

    Results, Temporal relationship between extracellularly and intracellularly recorded action potentials, paragraph beginning "Nine other pyramidal cell recordings" (8th or 9th); Figure 3C

    How it will be judged

    The amplitudes and latencies of fig3b-latency-vs-amplitude; distance from the soma = 4.9 um/mV times the amplitude deficit (Results). How the deficit and the velocity are taken is open. Reference amplitude readings (a) the mean amplitude of the somatic cells, (b) the largest amplitude among all cells. Velocity readings (a) the inverse of the slope of a least-squares fit of latency on distance over all pyramidal cells, (b) the slope of distance on latency, (c) the mean over dendritic cells of distance divided by the latency in excess of the mean somatic latency. O6 applies. Reproduced when, under one reading, the velocity is within tolerance (the abstract's 1.68 m/s lies within it).

  • fig4bc-burst-attenuation

    During current-evoked trains the intracellular action potential becomes smaller and slower, through depolarization of the baseline with the peak unchanged, and the extracellular spike follows - its amplitude and the slope of its positive-going phase decrease and the notch fades.

    Over the course of the evoked depolarization, the amplitude of the action potential became smaller, and the slope of both the rising and falling phases decreased ... These data also indicate that the amplitude of the somatic intracellular action potential changes due to a depolarization of the baseline membrane potential and not due to changes in the peak potential of the action potential ... The amplitude of the extracellular spike decreased, the slope of the positive-going phase of the spike decreased and the “notch” became less prominent

    Results, Correlated variations in amplitude and shape between intracellular and extracellular spikes, paragraph 1; Figure 4A to C

    How it will be judged

    Cells with depolarizing current steps that evoke accommodating trains (Fig. 4A, 0.6 nA, 350 ms, first interspike interval about 10 ms). Per cell, waveforms averaged by spike order in the train, 1st to 5th (Fig. 4B); amplitudes O6 and O7. Readings for "decrease" (a) the 5th spike's value is below the 1st's, (b) the values fall from each spike to the next from the 2nd on (the paper notes the 1st differs). Reproduced when, under one reading, in more than half of these cells the intracellular amplitude, the maximal rising and falling slopes, the extracellular amplitude and the maximal slope of the extracellular positive-going phase all decrease, the pre-spike baseline rises and the intracellular peak potential changes by less than the baseline does. Partially reproduced when the amplitude parts hold but a slope or the baseline part does not.

  • fig4d-amplitude-correlation

    Within high-frequency evoked bursts (about 200 Hz) in one cell, extracellular spike amplitude correlates positively with intracellular action potential amplitude (R = 0.40, P < 0.0001).

    Figure 4D shows, for a different cell, the relationship between the extracellular spike amplitude and intracellular action potential amplitude for bursts of spikes occurring at a higher frequency ... illustrates the positive relationship between the intracellular and extracellular amplitudes

    Results, Correlated variations in amplitude and shape between intracellular and extracellular spikes, paragraph 1; Figure 4D

    How it will be judged

    Single spikes in bursts evoked by strong depolarizing steps with interspike intervals below 5 ms; amplitudes per spike, O6 and O7. The cell is not identified; it is any cell other than the Fig. 4A to C cell with such bursts. Per candidate cell, a least-squares regression of extracellular on intracellular amplitude, R and the two-sided P of the slope. Reproduced when, under one reading, some candidate cell gives a positive R within tolerance with P < 0.0001; partially reproduced when some cell gives a positive R with P < 0.0001 outside tolerance. R for every candidate cell is reported.

  • fig5-theta-smaller-spikes

    In sleeping rats, extracellular spike amplitudes of pyramidal cells (n = 202) and pyramidal-layer interneurons (n = 43) are smaller during REM sleep with theta than during slow-wave sleep without theta, sharp-wave bursts excluded.

    This comparison revealed that the mean spike amplitude of individual pyramidal units and pyramidal layer interneuronal units was lower during theta oscillations

    Results, Behavioral state effects on extracellular spike waveform, paragraph 1; Figure 5

    How it will be judged

    Per unit, the mean negative-to-positive peak amplitude of its spikes in theta (REM) epochs and in non-theta, non-sharp-wave slow-wave sleep epochs; paired t-test per cell class. Reproduced when both classes are smaller in theta with P below the legend's bounds. These are the behaving-animal recordings of Csicsvari et al. 1999a, which the data set does not hold by its description; expected to be untestable.

  • theta-higher-rates

    Pyramidal cells and interneurons fire at higher rates during theta than during periods without theta or sharp waves.

    the frequency of discharge of both pyramidal cells and interneurons is higher during theta activity than during “no-theta and no-sharp wave” periods

    Results, Behavioral state effects on extracellular spike waveform, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Per unit, firing rate in the two states as in fig5-theta-smaller-spikes; the paper gives no test. Behaving-animal data not held by the data set by its description; expected to be untestable.

  • fig6-amplitude-vs-distance

    Average extracellular spike amplitude falls steeply with distance from the soma - the largest spikes were within 50 um, small spikes below 60 uV were still seen beyond 50 um, and beyond 140 um no spike could be distinguished from noise even by averaging.

    We found that the amplitude of the average extracellular spike decreased rapidly as a function of distance of the tetrode tip from the soma ... The largest amplitude spikes were recorded ... from the soma ... no extracellular spike activity could be distinguished from the background noise activity even after averaging based on the occurrence of the intracellular action potentials

    Results, Field measurements of action potentials, paragraph 1; Figure 6A to C

    How it will be judged

    The 19 pyramidal cells with a histological distance (Fig. 6A legend); amplitude of the averaged spike on the tetrode channel with the largest peak amplitude (legend), readings O7; O2 and O5 apply. Needs the per-cell distances; not testable if the data set does not hold them. The paper reports no test; readings (a) Spearman, (b) Pearson correlation of amplitude with distance. Reproduced when, under one reading, the correlation is negative with two-sided P < 0.05, the cells within 50 um have average amplitudes of at least 60 uV, the cells beyond 50 um have below 60 uV, and no cell beyond 140 um has an averaged spike above 2 SD of the averaged noise (a standard detection rule, the paper names none). Partially reproduced when the correlation holds but a threshold part does not.

  • tetrode-cell-count-estimate

    With a CA1 density of 300,000 cells/mm3 and a 60-um thick layer, a tetrode can record spikes above 60 uV from cells within 50 um (a cylinder of 4.71 x 10-4 mm3, 141 cells, at least 13 of them displaced by the tetrode), and in principle from 1,108 neurons within 140 um.

    This volume is calculated to contain 141 cells ... theoretically one could record from as many as 1,108 neurons with a single tetrode with perfect recording and unit isolation methods

    Results, Field measurements of action potentials, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    Arithmetic on the paper's constants (density 300,000/mm3 from Aika et al. 1994, layer thickness 60 um, radii 50 and 140 um, tetrode radius 15 um), cylinder volume pi r^2 h. It uses no recordings beyond the radii of fig6-amplitude-vs-distance. Reproduced when both counts are within tolerance, and the radii hold in fig6-amplitude-vs-distance.

  • silent-cells-inference

    A tetrode should be able to separate 60 to 100 neurons (about 100 in the Discussion's summary, at least 60 after allowing for local damage), several times the units routinely separated per tetrode (about six in the abstract, 1 to 20 in the cited studies), which the authors explain by most pyramidal cells being silent in any given environment.

    A more likely potential reason for the large discrepancy between theory and practice is that the great majority of pyramidal cells are silent in any given environment

    Discussion, Sensitivity of tetrodes and spatial clustering techniques, paragraphs 2 and 3; Abstract; Discussion, opening paragraph

    How it will be judged

    An interpretation resting on unit counts from other studies (1 to 20 per tetrode) and on interneuron to pyramidal cell ratios from the literature; no test in the paper and no measurement in the anesthetized recordings bears on it. Recorded for coverage; expected to be untestable.

  • fig7-vertical-spread

    On a linear silicon probe, the spike of a cell can be recorded 100 um or more above and below the site nearest the soma (a vertical span of at least 200 um), and its rise and decay slopes decrease with distance from the soma along with its amplitude.

    The extracellular waveforms were averaged based on the occurrence of the simultaneously recorded intracellular action potentials ... above and below the putative location of the soma ... Coincident with the extracellular spike amplitude decrease, the rise and decay slopes of the extracellular spike also decreased with distance from the soma

    Results, Multiple recording of extracellular spikes along the somatodendritic axis, paragraphs 1 and 2; Figure 7

    How it will be judged

    Anesthetized recordings with a two-shank, six-site silicon probe and an intracellular recording; per cell, extracellular waveforms on each site averaged on the intracellular action potentials (O1, O2), filtered 1 Hz to 10 kHz as the Fig. 7 legend states (O5 with 10 kHz). The site with the largest spike is the soma (Results). A spike is present on a site when its averaged negative peak exceeds 2 SD of the averaged noise (a standard rule, the paper names none). The paper's distance is ">= 100 um" (Results); with 25-um site spacing, 10% of it does not reach the next site down, so the distance is four sites. Reproduced when, in more than half of the cells, the spike is present on sites at least 100 um (four sites) from the soma site on at least one side (a six-site shank spans 125 um, so both sides cannot reach 100 um at once), and amplitude and maximal rise and decay slopes decrease from the soma site outward on the shank.

  • propagation-velocity-range

    Propagation velocities from the delay between extracellular negative peaks on sites along the somatodendritic axis range from 0.4 to 2 m/s in pyramidal cells (n = 25) and 0.5 to 1.5 m/s in pyramidal-layer interneurons (n = 6), in agreement with the 1.61 m/s from the intracellular data.

    The propagation velocity of action potentials, estimated from the distance of the recording sites and the delay between the negative peaks of the extracellular spikes, varied from 0.4 to 2 m/s in pyramidal cells

    Results, Multiple recording of extracellular spikes along the somatodendritic axis, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    Which recordings are used is open; readings (a) the anesthetized silicon probe recordings (Fig. 7 setting), (b) the behaving-animal silicon probe recordings of Csicsvari et al. 1999a, which the data set does not hold by its description. The 6 interneurons exceed the one intracellular interneuron, so reading (b), or units sorted from the extracellular channels, is implied; testable only to the extent the data set holds such units. Per unit, velocity from site spacing (25 um) and negative peak delays; pairing readings (a) the slope of a fit of negative peak time on site position over the sites where the spike is present, (b) the largest-spike site against the farthest site with the spike. Reproduced when, under one reading, every unit's velocity lies within the range widened by 10% of each bound.

  • fig8-neighbouring-shanks

    Only two units, both putative interneurons and under 0.5% of clustered units, were recorded on two shanks 150 um apart; no pyramidal cell was, implying a larger vertical than horizontal spread of pyramidal cell spike currents.

    we found only two cases in our database from behaving animals ... when the extracellular spike, recorded at one shank, was also recorded by the neighboring shank ... Both units were classified as putative interneurons

    Results, Multiple recording of extracellular spikes along the somatodendritic axis, paragraph 3; Figure 8

    How it will be judged

    Cross-correlograms between units clustered separately per shank, a peak at 0 ms flanked by suppression within 5 ms. These are the behaving-animal recordings of Csicsvari et al. 1999a, which the data set does not hold by its description; expected to be untestable.
