Most of the paper's main findings held up in this data. VPM and fast-spiking cells rose with whisking (VPM 4.3 to 11.9 spikes/s), their spikes were phase-locked to whisking, and cutting the infraorbital nerve removed fast-spiking touch and whisking responses. Thalamic photoinhibition lowered fast-spiking rates in direction, but the percentages differed from the paper's. Layer 4 excitatory cells did not show the reported lack of a whisking effect (p about 0.02), and the eNpHR touch-detection findings did not reproduce, although one of them flips with how passive first touches are handled. The inhibition seen at depolarized potentials, the thalamic multiunit photoinhibition and the rabies tracing could not be tested because those recordings are not in the dataset. Several latency and amplitude values were off, and the files do not say which tracked whisker is the principal one.

  • openasked 2026-11-06about ion-cut-l4e-touch

    The criterion tests the touch PSP amplitude (peak of the average 0 to 50 ms after touch minus the mean 0 to 5 ms before) against zero. That measure is positive with no response at all, since the peak of a noisy average lies above its baseline. Does the paper's statement, that ION-cut neurons "did not depolarize after touch" next to a grand average compared with 15 controls, support a test against zero, or should the reference be the same measure at times without touch (or the controls)? Should a reading be added that can decide the outcome?

    Passage. As expected, L4 excitatory neurons did not depolarize after touch (Fig. 7b). (Results, Inhibition is driven by sensory input, paragraph 2.) Fig. 7b legend, "grand average of touch-aligned responses of 8 L4 excitatory neurons (black trace) and 15 L4 excitatory neurons recorded in control mice with matched resting membrane potential range".

    Why it matters. Against zero the amplitude is 0.321 mV, p = 0.0078 (not reproduced, as the criterion has it). The same measure at 200 random times per neuron gives 0.208 mV, and touch does not differ from it (p = 0.148), which would make the claim reproduced. The verifier of round 1 found the criterion biased in the same way.

    The criterion correlates light power with rate per neuron, with control trials as zero power. Most FS neurons have one or two light powers, so the correlation mostly tests light against no light, which thalamus-inhibition-fs already tests, and not a dependence on dose. Does the paper's "dose-dependent" (Supplementary Fig. 9i,j, rate as a function of laser power) call for a test among light powers only, without control trials?

    Passage. Photoinhibition reduced spike rates in FS interneurons ... in a dose-dependent manner (Supplementary Fig. 9i,j). (Results, Inhibition is driven by thalamus, paragraph 2.) Supplementary Fig. 9j legend, "Spike rate of FS interneurons as a function of laser power, separated into different behavioral epochs."

    Why it matters. With control as zero, all three epochs give p < 0.0013 (reproduced). Among light trials only, in the neurons with two powers, one epoch of three is significant (touch, p = 0.031; nonwhisking p = 0.31, whisking p = 0.074), which would not meet the criterion's two of three.

  • openasked 2026-11-06about enphr-auc

    The criterion correlates the control touch-evoked rate with the AUC change (eNpHR minus control). The control rate and the control AUC come from the same trials, so noise shared by them biases the correlation negative (regression to the mean). Does the paper's statement (Fig. 8g) call for that test as written, or for one that takes the rate and the control AUC from different trials, or compares with a no-light null?

    Passage. suppression of PV interneurons improved AUC for neurons with weak responses to touch and diminished AUC for neurons with robust responses to touch (Fig. 8f,g). (Results, Layer 4 FS interneurons suppress reafference in L4, paragraph 3.) Fig. 8g legend, "Relationship between change in AUC induced by eNpHR stimulation and the spike rate evoked by touch".

    Why it matters. Here the bias looks small: with control trials split in halves, the rate of one half does not correlate with the AUC difference between the halves (rho = 0.02). With rate and control AUC from different halves the correlation is rho = -0.19, p = 0.27, against rho = -0.28, p = 0.09 as written (reading b). Both are not reproduced in this attempt, but a criterion without the shared trials could decide a later one differently. The verifier of round 1 raised it.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • Trials whose median Vm lies more than 20 mV from the neuron's median are left out of the Vm measures: 13 trials of JY1139 (5 control trials), which starts near 0 mV and settles at about -41 mV. An assumption after the Methods' rejection of recordings "with slow baseline drift (>20 mV)" (Barrel cortex recordings, paragraph 3); keeping them is shown as a variant.fs-touch-vm fs-whisking-vm fs-hf-fluctuations
  • Touches are counted on the first tracked whisker series, taken as the principal whisker (an assumption; the files do not say which series is the principal whisker, see findings). The configuration-order reading is shown as a variant.behavior-performance
  • Proximal and distal trials are read as Go and Nogo trials (Online Methods, Behavior, "licked ... if the pole was in one of the posterior locations"). Trial counts pool the cortical recordings and each VPM session once; the paper's 4,147 and 4,173 trials cannot be matched to a set.behavior-performance
  • The data set's good-trial masks are applied.behavior-performance
  • Epoch rules follow M-EPOCH; whisking cycles run from one phase wrap to the next, and the margin readings (a) and (b) of the claims file are both shown.vpm-baseline-rate
  • Latency on 1 ms PSTH bins; the latency is the first bin after the dPSTH/dt crossing. The duration follows the M-LAT reading of the claims file.vpm-touch-response
  • The first touch of trials tagged passive touch is left out, as the data set's example code compute_touchNWB.m does; its touch-speed cutoff is not applied.vpm-touch-response
  • The readings of M-SIG give different counts; the criterion's rule of one matching reading decides.vpm-whisking-count
