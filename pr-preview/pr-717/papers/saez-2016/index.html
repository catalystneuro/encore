    Plasticity was analysed in 28 control cells (14 in voltage clamp, 14 in current clamp) and 26 cells with 2 uM bicuculline in the bath.

    Results, Dataset, paragraph 1; Methods, Electrophysiology

  • fig1-control-no-net-plasticity

    Over the 28 control cells, pairing gave no net plasticity of EPSPs or EPSCs.

    overall the pairing protocol resulted in no net plasticity of EPSPs (recorded under current clamp) or EPSCs (under voltage clamp) for the population of cells studied (n = 28; p>0.8, Student’s t-test, Fig 1A ).

    Results, Extracellular stimulation, paragraph 1; Figure 1B

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence, judged on the paper's Student's t-test at p < 0.05. The paper does not say which form. With EPSPs (mV) and EPSCs (pA) pooled, only normalized values can be tested, and a paired t-test of normalized pre (1) against post amplitude is the same test as a one-sample t-test of the 28 percent changes against 0, used here. Reproduced when the test does not reach p < 0.05, in each reading of (P) as in the opening comment; not reproduced when it does. The mean change and p are reported. A one-sample test on the paper's own mean and SD (-3.53 +/- 10.26%, n = 28) gives p near 0.08, so the printed p > 0.8 is not expected to be matched.

  • fig3c-random-mixing-narrower

    Simulated compound responses built by random mixing of unitary L4-L4 connections (18.05 +/- 6.19 inputs each) give a distribution of strength changes significantly narrower than both the ECS (bicuculline) and the SCS data, so random independent mixing would not produce the plasticity seen with ECS in bicuculline.

    We found that the distribution of SCR Δ N Strengths was significantly narrower than that of either ECS (p<0.001, Fisher’s F-test) or SCS (p<0.001, Fisher’s F-test) experimental data ( Fig 3C ).

    Results, Mixing of unitary L4-L4 inputs, paragraph 1; Figure 3C; Methods, Input mixing simulation

    How it will be judged

    Needs the SCS pre- and post-pairing strengths (in pA) of reference 17, not in the data set. As the Methods give it, connections drawn from the SCS database until their summed pre-pairing strength is 350 pA or more; SCR pre and SCR post are the summed pre and post strengths; the change is SCR post over SCR pre minus 1; 26 SCRs; F-tests of SCR variance against the 26 bicuculline ECS changes and the SCS changes. The ECS group is bicuculline (the Results compare with ECS, n = 26). Open, with readings. Sampling with replacement (a) or without replacement within an SCR (b). The random seed can change a run of 26, so (i) one run with a standard seed, and (ii) 1000 runs of 26, judged by the share of runs with p < 0.001. The tolerance of the input count is the standard error of the paper's mean (6.19 over the square root of 26). Reproduced when, under one reading, both F-tests give p < 0.001 with the SCR variance smaller (in (ii), in more than half of runs) and the input count is within tolerance; partially reproduced when both give p < 0.05.

  • fig3b-segregation-near-complete

    Adding a segregation parameter S that biases each SCR's connections toward the plasticity class of the first, the SCR distribution broadens with S, and only for S of 0.9 or more does its variance not differ from that of ECS in bicuculline; segregation of inputs by plasticity state would have to be almost complete.

    We found that for most S values, the variance of the SCRs is lower (p≤0.05, Fisher’s F-test); only for values of S≥0.9 was there no significant difference in variance between the SCR and ECS Δ N Strength distributions (p>0.05, Fisher’s F-test).

    Results, Mixing of unitary L4-L4 inputs, paragraph 1; Figure 3B and 3D; Methods, Input mixing simulation

    How it will be judged

    The simulation of claim fig3c-random-mixing-narrower for S from 0 to 1 in steps of 0.1. At S = 0 the class probabilities are the SCS proportions (7/43 LTP, 18/43 NC, 18/43 LTD, Results); at S = 1 every connection after the first is drawn from the first's class. The Methods say only that between these the probability of a same-class connection rises linearly; read here as P(same) = p + S(1 - p), p being the SCS proportion of the first's class. How the other two classes share 1 - P(same) is open, with readings (c) in their SCS proportions and (d) equally; they differ only when the first is NC or LTD. The readings of claim fig3c-random-mixing-narrower (replacement; one run or 1000 runs) apply. Reproduced when the F-test against the bicuculline ECS changes gives p of 0.05 or less for every S up to 0.8 (in (ii), the median p over runs) and above 0.05 for S of 0.9 and 1; partially reproduced when the smallest S without a difference is 0.7 or more and the SCR SD increases with S.
