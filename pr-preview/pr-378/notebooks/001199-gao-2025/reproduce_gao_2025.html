Reproducing Gao et al. (2025) from dandiset 001199¶

Paper. Gao H, Ramachandran S, Yu K, He B (2025). Transcranial Focused Ultrasound Modulates Feedforward and Feedback Cortico-Thalamo-Cortical Pathways by Selectively Activating Excitatory Neurons. J Neurosci 45(23):e2218242025. doi:10.1523/jneurosci.2218-24.2025. The claims file was written from the version of record in PubMed Central (PMC12139594), with its Methods and all figure legends; the paper has no supplement.

Dandiset. DANDI:001199, version 0.250829.1250. The paper does not cite this dandiset: its Methods name dandiset 000946 as the repository of all its data, with no version. 001199 has four published versions (0.250712.1551, 0.250713.1101, 0.250828.1941, 0.250829.1250). The cell below shows that all four and the draft hold the same 229 files (same paths and blobs) and differ only in metadata the analysis does not use (the order of contributors). The most recent published version is pinned, as the protocol says when the paper cites none.

What the dandiset holds. Sorted units (spike times only) and trial tables for 13 rats, recorded in August and September 2024 (4 rats) and February to July 2025 (9 rats). There are no spike waveforms and no RSU or FSU labels, every session uses PD 200 us, and AC recordings come from 7 rats.

The archive's summary says 13 subjects, as the files hold. Every file's age is P24W. The units tables hold spike times and, in 96 files, a celltype_label column described as "label 1 = RSU, label 2 = FSU". No file holds a 1 or a 2: the column holds 0 in the files of BH590 and BH594 and the area of the unit (S1 or POm) in those of BH596 and BH615. The 133 files of the 2025 cohort have no such column. No file holds waveforms. So the dandiset gives no way to type units as RSU or FSU, which the paper does from the waveforms (Methods, Neuronal type classification). The description says S1 is recorded with 64-channel probes and POm with 32-channel probes, and the files agree: S1 files list A1x64-Edge probes (four BH590 files a device "H9"), the POm and AC files of the 2025 cohort the A1x32-Poly3, and the 2024 files of BH596 and BH615 both. The paper used two 32-channel A1x32-Poly3 probes. Electrode locations are all "unknown".

def sessions_table(targets, min_spikes=tfus.MIN_SPIKES, cohorts=("2024", "2025")):
    rows = []
    for i in keep:

        if r.target not in targets or r.cohort not in cohorts:
            continue
        areas = {unit_area(i, k) for k, s in enumerate(files[i]["spikes"]) if len(s) > min_spikes}
        key = (r.rat, r.trial_key) if r.cohort == "2025" else (r.rat, i)
        rows += [{"rat": r.rat, "session": key, "area": a} for a in areas]
    s = pd.DataFrame(rows).groupby(["rat", "session"]).area.agg(lambda x: set(x)).reset_index()
    return s

def max_within(n_untyped, expected):
    """Most of a claim's typed counts that can fall within 10%, given the untyped count of the same
    area and condition. A typed count is at most the untyped one, so an RSU count needs at least
    0.9 x its expected value of units, and both counts of a pair need 0.9 x (RSU + FSU)."""
    rows = []
    for (key, n), (e_rsu, e_fsu) in zip(n_untyped.items(), expected):
        lo_r, lo_f = 0.9 * e_rsu, 0.9 * e_fsu
        best = 2 if n >= lo_r + lo_f else (1 if n >= min(lo_r, lo_f) else 0)
        rows.append({"group": key, "untyped units": n, "RSU needs >=": round(lo_r, 1), "FSU needs >=": round(lo_f, 1),
                     "both need >=": round(lo_r + lo_f, 1), "at most within 10%": best})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def ac_units(targets, sham=False):
    rows = []

def panel(df, label):
    out = []
    for w in ("0-67", "201-268"):
        for R in ("R1", "R2"):
            g = [df[df.prf == p][f"{R}_{w}"].values for p in tfus.PRFS]
            h, pk = tfus.kruskal(g)
            for T in ("T1", "T2"):
                p = np.array([tfus.vs_baseline(df[df.prf == q], w, R, T) for q in tfus.PRFS])
                row = {"set": label, "window": w, "R": R, "T": T, "KW_H": round(h, 2), "KW_p": pk}
                for q, m, pv in zip(tfus.PRFS, [np.nanmean(x) for x in g], p):
                    row[f"mean_{q}"] = round(m, 1)
                    row[f"p_{q}"] = pv
                for B, fac in (("B1", 5), ("B1x2", 10), ("B2", 15)):
                    row[f"sig_{B}"] = ",".join(str(q) for q, pv in zip(tfus.PRFS, p) if pv * fac < 0.01)
                row["lowest"] = tfus.PRFS[int(np.nanargmin([np.nanmean(x) for x in g]))]
                out.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

Result: not reproduced. There are 194 AC units with more than 2,000 spikes in 7 rats, against the paper's 453. In the main set no Kruskal-Wallis test across the five PRFs is significant at 0.01 (p from 0.013 to 0.74); with BH654 left out the R2 tests give 0.0095 and 0.006, with every R2 mean below 100% (48.6 to 66.6%). Against baseline only 1,500 Hz during sonication is significant, and only under R1 and T2 (mean 105.1%, uncorrected p = 6.4e-05, below 0.01 under every factor). The paper's pattern needs 1,500 and 3,000 Hz during sonication and 30, 1,500, 3,000 and 4,500 Hz after it. Here no PRF is above baseline after sonication under any reading, and 300 Hz has the lowest mean only under R1 during sonication without BH654. Per-rat R1 means lie between 88 and 127%. Under R2 every mean is below 100%, sham included (69.8%), because R2 keeps only trials with a prestimulus spike.

The outcome rests on two assumptions about the 2025 cohort, which holds every AC file: that its sessions were at the paper's 5 V, as nothing in the files states a voltage, and that its trial starts are the sonication onsets. The second is supported only in part. The population correlogram peaks within 0 to 400 ms of the trial start in 4 of 108 stimulated 2025 files, among them the BH648 4,500 Hz S1 file, and no other lag within 60 s fits. But untyped S1 units of the same cohort rise under T2 at 30 Hz (p = 7e-07 and 1.6e-12) and 1,500 Hz (2.7e-12 and 8.1e-10), where sham S1 units give p = 0.99 and 1 and sham AC units 0.57 (data check), so the S1 spikes lock to the trial starts in at least those sessions, mostly in BH646 and BH648. At the same PRFs and in the same rats, AC units show no increase after sonication at 30 or 1,500 Hz (R1 101.6% and 100.3%, T2 p = 0.5 and 0.34; BH646 101% and 109%, BH648 103% and 103%), where the paper reports one. So the failure of the pattern does not rest only on sessions whose timing is in doubt. For 300, 3,000 and 4,500 Hz the outputs cannot tell missing responses from trial times that are not onsets.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. Sessions are taken as the dandiset gives them, with "S1-6" counted as S1-targeted (a variant leaves it out). The 2024 sessions are limited to the paper's 5 V, as the claims file says; the 2025 sessions state no voltage and are all kept, taken to be at the paper's pressure, and their trial starts are taken as the sonication onsets. The unit counts of Fig. 4 are tested, and those of Fig. 5 bounded, through upper bounds from untyped units, as the units cannot be typed. AC units are all those with more than 2,000 spikes, as the Methods say, in the 7 rats with AC files.

What was found about the dataset. The dandiset holds spike times and trial tables only: no waveforms, so no RSU or FSU types. Its celltype_label column is described as RSU and FSU codes but holds 0 or the area in every file that has it. Only the 200 us PD series is here, with sessions at 10 and 15 V, POm targeting and probe variants the paper does not describe. Its 13 rats were recorded from August 2024 to July 2025, after the paper's preprint (June 2024), with 64-channel S1 probes. Of them only BH594 has a file in 000946, the dandiset the paper names for its data. Session start times do not date the sessions of the 2025 cohort. Two files are the same recording. Several target labels are unexplained. In most 2025 sessions the spikes show no locking to the trial starts at any lag within 60 s, though trial tables and spikes span the same times; untyped S1 units show weak locking at 30 and 1,500 Hz (T2, means 107 to 109%).
