Fmr1-null place cells resembled wild-type ones on single-cell measures, as the paper reports, but most evidence for discoordinated network discharge did not hold up here. Weaker pass-by-pass covariance between Fmr1-null place cell pairs held, though not at the paper's values; weaker Kendall correlations at gamma and theta timescales, less recurrent network states and weaker ensemble coordination during arena rotation were not seen. Spatial firing quality differed by genotype, mostly in interneurons. No isolation measures or waveforms are given, so all sorted clusters stood in for the paper's well-isolated cells. Slice physiology, LFP phase coupling and the link to place avoidance could not be tested, as those recordings were not deposited.

  • openasked 2026-11-05about overdispersion-passes

    The claims file reads the pass rule literally, a 5 s interval is a pass when exp, the expected spike count in the interval, is at least the cell's average rate in AP/s. Is the threshold meant as the count expected at the average rate over the interval (exp at least the average rate x 5 s), so that a pass is an interval in which the expected firing is at least the cell's average?     Passage. Only 5 s intervals when the mouse sampled a cell's firing field well were studied. These were identified by exp >= average rate for the cell (Fenton et al., 2010). To study discharge covariations between pairs of cells, we selected 5 s epochs when the mouse crossed the firing fields of both cells as epochs when expected spatial firing (exp) of each cell was above the individual cell's average rate. (STAR Methods, Overdispersion; the extracted text prints the sign as "R")

On the 1.25 cm maps the fig4b and overdispersion-cells outcomes do not change with it; on the code's 32 x 32 grid fig4b-covariance-40-passes meets its criterion with it (r 0.182 against 0.056, both within tolerance, p = 0.0004).
