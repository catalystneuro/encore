  • fig1d-cortical-feedback-alignment

    The alignment between feedback weights B and readout weights W_O in cortical neurons rises as learning performance improves, a sign of credit assignment in thalamocortical synapses.

    This improvement in learning performance is accompanied by an increase in the alignment between weights in the feedback pathway B (which communicate error signals to post-synaptic neurons) and readout weights W O

    Results, Thalamocortical learning rule, paragraph 2; Figure 1D (green)

    How it will be judged

    Same simulations as fig1c-tc-learning-improves-with-thalamus. Alignment is the normalized dot product of B and W_O (STAR Methods, Alignment between weights), normalized as (beta - beta_0)/(1 - beta_0) with beta_0 the alignment with a random direction (Figure 1 legend). The paper gives no test; the Spearman correlation is a standard choice. Reproduced when the median normalized alignment at the end of learning increases with M (Spearman correlation positive).

  • fig1cd-corticothalamic-plasticity-fails

    Local plasticity at corticothalamic as well as thalamocortical synapses gives no gain over thalamocortical learning with fixed random corticothalamic weights, and feedback alignment in thalamic neurons is no greater than chance.

    local plasticity operating simultaneously at thalamocortical and corticothalamic synapses does not yield any improvement in learning performance over what is obtained from learning only thalamocortical synapses with fixed random corticothalamic connections ... the alignment is no greater than chance in thalamic neurons

    Results, Thalamocortical learning rule, paragraphs 3 and 4; Figure 1C (gray) and 1D (black); Figure S1B and S1C

    How it will be judged

    The model of fig1c-tc-learning-improves-with-thalamus with corticothalamic weights also updated by Equation 6 (B' from U(0, 2/M)); the corticothalamic learning rate was set by hyperparameter optimization (Methods), whose search the paper does not give, so the rate with the lowest median error over 1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2 and 1e-1 is used. The paper gives no test. For each M, the median 1 - R2 of the two-site model is compared with the thalamocortical-only model by a two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum test over the 40 runs. Reproduced when for no M the two-site model is better at p < 0.05, and the median normalized thalamic alignment (B' against W_CT phi'(u) W_O) is within its bootstrap standard error of 0 for every M.

  • fig2d-metalearned-error-reduction

    The error dropped severalfold across both tasks when thalamocortical learning was performed using optimized corticothalamic weights as opposed to random corticothalamic weights ( Figure 2D ; median factor of reduction in error ... motor control, 5.8; working memory, 5.1)

    Results, Optimal corticothalamic connectivity improves learning performance, paragraph 3; Figure 2D

    How it will be judged

    Meta-learning as in STAR Methods (N = 256, M = 32, corticothalamic weights onto one thalamic unit optimized by BPTT with Adam, rate 1e-4, 10,000 epochs of 200 RFLO trials, thalamocortical weights reset each epoch, B^T = W_O, other weights fixed), then thalamocortical learning with the optimized and with random corticothalamic weights over 40 initializations. The factor is read as (R1) the median over runs of the ratio of errors, run by run, or (R2) the ratio of the median errors. Reproduced when both factors are within tolerance under one reading.
