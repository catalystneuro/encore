Summary: Most central findings held up in this data, one only in part, and several could not be tested. Optogenetic dopamine pairing raised target syllable use over controls (U = 198, paper 197), and dLight differed by syllable without identifying it. Dopamine also tracked syllable usage and transition entropy across syllables, and predicted later use at 6 of 10 windows. The catch-trial finding of no movement effect could not be tested, because the files do not mark catch trials. The food-reward data are missing, and seven analyses were skipped for time, including the reinforcement-learning model.

    These dopamine fluctuations (measured as both dLight transient rates and average binned amplitudes) were only weakly correlated with many aspects of movement kinematics ... but were significantly negatively correlated with translational velocity at short timescales (<10 s); this correlation reversed and became positive at longer timescales

    Results, Relating DLS dopamine to spontaneous behavior, paragraph 2; Figure 1e

    Over 121 DLS sessions from 14 mice, binned dLight amplitude and transient rate are both significantly negatively correlated with 2D speed at all 20 timescales below 10 s (amplitude mean r -0.201 to -0.109, rate -0.123 to -0.062; shuffle ranges within about 0.02 of zero), and all 14 mice are negative at 0.25 s for both. The transient rate turns positive from 18.75 s and is significantly positive at 47 of 100 timescales of 10 s or more (largest 0.113 at 58.75 s). The binned amplitude is still significantly negative at 49.75 s and is significantly positive at none of the 100 longer timescales (largest 0.005). The claim holds for one of the two measures under both readings of "short timescales".

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • dLight is re-referenced and z-scored from the stored dF/F series; the paper used a pre-computed z-scored trace that is not in the files.
    • 100 shuffles where the paper used 1,000.
    • 121 sessions where the paper's code reports 216 for this panel; the dandiset holds fewer sessions, and habituation sessions are left out here.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • dLight is re-referenced and z-scored from the stored dF/F series; the paper used a pre-computed z-scored trace that is not in the files.fig1e-velocity-timescales
  • 100 shuffles where the paper used 1,000.fig1e-velocity-timescales
  • 121 sessions where the paper's code reports 216 for this panel; the dandiset holds fewer sessions, and habituation sessions are left out here.fig1e-velocity-timescales
  • Instances from 177 DLS sessions without stimulation (habituation sessions left out); the paper used more sessions per mouse.fig1h-syllable-dlight-differs
  • A 100-tree sklearn random forest with at least 5 instances per leaf on 1,000 instances per syllable, where the paper used cuML with 1,000 trees; the paper gives no accuracy, so "unable to predict" was set as below twice chance.fig1l-identity-not-decodable
  • One label shuffle per feature set as the null, where the paper used 100.fig1l-identity-not-decodable
  • Fivefold cross-validation repeated 20 times and 200 shuffles, where the paper used 100 repeats and 1,000 shuffles.fig2b-dlight-usage-across-syllables
  • Plain Shannon entropy of the outgoing transition probabilities per experiment, as the Methods state; the code uses a Dirichlet entropy estimate.fig2i-dlight-entropy-across-syllables
  • Five equal time segments in place of the paper's five behavioural segments (Extended Data Fig. 2e).fig2e-dlight-predicts-future-usage
  • Correlations are compared with shuffles directly, not z-scored to them; 100 shuffles.fig2e-dlight-predicts-future-usage
  • Same deviations as fig2e-dlight-predicts-future-usage.ed7-dms-no-usage-prediction
  • Stimulation, baseline and post sessions are identified from stimulation pulses, session dates and descriptions, as the files give no session number or type.fig3e-optoda-learning
