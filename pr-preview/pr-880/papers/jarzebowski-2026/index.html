  • model-positive-error-short-latency

    In the two-compartment model, L4 sensory input without L5 IT predictive input makes the L2/3 neuron fire a short-latency singlet or doublet, depending on L4 strength, immediately after the input arrives; basket cell inhibition ends the response.

    When L4 sensory input arrives in the absence of L5 IT predictive input, the L2/3 neuron produces a singlet or doublet depending on the L4 input strength ... Because no ChC clamp is active, action potential initiation at the AIS is rapid and unimpeded. The resulting spike or burst occurs immediately following sensory input arrival

    Results, Positive prediction error, paragraph 1; Figure 1D

    How it will be judged

    Simulated with the Methods' parameters (L4 60 nS, tau 4 ms; basket 1.2 times L4, tau 6 ms, onset 2.5 ms after L4; no L5 IT or ChC input; 80 runs with noise). Reproduced when the neuron fires in most runs and the first spike falls within the first few ms after L4 onset, with no spike after the basket conductance peaks, and stronger L4 gives doublets. "Short" has no number in the paper; the latency is reported. The simulation time step is not given; a standard 0.1 ms step is used, a choice that should not change the outcome.

  • model-negative-error-delayed-release

    With L5 IT predictive input and no sensory input, the ChC clamp holds the AIS below threshold and the neuron fires a single gated release spike after the clamp decays, 15 to 20 ms after the time the same L5 IT input could have triggered the L5 ET bAP.

    In the absence of additional sensory drive from L4, the AIS cannot escape this clamp until ChC conductance sufficiently decays, at which point the accumulated depolarisation is released as a singlet if the soma remains sufficiently depolarised ( Fig. 1D ). In such case, the L2/3 input arrives to the L5 ET 15–20 ms delayed

    Results, Negative prediction error, paragraph 1; Figure 1D

    How it will be judged

    Simulated with the Methods' defaults (L5 IT 12 nS, tau 8 ms; ChC 25 nS, tau 4 ms, reversal -58 mV, onset 1.5 ms after L5 IT; no L4 input; 80 runs). The range 15 to 20 ms is widened by 10% of each bound (13.5 to 22 ms). The model has no L5 ET neuron, so the reference time is open. Readings (a) latency of the first spike from L5 IT onset; (b) the same, measured from the time of the L5 IT conductance peak (8 ms after onset). Reproduced when, under one reading, the median latency over runs that spike lies in the range and runs that spike fire a single spike.

The onset of L4 relative to L5 IT is not given for Figure 2A and 2B; simultaneous onset is the reading, as Figure 2C shows delays of up to 5 ms leave the behaviour unchanged.

  • fig2b-breakthrough-doublet-threshold
