Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Metadata errorRat_3_Type_HCage_Session_2.mat is not a separate recording. 287 of its 291 segments are segments of Rat_3_Type_HCage_Session_1.mat with the same LFP and movement values, 100 s later on that file's clock (they cover 106 to 750 s of it). The data set presents it as a second home-cage session.
  • Data missingThe data set holds only the BF LFP of six rats in home cage and arena. The ACC and VC recordings, single units, object sessions, video and behaviour scoring of the paper are not in it, and the paper names no other deposit; 16 of the 28 claims need data that are not in it.
  • Description missingThe sessions carry no day and no time of recording, and each file has its own clock starting at 0, so the paper's day factor, the order of home-cage and arena sessions and the transfer between them cannot be reconstructed. Which electrodes were bipolar is not stated.
  • Description missingThe LFP has no stated unit (its range is consistent with microvolts), and the movement value is dimensionless; its scale in rats 4 and 5 is about a quarter of that in the other rats, which is not explained.
  • Selection not documentedArtifact segments were removed without a stated rule. Removed segments make up 2.6% to 23.5% of each file's span, more than the 1-4% the SI reports for its rule, and the SI's rule applied to the delivered segments removes a further 16.8%.
  • Metadata errorThe descriptor names the home-cage condition 'Hcage' while the files use 'HCage', and describes basal_forebrain_lfp.tar.gz as a file of .mat files without saying it is a gzipped tar.
  • OtherIn 13 of 24 files the movement value of a segment correlates best with the 100-200 Hz LFP power of the next segment, and in 10 with the same segment, so the movement stream may lead the LFP by up to one 2-s segment. Rat 2's home-cage session 1 has a line at 50 Hz, 26 times its neighbours, inside the gamma band.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. What were the observations of the one-way ANOVAs over movement quartiles (Fig. 3B), per-hemisphere quartile means or epochs, and was gamma normalized per animal? On this data set per-rat means give p = 0.82 for the home-cage rise and p = 0.07 for the movement-matched comparison, while repeated-measures and paired tests give p < 0.01.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. How was the 35% reduction of gamma power computed (spectral estimate, scaling, averaging)? A periodogram mean over 30-80 Hz gives 48% on this data set, under every epoch, artifact and averaging reading.
  • Description missingThe descriptor calls sessions 1 and 2 two separate examples of each session type and does not say whether they are the first and second arena trials of one day, the paper's two daily 10-min trials (Fig. 3C). Here session 2 has 15.9% lower gamma than session 1, where the paper reports 5.62% higher in the second trial, so the order matters and cannot be recovered.

S4 and S5, what was the power scale (the threshold of 2.6), how were histogram bins set, and was there a minimum excursion length? Without one, the readings tried give 10.7 to 14.5 excursions per 20 min lasting 5 s on average, against 8 lasting 8 to 44 s.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. Both claims would be reproduced under any of these; the arena absence holds under all of them (p > 0.1). Raised by the round-1 verifier.

Reproduction Basal forebrain gamma in home cage and arena (Nair et al. 2018)

Tests the findings of Nair, Klaassen, Arato, Vyssotski, Harvey and Rainer (2018) on CRCNS data set bf-1, which holds basal forebrain LFP from six rats in two home-cage and two arena sessions each, with a movement-sensor value per 2-s segment. It measures the reduction of BF gamma power in the arena, its relation to movement and to time in the arena, the low-frequency bands, the unimodality of theta and gamma power, and excursions of gamma during exploration. The ACC, VC, single-unit, object and behaviour data of the paper are not in the data set. All files

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • The data set gives one BF signal per rat for six rats, so tests run over rats (n = 6), or session pairs as a variant, where the paper used 22 hemispheres (claims file header). The two sessions of a condition are averaged per rat (n = 6); rat 3's home-cage session 2 is left out as a second deposit of its session 1, so rat 3's home cage is session 1 alone.fig3a-bf-gamma-reduction fig3b-arena-gamma-unrelated-to-movement fig3b-home-cage-gamma-increases-with-movement fig3b-matched-movement fig3c-home-cage-above-both-sessions fig3d-gamma-rises-with-time-in-arena figs2-bf-low-frequency-bands figs3-unimodal-states figs5-arena-excursions
  • The segments come already down-sampled to 400 Hz and cleaned by the depositors. The SI's peak-to-peak rule applied again (claims file header, reading b) drops 16.8% of segments, against the SI's 1-4%; both readings are run.fig3a-bf-gamma-reduction fig3b-arena-gamma-unrelated-to-movement fig3b-home-cage-gamma-increases-with-movement fig3b-matched-movement fig3c-home-cage-above-both-sessions figs2-bf-low-frequency-bands
  • The paper does not say how the spectrum is scaled or windowed; a plain periodogram is used (claims file header, a standard choice that cannot change a ratio).fig3a-bf-gamma-reduction
  • The unit is a rat and session (12 recordings) where the paper tested 22 hemispheres (criterion).fig3a-each-hemisphere-significant
  • The paper does not say what the ANOVA's observations are; the criterion takes per-rat quartile means, and the epoch-level and repeated-measures tests are shown as variants.fig3b-home-cage-gamma-increases-with-movement
  • Movement values differ in scale between rats (rats 4 and 5 about a quarter of the others), so matching by the mean over rats of the quartile movement values is approximate; a within-rat match is shown as a variant.fig3b-matched-movement
  • The data set has no day labels, so the day factor is dropped and the arena sessions are compared within rat by a paired t test, as the criterion says.fig3c-home-cage-above-both-sessions
  • The data set's session numbers are not taken as the first and second daily arena sessions. The descriptor calls them two separate examples (Session 1 and 2) of each session type, and rat 3's home-cage session 2 is an excerpt of its session 1, so the files may be chosen excerpts. The arena 2 against arena 1 part of the criterion is therefore not judged (metadata_insufficient), and the outcome rests on the home-cage part.fig3c-home-cage-above-both-sessions
  • The paper's n = 22 matches its hemispheres; here 12 arena recordings.fig3d-gamma-rises-with-time-in-arena
  • Each file's clock starts at 0 and files run 8.8 to 13 min, longer than the SI's 10-min trial, and nothing says where arena entry is. The 10-min window is taken from the file start and, as a reading, from the file end; both give the same outcome, and reading (b) is shown over whole files, which needs no anchor.fig3d-gamma-rises-with-time-in-arena
  • Assumed 1-min bins for reading (a), as the criterion names them (Figure 3D's axis runs in minutes); the criterion says the bin width is open, and 30-s bins put r within tolerance (0.744 and 0.747), as do 20-s bins with the end anchor (0.662), which would give reproduced.   • The boxcar runs over the sequence of retained segments within a file; the two arena files are analysed as separate series, as they share no clock.figs5-arena-excursions
  • The paper's power scale, histogram bins and minimum excursion length are not given; the readings of the criterion are run and none gives durations of 8 s or more only.figs5-arena-excursions
