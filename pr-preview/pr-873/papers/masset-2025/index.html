From the paper Multi-timescale reinforcement learning in the brain. · claims file · checked 2026-11-20: ready

  • fig1f-multi-timescale-advantage

    In simulations of a linear maze, agents learning values with three discount factors (0.6, 0.9, 0.99) report reward timing independently of magnitude, the hyperbolic value of the cue, reward timing before convergence and the true value under noisy rewards more accurately than agents with one or two discount factors.

    Our result shows that only multi-timescale systems can reliably report the hyperbolic value of the cue given a diversity of exponential ones

    Results, Advantages of multi-timescale RL, Tasks 1 to 4; Figure 1e-f; Extended Data Fig. 1

    How it will be judged

    Run as the Methods (Decoding Tasks) and the script set it, 2,000 training episodes, accuracy over 100 test episodes and 3 trained networks. The accuracies are shown only in Fig. 1f, so the claim is judged by order. Reproduced when the three-discount agent has the highest mean accuracy in all four tasks; partially reproduced in two or three tasks.

  • mixing-model-accuracy

    With a small degree of shared value across discounts, the three-discount agent keeps its accuracy, 82, 94, 92 and 93% in Tasks 1 to 4.

    the accuracy of the report of the agent with three discounts {0.6,0.9,0.99} is 82 ± 5% in Task 1, 94 ± 2% in Task 2, 92 ± 3% in Task 3 and 93 ± 3% in Task 4

    Methods, Mixing in distributed RL models, paragraph 3
  • ed2e-branching-myopic-bias

    In a branching MDP learned from three trajectories, a two-discount agent with discounts 0.6 and 0.99 reaches 94% correct, more than single-discount agents (77% with 0.6, 83% with 0.99).

    For the single-discount agent, we report the maximum performance over the agents with discounts [0.6,0.6] and [0.99,0.99], which achieve a performance of 77±2% and 83±1% respectively. For the multi-discount agent, we use the discounts [0.6,0.99], which achieves a performance of 94±1%

    Methods, Myopic learning bias, branching task, paragraph 4; Extended Data Fig. 2e
  • ed2-myopic-bias-maze-lunar

    Myopic value estimates are more accurate near important events and far-sighted ones far from them, in a grid-world maze learned from two trajectories and in the auxiliary Q-values of a DQN trained on Lunar Lander.

    Q-values computed using a myopic discount provide a more accurate representation of the future when the agent is close to the landing site (blue in Extended Data Fig. 2k), whereas the opposite holds when the agent is far from the landing site

    Methods, Myopic learning bias, grid world and auxiliary tasks; Extended Data Fig. 2h and 2l

    How it will be judged

    Kendall rank correlation between estimated and true values as a function of the discount, as the Methods set it (10,000 sets of two trajectories for the maze, while the Extended Data Fig. 2h legend says error bars are across 300 sets; 10 networks, 25,000 states for Lunar Lander). The paper does not say where close and far from the landing site divide; the code (Fig_2_g_lunar_q_accuracy.py, compute_accuracy_for_states) splits states at the median height of the lander, and that split is used. Reproduced when, in both settings, the discount with the highest accuracy is lower for the near group (lower half of the maze; close to the landing site) than for the far group.

  • cued-delay-neurons

    Of 78 optogenetically identified VTA dopamine neurons recorded in the cued delay task, 50 pass the selection criteria.

    We recorded optogenetically identified single dopamine neurons in the ventral tegmental area (VTA) (n = 78, see Methods). We focused our analysis on neurons (n = 50) who passed the selection criteria

    Results, Discounting across dopamine neurons, paragraph 2; Methods, Fitting neural activity in the cued delay task, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged

    The selection is M3 on the fits of M1 and M2. The identified neurons are the units the dandiset marks as optogenetically identified dopamine neurons; the SALT threshold is not given in this paper (it is Kim et al. 2020's), so the dandiset's own marking is used, and the Mendeley flags (Valid.OdorNew) are a variant where they can be matched. The paper's exclusion counts (17, 11, 4) disagree with 78 minus 50; the counts found at each step are reported.

  • session-trial-counts

    Mice performed 81.4 +- 12.5 trials per session across 36 cued delay sessions and 58.8 +- 21.7 trials per session across 60 VR sessions with recorded neurons.

    Mice performed 81.4 ± 12.5 trials (mean ± s.d.) per session across the 36 sessions in which neurons were recorded in the task ... Mice performed 58.8 ± 21.7 trials (mean ± s.d.) per session across the 60 sessions in which neurons were recorded in the task

    Methods, Cued delay task, paragraph 2; Methods, Approach-to-target virtual reality task, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    The Methods count "sessions in which neurons were recorded in the task", which does not say whether the neurons are all recorded dopamine neurons or the analysed ones. Readings for the sessions are (a) sessions with at least one optogenetically identified dopamine neuron recorded in the task, (b) sessions with at least one neuron of the analysed set (M3 for the cued delay task, M6 for VR). Reproduced when the counts and means are within tolerance under one session reading and one trial reading.

  • fig2b-anticipatory-licking

    Mice lick in anticipation of reward, and the onset of anticipatory licking is later for cues predicting longer delays.

    Importantly, the onset of the anticipatory licking was delayed for trials with cues predicting longer reward delays, indicating that the mice learned the delay contingencies

    Results, Discounting across dopamine neurons, paragraph 2; Figure 2b

    How it will be judged

    The paper gives no test and no definition of onset. Lick rate per delay in 200 ms bins from -2 to 12 s around the odor (code LickPSTHs_Revision.m), computed for the session of each recorded neuron and averaged over neurons (Fig. 2b legend "mean across behavior for all recorded neurons"; the code loops over every loaded neuron). Whether "all recorded neurons" means the 78 recorded or the 50 analysed is open; both are run. Onset is open; readings (a) the first bin after the odor at which the lick rate exceeds the pre-cue mean by half of its rise to the pre-reward maximum, (b) the first bin exceeding the pre-cue mean by 2 s.d. of the pre-cue bins. Reproduced when, under one reading, onset increases with delay across the four cues (ties between the two shortest delays allowed, since 0.6 s is three bins).

  • fig2f-exponential-better-than-hyperbolic

    The exponential model provided a better fit to the neurons’ responses than the hyperbolic model (P = 2.2 x 10−5, two-tailed t-test, comparing the distribution across neurons of the mean (across bootstraps) difference in R2 between the two fits

    Results, Discounting across dopamine neurons, paragraph 3; Figure 2f; Extended Data Fig. 3c-e

    How it will be judged

Reproduced when the mean difference is positive and p < 0.05; the p-value is compared with 2.2e-5.
