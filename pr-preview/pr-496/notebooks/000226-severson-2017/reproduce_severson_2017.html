Reproducing Severson et al. (2017) from dandiset 000226¶

Paper. Severson KS, Xu D, Van de Loo M, Bai L, Ginty DD, O'Connor DH (2017). Active touch and self-motion encoding by Merkel cell-associated afferents. Neuron 94:666-676. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2017.03.045. This attempt used the full text of the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC5528144) and the publisher's Supplemental Information (Table S1 and the supplemental figure legends). The version of record is not open access. The paper's analysis code is not public; the Methods say it is available on request, and no repository for it was found.

Dandiset. DANDI:000226, version 0.230607.1747. The paper cites no DANDI version (it predates the deposit). This is the only published version; the draft has the same 60 assets and size, so the most recent published version is pinned.

What the dandiset holds. One NWB file per recording: 56 trigeminal afferents recorded with high-speed whisker video, and 4 EMG sessions, which match the 60 recordings of Table S1. Each file holds the spike times of one unit (none in three EMG files) and, for every 5 s trial, the whisker kinematics, contact, distance to the pole, curvature change, and for most recordings with a pole, forces and bending moment, at 500 Hz. The files do not say which experiment a recording belongs to, the afferent type, or which afferents were identified as Merkel afferents; these come from Table S1 (see table-s1-link).

Streaming. Each file is read whole into memory from the archive and never written to disk (see merkel.py). The run takes about 21 minutes, most of it reading 13.7 GB and fitting the spline models.

import re
def rec_of(identifier):
    # KS0240A -> 240A; KS0007A -> 7A; KS0125_ -> 125_ (matched by number in table-s1-link)
    m = re.fullmatch(r"KS0*(\d+)([A-Z_])", identifier)
    return f"{int(m.group(1))}{m.group(2)}"

def number_of(identifier):
    return int(re.fullmatch(r"KS0*(\d+)[A-Z_]", identifier).group(1))

# The touch and WT* recordings by mouse and date, to decide what to keep before the identifier is read.
def wanted(path):
    sub = path.split("/")[0].replace("sub-", "")
    date = path.split("ses-")[1][:8]
    rows = table[(table.mouse == sub) & (table.date.map(lambda d: pd.to_datetime(d, format="%m/%d/%y").strftime("%Y%m%d")) == date)]
    return set(rows.index)

def reduce_one(item):
    i, (path, size, url) = item
    cands = wanted(path)
    return merkel.reduce(path, url, seed=1000 + i,
                         keep_touch=bool(cands & set(TOUCH_33)), keep_wia=bool(cands & set(WT_STAR_15)))

with ThreadPoolExecutor(3) as ex:

Protraction (positive velocity) contacts mostly have M0 > 0, as Figure 3F has it, so the sign of M0 gives the contact direction.

Mean position is highest in the bins next to phase 0 in all 60 files, so phase 0 is full protraction, as in the Hilbert convention the paper cites (Hill et al. 2011). But the stored phase runs backwards in time: the sine of the stored phase is anticorrelated (median -0.9999) and its cosine correlated (median 0.9999) with those of the Hilbert phase computed here from the band-passed position, so the stored phase is the negative of the Hilbert phase, not a shift by pi. Velocity is negative at phases below 0 in every file. In the files, phases from -pi to 0 are retraction and 0 to pi protraction, the reverse of the usual convention. Neither the files nor the paper state which convention the stored phase follows.

The stored velocity, acceleration, jerk and the rates of change of moment and forces are per millisecond (ratio near 0.5 to the change per 2 ms frame), while the files state per second ("degree/s", "N*m/s"). Phase curves and the spline models do not depend on these scales; the viscoelastic model is given dM0 in N m per second.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper's GAMs (mgcv, 1 ms bins, UBRE smoothing) are replaced by penalized B-spline models fitted as binomial models with two trials per 2 ms frame, on at most 60,000 frames per recording; the viscoelastic model is fitted with L-BFGS-B from eight starts. Confidence intervals for the Glossary classes are bootstrapped over trials. The kinematic models of Figure S6 are tested on 15 of the paper's 28 afferents. Which recording belongs to which experiment, and the afferent types, come from Table S1. Two outcomes sit near their thresholds: the Rayleigh p of fig5e-phase-span moves across 0.05 with the preferred-phase estimator, and M0 and M0' rank second of the 45 pairs, 0.01 behind M0 and velocity, under every variant tried.

What was found about the dandiset. The 60 files match Table S1 one to one, and most values come close to the paper's frame counts, rates and model performance. The exceptions are the median non-preferred phase rate (16% above the paper's), the mean curvature noise (28% above), and direction selectivity (65% of Merkel and SA afferents at an index of 0.5 or more, against the 80% the criterion asks). But the files do not say which experiment each recording belongs to, which three touch recordings the paper excluded, the afferent type or Merkel identity, or the meaning of their series (every series is "no description"). The progressive cutting recordings label every trial intact, though their rates fall over the session. The intrinsic protractor EMG, the optogenetic tagging data and the whisker cut lengths are not in the dandiset. The derivatives are stored per millisecond but labelled per second, the stored whisk phase is the negative of the Hilbert phase, so it runs backwards in time, trial intervals are 7 s for 5 s of data, one file's spike times are unsorted, and two identifiers lack the unit letter. Two recordings made with the whisker cut (199B, 209A) hold distance-to-pole series and some contact frames, which the files do not explain.
