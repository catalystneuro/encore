  • fig4c-broad-ccg-peaks

    V1-V2 CCGs frequently have broad peaks, tens to hundreds of milliseconds wide, evident in site-average CCGs at all laminar locations.

    We frequently observed broad peaks in V1-V2 CCGs, tens to hundreds of milliseconds wide. These were evident in the average V1-V2 CCGs at all laminar locations

    Results, V1-V2 Recordings, paragraph 4 ("To understand how"); Figure 4C, left

    How it will be judged

    Site-average shuffle-corrected CCGs (shared CCG method, not jitter-corrected). The paper gives no test for a broad peak, so one is fixed here and is open. Readings, (a) the mean CCG over lags -50 to +50 ms is above the mean over the 75 to 125 ms flanks; (b) the mean over lags -50 to +50 ms is above zero. Reproduced when, under one reading, a majority of V2 sites have such a peak and sites both with and without a significant narrow peak have it. The fraction of sites and the width at half height of the across-site average are reported.

  • fig4c-narrow-peak-sites

    A significant narrow peak in the site-average V1-V2 CCG occurs at a minority of V2 sites (46 of 223).

    Average V1-V2 CCGs contained a significant narrow peak at 46 of 223 V2 sites

    Results, V1-V2 Recordings, paragraph 5 ("At a subset of V2 sites")

    How it will be judged

    Shared CCG method, site-average jitter-corrected CCG per V2 electrode and site, 5 SD rule. The paper's sample targeted middle-layer sites in some penetrations (Experimental Procedures, Physiology), so the fraction depends on sampling; the count of sites and of significant sites is reported beside the fraction.

  • fig5a-rf-alignment

    The probability that a V1-V2 pair has a significant narrow CCG peak is highest for RF offsets below 0.5 deg, about 1%, and falls quickly with greater offset.

    The probability was highest for pairs with offsets of <0.5�, and decayed quickly with greater offsets (Figure 5A). Even for pairs with well-aligned RFs, the probability was only �1%

    Results, V1-V2 Recordings, paragraph 6 ("The probability of observing"); Figure 5A

    How it will be judged

    Pairs judged by the 5 SD rule (shared CCG method); RF centre-to-centre distance from the 2D Gaussian fits (Visual Stimuli). Reproduced when the 0 to 0.5 deg bin has the highest probability, the probability in the 1 to 1.5 deg bin is below half of it, and the 0 to 0.5 deg value is within tolerance; partially reproduced when the ordering holds and the value does not. Open, the conversion of RF positions to degrees, which the paper does not give (it gives 1024 x 768 pixels at 110 cm but no screen size); the attempt states the scale it uses and where it comes from.

  • fig5b-v1v2-latency

    Narrow V1-V2 CCG peaks are offset from zero lag by a median of 2.8 ms, significantly different from zero, with a mean peak width of 3.71 ms, consistent with direct V1 to V2 input.

    Narrow peaks were offset from zero time lag by a median of 2.8 ms (Figure 5B, gray; p < 0.001 for difference with 0), with an average peak width of 3.71 ± 0.05 ms (SEM)

    Results, V1-V2 Recordings, paragraph 7 ("We next analyzed"); Figure 5B, gray

    How it will be judged

    Significant pairs as in fig5-narrow-pairs-rare; offset is the lag of the maximum of the smoothed jitter-corrected CCG within 10 ms of zero, positive for V2 after V1. The width tolerance is twice the paper's SEM, about its 95% interval. Open, the test against zero, which the paper does not name; readings (a) Wilcoxon signed-rank, (b) sign test, both two-sided. Open, the width measure, which the paper does not define; readings (a) full width at half the peak height of the smoothed jitter-corrected CCG, (b) the same on the unsmoothed CCG. Reproduced when the test gives p < 0.001 and the median offset is within tolerance; the width is judged under either reading.

  • fig5b-intra-areal-zero-lag

    Significant narrow peaks of V1-V1 and V2-V2 jitter-corrected CCGs have a median offset of 0.0 ms, not different from zero.

    significant peaks in V1-V1 (Figure 5B; blue, n = 2845) and V2-V2 (red, n = 368) jitter-corrected CCGs had a median offset of 0.0 ms (p = 0.45 and 0.42, respectively, for difference from zero)

    Results, V1-V2 Recordings, paragraph 7 ("We next analyzed"); Figure 5B, blue and red

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. Pairs within each area with a significant narrow peak (shared CCG method, 5 SD rule). The lag sign is arbitrary within an area; the pair order is the order of the unit lists, a standard choice. The paper does not name its test; readings (a) Wilcoxon signed-rank, (b) sign test, two-sided at p < 0.05. Reproduced when the test finds no difference from zero in both areas; the medians are reported either way.

  • fig7a-threefold

    For jitter windows of 5 to 80 ms, divergence before middle-layer V2 spikes is roughly 3-fold that of control epochs.

    Divergence values for epochs associated with V2 spiking were roughly 3-fold greater than in control epochs

    Results, V2 Spiking Is Preceded by Stronger V1 Coordination, paragraph 4 ("For epochs 1.5-3.5 ms before"); Figure 7A

    How it will be judged

    Ratio of the divergence values of fig6a-coordination-before-v2 at 5, 10, 20, 40 and 80 ms. The tolerance is wider than 10% because the paper says "roughly" and reads it off a log axis.   • fig7a-above-shuffle

    Divergence both before middle-layer V2 spikes and in control epochs is significantly larger than divergence of shuffled data, so V1 activity is coordinated also in control epochs.

    Note that both for epochs preceding V2 spiking and for control epochs, the divergence values were significantly larger than those obtained from shuffled data (Figure 7A, black)

    Results, V2 Spiking Is Preceded by Stronger V1 Coordination, paragraph 4 ("For epochs 1.5-3.5 ms before"); Figure 7A, black

    How it will be judged

    Shuffled data remove correlations (Results, same paragraph); the paper does not say how. Readings (a) each V1 neuron's trials permuted independently among trials of the same orientation, (b) among all trials. Divergence of shuffled data against their own jitter surrogate. The epochs of the shuffled data are not given either; readings (c) the spike-triggered epochs, (d) the control epochs, the same number in both. Reproduced when the lower 95% bootstrap bound of both spike and control divergence is above the shuffled value at every jitter window from 5 to 80 ms (the paper's statement has no exception); partially reproduced at a majority of them.
