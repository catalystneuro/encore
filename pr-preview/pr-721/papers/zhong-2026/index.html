  • eq1-new-magic-number

    With hierarchical chunking the largest number of items that can be held and retrieved is M* = 2^(C-1), with C the basic capacity; for C = 4 this is 8, reached by a binary tree of three levels (four chunks of two in two meta-chunks).

    Let us consider the example corresponding to C = 4, with a capacity of M ∗ = 24−1 = 8. In this case, the optimal chunking structure is a binary tree with three levels

    Results, Hierarchical chunking predicts a new capacity, paragraphs 1 to 3; Methods B

    How it will be judged

    A derivation (Methods B), not a finding on data; DANDI:000207 cannot test it. It holds when maximising the number of stimulus clusters in a tree of K levels with integer branching ratios c_k, subject to c_K plus the sum over k < K of (c_k - 1) being at most C, gives 2^(C-1), and 8 for C = 4 with c = 2 at every level. Equations (9) to (14) are lost in the text read; the constraint is taken from the words of Methods B. Tolerance 0, as a count.

  • fig3a-dip-after-boundary

    Aligned to movie cuts and averaged over subjects and trials, the firing of non-boundary neurons dips about 130 ms after the peak of boundary-neuron firing, then ramps up continuously, as the chunking model predicts.

    we find that after the peak in the firing rate of boundary neurons (top panel of Fig. 3 (a)), about ~ 130 ms later, there is a dip in the average activities of the rest of the recorded neurons (bottom panel of Fig. 3 (a)). Further-more, there is a continuous ramp-up of activities following the dip.

    Results, Experimental evidence for the existence of chunking clusters, paragraph 2; Figure 3A

    How it will be judged

    From the paper (Methods E, Fig. 3 legend). The 14 included subjects; trials aligned to the cut (time zero); soft and hard boundary neurons pooled as boundary neurons and all other detected neurons as non-boundary; per subject, the raw firing rates of all boundary (or non-boundary) neurons pooled over neurons and trials, z-scored, and averaged across subjects; the mean is the trace, one standard deviation the shade. Open, with the readings run. (1) Boundary-neuron selection, which the paper takes from [41] without giving the test; (a) the classification of [41] where the dataset or [41] gives it, (b) re-selection by the method in the Methods of [41], cuts of either type. (2) Trials; clips with a cut (soft and hard) only, the reading the text supports ("each consisting of two episodes separated by a cut"). (3) Bins and smoothing; 10 ms and 25 ms bins, each with no smoothing and with a Gaussian kernel of 50 ms SD. (4) Window, -1 to 2 s around the cut, a standard choice. (5) Z-score reference; (a) the subject's whole trace in the window, (b) its pre-cut part (-1 to 0 s). Measures fixed here as the paper gives none. Boundary peak is the time of the maximum of the boundary trace after time zero; the dip is the minimum of the non-boundary trace from that peak to 500 ms after it; the lag is the dip time minus the peak time. A dip is present when its value is below the mean of the non-boundary trace from -1 to 0 s; a ramp is present when a line fitted to the non-boundary trace from the dip to 1 s after the cut has positive slope. Reproduced when the dip and the ramp are present and the lag is within tolerance; partially reproduced when dip and ramp are present but the lag is outside it. Tolerance is 10% of 130 ms, widened to one bin when the bin is wider, since the lag cannot be resolved finer than a bin. The paper's "about ~" marks the value as approximate.

    While some subjects do not exhibit the qualitative trend as predicted (e.g., the firing rate of subject P64CS does not have a ramp, and TWH120 does not have a dip), most of the subjects’ firing rates follow the same qualitative trend as observed in the average plot in Fig. 3(a).

"Most" is read as more than half of the 14 subjects (8 or more). Reproduced when, under one reading, 8 or more subjects show both a dip and a ramp, P64CS shows no ramp and TWH120 shows no dip; partially reproduced when 8 or more show both under a reading but under no reading do both named exceptions hold. Not reproduced when fewer than 8 show both under every reading. The count, and the subjects sorted by dip time as in Fig. 3(b), are reported.
