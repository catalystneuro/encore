Reproducing Chowdhury, Glaser & Miller (2020) from dandiset 000127¶

Paper: Chowdhury RH, Glaser JI, Miller LE (2020). Area 2 of primary somatosensory cortex encodes kinematics of the whole arm. eLife 9:e48198. doi:10.7554/eLife.48198 (open access; the full text and methods were used).

Data: DANDI:000127, version 0.220113.0359, streamed from the archive.

What can and cannot be tested¶

The paper has two experiments. Eight sessions from three monkeys compare reaching in two workspaces (Figures 4 to 7). Four sessions from two monkeys compare active reaches with passive bumps of the hand (Figures 8 to 11).

The dandiset holds one session: monkey H ("Han") in the active/passive task on 2017-12-07, as packaged for the Neural Latents Benchmark. So:

The stand-in. The paper's whole-arm model uses the positions and velocities of markers on the hand and elbow. The marker data is not in the dandiset. It does hold the seven joint angles and their velocities computed from those markers, so the whole-arm model here is hand kinematics plus joint kinematics. The paper's appendix reports that, on the two-workspace data, a joint-kinematics model gave predictions similar to its whole-arm model, though somewhat worse.

Everything below is from one session of one monkey, where the paper has four sessions from two.

Checks against the file's own descriptions and the paper:

  • Units. The descriptions of hand position and velocity say centimetres, while the stated unit is metres with a conversion of 0.01. Applying the conversion gives a workspace about 0.3 m across and speeds under 1 m/s, which is plausible for a monkey reaching on a manipulandum, so the values are stored in centimetres and are converted to metres here.
  • Directions. This session has eight target and bump directions. The paper's methods describe four.
  • Onset. For passive trials the recorded movement onset is within a few milliseconds of the bump time for the typical trial (range -49 to +27 ms), so it marks the bump, as the analysis needs.
  • Trial types. In the file's result column, "R" marks rewarded trials; only those are used, as in the paper.

Claim fig8-neurons-separable¶

"Many neurons had an above chance separability index." The separability index of a neuron is the cross-validated accuracy of a linear discriminant predicting the trial type from that neuron's spike count in the window.

Neurons firing below 1 Hz in the window are left out of this and the model analyses. With a 120 ms window they have almost no spikes, and model fits for them are meaningless. The paper does not state a firing-rate criterion.

Models of neural activity¶

As in the paper: a Poisson generalised linear model per neuron predicts its spike count in the window from kinematics, scored by pseudo-R2 on held-out trials over 20 repeats of 5-fold cross-validation, with models compared by the corrected resampled t-test.

  • Hand-only: hand position and velocity in the plane (5 parameters; the paper's has 7, in three dimensions).
  • Whole-arm stand-in: hand kinematics plus seven joint angles and velocities (19 parameters; the paper's whole-arm model has 13).

Two deviations from the paper's method, both to keep the fits stable at these low counts: a small ridge penalty on the weights, and pseudo-R2 for one trial type is computed on the pooled held-out predictions of a whole repeat instead of on single folds.

Read literally the claim holds: 19 of 50 neurons scored on active trials and 23 of 50 scored on passive trials have negative pseudo-R2 for the model trained on both conditions. But about as many are negative for models trained within one condition (17 and 25), so most of these are neurons that no kinematic model fits, not neurons with an inconsistent relationship. The pseudo-R2 for one trial type is measured against that type's own mean firing in the held-out trials. Measured against a mean taken over both types, fewer negative values would be expected; that was not computed here. The paper does not say which it used.

"Model consistency actually correlated negatively with the separability index." Consistency is the pseudo-R2 of the whole-arm model trained on both trial types.

def bootstrap_ci(x, y, n=2000):
    idx = rng.integers(0, len(x), size=(n, len(x)))
    r = [stats.spearmanr(x[i], y[i])[0] for i in idx]
    return np.nanpercentile(r, [2.5, 97.5])

How this differs from the paper's analysis

  • One session from one monkey, where the paper has four from two.
  • The whole-arm model is hand plus joint kinematics, because the marker positions the paper's model uses are not in the dandiset.
  • The hand-only model is in two dimensions, the session has eight movement directions, neurons below 1 Hz are left out, the fits carry a small ridge penalty, and no Bonferroni correction is applied.
  • Pseudo-R2 for one trial type is computed on a repeat's pooled held-out predictions, against that type's own mean firing.

With one session and a substituted model, the weaker results for Figures 10 and 11 cannot be attributed to any one of these.

What this attempt found about the dandiset¶

  • It holds one of the twelve sessions analysed in the paper, and none from the two-workspace experiment, so four of the paper's main findings cannot be tested from it.
  • The positions of the arm markers, which the paper's whole-arm model is built on, are not included; the file does hold joint angles and muscle lengths derived from them.
  • The text descriptions of hand position and velocity give centimetres while the stated unit is metres. The stated unit, with the file's conversion factor, is the correct one.
  • The dandiset's metadata on the archive names the paper without saying which part of it the data covers.
