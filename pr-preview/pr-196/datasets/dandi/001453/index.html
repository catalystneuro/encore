    DC lack the direction-dependent velocity modulation seen in ZF.

    In contrast, DC show a significantly higher median velocity across all stimuli than ZF ... and lacks direction-dependent velocity modulation

    Results, Larval DC exhibit higher average swimming speeds, paragraph 1; Figure 1D legend

    By the paper's test (Tukey's HSD over the 18 species and stimulus groups) 3 of 36 DC pairs differ, all static against a motion direction (backward left, backward right, forward left; adjusted p 0.030 to 0.049). The DC static values come from 9 fish and include one at 106.5 mm/s and two near 0. Among the eight directions no DC pair differs (smallest adjusted p 0.999), DC per-stimulus medians span 8.81 to 10.52 mm/s (ratio 1.19, ZF 2.03), and a one-way ANOVA across the nine DC stimuli gives p = 0.479 (ZF p = 0.00082). With values above 100 mm/s left out, no DC pair differs. The same Tukey test finds no ZF pair over the 18 groups (smallest p 0.835). The printed output of Fig1-S1.ipynb finds the same three DC pairs significant (adjusted p 0.0236, 0.0373 and 0.0397), so the paper's own test disagrees with the claim and this result matches the paper's computation. The DC-sub-2 static value of 106.5 mm/s is one trial measured over a 14.6 s motion phase in a 100 mm dish, likely a tracking artefact. It passes the code's speed check because the check covers 0 to 35 s (21.9 mm/s from the stored `dist`, 92.5 from positions); computing the check from positions, or leaving out zero medians as the code does, gives the same 3 pairs. The outcome is a property of the paper's test on this data, not evidence that DC modulate their speed with stimulus direction.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The DC static block is the code's window, but the code's filter on DC `vel` cannot be applied (DC static 10.52 against the code's printed 11.9 mm/s).

    Across directional stimuli ZF have higher angular acceleration than DC, and the distributions of the two species differ for every stimulus.

    Across directional stimuli, ZF maintained higher angular acceleration than DC

    Results, Optic flow induces angular matching and turns in DC, paragraph 2; Figure 1H (Kruskal-Wallis test with Bonferroni correction)

    What the paper reports

    • Kruskal-Wallis test, p < 0.05, Bonferroni-corrected

    With angular acceleration as the Methods define it, DC are higher than ZF for all nine stimuli (medians 143 to 191 against 55.4 to 66.4 deg/s2, Kruskal-Wallis p below 0.05/9 for each), the reverse of the claim. Per-fish medians, and the files at 128 to 171 Hz alone (ZF 84.9, DC 178.6 deg/s2, p = 6.3e-05), give the same. The quantity Fig1-S1.ipynb computes gives ZF higher with p below 0.05/9 for 7 of 9 stimuli; it divides the heading change per frame by the file's mean frame interval, which in DC files is a median 8.73 times the frame interval (ZF 1.37), as DC files hold only trial frames. Part of the DC values may be tracking noise. In the 3 s stationary phase the DC median is 205 deg/s2, higher than the 171 during motion (per fish median ratio 1.24, p = 0.016), but ZF are also higher at rest (66.9 and 60.8, ratio 1.07, p = 0.00012), so this does not separate the species. In frames where the fish is still (below 1 mm/s) DC are higher than ZF (per-fish medians 72.4 and 34.9 deg/s2, Mann-Whitney p = 0.00014), and DC are higher in every speed band (at 5 mm/s or more, 207 and 85.8). The outputs cannot say whether the higher values in moving DC are noise or turning during continuous swimming. With the heading from the unwrapped `f0_theta` DC are higher still (218 to 278 against ZF 61.8 to 77.3 deg/s2, 0 of 9). The reversal should not be read as evidence against the paper.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Follows the Methods' definition (degrees per second squared) on heading resampled to 30 Hz; the code's quantity, which disagrees with the Methods, is shown as a variant.
    • Heading is taken from the cumulative sum of `f0_vtheta`, as the code does; in DC files it does not match `f0_theta`.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds only the freely swimming tracking of Figure 1 and Figure S1. The two-photon imaging and the head-fixed tail tracking behind Figures 2 to 4 are not in it, and the GitLab repository the paper names for all behavioural and imaging data holds analysis notebooks only. Nine of the paper's main findings cannot be tested.
  • Description missingThe stimulus codes 0 to 20 have no names or description in the files or the dandiset metadata. They can be named only from the paper's analysis notebook and the protocol file of its stimulus software.
  • Published code disagreesThe paper's analysis notebook labels code 9 as lateral right and 11 as lateral left, while its conversion of the stimulus software's names gives the reverse (it carries a remap that swaps them). The data do not settle which is right; zebrafish barely turn to either, and Danionella turn more to the right for code 11, which fits the stimulus software's assignment only if the fish turn with lateral motion.
  • Metadata errorSubject ids 0 to 11 are each used for one zebrafish and one Danionella cerebrum, so subject_id does not identify an animal. The archive's summary counts 25 subjects and lists only Danio rerio; the files hold 37 fish of two species.
  • Metadata errorf0_vtheta is stated in radians/s but holds the heading change per frame in radians, and f0_vx and f0_vy are stated in pixels/s but are per frame. In most Danionella files the sum of f0_vtheta over a trial does not match the change of f0_theta (median ratios 0.09 to 0.89).
  • Other18 of 25 zebrafish files and 1 of 12 Danionella files are sampled at 32 to 35 Hz, where the Methods state tracking at more than 150 Hz. Their timestamp intervals are regular (28.8 to 30.8 ms) and their trials last the protocol's 35 s, so these are the acquisition rates. Some measures differ with frame rate, such as the zebrafish static speed (8.31 against 4.50 mm/s).
  • Description missingNo spatial scale is given; positions are in pixels. They are consistent with the 10 pixels per mm in the paper's code, except one Danionella file (sub-5) whose positions reach 1490 pixels against about 1120 in the others.
  • OtherZebrafish and Danionella files were exported differently. Zebrafish files keep untracked frames and frames between trials; Danionella files hold only tracked trial frames and add a `dist` series. The paper's code filters Danionella frames on a `vel` column that is not in the files.
  • Published code disagreesThe paper's code computes the angular acceleration of Figure 1H as the heading change per frame divided by the file's mean frame interval, an angular velocity. In Danionella files the mean interval includes the gaps between trials (a median 8.73 times the frame interval), which scales their values down. With the Methods' definition Danionella have the higher angular acceleration.
  • Published code disagreesFigure 1H and its legend state a Kruskal-Wallis test with Bonferroni correction; the code runs two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests, and its loop selects the stimulus with a variable left from an earlier loop, so the nine tests compare the same stimulus.
  • Published code disagreesThe Results state that Danionella lack the direction-dependent speed modulation of zebrafish, but the Tukey test printed by Fig1-S1.ipynb for Figure 1D finds three Danionella pairs significant (static against backward left, backward right and forward left, adjusted p 0.024 to 0.040), as this attempt does.
  • OtherThe `dist` series of the Danionella files does not agree with the positions; over the first 35 s of a trial its sum is a median 1.11 times the path length of the smoothed positions, with a 5th to 95th percentile of 0.31 to 3.79. The paper's speed check uses it, though computing the check from positions passes the same trials.
  • Published code disagreesThe Figure 1D legend gives 26 zebrafish; the paper's code lists 25, its ANOVA degrees of freedom fit 25, and the dandiset holds 25.
  • Selection not documentedThe dandiset holds the 25 zebrafish and 12 Danionella the code keeps after exclusion (its comments give 28 and 40 recorded). The dandiset does not say that the excluded fish were left out or why.

It compares swimming speed, turning and angular acceleration between species and stimuli, the response to monocular stimuli, and how steady the swimming is.
