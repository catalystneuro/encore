  • fig4-unipolar-strongest

    A single unipolar 600-ns pulse at 15 kV/cm causes the strongest YP uptake of all treatments in single CHO cells, reaching 126 +/- 14 a.u. (n = 25) by the end of recording.

    A single 600-ns pulse at 15 kV/cm caused by far the strongest YP uptake, reaching 126±14 a.u. (n=25) by the end of recording.

    Results, 3.1, paragraph 1; Figure 4

    How it will be judged

  • fig4-bipolar-weaker

    Bipolar signals starting with the same pulse cause significantly weaker uptake than the single pulse (p < 0.001), reaching 20.8 +/- 2.0 a.u. (4-phase), 6.9 +/- 1.6 a.u. (8-phase) and 25.4 +/- 2.8 a.u. (8-phase with 5-us delays) by the end of recording.

    Bipolar signals started with exactly the same pulse (600 ns, 15 kV/cm) but caused significantly weaker effects (p<0.001). By the end of recording, YP signal reached only 20.8±2.0 a.u. for the 4-phasic signal; 6.9±1.6 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal; and 25.4±2.8 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal with 5-μs delays between phases.

    Results, 3.1, paragraph 1; Figure 4

    How it will be judged

    F-F0 per cell at the last image, as in fig4-unipolar-strongest; tolerances are the paper's s.e. Section 2.2 says F-F0 was used for statistical analyses but names no test; the only test the Methods name is the two-sided Student's t-test (Section 2.4), so each bipolar group is compared with the unipolar group by that test on per-cell values, and this choice is recorded as open. Reproduced when all three means are within tolerance and all three tests give p < 0.001; partially reproduced when the tests hold but a mean falls outside tolerance.

  • fig4-sham-no-uptake

    Sham-exposed cells show no gain in YP fluorescence over the 10 min of observation.

    Sham-exposed cells showed no gain in YP fluorescence during the 10 min of observation.

    Results, 3.1, paragraph 1; Figure 4

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. The paper names no test for it (Section 2.2 names none); its only named test is the two-sided Student's t-test at p < 0.05 (Section 2.4), applied here as a one-sample test of per-cell F-F0 at the last image against 0, which is the same as a paired test of F against F0. That this is the test is open and recorded as such. Reproduced when p >= 0.05; the mean F-F0 and its s.e. are reported either way.

Reproduced when both readings hold, not reproduced when neither does, and blocked as methods_underspecified when they differ; outcomes under each reading are reported.

  • fig7bd-targeting-independent-of-amplitude

    Normalized to the most peripheral ROI, the profiles for different amplitudes fully overlap, in the small array with 1Px4 and in the large array with 10Px4, so targeting does not depend on nsEP amplitude within the range studied.

    When the YP emission was normalized to the most peripheral ROI, the data for different nsEP amplitudes fully overlapped ( Fig. 7B ). ... The findings held true for rotating multiple packets and when using the larger array ( Fig. 7C and D ).

    Results, 3.3, paragraph 2; Figure 7B and 7D

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. The paper reports no test for it; the Methods' test is the two-sided Student's t-test at p < 0.05 over dishes (Section 2.4). The text concludes that targeting does not depend on amplitude, and Section 3.3, paragraph 1, defines targeting efficiency as the ratio of the effect in the center to that near the electrodes, so the measure judged is the normalized center value. In each array it is compared between every pair of amplitudes. Reproduced when no comparison is significant; not reproduced when any is. The effect sizes, and the same tests at the half-way ROI and at every ROI ("fully overlapped" read as whole profiles), are shown as variants.
