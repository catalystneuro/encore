Dandiset. DANDI:000945, version 0.250213.2212. This is the version the paper cites (reference 50, doi:10.48324/dandi.000945/0.250213.2212). The four later published versions (0.250711.1746, 0.250829.0425, 0.250829.1250) and the draft hold the same 75 files with the same blobs and sizes, as the archive's asset lists show, so the choice does not change the data analysed.

What the dandiset holds. Sorted units (spike times; a cell-type label in 60 of 75 files) and the trial table of each recording. There are no waveforms, no raw or filtered voltage, no electrode positions and no sham recordings. The condition of each recording is coded only in the file's identifier (<rat>_<PRF>_<TBD>[_2][_anes]).

In these recordings, we tested different PRFs (pulse repetition frequencies) of ultrasound stimulation using a 128-element random array ultrasound transducer to stimulate the somatosensory cortex of awake head-fixed rats. There are no waveforms, voltage traces, electrode positions or processing modules: units hold only spike times and, in 60 files, a label ("1 = RSU, 2 = FSU"). Every file says age P24W (about 5.5 months), where the Methods give 7, 9 and 10 months for the three series. The 15 files without labels are all anesthetized recordings, and they are the only files whose description says "Awake Model Anes"; the labelled anesthetized copies (subject folders BH497 and BH514) are described as "Awake S1 Stimulation". The identifiers of those copies name rats BH498 and BH512. Five files hold fewer than the 500 trials the Methods describe.

The dandiset's description explains the _2 files: recordings were "taken first at 8-10 months, and then some repeats taken at 12 months", and "Some subjects have duplicate recordings taken a few months after the original ones". The session start times (table above) do not always agree with the suffix; see the check after the next one.

def shared_spikes(p1, p2):
    out = []
    for p in (p1, p2):
        url = next(a["url"] for a in assets if a["path"] == p)
        with h5py.File(remfile.File(url), "r") as f:
            out.append(np.unique(np.round(f["units/spike_times"][:], 5)))
    return len(out[0]), len(out[1]), len(np.intersect1d(out[0], out[1]))

Readings and selections used in every claim¶

The claims file's header fixes the method; the dataset decides which of its readings can run.

  • Cell type. K0: the dataset carries labels, so they are used. K1 and K2 (k-means on the initial and after-hyperpolarization phase durations) cannot run: the files hold no waveforms. That the labels are the paper's k-means output is an assumption; the baseline rates above are consistent with it and do not prove it.
  • Trials. The files hold 500 trials, the 505 delivered less the first five (T1, Methods 2.4); T2 (all 505) cannot run. That the 500 are trials 6 to 505 cannot be checked: the dandiset's description says only that "each recording has 500 trials". Five recordings hold 203 to 339 trials and are used as they are.
  • Exclusion. E1 = E2: the dataset marks no excluded unit or interval.
  • Windows. W1 (exact window) and W2 (overlapping PSTH bins) are both run.
  • Groups. G1 only: there are no sham recordings, so G2 cannot run.
  • Repeats (not named by the claims file; the dandiset adds them). Ten awake recordings repeat a condition in the same rat. The paper mentions no repeats, and Methods 2.3 fits one recording per parameter set per rat without stating it ("Recordings for a single rat for each set of parameters were completed on the same day" says when a rat's recordings were made), and the dandiset's description calls them repeats taken "a few months after the original ones". Three readings are run: Rep1 counts every recording once, repeats included; Rep2 leaves out the _2 recordings; Rep3 keeps the earliest recording of each rat and condition, following the description's timing where the suffix and the dates disagree (three pairs, see above). Rep2 and Rep3 both give one recording per rat and condition. Where Rep1 and those two give different outcomes, one recording per rat and condition is adopted as an assumption (Methods 2.3 and the dandiset's description); where Rep2 and Rep3 differ from each other, neither is preferred, as the BH498 pairs are a day apart.
  • Anesthetized data. Only two rats' anesthetized recordings carry labels (BH498 at all five PRFs, BH512 without 30 Hz); BH521's three are unlabelled and cannot enter a cell-type test. Rat and PRF are therefore confounded under anesthesia: the 30 Hz group is BH498 alone.
  • Sham. The dandiset holds no sham recording. Parts of a criterion that compare with sham are reported as not tested. A claim with an untested part is not judged reproduced: its best outcome is partially reproduced. A claim is settled as not reproduced only when the parts tested rule out the partial outcome whatever the untested parts would show; a claim that could still be partially reproduced if an untested part held is blocked as data missing (fig4 below). The claims file's header provides this for an anesthetized sham; the same is done for the awake sham.
  • Readings of the claims file that disagree. Where the readings the claims file names (W1/W2, S1/S2, D1/D2, L1/L2, A1/A2) give different outcomes, the claim is blocked as methods_underspecified, as the header says, with every result shown.
  • The 30% duty cycle. The paper's DC series had its own 30% recordings, which are not in the dandiset. The substitutes (C1, C2, defined before the duty cycle claims) and C0, without a 30% group, are shown. A part of a criterion that needs the 30% group is treated as data missing, and a claim whose outcome depends on that part is blocked as data missing.

    d = select(state, ct, rep)

def sig(post, a, b):
    return bool(tfus.pair(post, a, b).p_bonf < 0.05)


def lower(post, a, b):
    """a significantly below b."""
    r = tfus.pair(post, a, b)
    return bool(r.p_bonf < 0.05 and r.mean_a < r.mean_b)

def anova(ct, kind, w, rep, model, drop=()):
    d = pd.concat([select(st, ct, rep) for st in ("awake", "anesthetized")])
    d = d[~d.session.isin(drop)]
    d = d.assign(y=[r[col(kind, r.state, w)] for _, r in d.iterrows()])
    form = "y ~ C(state) * C(prf)" if model == "A1" else "y ~ C(state) + C(prf)"
    a = sm.stats.anova_lm(ols(form, d).fit(), typ=2)
    return {"reading": f"{model} {w} {rep}", "n": len(d), "F state": a.loc["C(state)", "F"], "p state": a.loc["C(state)", "PR(>F)"],
            "awake mean": d[d.state == "awake"].y.mean(), "anes mean": d[d.state == "anesthetized"].y.mean()}

How the analysis differs from the paper's. Cell types are the dataset's labels; the k-means on waveform features cannot be run, as there are no waveforms. The files hold 500 trials (the paper's 505 less five), and five recordings hold 203 to 339. No sham recording exists, so every comparison with sham is left out, and no claim with a sham part is judged reproduced. The anesthetized PRF series has labelled units from two rats (the paper: ten), and BH512 has no 30 Hz recording, so rat and PRF are confounded under anesthesia. The DC series has 6% and 60% recordings from three rats and no 30% recording of its own; the PRF series' 1500 Hz, 200 us recordings stand in for it, without deciding any outcome. Ten awake recordings repeat a condition in the same rat; results are shown with all of them (Rep1), without the _2 recordings (Rep2) and with the earliest recording of each pair (Rep3). No unit was excluded for a fluctuating baseline or for movement, as the dataset marks none. Most 200 us recordings show no time-locked population response, which limits every time-locked claim.

What was found about the dataset. Eleven of the 75 files are second copies of other files: the anesthetized recordings of BH498 and BH512 are each deposited twice (with labels under subject folders BH497 and BH514, without labels under BH498 and BH512), BH512's 1500 Hz anesthetized recording a third time, and one 30 Hz recording is filed under both BH498 and BH508, with start times 6.5 hours apart. The second batch of five rats, the sham condition and the 30% DC recordings are not in the dandiset.
