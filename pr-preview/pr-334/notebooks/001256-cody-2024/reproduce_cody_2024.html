With one pass, several animals keep pupil "maxima" of 200 to 550000 pixels, against medians of 20 to 100, so a baseline in % of the animal's maximum would be near 0 for every trial of those animals. The round-1 cleaning (Hampel spike filter, the derivative step repeated, and trials with more than half of their frames missing dropped) still left tracking failures in two mice: in AA0319 and AA0334 the failures come as runs at a second level (130 to 160, against a pupil of about 25), which set their maxima at 332.7 and 162.1. The main cleaning also drops every trial whose raw trace steps by more than a factor 2 between consecutive tracked frames (118 trials, 15 mice); a pupil does not double or halve its radius in 100 ms. This changes the maximum of those two mice only (78.3 and 70.9), and the largest trial maximum is now at most 3.1 times its animal's median. The rule reads the raw trace, before the spike filter, so it also drops trials whose only fault is a single-frame spike that the filter removes: it is broader than the two mice need. Counting the steps after the spike filter drops 39 trials in 6 mice and gives the same animal maxima (max_narrow); it is run as a variant at the end. Where an outcome differs between the two, the difference comes from the 79 trials in other mice that only the broad rule drops. All three steps are assumptions; the paper's own DeepLabCut points and likelihoods, which would let the frames be dropped as it describes, are not in the files. The other cleanings are run as variants at the end of the notebook.

Clock check. The pupil series start at their trial's start time (asserted on loading). Sound onset is stimDelay after the first frame. If this is right, the population calcium response should rise at the pure tone (2 s after onset, in both contrasts) and not at other times, and the pupil should dilate after onset, not before. Shifting the assumed onset by whole seconds moves the tone response away from 2 s. The DRC structure (sound-onset delays, which contrast comes first, mapping trials) is printed after the duplicate check.

def reliability(d, how):
    d = d.copy()
    d["bin"] = zp.pd_bin(d.base.values)
    out = []
    for (cell, b), g in d.groupby(["cell", "bin"]):
        if len(g) < 2:
            continue
        t_ = g.t.iloc[0]
        X = np.vstack(g.trace.values)[:, (t_ >= 2.0) & (t_ < 3.2)]
        if how == "pearson":
            X = X - X.mean(1, keepdims=True)
        nrm = np.linalg.norm(X, axis=1)
        ok = nrm > 0
        X = X[ok] / nrm[ok, None]
        if len(X) < 2:
            continue
        C = X @ X.T
        iu = np.triu_indices(len(X), 1)
        out.append({"cell": cell, "bin": b, "value": C[iu].mean(), "n": len(X)})
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

def anova_1i(d, w, mode, pairing):
    vals = bin_values(d, f"peak_{w[0]}_{w[1]}", w, mode, "open", by=("cell", "bin", "contrast"))
    if pairing == "both":
        pv = vals.pivot_table(index=["cell", "bin"], columns="contrast", values="value").dropna()
        keep = set(pv.index)
        vals = vals[[k in keep for k in zip(vals.cell, vals.bin)]]
    m = smf.ols("value ~ C(bin) * C(contrast)", data=vals).fit()
    a = sm.stats.anova_lm(m, typ=2)
    return vals, m, a

def fit_fig3(d, formula):
    """ML fit; tries several optimizers and keeps the best likelihood (the fits are fragile)."""
    m = smf.mixedlm(formula, d, groups=d["subject"], re_formula="1", vc_formula={"trial": "0 + C(trial)"})
    best = None
    for method in ("lbfgs", "bfgs", "nm", "powell"):
        try:
            r = m.fit(reml=False, method=method, maxiter=2000)
        except Exception:
            continue
        if np.isfinite(r.llf) and (best is None or r.llf > best.llf):
            best = r
    return best

def code(long, factor_col, level, coding):

def gca(trs, factor="treat", level="ZX1", forms=FORMS, codings=("reference", "sum")):
    long = zp.gca_long(trs)
    long["c"] = 0.0
    out, fits = [], {}

def emmeans_tukey(m, long, coding):
    """Cell means at tPoly1 = tPoly2 = 0 for the 4 contrast x treatment cells, Tukey-adjusted pairs."""
    names = m.fe_params.index
    cov = m.cov_params().loc[names, names].values
    lv = {"reference": (0.0, 1.0), "sum": (-1.0, 1.0)}[coding]
    cells = {}
    for cn, cv in (("LOW", lv[0]), ("HIGH", lv[1])):
        for xn, xv in (("CTRL", lv[0]), ("X", lv[1])):
            L = np.zeros(len(names))
            for i, n in enumerate(names):
                if n == "Intercept": L[i] = 1
                elif n == "c": L[i] = cv
                elif n == "x": L[i] = xv
                elif n == "c:x": L[i] = cv * xv
            cells[(cn, xn)] = L
    df_ = len(long) - len(names)
    rows = []
    keys = list(cells)
    for i in range(4):
        for j in range(i + 1, 4):
            L = cells[keys[i]] - cells[keys[j]]
            est = L @ m.fe_params.values
            se = np.sqrt(L @ cov @ L)
            tval = est / se
            p = stats.studentized_range.sf(abs(tval) * np.sqrt(2), 4, df_)
            rows.append({"pair": f"{keys[i][0]} {keys[i][1]} - {keys[j][0]} {keys[j][1]}", "estimate": est, "z": tval, "p_tukey": p})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows), {k: v @ m.fe_params.values for k, v in cells.items()}

How the analysis differs from the paper's:

  • The files hold the pupil radius per video frame, with dropped frames and tracking failures (up to 1.5e6 pixels), not the DeepLabCut points. The Methods' single derivative pass leaves these failures in, so a spike filter was added, the pass repeated, trials with more than half their frames missing dropped, and trials whose raw trace steps by more than a factor 2 between tracked frames dropped (118 of 2113). This sets each animal's maximum PD, hence the baseline in % of maximum and the 50% selection. The step rule reads the raw trace and so drops trials in 15 mice, not only the two whose failure runs it targets; counted after the spike filter it drops 39 trials in 6 mice and gives the same animal maxima. Several outcomes depend on the cleaning (variants at the end): with the round-1 cleaning, which kept the step trials, fig4c-wt-contrast-zx1-effects would be not reproduced (no interaction); fig4-wt-posthoc fails under every cleaning but the narrower step rule, which meets it; with the single pass, fig6c-no-baseline-evoked-correlation would be not reproduced, fig6d-ko-reduced-high-contrast not reproduced (p = 0.20), and the fig1i interaction not significant (p = 0.17), which would make fig1i-baseline-contrast-interaction partially reproduced.
  • The DRC trials without the tone switch contrast after 10 s (both orders, about equally often); a trial's contrast is that of its first 10 s, which covers the analysed 0 to 2 s. The 32 ZX1 DRC trials of AA0327 start the sound 10 s after the first frame, and the pupil dilates after that onset.
  • Groups are taken from the files' analysis labels; group A holds ten mice (paper eight; one is ZnT3-WT), group B twelve (paper fourteen C57BL6/J; six here are C57BL6/J), and the ZnT3-WT group five F and five M (paper six F and four M). Keeping the ZnT3-WT mouse AA0335 in group A is an assumption the Fig. 2b and Fig. 3b outcomes rest on: without it group A's Fig. 2b trial test becomes significant (p = 0.028), so the readings would disagree, and the Fig. 3 baseline coefficient is not significant in the trials below 50% (p = 0.087), which would make fig3b-lme-coefficients partially reproduced.
  • LME tests are Wald z tests from statsmodels, not Satterthwaite F tests; the Fig. 3 random intercepts for baseline and evoked PD are fitted as one trial intercept.
  • Recording time for Fig. 6a comes from the .tif creation times, since the table's starting times wrap by one day for trials recorded before the reference file.

What was found about the dataset: the files carry labels naming the paper's analyses, a per-trial table of sounds and treatments, ROI fluorescence and pupil radius, enough to test 21 of 24 claims. The widefield experiments of Figure 5 are not in it.
