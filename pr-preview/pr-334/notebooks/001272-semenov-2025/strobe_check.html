Strobe imaging of membrane charging (Figure 4), on DANDI:001273¶

Paper: Semenov et al. (2025), Cell Reports Physical Science 6, 102660, doi:10.1016/j.xcrp.2025.102660. Dataset: DANDI:001273, "Kinetics of Membrane Charging and Its Dependence on Neuron Shape and Alignment with the Electric Field", version 0.250805.1356, the most recent published version (the paper cites none).

The claims fig4-pooled-time-constants and fig4-orientation-and-poles of claims/001272/10.1016-j.xcrp.2025.102660.yaml defer their fits and readings to the claims file for DANDI:001273, whose header items are cited as 001273-F1 to F5 and 001273-O1 to O5. That header (F2) says the fits are run unscaled where the debris ROI is not available, recorded as a deviation. No debris ROI is deposited, so the fits here are unscaled. This notebook first shows one experiment in detail, then fits every experiment of 7 or 8 replicates under every reading.

One experiment, in detail¶

Cell 1_20240119 with the electrodes right and left of it, 8 replicates at 40 V nominal. The claims file's ROIs are rebuilt from the RATIO image (001273-O1 a and b), the traces are averaged over the replicates (001273-F3) and fitted under the weighting and amplitude readings (001273-O2, O3). The paper scaled the ROI data to a "debris" ROI to remove laser-flash fluctuations (001273-F2); no debris ROI is deposited, so the traces are unscaled, and, as a variant made here, scaled by the whole-frame mean of each frame. A tau within 1% of a fit bound (0.01 or 20 us) is flagged: such a fit has not found a time constant.

In this experiment the replicate-mean traces carry a frame-to-frame fluctuation common to both poles, which the paper removed by scaling to the debris ROI. The noise before the pulse and the dF late in the pulse, and the spread of the fitted time constants over the readings, are printed above.

fig4-pooled-time-constants¶

Membrane charging by 2-us pulses is fast; pooled over 19 experiments, charging and discharging time constants averaged 0.7 and 0.91 us at the cathodic pole and 0.65 and 0.8 us at the anodic pole, so membranes charge fully within each sine wave cycle up to 100 to 200 kHz.

Criterion (deferred to the 001273 claims file): the mean over all experiment fits of the four time constants, each within the paper's SEM (0.7 +/- 0.08, 0.91 +/- 0.08, 0.65 +/- 0.05, 0.8 +/- 0.07 us), under every reading of 001273-O1 (ROI a, b), O2 (weighting a, b), O3 (discharge amplitude a, b) and F2 (here unscaled only); blocked as methods_underspecified when the readings disagree.

Every group of 7 or 8 replicates of one cell, electrode orientation and amplitude is taken as one experiment; the files hold 22 such groups where the paper reports 19, with no mapping, so the mean is over all 22 (a deviation). Fits with a tau at a bound are counted as failed and left out of the mean; their number is printed. The whole-frame scaling is shown as a variant.

    dark += X["dark"]

        ym = np.nanmean(v, axis=0)

                        late_pulse_dF=np.nanmean(ym[(tt >= 1.5) & (tt < 2.0)])))
        for scaling in ("unscaled", "whole-frame scaled"):

A failure to reproduce here may be a failure of this attempt, not of the paper: see the reasoning and the differences from the paper's method.

How the analysis differs from the paper's: the ROIs are rebuilt from the RATIO image under the 001273 claims file's two readings; the fits are unscaled, as the debris ROI is not deposited; the mean is over the 22 groups of 7 or 8 replicates in the files, where the paper reports 19 experiments; fits with a time constant at a fit bound are left out.

What was found about the dataset: the debris ROI the paper scales to is not deposited; the experiments do not map to the paper's 19; the cell shapes (elongated or random) are not labelled; 164 of the 175 replicates fitted have at least one dark frame (257 in all, 0.34 to 0.80 of the median frame mean), in 95 of them only the first acquired frame; dark frames are left out.
