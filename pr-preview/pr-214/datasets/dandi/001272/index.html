  • figs1-noisier-than-fluovolt

    Di-8-ANEPPS traces were noisier than FluoVolt traces, as the dye has a lower voltage sensitivity.

    Results, Sensitivity to unmodulated sine waves was not caused by FluoVolt dye, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

As a variant on this dataset alone, the median Di-8-ANEPPS AP height is compared with the 11.3% typical of FluoVolt (Methods).

  • figs1-effects-same-as-fluovolt

    Overall, the effects of 2-kHz sine waves in Di-8-ANEPPS neurons did not differ from those with FluoVolt, so the excitation by unmodulated sine waves is not a sensitization by FluoVolt.

    Overall, stimulation effects were not different from those observed with FluoVolt dye

    Results, Sensitivity to unmodulated sine waves was not caused by FluoVolt dye, paragraph 1; Discussion, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence, for which the paper names no test, and the paper does not say which effects were compared; the threshold comparison below is a reading of "not different", not a measure the paper names. The response pattern holds when figs1-near-threshold-delayed-aps, figs1-stronger-field-shorter-delay-sustained-firing and figs1-culminates-in-sustained-depolarization are reproduced. The paper's own concern is a sensitization by FluoVolt (Discussion, "we ruled out possible sensitization"), which would show as lower thresholds with FluoVolt; so the 1-AP threshold (F4) of the Di-8-ANEPPS neurons is compared with that of the FluoVolt neurons of figs1-noisier-than-fluovolt by a two-sided unpaired t test at p < 0.05 (F6). Reproduced when the pattern holds and the test finds no difference; not reproduced when the test finds a difference in either direction. The mean FluoVolt threshold of the neurons used is reported beside the paper's 57.7 ± 3.6 V/cm (Results, Asymmetric and less-efficient membrane charging, last paragraph, from Figure 2D) as a check on the reference group, not judged. A one-sample t test of the Di-8-ANEPPS thresholds against 57.7 V/cm is shown as a variant on this dataset alone. The difference of means and its 95% interval are reported. The Di-8-ANEPPS sessions may not have been a threshold search (fields in a random sequence), so the threshold is the lowest exciting field tested, which is an upper bound; the notebook reports the gap to the next lower field tested.

  • fig1-pure-sine-excites-all

    Pure sine waves at 2 and even 20 kHz excited 100% of neurons ... The maximum firing rate increased linearly until reaching a ceiling of 60-90 Hz

    Results, Stimulation by 2- and 20-kHz unmodulated sine waves, paragraph 1; Figures 1A to 1C and inset

    How it will be judged

    F1 to F5 and O1 to O3 and O6 of the header, applied to FluoVolt traces without inversion. Reproduced when, under every reading, at least 90% of neurons are excited at each frequency, the median ceiling lies within 54 to 99 Hz (60 to 90 Hz widened by 10% of each bound, written above as its midpoint and half-width) and the median first-AP latency at threshold lies within 90 to 220 ms (100 to 200 ms widened the same way); blocked as methods_underspecified when the readings disagree. Whether the ceiling is meant for the median neuron or every neuron is open (the inset shows three); the median is a standard choice and the range over neurons is reported. The block is reported under O6, not judged.

Reproduced when each ratio is within tolerance and 20 against 2 kHz reaches p < 0.0001 under every reading; blocked as methods_underspecified when they disagree.

  • fig2d-modulated-thresholds-higher

    thresholds for modulated sine waves, which emulate the TI stimulus at the target, were significantly higher than those for pure sine waves at the same frequency ... p = 0.0079 ... p = 0.0142 and p = 0.0037

  • fig2-modulated-locks-to-beats

    With 20-Hz modulation, firing above threshold locked to the beats, one AP near the peak of each of the five beats and at most two per beat (ten in all), and modulated sine waves triggered no sustained firing or sustained depolarization.

    single APs emerged near the peak of each beat, so the firing rate equaled the modulation rate ... the maximum number of APs evoked by five beats was limited to ten. Unlike pure sine waves, modulated stimuli did not trigger sustained firing or sustained depolarization

    Results, Stimulation by 2- and 20-kHz sinusoids modulated at 20 Hz, paragraph 1; Figures 2A and 2B

    How it will be judged

    AP readings of the header, modulated sessions only. The 250-ms wave holds five 50-ms beats (Results), and modulated waves begin and end at minimal amplitude (Methods, Pulsed laser fluorescence microscopy, F0 for sine waves), so each beat peaks 25 ms into it; the stimulus record of the dataset is used instead when it gives the envelope. The paper does not quantify "near the peak"; as standard choices, reproduced when no session has more than 10 APs, in sessions with 5 APs or more most APs fall within 12.5 to 37.5 ms of each 50-ms beat, and no session shows sustained depolarization (O6). The AP phase histogram is reported.

  • threshold-depolarization-estimate

    This threshold corresponds to a 0.68% ± 0.11% FluoVolt emission change (11.8 × 57.7/1,000) ... a 0.68% emission change corresponds to a 6.8-mV depolarization at the AP threshold

    How it will be judged

Tolerances are the paper's SEMs, the last being 0.11% times 10 mV per 1%. Reproduced when all three are within tolerance.
