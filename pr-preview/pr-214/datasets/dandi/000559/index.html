    Results, DLS dopamine does not predict syllable identity, paragraph 2; Figure 1l

    Held-out accuracy for 37 syllables is 0.0271 from peak amplitude and 0.0323 from the waveform and its derivative, 1.00 and 1.20 times chance (0.0270), below the criterion of 0.0541. Shuffled labels give 0.0278 and 0.0279.

  • ◐Partially reproducedResults, Relating DLS dopamine to spontaneous behavior, paragraph 2; Figure 1e

    DLS dopamine fluctuations are significantly negatively correlated with translational velocity at short timescales (under 10 s), and the correlation reverses and becomes positive at longer timescales, for both dLight transient rates and binned amplitudes.

    These dopamine fluctuations (measured as both dLight transient rates and average binned amplitudes) were only weakly correlated with many aspects of movement kinematics ... but were significantly negatively correlated with translational velocity at short timescales (<10 s); this correlation reversed and became positive at longer timescales

    Results, Relating DLS dopamine to spontaneous behavior, paragraph 2; Figure 1e

    Over 121 DLS sessions from 14 mice, binned dLight amplitude and transient rate are both significantly negatively correlated with 2D speed at all 20 timescales below 10 s (amplitude mean r -0.201 to -0.109, rate -0.123 to -0.062; shuffle ranges within about 0.02 of zero), and all 14 mice are negative at 0.25 s for both. The transient rate turns positive from 18.75 s and is significantly positive at 47 of 100 timescales of 10 s or more (largest 0.113 at 58.75 s). The binned amplitude is still significantly negative at 49.75 s and is significantly positive at none of the 100 longer timescales (largest 0.005). The claim holds for one of the two measures under both readings of "short timescales".

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • dLight is re-referenced and z-scored from the stored dF/F series; the paper used a pre-computed z-scored trace that is not in the files.
    • 100 shuffles where the paper used 1,000.
    • 121 sessions where the paper's code reports 216 for this panel; the dandiset holds fewer sessions, and habituation sessions are left out here.
  • ◐Partially reproducedFigure 1h legend

    Average syllable-associated dLight amplitudes differ across syllables (Kruskal-Wallis H = 209.29, P < 1e-25, n = 518 mouse-syllable pairs).

    Right, z-scored average syllable-associated waveforms, sorted by peak fluorescence ... Kruskal–Wallis H test on average syllable-associated fluorescence amplitudes

    Figure 1h legend

    What the paper reports

    • Kruskal–Wallis H test, H = 209.29, p < 10⁻²⁵ (n = 518 mouse–syllable pairs)

    The 518 mouse-syllable pairs (14 mice x 37 syllables) match the paper's n. Kruskal-Wallis P = 5.98e-37, below the paper's 1e-25, but H = 266.40 is 27 % above 209.29 and outside the 10 % tolerance. Variants give H 266.99 (target instances kept), 268.58 (pairs with at least 50 instances) and 265.62 (the 9 DLS sessions failing the quality criteria added), so neither the quality filter nor the post-session rule explains the difference. Per-mouse tests are significant in 14 of 14 mice.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Instances from 177 DLS sessions without stimulation (habituation sessions left out); the paper used more sessions per mouse.
  • ◐Partially reproducedResults, Dopamine predicts future syllable use and sequencing, paragraph 2; Figure 2c-g

    When an instance of a syllable coincides with a higher-amplitude dopamine transient, that syllable is used more over the following minutes.

    if a given instance of a syllable coincided with a relatively high amplitude dopamine transient, that syllable was then used more over the next several minutes

    Results, Dopamine predicts future syllable use and sequencing, paragraph 2; Figure 2c-g

    The mean correlation between an instance's dLight and the count of the same syllable in the next n syllables is positive at every n (0.0043 to 0.0251) and significant from n = 10 to 90 (P < 0.01), 11 of 14 mice positive at n = 10. It is not significant at n = 110, 130, 150 and 170 (P = 0.09, 0.15, 0.18 and 0.05), so it holds at 6 of 10 values up to 190 and fails the criterion's "throughout"; it is significant again from 190 to 330. The shuffles permute only instances with a dLight peak, so the same groups enter the observed value and every shuffle (15,480 at n = 10).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Five equal time segments in place of the paper's five behavioural segments (Extended Data Fig. 2e).
    • Correlations are compared with shuffles directly, not z-scored to them; 100 shuffles.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Description missingThe closed-loop files mark stimulation onsets but not catch trials (target detections on which stimulation was withheld); the conversion kept only the stimulated detections. Comparisons of stimulation with catch trials (Fig. 3i, 3l, 3m, 4f) cannot be made, and a substitute selection shows stimulation-catch differences in 9 of 12 no-opsin controls.
  • Description missingNo file states the genotype or opsin of a mouse. Opto-DA (Ai32) and no-opsin control mice are told apart only by the stimulation site ("snc (axon)" or "ctrl"), and the dLight+ChrimsonR mice only by their subject names.
  • Description missingSession type (baseline, stimulation, post) and session number are not stored; they must be inferred from free-text descriptions, dates and stimulation pulses. Five files with stimulation are described as recording sessions.
  • Metadata errorEvery closed-loop file states a 635-nm stimulator (Opto Engine MRL-III-635), including the Ai32 (ChR2) mice, which the Methods say were stimulated with 470-nm light.
  • Metadata errorThe dLight-only mice (C57BL/6J in the Methods) carry the subject description "wild-type DAT-IRES-Cre and Ai32 mice", the same as the optogenetic mice.
  • File format errorIn 28 photometry and 547 closed-loop files the syllable timestamps are not ascending. In 573 of these 575 the rows are rotated, with one backward step, which is at least 0.95 of the session length in 558 files and 0.635 of it at the least. Sorting by timestamp restores a regular 30-Hz clock (median interval 0.03337 s in every file).
  • File format errorTwo closed-loop files of mouse 3155 store every syllable frame twice (53,822 and 53,862 repeated timestamps), and have two and three non-ascending steps.
  • Data missing14 files of dLight+ChrimsonR mice hold empty syllable series; all list two target syllables.
  • Data missingFor the dLight+ChrimsonR mice only the 250-ms closed-loop sessions are present; the 2-s and pulsed sessions the Methods describe are not. The dandiset holds 224 DLS photometry files without stimulation (210 with syllables, 193 of them not habituation sessions, 177 of those passing the quality criteria), where the Methods give 379 experiments.
  • Data missingThe food-reward sessions and the consumption times of Fig. 2a are not in the dandiset.
  • Selection not documentedThe dandiset includes behaviour-only files for photometry sessions without a dLight series (111 files, among them all files of dls-dlight-6, dls-dlight-8, dms-dlight-7 and dms-dlight-9) and sessions that fail the paper's quality criteria (16 DLS and 11 DMS), with no flag saying which were excluded from the paper.
  • OtherThe re-referenced, z-scored dLight trace used by every photometry analysis is not stored; it must be recomputed from the stored dF/F and smoothed reference, whose regression fit is not stored either.

Reproduction Dopamine and spontaneous behaviour in mice (Markowitz, Gillis, Jay et al. 2023)

Tests the findings of Markowitz, Gillis, Jay et al. (2023) on dandiset 000559, which holds the dLight photometry, MoSeq syllables and closed-loop optogenetic sessions of the paper. It relates DLS dopamine to velocity, syllable identity, syllable use and sequence entropy, compares DMS with DLS, and tests the reinforcement of target syllables by closed-loop dopamine release. The modelling of Figures 4 and 5 is not attempted. All files
