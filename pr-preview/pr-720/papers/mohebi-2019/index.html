The paper's findings on VTA dopamine cell firing mostly held up in this data, while its findings on dopamine release could not be tested, as the microdialysis and dLight recordings are not in the dataset. Latency tracked reward rate, and firing coded positive reward prediction errors and did not ramp before approach. Tonic firing changed little with reward rate, but some cells did change, and early-trial firing correlated with reward rate in 8 of 29 cells, which may come mostly from slow drift. Fewer cells responded to the light cue than reported, and the window and correction of the cue-response test are not given in the text read. Minor claims on cell identification and Pavlovian responses held in part.

    Results, Dopamine tracks motivation in key loci, paragraph 1; Figure 1b

    21 distinct bandit sessions (29 units; units of one session share identical events and are counted once), 6189 trials with a latency. Pearson r of the leaky-integrator reward rate at Light-On with log(latency) is negative in 21 of 21 sessions, mean -0.307 (one-sample t = -12.50, p = 6.6e-11), range -0.496 to -0.136; best tau 12 to 295 s, median 58 s, none at the grid's edge. Because tau is chosen to make r negative, a null was computed by repeating the choice of tau on latencies shuffled within the session (200 shuffles); 19 of 21 sessions have r below the null's 1st percentile. With one tau of 58 s for every session, r is negative in 21 of 21 (mean -0.291). The paper's statement covers its photometry and microdialysis rats too; only the electrophysiology sessions are in the data set.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Only the 21 electrophysiology bandit sessions are in the data set; the criterion names the photometry and microdialysis sessions as further readings where available.
    • A reward is counted at the rewarded Side-In, when the Results say the food hopper clicks; the Methods say only that reward rate is incremented each time a reward was received. A standard choice; no Light-On falls between Side-In and Food-Port-In.
    • Latency is Center-In minus Light-On from the event table, as the claims file defines it; the CNIlatency field is clipped at 65535 ms and not used. One trial with no latency (IM-1002_170118) is left out.
    • tau searched on a 1 s grid from 1 to 1200 s (Extended Data Fig. 1 legend gives the range); the shuffled-latency null uses 76 log-spaced values to keep the run short.

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Paper inconsistentThe Methods give the tagged cells per rat as IM657 1, IM1002 3, IM1003 15 and IM1037 9, which sums to 28, while the text says 27 VTA-l cells. The data hold 8 IM1037 cells (27 in all). Extended Data Fig. 5b gives 9 cells with 75/25% Pavlovian tones and 18 with 100/50%; the data hold 8 and 19, the IM657 cell having 100/50% tones.
  • Published code disagreesThe authors' script in the data set (Fig5_replicate.m) splits trials by the number of rewards in the previous 10 trials into four groups (0-2, 3-5, 6-7, 8-10), while Fig. 5a and Extended Data Fig. 8 use terciles of the leaky-integrator reward rate. Its Gaussian kernel has SD 20 ms (getFiringRate.m, sigma = twsize = 0.02), where the Methods say variance 20 ms.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors on Fig. 2f,h: which per-cell test compared tonic firing between high- and low-reward blocks, and on what samples (1 min bins, as the Methods count tonic spikes, or block means), and how was the median block handled with an odd number of blocks? With rank-sum tests on 1 min bins 18 of 27 cells differ (p < 0.05), 9 against circular shifts of the block labels, and firing is higher in high-reward blocks by a median 0.50 Hz; with block means 2 (or 0) cells differ, but 6 of 27 cells have too few blocks for that test to reach p < 0.05.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors on the time-to-cue-response test (Methods, Analysis, paragraph 1, and Fig. 4c,e): what was shuffled, how was the correction for multiple comparisons made, and over what post-cue window? With paired shuffles and Bonferroni over the bins of a 0.5 s window, 16 of 27 cells respond to Light-On (13 with 1 s, 22 without correction), where the paper reports 25. This attempt adopted the 0.5 s window.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors on Extended Data Fig. 8: is the pause the longest interval between spikes within the 2 s after unrewarded Side-In, does it include the intervals to the window's bounds, or is it the longest whole inter-spike interval overlapping the window, and what smoothing gave the valley? Intervals between spikes only give the paper's 2/29 pause cells; counting the bounds gives 8 (5 after correction) and whole intervals 9 (5). With the 20 ms kernel, or the 4.5 ms one, the valley is 0 Hz on nearly every unrewarded trial, so it cannot correlate with reward rate. The data set's Fig5_replicate.m computes neither.
