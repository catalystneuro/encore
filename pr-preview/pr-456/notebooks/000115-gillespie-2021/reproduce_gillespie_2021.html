Reproducing Gillespie et al. (2021) from dandiset 000115¶

Paper. Gillespie AK, Astudillo Maya DA, Denovellis EL, Liu DF, Kastner DB, Coulter ME, Roumis DK, Eden UT, Frank LM (2021). Neuron 109, 3149-3163. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2021.07.029. The claims file was written from the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC8497431) with its Supplemental Information, and from the paper's code (LorenFrankLab/Gillespie_Neuron_2021, commit 1be6a6a).

Dandiset. DANDI:000115, version 0.210914.1732 (doi:10.48324/dandi.000115/0.210914.1732). The paper cites no version, and its code's README links the draft. 0.210914.1732 is the only published version, and it is pinned. The draft (modified 2024-02-22) has the same 57 paths, but 55 of its files differ in content from the published version's (their draft assets were modified in February and March 2022). The data check below compares one of them in detail, written as NWB 2.4.0 in January 2022: the DIO timestamps agree to within a microsecond, but the draft's position timestamps are no longer on the DIO clock, while the published version's are. The published version therefore fits the analysis better as well as being fixed.

What the dandiset holds. One NWB file per recording day for each of four rats (jaq, roquefort, despereaux, montague): the raw 30 kHz broadband of 128 channels, port beam breaks, lights and pumps, head position, and the StateScript log of each epoch, which records every port entry, reward, timeout and the current goal arm. It holds no spike times, LFP, ripple times, linearised position or decoder output. Every replay and ripple claim would have to start from the raw broadband, about 9.1 TB in all.

Questions answered. Two questions on fig1d-sessions-blocks were answered by a reader and the claims file updated. Which jaq sessions are "sessions 1 and 3": the paper does not say, and the criterion now allows (a) the first and third run sessions in order of recording and (b) the two sessions the Methods describe, with (b) deciding when they differ. Which trial closes a block: the first valid trial after each goal change, whatever arm it visits, as the paper's code does. Both claims are re-run below under these rules. After the first verification round, the data check adds a per-session table, the arms entered in the DIO, and trial counts against the legends; fig1ef-reward-rate adds the per-trial values; and the behavioural half of fig6b-visits-decay is computed.

fig1ef-reward-rate¶

Aligned to the goal change, the outer-arm reward rate starts at chance (0.125) and rises; aligned to the first reward at the new goal, it is well above chance, so all subjects keep visiting a goal once found.

Criterion: per subject over the blocks with at least four rewards, the final unrewarded trial dropped; a trial scores 1 when rewarded; binomial S.E.M.; trials 1 to 15. Reproduced when, in all four subjects, the band on trial 1 of Figure 1E reaches 0.125 or lies below it, the rate rises over trials 1 to 15, and every point of Figure 1F has its band above 0.125. "Rises" is shown two ways: trial 15 against trial 1 (non-overlapping bands), and the Spearman correlation of the rate with trial number. Blocks are those of fig1d-sessions-blocks, closed by the first trial after each goal change (replaytask.FINAL_TRIAL); that closing trial is the one dropped. Sessions follow readings (a) and (b), and for (b) every candidate first session of jaq is run. The old-goal-visit rule is shown only to say what the answer changed.

def curves(reading=None, final=rt.FINAL_TRIAL, exclude=None, subjects=rt.SUBJECTS):
    ss = session_set(reading, exclude)
    out = {}
    for s in subjects:
        S, R = [], []
        for r in ss[ss.subject == s].run:
            a, b = rt.reward_curves(rt.label_task_phase(r["trials"], final))
            S.append(a); R.append(b)
        out[s] = (np.vstack(S), np.vstack(R))
    return out

def summarise_1ef(cv):
    rows = []
    for s, (E, F) in cv.items():
        p, se, n = rt.binomial_curve(E)
        p2, se2, n2 = rt.binomial_curve(F)
        rho, prho = stats.spearmanr(rt.CURVE_TRIALS, p)
        rows.append({"subject": s, "blocks": len(E),
                     "E_trial1": round(p[0], 3), "E_trial1_lower": round(p[0] - se[0], 3),
                     "E_trial15": round(p[14], 3), "E_trial15_lower": round(p[14] - se[14], 3),
                     "E_rho": round(rho, 3), "E_rho_p": prho,
                     "F_min": round(p2.min(), 3), "F_min_lower": round((p2 - se2).min(), 3),
                     "F_n_trial15": int(n2[14])})
    out = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    out["E_starts_at_chance"] = out.E_trial1_lower <= rt.CHANCE
    out["E_rises"] = (out.E_trial15_lower > out.E_trial1 + (out.E_trial1 - out.E_trial1_lower)) & (out.E_rho > 0) & (out.E_rho_p < 0.05)
    out["E_rises_plain"] = out.E_trial15 > out.E_trial1
    out["F_above_chance"] = out.F_min_lower > rt.CHANCE
    return out

def visit_sets(ss, completed_only=False):
    out = {}
    for s in rt.SUBJECTS:
        G, N = zip(*[rt.visit_traces(rt.label_task_phase(r["trials"]), completed_only) for r in ss[ss.subject == s].run])
        out[s] = (np.vstack(G), np.vstack(N))

def trialwise(G, N, test="mannwhitneyu"):
    p = np.full(len(OFFS), np.nan)
    for j in range(len(OFFS)):
        g, n = G[:, j][~np.isnan(G[:, j])], N[:, j][~np.isnan(N[:, j])]
        if len(g) and len(n):
            p[j] = (stats.mannwhitneyu(g, n, alternative="two-sided", method="asymptotic").pvalue if test == "mannwhitneyu"
                    else stats.ranksums(g, n).pvalue)
    return np.nanmean(G, axis=0), np.nanmean(N, axis=0), p

def summarise_6b(vs, test="mannwhitneyu"):
    rows = []
    for s, (G, N) in vs.items():
        gm, nm, p = trialwise(G, N, test)
        for lab, k in N_TESTS.items():

In jaq, despereaux and montague the last trial after 0 with the goal significantly above the never-goal arms is +2 or +3, within both bounds, at 0.05/80 and 0.05/81, under every session reading, with completed blocks only, and for jaq over all 26 reading (b) candidates (+2 to +3). In roquefort trials +1 to +3 are significant, and so is the single trial +29 (goal visited in 9 of 39 traces, never-goal arms in 8 of 170, p = 1.6e-4), so its last significant trial is +29 and the behavioural half fails under both bounds. Trial +29 stands alone: trials +20 to +40 other than +29 have p of 6.7e-3 or more, against 6.3e-4 and 6.2e-4 for 0.05/80 and 0.05/81, and its neighbours +28 and +30 have p = 0.74 and 0.45. Its 9 goal visits come from 9 different sessions, all in a later block, none rewarded and none made while the arm was the goal again. Without tie correction (scipy's ranksums), +29 is not significant (p = 0.074) and every subject ends at +2, within both bounds. The claims file says only "Wilcoxon rank-sum test", so the behavioural half's result depends on this choice, and a question is asked. No subject has a trial after 0 with the goal significantly below the never-goal arms. The goal traces are 94, 95, 112 and 169 under reading (a), against the 89, 85, 104 and 161 goal arms of the legend.

Claims not tested¶

The 31 other claims (fig6b-visits-decay among them, for its replay half) rest on sharp-wave ripples, multiunit events or the clusterless state space decoder. The dandiset holds neither these nor the spikes or LFP they are computed from, only the raw 30 kHz broadband, about 9.1 TB in total and stored one channel per chunk. The data check above measures the streaming rate and shows that reading the run epochs once would take far longer than the 30-minute limit, before any spike detection, ripple filtering or decoding. They are blocked as too_large. Ripple detection would also need the CA1 cell-layer tetrodes and the corpus callosum reference tetrode, which the electrode table does not identify (every location is "None"). fig6b-visits-decay is behavioural in part: its behavioural half is computed in the section above, but its criterion compares the visit decay with the replay enrichment of fig6a-pg-replay-persists, so it is blocked with it.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. Trials are rebuilt from the StateScript logs in the files, not from the paper's preprocessed trial structures. Task phases follow the claims file's account of label_trial_interval.m, with the first valid trial after each goal change in the old block, as a reader's answer settled from the code. Which jaq sessions are "sessions 1 and 3" is not fixed by the paper. Both readings the criterion allows are run: (a) in order of recording, and (b) the sessions the Methods describe. Under (b), day 6 epoch 4 is the one session where not all arms were visited, and the session with poor decoding cannot be found without the decoder, so every candidate is run. All readings give the same outcomes. Sessions are not selected by decode quality (the code's gooddecode), which needs the decoder. The rank-sum test of fig6b-visits-decay is run with and without tie correction, since the claims file does not say which.

What was found about the dataset.

  • The files hold raw broadband, behavioural events, position and task logs, but no spikes, LFP, ripple times, linearised position or decoding results. Every replay finding of the paper has to be recomputed from about 9.1 TB of raw data, which this registry cannot do.
  • The electrode table gives no location for any of the 128 channels, so the CA1 tetrodes and the reference tetrode cannot be identified.
  • The files do not mark which run sessions the paper analysed. They hold 27 and 24 run epochs for jaq and despereaux, where the paper used 26 (before excluding two) and 23. Which epoch is extra cannot be told; leaving out any one keeps the block counts within tolerance.
  • No log uses forage assist, including the five without a header. Two blocks have one reward fewer than programmed because the first trial after the switch, a rewarded visit to the new goal, belongs to the old block.
  • Trial counts are close to the legends' for search and correct repeat trials, but error trials are 11 to 24% more than Figure 5B's.
  • The behaviour in the logs matches the hardware: rewards equal the pump onsets in 115 of 116 epochs. The block counts are within 5% of the paper's for jaq (88 to 93 against 89) and despereaux (109 against 105), and equal for roquefort (85) and montague (166).
  • The draft version's files were rewritten in 2022, and in the file compared its position timestamps are off the DIO clock by about -1.59e9 s; the published version is consistent.
  • Position is sampled at about 33 Hz (Methods: 30 Hz); most epochs end with NaN timestamps.
  • In one epoch (montague day 16 epoch 2) the log's clock steps by about 3.4 s against the DIO, and in one (jaq day 6 epoch 4) the log stops before a final pump.
