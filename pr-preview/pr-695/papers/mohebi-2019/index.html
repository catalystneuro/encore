Mohebi et al. 2019 · Nature

Dissociable dopamine dynamics for learning and motivation

doi:10.1038/s41586-019-1235-y · paper key mohebi-2019

Datasets

  • CRCNS:vta-2

The paper's findings on VTA dopamine cell firing mostly held up in this data, while its findings on dopamine release could not be tested, as the microdialysis and dLight recordings are not in the dataset. Latency tracked reward rate, and firing coded positive reward prediction errors and did not ramp before approach. Tonic firing changed little with reward rate, but some cells did change, and early-trial firing correlated with reward rate in 8 of 29 cells, which may come mostly from slow drift. Fewer cells responded to the light cue than reported, and the window and correction of the cue-response test are not given in the text read. Minor claims on cell identification and Pavlovian responses held in part.

  • ✓Reproduced Rats were more motivated when rewards were more likely; their latency from Light-On to Center-In scaled inversely with reward rate.
  • ⊘Blocked In microdialysis sessions, (log) latency depended on the outcomes of the preceding few trials, with regression weights of recent trials significantly different from zero.

  • ⊘Blocked In frontal cortex, microdialysis dopamine correlated with reward rate in ventral prelimbic cortex but not in more dorsal or more ventral subregions.
  • ⊘Blocked Overall behaviour did not differ between the microdialysis sessions sampling each of the seven subregions, in rewards per min, attempts per min or latency.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Optogenetic tagging identified 27 well-isolated VTA-l cells with reliable short-latency light-evoked spikes as dopamine neurons, plus 2 retrogradely tagged cells from a rat with NAc core virus.
  • ✓Reproduced Identified dopamine neurons were tonically active with low firing rates, mean 7.7 Hz, range 3.7 to 12.9 Hz.
  • ⊘Blocked Dopamine neurons fired at lower rates than all VTA-l neurons recorded with them.
  • ⊘Blocked Dopamine neurons had longer spike waveforms than other VTA-l neurons, with exceptions.
  • ⊘Blocked A distinct cluster of 38 VTA-l neurons from the same sessions had brief waveforms and high firing rates (mean 41.3 Hz, range 20.1 to 97.1 Hz) and included no tagged cells.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In the Pavlovian task, dopamine cells responded strongly to randomly timed food hopper clicks and progressively less strongly when the clicks were made more predictable by preceding cues.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Dopamine cell responses to the Pavlovian cues followed the cues' reward probability, in line with RPE-like coding.
  • ✓Reproduced Rats learned the cue probabilities; in an example session the rat entered the food port more during the 75% cue than the 25% and 0% cues.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Tonic dopamine cell firing in each block was indifferent to reward rate, with no significant change in the firing rate of any individual dopamine cell between higher- and lower-reward blocks.
  • ⊘Blocked No other VTA-l neurons changed firing rate between higher- and lower-reward blocks.
  • ✓Reproduced There was no overall change in the rate at which dopamine cells fired bursts between higher- and lower-reward blocks.
  • ✓Reproduced Dopamine cells never switched between active and inactive states; the proportion of time spent inactive (long inter-spike intervals) was very low and did not change between higher- and lower-reward blocks.
