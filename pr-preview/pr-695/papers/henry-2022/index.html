Henry et al. 2022 · Current Biology

Feature representation under crowding in macaque V1 and V4 neuronal populations

doi:10.1016/j.cub.2022.10.049 · paper key henry-2022

Datasets

  • CRCNS:v4-2

Most of the paper's main findings held up in direction in this data: distractors impair orientation discriminability more in V4 than in V1 for single neurons and populations, modulate V4 responses more strongly, and reduce noise correlations and Fano factors. The size of the crowding effect did not hold: the V4 threshold elevation was 7.30 against the paper's 2.52 at the closest spacing, and V1 values were also higher, with decoder settings the paper does not give. The tuning model's terms, the lower tuning correlation in V4 and the fixed decoder's failure to generalize in V4 held in direction with different values. Whether V4 receptive fields are larger could not be tested, as the mapping data are not deposited.

  • ◐Partially reproduced Eleven sessions gave 22 neuronal populations in each area, of on average 19 +/- 1.9 V1 neurons and 31 +/- 3.4 V4 neurons, 396 V1 and 691 V4 neurons in the single-neuron analyses.
  • ⊘Blocked V4 receptive fields are many-fold larger than V1 receptive fields, and the aggregate receptive field maps of the V1 and V4 populations overlap substantially in each animal.
  • ✓Reproduced V1 single-neuron discriminability of target orientation is lower with the nearest distractors than for targets alone, with a regression slope of 0.60 (95% CI 0.50 to 0.69).
  • ✓Reproduced In V4 the loss of single-neuron discriminability with the nearest distractors is stronger, with a regression slope of 0.20 (95% CI 0.14 to 0.26).
  • ✓Reproduced At the farthest spacing the loss of single-neuron discriminability is more moderate, with slopes of 0.74 (0.66 to 0.82) in V1 and 0.44 (0.37 to 0.50) in V4.
  • ✓Reproduced Distractors impair single-neuron discriminability more in V4 than in V1 at every target-distractor spacing.
  • ✓Reproduced In one animal at the nearest spacing, V4 population decoding performance with distractors tends to be lower than for targets alone when performance is above chance (p < 0.001), while in V1 the loss is not significant (p > 0.05).
  • ?Not reproduced In V1, distractors elevate the estimated discrimination threshold of the populations by 1.31 +/- 0.10 at the closest spacing and 1.15 +/- 0.08 at the farthest, significant at all but the farthest spacing (permutation test, p < 0.05).
  • ◐Partially reproduced In V4 the estimated threshold elevations are greater than in V1, 2.52 +/- 0.23 for the closest distractors and 1.89 +/- 0.19 for the farthest, significant at all spacings (permutation test, p < 0.001).
  • ✓Reproduced Pseudo-populations built by combining neurons across sessions give a broader range of decoding performance and similar estimates of threshold elevation.
  • ✓Reproduced Distractors reduce noise correlations, from 0.07 +/- 0.003 to 0.05 +/- 0.002 in V1 and from 0.11 +/- 0.001 to 0.06 +/- 0.001 in V4 with the nearest distractors (paired t test, p < 0.001).
  • ✓Reproduced When decoders are fit to trial-shuffled responses, distractors still lead to threshold elevations similar to those in the original data, so the change in correlations plays a minimal role in the information loss.
  • ✓Reproduced In V1, distractors at the closest spacing slightly reduce variability, the Fano factor falling from 1.05 +/- 0.02 for targets alone to 0.98 +/- 0.01 (paired t test, p < 0.001).
  • ✓Reproduced In V4, distractors at all spacings significantly reduce variability, the Fano factor falling from 1.00 +/- 0.01 for targets alone to 0.93 +/- 0.01 with the closest distractors (paired t test, p < 0.001 for each spacing).
  • ◐Partially reproduced In V1, distractors at the closest spacing suppress and facilitate responses in roughly equal proportion, with significant suppression in 23% and facilitation in 27% of neurons; for farther distractors modulation is weaker, with 2.5% significant facilitation and 13% suppression.
  • ✓Reproduced For the closest distractors most V4 neurons are facilitated (mean index 1.11 +/- 0.38, t test, p < 0.001; significant facilitation in 51% and suppression in 14% of cases), and the average index is larger than in V1 (one-sided t test, p < 0.01); at larger spacing facilitation and suppression are roughly equal (0.97 +/- 0.48; 24% and 32% significant).
  • ✓Reproduced The strength of modulation, the mean absolute log modulation index, is significant in both areas at all spacings (permutation test, p < 0.01).
  • ✓Reproduced Modulation strength is stronger in V4 than in V1 at all separations (one-sided t test, p = 0.01 for the closest distractors, p < 0.001 for the others).
  • ✓Reproduced Modulation strength in V4 is largely maintained across spacings (one-sided t test nearest against farthest, p = 0.44), whereas in V1 the farthest separation yields weaker modulation than the nearest (one-sided t test, p < 0.001).
  • ⊘Blocked Whether distractors suppress or facilitate responses depends on the alignment of the receptive field with the target; V1 neurons driven by the target alone are suppressed, most by near distractors, no V4 neuron is driven by the target alone, and neurons whose receptive fields straddle target and distractors are facilitated by near and suppressed by far distractors in both areas.
  • ✓Reproduced Fitting either a multiplicative or an additive change of target tuning alone, neurons facilitated by distractors are better described by the additive model and suppressed neurons by the multiplicative model, in V1 and V4; each single form reduces discriminability, but less than in the measured responses.
  • ◐Partially reproduced With both terms allowed, V1 tuning amid distractors has a multiplicative term below 1 (0.59 +/- 0.03) and a positive additive term (0.39 +/- 0.16); in V4 the multiplicative term is 0.36 +/- 0.01 and the additive term 0.90 +/- 0.02; the multiplicative term is smaller and the additive term larger in V4 than in V1 (t test).
  • ?Not reproduced In the neurons fitted with the tuning model, the fit quality (a normalized likelihood between a null model at 0 and an oracle at 1) is 0.63 in V1 and 0.73 in V4.
  • ✓Reproduced In both V1 and V4 the additive and multiplicative terms covary strongly, consistent with a flattening of tuning with maintained responsivity.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Some fits give a negative multiplicative term, offset by a large additive term; such cases are rare in V1 and more frequent in V4.
  • ✓Reproduced Simulating crowded responses with the fitted multiplicative and additive terms yields some loss of discriminability in V1 and more in V4, similar to but slightly stronger than the measured loss, and depending weakly on spacing.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Tuning for targets alone correlates well with tuning for targets with nearby distractors in V1 (0.38 +/- 0.03 for cells above a reliability criterion of 0.5), and significantly less in V4 (0.22 +/- 0.03; one-sided t test, p < 0.001).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Target tuning with near and with far distractors correlates less in V4 than V1 (0.17 +/- 0.02 against 0.36 +/- 0.02 at criterion 0.5; one-sided t test, p < 0.001).
  • ✓Reproduced In 4 further sessions, target tuning with two distractor configurations whose orientations differ by 20 degrees differs substantially in V4 (correlation 0.24 +/- 0.06) and is nearly unaffected in V1 (0.65 +/- 0.06; p < 0.001, one-sided t test).
