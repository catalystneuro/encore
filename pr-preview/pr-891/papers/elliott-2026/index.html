Summary: The paper's microcircuit findings hold up with its curated spike sortings: the significant unit pairs (all eight counts exact), the bipolar circuits of the two kainic acid slices and the geometry test reproduce. Its findings on seizure dynamics did not: kainic acid bursting, theta propagation in stages and sub-bursts tracking theta all failed here, largely because neither the paper nor its code defines bursts, onset or stages. Phase-locked units form two spatial clusters only with the code's spike selection, not the Methods', and the STTC clusters hold in one slice of two. The dataset holds raw voltage only, without the sortings, the kainic acid timing or two of the recordings.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Paper inconsistentThe Methods state that the STTC matrix is positive semidefinite and interpret its eigendecomposition like PCA; the S1 STTC matrix has negative eigenvalues (smallest -2.64).
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. How were bursts and superbursts detected for the counts of 67 bursts and 11 superbursts (S1) and 47 bursts (S2), and for the 6 to 8 bursts per epoch? Neither the paper nor its code defines them; the readings named in the claims file (population-rate peaks above twice a pre-KA mean or the mean plus 3 SD, gaps under 5 s) give 124 to 353 bursts and 12 epochs in S1.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. Which spike selection produced Extended Data Table 2? The Methods keep spikes while the theta envelope exceeds the RMS; the code keeps spikes in theta cycles whose peak exceeds the RMS of the envelope. Here the code's selection gives 73 and 107 phase-locked units in the S1 and S2 seizure windows (70 and 101 reported) and the Methods' gives 60 and 104.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. How were the two phase clusters of Fig. 2b formed for the Kuiper test? The Methods partition units by their angular difference from the polar mode, which makes the angle distributions differ by construction; the code sets position thresholds by hand. Was p = 0.01 from a spatial split?
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. How were the onset of seizure-like activity, the stage windows, the propagation directions and the rolling waves defined and counted? ◐Partially reproduced The curated spike sortings of the paper's repository can be linked to the dandiset's raw recordings; each recording's units sit on the electrodes of one dandiset file, and its spike times align with that file's raw voltage.
Every unit position of each sorting is an electrode position of one slice's files. The offset scan links recordings 1 to 6 to single files with peaks 74 to 169 times the chance level (S1 KA sub-10F-6 from 0 s, S1 baseline sub-10F-0 from 20.0 s, S2 KA sub-11G-2, S2 baseline sub-11G-4, S3 sub-1A-0, S4 sub-9E-2 from 30.0 s). Recordings 7 (S5) and 8 (S6) match the layout of the sub-3C and sub-8D files but no file by time (best ratio 1.3 and 1.4), so their raw recordings are not in the dandiset. The same scan run in lfp_elliott_2026.ipynb gives the same links. Each sorting file is the one the paper's latency notebook for that recording loads (printed with its cell and line). The scan finds the S3 sorting in a 40 s piece of its file (ratio 39 at the expected offset), and every sub-3C file is at least 40 s long, so no sub-3C file holds a stretch of the S5 recording, alone or joined to the others. The unit counts and durations of the linked records are compared in table1-unit-counts.
