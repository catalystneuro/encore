Wang et al. 2019

Different paths, same destination: divergent action potential responses produce conserved cardiac fight‐or‐flight response in mouse and rabbit hearts

Outcomes of 25 claims

  • ✓11 reproduced
  • 2 not reproduced
  • ⊘12 blocked

Summary: In this data, sympathetic nerve stimulation raised heart rate in rabbit and mouse hearts, shortened the rabbit action potential and calcium transient, prolonged mouse APD transiently and raised calcium transient amplitude in both species. APD also changed more at the base than at the apex in both species. The paper's monotonic rabbit time course did not hold: mean heart rate peaked at 20 s of stimulation and then fell by 60 s. The repolarization reversal, the paced-heart experiments and the model simulations could not be tested, as the dataset holds only per-heart summary tables and the MATLAB models could not run in the entry's environment. The propranolol control did not reproduce, on a single unadjusted test.

Summary written by the agent that recorded the attempt, from its claims and the agents’ reports. A failure to reproduce may be a failure of the agent.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. The paper uses "Repeated measures ANOVA or paired t-tests" but does not say which test gives each "*p<0.05 vs. time 0" or which comparison follows the ANOVA. This attempt runs both, with Holm-Sidak comparisons after the ANOVA, on paired t-tests and, as a variant, on the ANOVA's pooled error. For the propranolol claim (Figure 3D) they disagree; an unadjusted paired t-test finds APD80 at 10 s above time 0 (p = 0.010), Holm-Sidak on paired t-tests finds nothing, and Holm-Sidak on the pooled error, after the heart rate ANOVA (p = 0.031, 0.23 with the Greenhouse-Geisser correction), finds heart rate above time 0 at 30 to 60 s (smallest p = 0.0048). The claims file's rule (either test) makes the claim not reproduced. Which test and comparison were used, with what software, and was sphericity corrected for?
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. Are the columns of the Figure 3F no-SNS and SNS tables the same hearts in the same order, and why is the second no-SNS column empty? The test of SNS against no SNS is significant when paired by position (p = 0.0023 at 15 s) and not when unpaired.

  • The sheet gives one SNS value per heart and does not say which window of the 20 s it is, so the claims file's three window readings (at 20 s, mean, peak) cannot be told apart; the one value is tested.fig1a-rabbit-hr-increase

  • The dataset holds no APD80, CaTD80 or CaT amplitude for the 20 s SNS hearts of Figure 1. The claim is tested on the first 20 s of the 60 s SNS series of Figure 2, assuming that the first 20 s of a 60 s stimulation stands for a 20 s stimulation (Methods, Sympathetic nerve stimulation, give one protocol of 5 to 15 Hz for up to 60 s).   • Window readings as the claims file gives them, at 20 s, the mean of 5 to 20 s, and the most extreme value from 5 to 20 s; the series is sampled at 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 s.fig1bc-rabbit-ap-cat-response
  • As fig1bc-rabbit-ap-cat-response, tested on the first 20 s of the Figure 2 series, as the Figure 1 values are not in the dataset.fig1ef-mouse-ap-cat-response
  • The comparisons against time 0 after a repeated measures ANOVA are not named in the paper, which names no software; Holm-Sidak adjusted paired t-tests were used (a standard choice), and as a variant Holm-Sidak comparisons on the ANOVA's pooled residual mean square, as SigmaPlot computes them. Neither corrects for sphericity; the ANOVA's Greenhouse-Geisser corrected p is printed beside it. The outcome does not depend on the choice, as heart rate fails monotonicity under both readings.fig2a-rabbit-monotonic
