fig2g-nmf-six-factors¶

Non-negative matrix factorisation of trial-averaged, puff-triggered channel spectrograms yields six shared spectrotemporal factors, with explained variance 0.47 on held-out trials, from 458 channels of 7 participants.

The training set is the mean of the preinfusion and postinfusion training halves (odd trials), the test set the same for the even trials, as in the code (compute_factors_using = 'prepost'). NMF is fitted with k = 1 to 14 components per part (2 to 28 in all), as in fig. S17A. Held-out explained variance is read two ways: (a) the code's, the training reconstruction compared with the test data; (b) loadings refitted on the test data. The refit with k = 3 is compared with the paper's deposited factors and loadings.

def run_units(G, units, thresh_p=0.01):
    out = {}
    for u in units:
        j = G["units"].index(u)
        pre = G["h"]["scratch/puff_region_sgram_preinfusion"][j]; inf = G["h"]["scratch/puff_region_sgram_infusion"][j]

    return out

figs15-ketamine-spectral-changes-frontal¶

Ketamine changes spectral power across the brain, with the largest changes in frontal cortex, led by orbitofrontal cortex.

An approximate test on the per-participant 20-min LFP files. Timing: the Methods (iEEG ketamine infusion protocol) say "The eyepuff assay was administered preceding infusion, at approximately 30 minutes after the infusion start, and 110 minutes after infusion start". Each file runs from 10 min before to 10 min after the first puff, so the infusion file covers about minutes 20 to 40 of the infusion, and its second half (first puff to the end) is the 30 to 40 min peak window of fig. S15C. Because the start is approximate and the infusion start time is not in the dataset, the first half (about 20 to 30 min), the whole file, and the second half without the eyepuff assay (from 2 s after the last puff) are run as well.

Baseline: the paper's 25-min pre-ketamine window does not fit in a 20-min file. The preinfusion eyepuff file is used (the code's preinfusion session: Baseline for SD168, SD172 and SD245, Preinfusion for the others; ieeg_cd.PRE_SESSION_CODE). Main: its first 10 min, before the first puff, since the Methods exclude the eyepuff assay from preinfusion windows ("excluding periods of time in which the patient was undergoing the eyepuff assay", Methods, phase locking); variants: the whole file, and SD245's Preinfusion file in place of its Baseline file.

Spectrogram: multitaper with the parameters the Methods give for fig. S27 (time-half-bandwidth 30, 59 tapers, 30-s windows, 15-s stride); the paper gives none for fig. S15, so this is an assumption. Power is taken as log10 (an assumption; linear power is a variant), z-scored per channel and frequency to the baseline windows and averaged over the ketamine windows. Magnitude (criterion): per participant and region the mean over channels of the mean |z| over 1 to 100 Hz (58 to 62 Hz, the notch, left out), then the median over participants. Channels: within-shank bipolar pairs whose atlas label in the code record (pt_elec_anats_bipo) is a single one of the 21 regions of Fig. 3C, without out-of-brain or excluded contacts and without the code's bad channels (utils.bad_ch). The files carry no sleep or seizure annotations, so no period is excluded for them. Cohorts: (a) the 7 included participants; (b) every participant with a 40-min infusion in the dataset. Only the 7 included participants have LFP files, and the dataset does not say which of them had a continuous 40-min infusion (the Methods mention target-controlled and bolus infusions for some), so (b) is the same 7 or an unidentifiable subset of them; only (a) can be run.

figs27-slow-oscillations-with-strong-dissociation¶

All participants with strong dissociation (CADSS above 10) show ketamine-induced oscillations below 10 Hz, and high-CADSS participants tend to have a larger fraction of channels with such oscillations.

The detection of the Methods (Band-limited oscillation detection; criterion), on the channels and spectrograms of figs15: segment spectra are the mean of the 30-s multitaper windows inside each 3-min segment; FOOOF removes the aperiodic part; scipy.signal.find_peaks finds the peaks; peaks are clustered by 80% overlap of peak width; clusters in fewer than 40% of a window's segments are dropped; a ketamine cluster below 10 Hz counts if no baseline cluster lies within 2 Hz (new frequency) or if a one-sided rank-sum test of its peak heights against those of a baseline cluster within 2 Hz gives p < 0.001. Details the paper does not give, each an assumption: FOOOF 'fixed' aperiodic mode with default settings, fitted on 1 to 40 Hz; peaks above the aperiodic fit with a prominence of at least 0.1 log10 units (0.05 and 0.2 as variants); a peak's width at half its prominence; clusters by single linkage, two peaks joined when their overlap is at least 80% of the narrower one's width ('min'; 80% of the wider one's, 'max', as a variant); one height per segment (the highest); a cluster's frequency the median of its peak centres, which also decides "within 2 Hz" and "below 10 Hz".

Windows: baseline the preinfusion and postinfusion files combined (Methods: "pre- and post-infusion windows were combined"; the whole 20-min files, 12 segments), with the preinfusion file alone (6 segments) and its first half (3) as variants; ketamine window the second half of the infusion file (30 to 40 min, the criterion's fallback, 3 segments), with the first half (3) and the whole file (6) as variants. No sleep or ictal annotations exist, so none are excluded. As a reference without ketamine, the same detection tests the second half of the postinfusion file against the preinfusion file.

CADSS: the infusion score per participant (maximum over the infusion time points, as the code's utils.load_full_cadss and notebook 01) from the code record's data/cadss_across_pts.csv.
