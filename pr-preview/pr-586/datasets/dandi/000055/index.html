The paper's main findings hold up in this data: at the onset of naturalistic arm movements, low-frequency power falls and high-frequency power rises, most in precentral, postcentral and inferior parietal cortex, and behavior and this power vary significantly across subjects and recording days. Regression models of single-event power explain little variance (largest withheld R2 0.189), with reach angle and recording day the most informative features. The per-subject event counts did not reproduce, likely because the files hold 8088 events with none marked as a false positive. Whether low-frequency decreases are more widespread could not be tested, as the paper does not say how this was measured. A few minor details, such as the reach angle peaks, partly differ.

    The criterion judges the angle part on a Gaussian KDE with Scott's bandwidth evaluated on -90 to 90, with peaks required within 15 degrees of +-90. For angles bounded at +-90 such a KDE without boundary correction cannot place its peaks near +-90. Should the criterion use a boundary-corrected density (for example reflection at +-90), a histogram, or a wider window around +-90?

    Passage. For reach angle, the distributions tend to be bimodal, with peaks at +-90 (Results, Behavior during naturalistic movements, paragraph 3; Figure 2, drawn with seaborn kdeplot in the code)

    Why it matters. In this attempt the angle part fails in 0 of 12 subjects with the criterion's KDE and holds in 6 of 12 (and pooled) with reflection, still not more than half, so the outcome does not change here, but a criterion that a true peak at +-90 can pass would be judged differently on other data.
