    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The 249 labelled OL_L trials of 489 are used; the paper's 266 (Fig. 4B) or 520 (Fig. S7C-D) cannot be identified among the dataset's trials.
    • RT maxima at the edge of the search window are not peaks and those trials are dropped (2 to 5 of 249), a standard choice.
    • Early RTs. Between 8 and 13 RTs per reading fall below 0.1 s, before the leg push the SI says the peak marks (SI, Fitting of drift diffusion models), and the paper's RT histogram of 489 (Fig. S7D) has none below about 0.2 s. Fits with cutoffs of 0.05 to 0.2 s and a mixture limit of 0.3 are shown as checks, not readings, since a cutoff sets the NDT.
    • The GDDM was fitted only under sigma 0.25 and the 0 to 1 s window (4 of 16 readings per handling); as the NDT condition fails in every reading, fits in the other readings could not change the outcome.
    • The GDDM leak, collapse and fitting ranges, the time step and the loss (PyDDM's BIC) are standard choices; the paper names the components but not their parameterisation (Results, Ramping neural activity, paragraph 1).

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Description missingThe spike times are not on the clock of the trials table and the behaviour, and the files hold no synchronisation signal or offset (processing, analysis and stimulus are empty), though the SI says LabVIEW TTL signals were used to synchronise the recordings with the behaviour. At the stored turn onsets neither session shows a trial-locked population response, and turn type cannot be decoded from the units' spike counts, while the animal's lateral position decodes it at 0.92 to 0.95. In 503 the lag of the best correlation between spike rate and running speed grows smoothly from 12.9 to 19.75 s over the first 5200 s; a line through it (11.86 s plus 1.37 ms per s, residual SD 0.56 s) recovers trial-locked, turn-specific responses, so the spike times of 503 appear to be about 12 s later than the trial times of the same events, with a gap growing by about 1.4 ms per s. That clock is fitted to running speed, and 503 runs in 1.7% of OL_L and 31.8% of OL_R rows, so the turn-specific responses on it may in part reflect running. In 489 the onset-triggered best lags rise from 4.36 to 12.23 s over the first seven windows, which suggests a similar drifting offset, but no window's lag is separated from chance and the speed correlations are weak, so no clock can be fitted. The spikes of 489 end at 6255 s and its behaviour at 6218 s. None of the paper's findings on spike timing can be tested.
  • Data missingThe dataset holds 2 of the paper's 19 animals (489 and 503), with units, trials and ball tracking. The other 11 ephys sessions, the 6 behaviour-only sessions, and the wall positions, whisker tracking, touch times and gait of any session are not in it, though the SI says the spikes of all filtered units are packaged with the behavioural data in one NWB file per animal. Twelve claims are blocked for these data.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • Only animals 489 and 503 are in the dataset.table-s1-recordings
  • For 489 the trials are counted on the first 1000 rows, whose labels and times are usable; the 611 rows after them repeat one row of the table. That these 1000 rows are the 1000 trials of Table S1 is an assumption; the count of all 1611 rows is also given.table-s1-recordings
  • The spike times are not on the trial clock, so the epoch 1 rate is computed at every lag from -20 to 60 s in 0.1 s steps, the range searched in the data check, and the three timed values are not tested.fig1n-epoch-rates
  • The dataset has 61 units for 489 and 249 and 251 labelled OL_L and OL_R trials, where the paper has 65 units and 266 and 265 trials.fig1n-epoch-rates
  • Reading (c) uses the 2 animals in the dataset where the criterion has 19.figs1-running-speed
  • The dataset has 513 OL trials for 503 (230 OL_L, 283 OL_R); the paper's 414 cannot be identified among them.fig2a-success-rate
  • Following the legends, 503 pools OL_L and OL_R (Fig. 2B) and 489 uses OL_L (Fig. S6A-B), 249 labelled OL_L trials where the paper has 520.fig2b-lateral-spread
  • The dataset holds no wall positions, so lateral position relative to the corridor cannot be computed; the readings use the ball's lateral displacement.fig2b-lateral-spread
  • The 249 labelled OL_L trials of 489 are used; the paper's 266 (Fig. 4B) or 520 (Fig. S7C-D) cannot be identified among the dataset's trials.fig4-ddm-fit
  • RT maxima at the edge of the search window are not peaks and those trials are dropped (2 to 5 of 249), a standard choice.fig4-ddm-fit
  • Early RTs. Between 8 and 13 RTs per reading fall below 0.1 s, before the leg push the SI says the peak marks (SI, Fitting of drift diffusion models), and the paper's RT histogram of 489 (Fig. S7D) has none below about 0.2 s. The paper does not say how they were handled, so each fit is run with all RTs kept, with a uniform mixture of weight up to 0.1 (PyDDM's OverlayUniformMixture, which its documentation gives to confer robustness in likelihood fits), and with RTs below 0.1 s dropped (an assumption). Fits with cutoffs of 0.05 to 0.2 s and a mixture limit of 0.3 are shown as checks, not readings, since a cutoff sets the NDT.fig4-ddm-fit
  • The GDDM was fitted only under sigma 0.25 and the 0 to 1 s window (4 of 16 readings per handling); as the NDT condition fails in every reading, fits in the other readings could not change the outcome.fig4-ddm-fit
  • The GDDM leak, collapse and fitting ranges, the time step and the loss (PyDDM's BIC) are standard choices; the paper names the components but not their parameterisation (Results, Ramping neural activity, paragraph 1).fig4-ddm-fit
