  • methods-recording-counts

    Of 194 A1 cells recorded in current clamp, 123 met the analysis criterion (39 tested with natural sounds, 14 with TORCs, 6 with DMRs, 39 with modulated harmonic tones, 27 with modulated colored noise); 14 of 42 voltage-clamped cells and 14 of 16 MGB cells were analysed.

    All together, we recorded from 194 cells in 139 animals in current-clamp mode, of which 123 cells met our criterion for the analysis ... Of these, 39 cells were examined with natural sound ensembles, 14 cells with ensembles of temporally orthogonal ripple combinations, 6 cells with ensembles of dynamic moving ripples, 39 cells with ensembles of modulated harmonic tones, and 27 cells with ensembles of modulated colored noise ensembles

    Methods, Electrophysiology, paragraphs 2 and 3; Table 1

    How it will be judged

    Counts of cells and probes passing the paper's inclusion rule (Methods, Context dependence at subthreshold voltage level; Table 1 legend), at least two conditioning stimuli each with at least 4 trials (current clamp), at least 2 trials at all three holding potentials (voltage clamp) and at least 10 trials (MGB). Tolerance 0 for counts, as they define the sample; a count that differs is reported with the probes that make the difference. The paper does not say whether "silence" counts as one of the two conditioning stimuli; both readings (counted, not counted) are run, and the claim holds under either. The counts per cell for the other ensembles (TORC, DMR, MHT, MCN) and Table 1 rows are reported. Recordings in the dataset that are not in the paper (fewer repeats) are expected and are not a failure.

  • a1-mgb-firing-rates

    Firing rates were low in A1 (spontaneous 0.47 +/- 0.61 Hz, evoked 0.57 +/- 0.77 Hz, mean +/- SD, 194 cells) and high in MGB (spontaneous 0.78 +/- 1.25 Hz, evoked 11.4 +/- 16.9 Hz, 16 cells).

    Because of the low firing rates in A1 (spontaneous, 0.47 ... 0.61 Hz; evoked, 0.57 ... 0.77 Hz ... 194 cells ... Sufficiently high firing rates in MGB allowed us to analyze the context dependence at the spike level (spontaneous, 0.78 ... 1.25 Hz; evoked, 11.4 ... 16.9 Hz

    Methods, Analysis, paragraphs 2 and 3; Results, Context dependence, paragraph 1; Results, Subcortical contribution to context dependence, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    Tolerances are 10% of each mean; the printed SDs (0.61, 0.77, 1.25, 16.9 Hz) are the spread over cells, not an uncertainty of the mean, and are reported for comparison (within 10% of each). Spikes in MGB are extracted by a high-pass filter and a threshold with spike times at the peaks (Methods, Analysis, paragraph 3). The paper fixes neither the filter cutoff, the threshold, nor how A1 spikes were detected, nor the windows for "spontaneous" (readings are the intersequence silence, about 6 s, or a pre-stimulus baseline) and "evoked" (readings are all sound periods, or the probe periods analysed). The author runs a standard detector on each reading of the windows; reproduced when the four means are within tolerance under one reading, partially reproduced when the A1 means are below 1 Hz and the MGB evoked mean above 5 Hz but some means fall outside tolerance.

  • fig2-example-reliability

    The example A1 neuron of Figure 2 had high trial-to-trial reliability (correlation across trials 0.74 +/- 0.08 for the shown probe and 0.61 +/- 0.07 over the seven natural sound fragments tested), and the mean correlation over the population was 0.31 +/- 0.09.

    the correlation coefficient of the response traces across trials in a given context was 0.61 ... 0.07 (mean ... SD) for the seven natural sound fragments tested in this cell. The reliability varied within a given neuron as a function of the stimuli tested and across neurons; the mean correlation coefficient was 0.31 ... 0.09

    Results, Context dependence, paragraph 2; Figure 2A legend

    How it will be judged

    Tolerances are the stated SDs, as the paper gives them as the spread of the values (protocol, expected). The correlation is between response traces (median-filtered, zero-mean membrane potential) of trials within one context, over a probe. The paper does not say whether the coefficient is the mean over all pairs of trials or the correlation of each trial with the mean of the others, nor what the population mean is taken over (cells, or probes); each reading is run. The example cell is not identified; the author looks for a current-clamp cell tested with seven natural sound fragments over six repeats, and the example values are not tested when no cell can be matched. The population value decides the claim.

  • fig2-example-long-context

    In an example A1 neuron, changing the conditioning stimulus changed the response to the probe for about 4 s; responses did not differ between 2 and 4 s after probe onset and diverged again after about 4 s.

    Interestingly, context-induced differences could sometimes be intermittent; the three average response traces showed no difference in the interval 2 to 4 s after the onset of the probe, but diverged again after about 4 s

    Figure 2B and 2D; Results, Context dependence, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    The significance measure of the Methods (Kruskal-Wallis across the three contexts at each time point, significant where P < 0.01 holds for at least 50 consecutive 0.1-ms points). The cell and probe are not identified; the author looks for a current-clamp cell with seven natural sound fragments and a probe tested after silence and two other fragments, with six repeats in at least one context (the paper gives the repeats of the other two contexts only as at least 4, the analysis rule). Reproduced when, for the matched probe, significant periods occur both before 2 s and after 4 s and none between 2 and 4 s; not testable when no probe matches. As a weaker check that does not need the example, the number of natural sound probes with a significant period later than 4 s is reported.

  • fig3a-proportion-significant

    Of 305 natural sound probe stimuli tested with different ... typically around five to eight ... natural sound contexts in 39 cells, significant effects were observed in 204 probes (66.9%; Fig. 3A), and about a quarter (23.7%) of the events occurred longer than one second after the onset of a probe stimulus

    How it will be judged

    A probe is significant when the significance measure (Methods) finds at least one period with P < 0.01 for at least 5 ms. The late fraction follows the Figure 3A legend, one minus the cumulative probability at 1 s, where the cumulative probability is the running integral of the proportion curve less the noise floor (1000 shuffles per probe), normalized at its peak (4.72 s in the paper). The intersequence silences are not probes here (Methods, Stimulus design, paragraph 2, which leaves the "silence probe" periods out of Figure 3). Reproduced when, under one reading, all three values are within tolerance; partially reproduced when the count is within tolerance and the late fraction is not.

  • timescale-longer-than-membrane

    The trial-to-trial noise autocorrelation and the autocorrelation of spontaneous activity decay within about 100 ms, much faster than the second-long timescale of context dependence in A1.

    we found that ... had a sharp peak with a rapid decay (within ... 100 ms; data not shown), much faster than the slow timescale identified in neural responses in area A1

    Methods, Analysis, Timescale of intrinsic membrane properties; Results, Context dependence, paragraph 7

    How it will be judged

    Noise is the residual of each trial from its context mean (Eq. 14), its autocorrelation normalized by the noise variance (Eq. 45), averaged over trials and probes of the natural sound current-clamp data. The paper does not say which period gives the spontaneous activity; the only silences in the design are the intersequence intervals (about 6 s), which are used, whole as the main reading and without their first 1 s as a variant, since the Methods (Stimulus design, paragraph 2) say context effects persist soon after the onset of these intervals and fade within about 1 s. "Decay" is not defined; the reading used is the lag at which the mean autocorrelation first falls below 1/e, with the lag where it falls below 0.1 shown as a variant. Reproduced when both lags are under 150 ms for both noise and spontaneous activity under the 1/e reading, and the 1/e lag is below a third of 1.04 s.

  • fig4-strf-performance-low

    Linear STRF models of 20 A1 cells from Machens et al. (2004) predicted less than about 20% of the predictable response power, and extending the window from 180 ms to about 4 s did not improve them significantly, with or without a static nonlinearity; the LN model did only slightly better.

    The performance of STRF-based models was in general unsatisfactory ... consistent with previous work (Ahrens et al. 2008; Machens et al. 2004; Sahani and Linden 2003). The performance did not improve significantly, however, when we extended the window length

    Results, Relation to response predictability, paragraph 5; Figure 4; Discussion, Context dependence and model construction, paragraph 4

    How it will be judged

    Per the Methods (Neural encoding models; Model performance), the 20 Wehr cells of Machens et al. (2004), spectrogram (Eq. 47) at 3 bins per octave from 0.40 to 22 kHz (17 bins), time bins of 2^k ms for k = 2 to 10 with 45 bins split as the Methods list, giving windows of 180, 324, 548, 884, 1364, 2004, 2772, 3540 and 4052 ms (765 parameters); ridge regression (Eq. 50) with 10-fold cross-validation choosing the ridge parameter on the validation fold; LN by robust lowess (5% span, 5 iterations) on the training set; performance by Eq. 53 on training (upper) and validation (lower) data, averaged over cells. Open, with readings run, the spectrogram window h(t) (readings 10-ms and 20-ms Hann), the response sampling (4-ms bins, the finest time bin, or the 10-kHz trace), the range of ridge values (a log grid wide enough that the best value is interior, a standard choice), and how folds are formed (contiguous blocks, or by stimulus). The paper does not say whether the 20% refers to the validation (lower) or the training (upper) estimate; both are readings. "Did not improve significantly" is a claim of absence and the paper names no test; the test fixed here, as an assumption the outcome rests on, is a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the 20 cells of validation performance at 4052 ms against 180 ms, one-sided for an improvement, at 0.05, for STRF and for LN; a Friedman test over the nine windows is shown as a variant and does not decide.
