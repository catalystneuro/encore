    Findings about the data

    What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

    • Metadata errorEvery file's session description says the enclosure was 1 x 1 m, but in 23 of the 118 sessions the tracked positions span 1.5 to 2.5 m.
    • Metadata errorThe session descriptions say position is given for two LEDs, but 47 of the 118 sessions have one, so head direction cannot be computed for 167 of 620 cell-sessions.
    • Identifier missingNo identifier links a cell across sessions, so the same cell may be counted more than once.
    • Selection not documentedThe dandiset is described as a sample of the paper's cells without saying how they were selected.
    • Methods not availableThe paper is not open access and the dandiset does not give the analysis parameters, such as the criterion for a grid cell.

    • answered 2026-10-20asked 2026-10-20about layer2-grid, grid-all-layers, gridness-range-similar, grid-stable, grid-phase-orientation, deep-hd-conjunctive, gridness-hd-continuous, conj-tuning-similar, speed, speed-slope-steeper, speed-intercept

      Reading O3 sets the gridness circle by "the distance from the centre to the outermost peak's centre plus that peak's radius", and removes the central peak "as a disc of the radius of that peak". Is a peak's centre the pixel of its maximum correlation or the centroid of its pixels, and is its radius that of a disc of the same area, or the largest distance from its centre to one of its pixels? The same choice sets the spacing (median distance to the six inner peaks) and the orientation.

      Passage. SOM, Autocorrelation of rate maps, "peaks defined as 100 or more contiguous pixels of 1.5 x 1.5 cm2 above a fixed threshold"; "the circle was fitted around the outermost peak". The SOM gives no rule for a peak's position or size.

      Why it matters. The attempt runs both centres (maximum and centroid) and takes the radius as that of a disc of the same area. Between the two centres the fraction of grid cells changes by up to 0.06 in a layer (layer II 0.620 to 0.718 over all readings), and the readings decide whether layer II is within tolerance (13 of 32 readings) and whether layers V and VI hold the orientation part of grid-phase-orientation.

      The reader’s answer. The paper does not give it. The SOM defines spacing as "the distance from the central peak to the vertices of the inner hexagon" and the circle as fitted "around the outermost peak", with peaks "100 or more contiguous pixels", and says nothing of a peak's centre or radius. Both centres (the pixel of highest correlation, the centroid) and both radii (the disc of the same area, the largest distance to a pixel of the peak) are now named as readings under O3 in the claims file, and the same centre serves spacing and orientation.
    • answered 2026-10-20asked 2026-10-20about hd-tuning-breadth-layers

      The claims file says breadth is "the angular standard deviation of the mean vector". Batschelet (1981, the SOM's reference S8) defines two such quantities from the mean vector length r, the angular deviation s = sqrt(2(1 - r)) and the circular standard deviation s0 = sqrt(-2 ln r). Which one do the paper's means (55.9, 52.2 and 58.2 degrees) use?

      Passage. SOM, Analysis of head-direction cells, "The breadth of directional tuning was expressed by the angular standard deviation of the mean vector"; fig. S2 and S4 legends, "Angular deviation refers to the circular standard deviation as defined in the Supplementary Materials and Methods".

      Why it matters. The circular standard deviation is larger than the angular deviation for the same cells (layer III means 62.5 to 76.2 against 49.1 to 56.5), so only the angular deviation can come near the paper's means, and the two give different numbers of significant layer differences (ANOVA 28 against 45 of 68 rows). The attempt runs both.

      The reader’s answer. The paper does not settle it. The SOM says "the angular standard deviation of the mean vector" and cites Batschelet (S8); the fig. S2 and S4 legends say "Angular deviation refers to the circular standard deviation", which names one of Batschelet's quantities in the label and the other in the gloss, and the histogram axes (0 to 100 degrees) fit both. The criterion now names both, sqrt(2(1 - r)) and sqrt(-2 ln r), as readings; the claim's rule for readings applies.
    • answered 2026-10-20asked 2026-10-20about speed, speed-slope-steeper, speed-intercept

      The criterion says the claim is reproduced "when, under a combination of readings, all three fractions are within tolerance", and methods_underspecified "when readings disagree between these outcomes". Under counting reading (b), positive slope, all three fractions are within tolerance under 54 combinations of the other readings (speed measure, positions, trials, cell classes) and only the order holds under 200. Is the claim then reproduced (some combination matches) or methods_underspecified (the combinations disagree)? Which of the readings are readings of the reported number (where any one matching suffices) and which are method details (where all must agree)?

      Passage. Main text, paragraph 6, "141/150 cells, P < 0.001"; Fig. 4B legend, "Nearly all grid cells, head-direction cells, and grid x head-direction cells had positive slope values".

      Why it matters. The attempt follows the last sentence and blocks speed as methods_underspecified, which by the protocol also blocks speed-slope-steeper and speed-intercept, whose analyses ran (partially reproduced and not reproduced under every reading). Under the first sentence speed would be reproduced.

      The reader’s answer. This is a question of the criterion, which was unclear, and it is now rewritten. The counting readings (a) and (b) and the three significance measures are readings of the reported number (what "141/150 cells, P < 0.001" counts), so for given method details the best of them is taken. The speed measure, the positions, the trials and the cell classes are method details, and the outcome must agree across all their combinations, or the claim is methods_underspecified. The statement and expected values are unchanged.

      Are the "head-direction cells" whose units are counted the pure head-direction cells of table S1B, or every cell passing the head-direction criteria including conjunctive cells; and must the grid or conjunctive cell in the same unit be another cell?

      Passage. Main text, paragraph 4, "Head-direction cells were usually encountered together with grid cells (fig. S6)"; fig. S6 counts grid cells, head-direction cells and grid x head-direction cells as three classes.

      Why it matters. With conjunctive cells counted as head-direction cells and allowed to stand for the grid cell themselves, every unit holding a conjunctive cell passes, which raises the tetrode fraction (0.39 to 0.48 for pure head-direction cells, below the criterion's one half). The attempt uses pure head-direction cells and another cell.

      The reader’s answer. The paper gives the first part. Fig. S6 counts "grid cells, head-direction cells and grid x head-direction cells" as three classes, and table S1B and the speed paragraph ("headdirection cells (153/220 cells") use head-direction cells for the 220 pure ones, so the cells are the pure head-direction cells, and the cell with them is another cell. Whether that cell must be a pure grid cell (fig. S6's class) or may be a conjunctive cell (which meets the grid criterion) is not given; the criterion now names both readings.

    Reproduction Grid, head-direction and conjunctive cells by entorhinal layer (Sargolini et al. 2006)

    Tests the 21 claims of Sargolini et al. (2006) on dandiset 000582, which holds spike times and tracked positions for 620 cell-sessions from the medial entorhinal cortex of 15 rats. For every cell it computes gridness, head-direction tuning and speed regressions by the paper's Supporting Online Material, under every reading the claims file names, and compares the cell classes found in each cortical layer.
