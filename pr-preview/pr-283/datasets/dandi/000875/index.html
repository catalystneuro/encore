  • fig4-bipolar-cancellation

    A single 600-ns unipolar pulse at 15 kV/cm caused by far the strongest YP uptake in single CHO cells (126 +/- 14 a.u. at the end of recording, n = 25), while bipolar signals starting with the same pulse caused significantly weaker uptake (4-phase 20.8 +/- 2.0, 8-phase 6.9 +/- 1.6, 8-phase with 5-us delays 25.4 +/- 2.8 a.u.; p < 0.001); sham caused no gain.

    Sham-exposed cells showed no gain in YP fluorescence during the 10 min of observation ... A single 600-ns pulse at 15 kV/cm caused by far the strongest YP uptake, reaching 126±14 a.u. (n=25) by the end of recording. Bipolar signals started with exactly the same pulse (600 ns, 15 kV/cm) but caused significantly weaker effects (p<0.001). By the end of recording, YP signal reached only 20.8±2.0 a.u. for the 4-phasic signal; 6.9±1.6 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal; and 25.4±2.8 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal with 5-μs delays between phases

    Results, 3.1, paragraph 1; Figure 4

    How it will be judged

    Per cell, F-F0 at the last image (600 s), F the mean intensity in a ROI drawn around the cell and F0 the mean of the six images before the pulse; F-F0 is what Methods 2.2 calls the background-corrected emission, and no other subtraction is named. Tolerances are the paper's s.e. Reproduced when the four group means are within tolerance and each bipolar group is below the unipolar group by a two-sided Student's t-test at p < 0.001; partially reproduced when the order and the tests hold but some means fall outside tolerance. The sham group is reported and not judged, as the paper gives no value or test for it. Whether the t-test is the test used for single cells is open (Methods 2.2 names none); a Welch t-test is shown as a variant.

The paper's own end values give 18.3, 6.1 and 5.0, so the 4-phase ratio of Section 3.4 does not match its Section 3.1 numbers; the ratio from the paper's means is reported beside the range.

Partially reproduced when two of the three hold.

  • fig7-targeting-independent-of-array-size

    The electric field distribution and strength achieved within each array at a given pulse voltage were obviously different, but the remote targeting, at least within studied limits, did not depend on either the electric pulse amplitude or array size ( Fig. 7 )

    Discussion, paragraph 4; Figure 7

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence, judged on the paper's test, the two-sided Student's t-test at p < 0.05 (Methods 2.4), on per-dish normalized center values (n = 3 per group, Fig. 7 legend), small array (Fig. 7B) against large array (Fig. 7D), at each first-pulse voltage the two share and pooled over voltages. Reproduced when the test finds no difference, not reproduced when it does; the difference in percentage points is given either way. The paper names no comparison measure, and Fig. 7 compares arrays under different protocols (1Px4 small, 10Px4 large), which Fig. 6 shows changes the normalized center; this confound is reported with the result. Comparing whole normalized profiles on a distance scaled to each array's measured half-diagonal is shown as a variant.
