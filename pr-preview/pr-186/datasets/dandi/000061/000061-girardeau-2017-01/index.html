      Over 15 sessions, post minus pre reactivation of the air-puff templates exceeds that of the safe templates by a median of 0.0133, in 9 of 15 sessions, Wilcoxon p = 0.11; the criterion needs p < 0.05, and p < 0.1 for a partial outcome. Positive in 6 of 9 Rat08, 2 of 3 Rat10 and 1 of 3 Rat11 sessions. Variants lean the same way with mixed strength; the first pattern of each direction gives p = 0.048 (10 of 15), the pooled per-pattern Mann-Whitney test p = 0.083, equal numbers of template bins (a random subset) p = 0.33, post-sleep strength alone p = 0.45, leaving out the two sessions with fewer than 5 puffs p = 0.15, and templates from the travel part of each lap only (its last part, as long as the session's 10th-percentile lap) p = 0.19. Laps from reward times include the time spent at the end where a lap starts; a median of 33% of the air-puff and 43% of the safe template time is in the travel part, and in the early Rat08 sessions laps last up to 2310 s, so the safe templates of some sessions rest on few bins (5610 against 66804 in Rat08-20130709).

      How the test differed from the paper’s method

      • Laps, and so run direction, come from the reward times, as the dandiset holds no position; the paper presumably used the position on the track.
      • The air-puff direction is the lap direction of the run holding most puffs; in the 15 sessions tested, 295 of their 304 puffs fall in laps of that direction (927 of 957 over the 31 files with units and at least 5 puffs). No minimum number of puffs was set; two sessions with 2 and 3 puffs enter the test.
      • Laps include the time spent at the starting end, not only running; templates from the travel part of laps are a variant.
      • The measure (mean over significant patterns of post minus pre strength, compared across sessions) was chosen here before the analysis; the paper's is not known here.

      Hippocampus-BLA pyramidal cell pair correlations of the run are reinstated in post-sleep NREM, measured as explained variance (EV) exceeding the reverse explained variance (REV) across sessions.

      The EV and REV, calculated using hippocampus–BLA pyramidal cell pairs, showed significant reactivations between the hippocampus and BLA during post-NREM (Fig. 3c).

      Results, BLA-hippocampus coordinated reactivations during NREM sleep, paragraph 1; Figure 3a-c

      What the paper reports

      • EV against REV, NREM, pyramidal pairs: Wilcoxon signed rank test, z = 3.72, p = 1.9 × 10⁻⁴ (n = 25 sessions in 3 rats)

      15 sessions (9 Rat08, 3 Rat10, 3 Rat11) with 341 hippocampal and 483 BLA units; every session has at least one significant joint pattern, 36 in all. Post-sleep minus pre-sleep Non-REM strength has a median of 0.0119, positive in 28 of 36, Wilcoxon p = 3.6e-05. The median is above zero in each rat (Rat08 0.0170, 18 of 21, p = 0.00013; Rat10 0.0024, 5 of 8, p = 0.74; Rat11 0.0190, 5 of 7, p = 0.11). Non-significant patterns change by a median of 0.0001. The variants (25 and 100 ms bins, the wider BLA labels over 21 sessions, the first pattern of each session) all give positive medians with p of 0.0084 or less. The comparison does not separate reactivation from slow drift of the recording, since post-sleep is next to the run in time.

      How the test differed from the paper’s method

      • The paper's methods were not available; the joint-pattern method (singular vectors of the run's hippocampus by BLA cross-correlation matrix, shuffle threshold, product of projections), the 50 ms bins and the unit criteria are common choices fixed in the claims file.
      • BLA is taken as the units labelled bla or BLA; other undescribed labels that may be parts of the basolateral complex are a variant only.
      • Sessions without sleep states or ripples, and Rat09 (no hippocampal units), are left out; which sessions and rats the paper used is not known here.
      • Units 0 and 1 of ten files are left out because their spike times are mixed (see findings).

      Reading (a) holds. The mean ripple-triggered strength in post-sleep Non-REM is highest in the bin of the ripple peak, 4.36 times its mean at 0.5 to 1 s; the peak bin exceeds the flanks in 30 of 36 patterns (median 0.103, p = 6e-06), with a positive median in each rat. 22 of 36 patterns have their own maximum within 100 ms of the ripple.       • Ripples are the dandiset's `rip` annotations, not a detection run here.

      Results, A subset of BLA cells are modulated during hippocampal SPW-Rs, paragraph 1; Figure 4a

      103 of 822 BLA units in 17 sessions (12.5%) are positively modulated in Non-REM ripple windows, against a chance level of 0.7% (6 units) from the same procedure on windows shifted by 7 s; binomial p = 9.8e-90. Above chance in each rat (Rat08 51 of 496, Rat10 4 of 84, Rat11 48 of 242), also against each rat's own chance level (Rat10 has 0 chance units; p = 0.018 against one unit). 104 units (12.7%) are negatively modulated. At p of 0.05 or less the fraction is 16.3% against 5.6% chance, at 0.002 or less 10.8% against 0.2%. The shifted windows stay mostly in Non-REM (a median of 0.9% of randomly shifted and 1.4% of 7 s shifted ripple peaks fall outside it).

      How the test differed from the paper’s method

      • The test of modulation (spike count in ripple windows against windows shifted together by 0.5 to 5 s, one-sided p of 0.01 or less) is a common choice; the paper's test is not known here.
      • Units are counted once per session; a cell recorded on several days may be counted more than once, as the dandiset does not match units across days.

      Among nine groups of hippocampus-BLA pyramidal pairs formed by run correlation and BLA ripple modulation, the pairs with a significant positive run correlation and an upmodulated BLA partner show the largest pre-NREM to post-NREM increase in correlation, and differ from every other group.

      Hippocampus–BLA pairs with significant, positive run correlations and SPW-R upmodulation of the BLA partner showed the largest pre-NREM to post-NREM change

      Results, Ripple-modulated BLA cells are preferentially involved in reactivations, paragraph 1; Figure 5a; Supplementary Fig. 6a; Supplementary Table 2

      What the paper reports

      • the nine groups of pairs by run correlation and BLA ripple modulation: one-way ANOVA, F = 29.09, d.f. = 8, p = 1.1 × 10⁻⁴⁵ (n = 37,660 pairs in 3 rats)
      • the upmodulated, positively correlated group against the other groups: post hoc comparisons, p < 0.001, Tukey-Kramer (Fig. 5a legend, which gives P < 0.01; the Online Methods say Bonferroni)-corrected

      In 31 significant patterns with both kinds of BLA unit, the mean squared BLA weight of the ripple-up units exceeds that of the other BLA units (medians 0.0505 and 0.0181; difference positive in 24 of 31, Wilcoxon p = 0.0013). The ripple-up units are a median 20% of a pattern's BLA units and carry 34% of its BLA weight. It holds in Rat08 (17 of 21) and Rat11 (6 of 7) and not in Rat10 (1 of 3). In patterns whose post minus pre reactivation is positive, p = 0.0018. Firing rate accounts for part of the effect; up units fire faster in the run (median 1.66 against 0.41 Hz) and squared weight rises with rate within a pattern (median Spearman 0.46). Against other BLA units of the nearest rate, up units still carry more weight (24 of 31 patterns, p = 0.012). At session level (13 sessions) the difference holds (10 of 13, p = 0.027), the rate-matched difference does not (9 of 13, p = 0.22). The criterion is the pooled per-pattern test, which holds.

      How the test differed from the paper’s method

      • The abstract does not say how involvement was measured; the pattern weights were chosen here before the analysis.

      Rats learned the daily location of the air puff. The speed ratio between the previous and the current danger zone rises from pre-run to run and post-run, reversing between pre-run and post-run.

      Therefore, the systematic reversal in the speed ratio (previous/current danger zone) between pre- and ... post-run indicates learning of the new air puff location (Fig. 1b and Supplementary Fig. 1b).

      Results, Rats learn the daily location of an aversive air puff on a linear track, paragraph 1; Figure 1b, right; Supplementary Fig. 1b

      What the paper reports

      • speed ratio over pre-run, run and post-run: one-way repeated measure ANOVA, F = 40.48, d.f. = 2, p = 1.3 × 10⁻¹³ (n = 55 sessions in 4 animals)
      • speed ratio, pre-run against run: post hoc t-test, t(51) = -8.49, p = 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹, Bonferroni (on the significance thresholds; the P shown is uncorrected)-corrected
      • speed ratio, pre-run against post-run: post hoc t-test, t(51) = -5.08, p = 5.3 × 10⁻⁶, Bonferroni (on the significance thresholds; the P shown is uncorrected)-corrected
      • speed ratio, run against post-run: post hoc t-test, t(50) = 4.43, p = 5.1 × 10⁻⁵, Bonferroni (on the significance thresholds; the P shown is uncorrected)-corrected

      Learning the location of the puff is behaviour along the track, which needs the rat's position. The dandiset holds no position tracking. The files hold the raw video frames (360 by 640 pixels, gzip), so position could in principle be tracked, but the 40 run videos have 1920573 frames (1328 GB uncompressed) and streaming 20 frames took 7.2 s in the final run, about 190 hours for the run frames alone (the rate varies between runs); the video timestamps are also not on the session clock. No public copy of the tracking linked to the paper or the dandiset was found. As a description only, laps toward the air-puff end take longer than the other way in the run (median ratio 1.18 over 38 sessions, p = 0.0063) and about as long in the pre-run (0.95) and the post-run (1.00); lap times give no location on the track, and slower laps where puffs are given do not separate learning from the puffs themselves. No criterion was written, so this decides nothing.

    Findings about the data

    What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

    • Metadata errorThe epochs table is stored in milliseconds although its columns say seconds; in all 40 files its last stop time is exactly 1000 times the LFP duration and the last spike time.
    • Metadata errorVideo timestamps are stated in seconds but are in milliseconds and are not on the session clock. The first timestamp matches the start of the video's epoch in 1 of 114 videos, while the spans match the epoch durations in 93 of 114.
    • File format errorIn ten files the spike_times_index is wrong at the first units. Unit 1's spike times fall back once, after 65536 times k spikes, and unit 0 stops part way through the session at the time unit 1 starts, so the first part of unit 1 is the end of unit 0. Units 0 and 1 of these files were left out.
    • Data missingSeven files have no units table (Rat08-20130722, five Rat09 files and Rat11-20150403), sixteen have no sleep states and fourteen have no ripple events. The Rat09 files have no hippocampal units and no ripples, so Rat09 cannot enter a hippocampus and BLA analysis.
    • Data missingThere is no position tracking, only the raw video frames, so behaviour on the track, including the learning of the puff location, cannot be measured without tracking about 1.9 million frames; run direction can only be inferred from reward times.
    • Description missingUnit region labels are undescribed short codes (bla, blv, bmp, ladl, cen, den, ven, pir, plc01, PLCo1, cpu, hpc, HIP, no label), some in two cases for the same region, and Rat09-20140328 has no location column; the electrode table gives every channel as unknown. Which labels make up the paper's BLA cannot be told from the dandiset.
    • Description missingThe `trials` table of the Rat08 and Rat09 files is described only as "experimental trials"; its intervals of 3 to 5 s fall partly outside the run and are not laps.
    • Description missingThe ripple annotations carry a number in each label (for example "Ripple peak 156"), presumably the detection channel, with no description; ripples, puffs and rewards are stored as annotation series under stimulus presentation.
    • Methods not availableThe paper is not open access and neither it (as far as the abstract shows) nor the dandiset links analysis code, so the reactivation method, unit selection and statistics had to be chosen here.

    Reproduction Hippocampus and amygdala reactivation during sleep (Girardeau et al. 2017)

    Tests the claims of Girardeau, Inema and Buzsaki (2017), written from the full text, the supplement and the paper's code, on dandiset 000061, which holds sorted units of the hippocampus and the amygdala, sleep states, ripple, air puff and reward events from four rats on an air puff linear track task. It tests the unit counts, habituation over days, BLA firing by sleep state, hippocampus-BLA and hippocampus-central nuclei reactivation (explained variance) with all cells, and the ripple rate before and after training. Claims on putative pyramidal cells and on position are blocked, as the dandiset holds neither. All files
