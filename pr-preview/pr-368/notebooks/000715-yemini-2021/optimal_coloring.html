Optimal coloring (Yemini et al. 2021) on the atlas of dandiset 000715¶

Paper. Yemini et al. (2021), Cell 184:272-288, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2020.12.012; claims written from the accepted author manuscript (PMC10494711) with Methods S1 and S2.

Data. Neighbors come from the statistical atlas of neuron positions fit on the ten heads of DANDI:000715, version 0.241009.1514, the same atlas and adjacency as ms2-neuropal-color-violations in reproduce_yemini_2021.ipynb (the version is chosen there). The reporters and their expression come from data.mat of the paper's Optimal_Coloring code (github.com/Eviatar/Optimal_Coloring at 990c597), an outside input: the claim is data: with_inputs. The adjacency stored in the same data.mat, the paper's own input for its 235 head and tail neurons, is run as a reference.

fig7de-optimal-coloring-violations¶

The optimal-coloring algorithm, using WormBase reporters, finds NeuroPAL alternatives with 1.7% color violations with 39 reporters and 10.5% when restricted to 3 reporters.

Criterion: adjacency as in ms2-neuropal-color-violations; the Brain Atlas reporters of data.mat; 3 colors; margin 1/3; optimal_color_solver.m; the best of 10,000 runs at sparsity 100 for the roughly 40-reporter case and the best of 7,500 runs for the 3-reporter case, at the sparsity whose solutions use 3 reporters after the code's Otsu threshold. Violations are the code's mean shortfall below the margin over neighboring pairs (find_optimal_colors.m), which decides the claim; the Methods share of neighboring pairs below the margin is shown as a variant.

Details taken from the paper and its code:

  • Three intensities is the margin of 1/3: "1/3 as our minimum color margin (corresponding to discriminating 3 levels of color intensity)" (Methods S2 section 4.2). The solver has no other intensity setting.
  • 300 iterations: optimal_color_parameters.m (iterations = 300) and Methods S2 section 4.2 ("about 25 seconds for 300 iterations in total").
  • The solution kept from a set of runs is the one with the fewest violations: "chosen as those with the minimal color violations, among 10,000 simulations" and "among 7,500 simulations" (Methods S2 Figure 1 legend). The figure's labels give the reporter counts of the chosen solutions (Reporters=39, Reporters=3). The best run over all 10,000 is the main reading of the first value, and the best run keeping exactly 39 reporters a variant; for the second value the best run keeping exactly 3 reporters is the main reading, and the best over all runs a variant.
  • Each solution's colors are those of find_optimal_colors.m: reporters whose summed weight exceeds the Otsu threshold of X are kept, and R(:, kept) X(kept, :) is scaled by each channel's maximum.

Runs are drawn in batches of 250 from one generator per part, with fixed seeds.

K, MARGIN, ITERS, BATCH = 3, 1 / 3, 300, 250

def run_many(A, R, sparsity, runs, rng):
    out = []
    for b in range(0, runs, BATCH):
        X = npl.optimal_color_solver(A, R, K, MARGIN, sparsity, ITERS, min(BATCH, runs - b), rng)
        out.append(npl.solution_violations(X, R, A, MARGIN))
    t = pd.DataFrame(np.concatenate(out), columns=["reporters", "share", "shortfall"])
    t["reporters"] = t.reporters.astype(int)
    t[["share", "shortfall"]] *= 100
    return t

def best(t, label, reporters=None):
    s = t if reporters is None else t[t.reporters == reporters]
    if len(s) == 0:
        return dict(selection=label, runs=0)
    r = s.loc[s.shortfall.idxmin()]
    return dict(selection=label, runs=len(s), reporters_of_best=int(r.reporters), shortfall_pct=r.shortfall,
                methods_share_pct=r.share, lowest_share_pct_any_run=s.share.min())

t0 = time.time()

Result: blocked: the 3-reporter value depends on the sparsity, which the paper does not give. On the dandiset's head atlas, the best of 10,000 runs at sparsity 100 has a mean shortfall inside 1.7 +/- 0.17% (1.71 to 1.72% in the runs made for this attempt, keeping 27 to 30 reporters), and the best run keeping exactly 39 reporters is also inside (1.83 to 1.86%); the values printed above are this run's, and they differ in the second decimal between machines with the same seeds. For 3 reporters the best of the 7,500 runs at sparsity 1500 (7,106 of them keep 3 reporters) has 6.95%, below the tolerance of 9.45 to 11.55%. The 3-reporter solutions take few distinct values; 9.90% is their 5th percentile and only 30 runs (0.4%) fall below 9.45%, so the best of 10 runs lies within the tolerance in 95% of random subsets, the best of 100 runs in 66% and the best of 1,000 runs in 1%. On the paper's own adjacency in data.mat (1,000 runs each) the best is 1.74 to 1.81% at sparsity 100 and 8.71% for 3 reporters. The Methods share of neighboring pairs below the margin, a variant, is 13.65% and 20.84% for the two best solutions on the head atlas, far above both expected values.

The criterion reads the sparsity for 3 reporters as the sparsity whose solutions use 3 reporters, which fits 1500, 2000 and 3000 of the scan equally well. optimal_coloring_sparsity.ipynb runs the others. With the criterion's 7,500 runs the best 3-reporter value is 8.38% at 2000, below the tolerance, and 9.90% at 3000, inside it (6,253 runs keep 3 reporters, none below 9.45%); at 1000, where about 56% of runs keep 3 reporters, the best of 1,000 runs is 7.83%. The outcome would be partially reproduced at 1500 and 2000 and reproduced at 3000. No sparsity is better supported than the others, so the claim is blocked (methods_underspecified) with every result shown, as for the other claims of this attempt whose readings disagree. The first record gave partially reproduced at 1500; the change was proposed after verification round 2 and agreed in round 3. Whether the paper's 3-reporter value is the best of 7,500 runs of this one setting, as the criterion reads it, or of the smaller part of its 7,500 simulations that had this setting, is asked of a reader.

Summary¶

Claim Outcome
fig7de-optimal-coloring-violations blocked: the 3-reporter value is below the tolerance at sparsities 1500 and 2000 and inside it at 3000, and the paper does not give the sparsity (head atlas, with the outside reporter table)

How the analysis differs from the paper's: the neighbors come from the head atlas of the dandiset (192 neurons), since its tails are not there, where the paper used 235 head and tail neurons; the solver is a Python port of optimal_color_solver.m with its own random starts; the sparsity for 3 reporters is not given, so 1500, the sparsity of the scan whose runs most often keep 3 reporters, is run here and 1000, 2000 and 3000 in optimal_coloring_sparsity.ipynb; the runs on the paper's own adjacency are 1,000 per setting, not 10,000 and 7,500; sparsity 1000 has 1,000 runs. The solver's results at sparsity 100 differ in the second decimal between machines (the best was 1.71 to 1.72% in the runs made for this attempt), though seeds are fixed.

What was found about the dataset is reported in reproduce_yemini_2021.ipynb; this notebook uses only the dandiset's annotated positions, through the atlas.
