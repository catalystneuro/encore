Reproducing Grosmark & Buzsáki (2016) from dandiset 000044¶

Paper: A. D. Grosmark and G. Buzsáki, "Diversity in neural firing dynamics supports both rigid and learned hippocampal sequences", Science 351:1440 (2016), doi:10.1126/science.aad1935.

Dandiset: DANDI:000044, version 0.261005.2101. The paper cites no version (it predates the dandiset and points to CRCNS). This is the most recent published version. It is also the one that matches the files best: in the two earlier versions (0.210812.1516 and 0.250624.0426) the rate of every position series holds the sampling period (0.0256 s) in place of the rate (39.06 Hz), so the positions would span 880 to 2360 hours in place of the MAZE epoch. The cell below shows this. Otherwise the versions differ only in subject metadata.

Text used: the full main text and the legends of Figures 1 to 4, from the NIH author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC4919122). The supplementary Materials and Methods and figs. S1 to S22 could not be retrieved (PMC and Science serve them only behind a browser check), so every definition the main text does not give is a common choice, fixed in the claims file before the analysis. Neither the paper nor the dandiset links analysis code.

Eight claims rest on the selection of strongly contributing cells (PCC). The test fixed for it before the analysis has a false-positive rate of 56%, far from the 5% that "significant" implies, so these claims are blocked as methods_underspecified; their analyses are still shown, with a best-template variant of the contribution whose false-positive rate (21%) is also far above 5%. fig2d-shuffle-high-dpcc ranks all place cells by dPCC and does not use that selection, so it is tested.

Checks of the description against the data, from the outputs above:

  • Four subjects and eight sessions, each with PRE, MAZE and POST epochs, as the metadata state. Durations are printed; the description's "~4 hour" PRE and POST and "~45 minute" MAZE are compared there.
  • Units are labelled excitatory or inhibitory; pyramidal cells here are the excitatory units. Their hemisphere is in location (lCA1 or rCA1); the electrodes' location is "unknown".
  • Positions are stated in metres. The linearized position spans 0 to 1.6 m on the 1.6 m tracks and 0 to 2 m on the 2 m track, consistent with the description. On the circular track (described as 1 m in diameter, a circumference of 3.14 m) it reaches 2.84 to 2.9 m, consistent with the series description, which has it run from one edge of the reward area to the other. The running speed from the 2D positions agrees with that from the linearized position, so the 2D positions are consistent with metres.
  • The sleep states are consistent with the LFP: the theta/delta ratio is far higher in REM than in non-REM in every session, and the contrast is lost when the labels are shifted by 600 s.
  • Spikes and LFP share a clock: the population bursts in non-REM hold a ripple far more often with the envelope as stored than shifted by 0.5 or 2 s.
  • Spikes and positions share a clock: the decoding error of position is smallest at lag 0.
  • No unit spike train or position series appears in more than one file.
  • In Gatsby_08022013 the POST epoch ends at 30413.6 s, while the LFP, the spikes and the sleep states run to about 32000 s. Firing rates over the session use the LFP duration.
  • On the circular track the linearized position mostly decreases while the animal runs (run_down_frac against run_up_frac), so its single template is built from running toward decreasing position. The description says the position increases clockwise, not which way the animals ran.
  • The description mentions neck EMG and accelerometer signals; the files hold neither, and no raw wide-band data (acquisition is empty). These are not needed here.

Templates, event scores and per-cell contributions¶

For each session: rate maps on running in each direction, place cells, the event scores (rZ) of every candidate event, each active cell's contribution to its events (for PCC), and the same contributions with every rate map shifted (the false-positive rate of the PCC test). Seeds are fixed per session and per step. The contributions are computed twice: as the criterion defines them, on the template the event was scored with, and as a variant on the better of the event's templates (see fig2b-strong-contributors).

fig2b-strong-contributors¶

216 neurons showed significant PCC scores in PRE or POST and were considered to contribute strongly to sequences.

A cell's contribution to an event is |r| minus the mean |r| over 20 decodings with that cell's rate map circularly shifted, with shifts drawn afresh for every event. Strongly contributing in an epoch: one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test of its contributions against 0, p < 0.05, with at least 5 events. The false-positive rate comes from the same test with every rate map shifted.

On the linear tracks the criterion takes |r| on the template the event was scored with, the one of the two with the larger rZ. That |r| was selected for being the larger, so it tends to fall when any part of the template is perturbed, whatever the cell. A variant ("best template") removes this: |r| is the larger over the event's templates both with the original maps and with the cell's map shifted.

def pcc_table(rows, s, place):
    df = pd.DataFrame([(e, ep, c, r0 - np.nanmean(rs)) for e, ep, c, r0, rs in rows],
                      columns=["event", "epoch", "cell", "contrib"])
    out = []
    for j in place:
        rec = dict(session=s["session"], subject=s["subject"], cell=j)
        for e in ("PRE", "POST"):
            v = df.loc[(df.cell == j) & (df.epoch == e), "contrib"].values
            rec[f"n_{e}"] = len(v)
            rec[f"PCC_{e}"] = v.mean() if len(v) else np.nan
            rec[f"p_{e}"] = stats.wilcoxon(v, alternative="greater").pvalue if len(v) >= 5 and np.any(v != 0) else np.nan
        out.append(rec)
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

def strong_cols(df):
    df["strong_PRE"] = df.p_PRE < 0.05

    df["strong"] = df.strong_PRE | df.strong_POST
    df["dPCC"] = df.PCC_POST - df.PCC_PRE
    return df

Re-decoding for the variant templates¶

Within each session: the top or bottom 10, 20, 50 and 75% of place cells by dPCC shuffled (fig2d), plastic or rigid cells removed (fig2e), templates of the low or high-rate half (fig4d) and of the low or high-gain half (fig4e). The rate split is made here with reading (a), a median split within each session; the section rate-split-medians shows both readings. 50 column-cycle shuffles per event here, in place of 200, so the base scores differ a little from those of fig1c. The variants no_plastic_v and no_rigid_v use the best-template classes.

Controls for the selection by dPCC, which is measured on the same PRE and POST events that are decoded again: random{X} shuffles a random X% of the place cells with a dPCC, and no_random removes as many randomly chosen strongly contributing cells as there are plastic cells. A cross-validated control (_cv) ranks and classes cells by the dPCC of the even-numbered candidate events only, and its replay table uses only the odd-numbered events, which played no part in choosing the cells. _best variants rank cells by the best-template dPCC.

Replay is judged, as the criteria fix it, on all events pooled (present); diff_within and p_within are a variant with each session's PRE mean subtracted from its PRE and POST scores. The same table is then given for the five linear-track sessions alone.

def variants_for(i, s, fp):
    df = CELLS[CELLS.session == s["session"]]
    cells = fp["cells"]
    rng = np.random.default_rng(9000 + i)
    def restrict(keep):
        keep = set(int(k) for k in keep)
        return {d: np.array([c for c in cl if c in keep], int) for d, cl in cells.items()}
    def drop(rem):
        rem = set(int(k) for k in rem)
        return {d: np.array([c for c in cl if c not in rem], int) for d, cl in cells.items()}
    v = {"base": (cells, None, 5000 + i)}
    ranked = df[df.dPCC.notna()].sort_values("dPCC")
    n = len(ranked)
    for k, X in enumerate(LEVELS):
        m = int(round(n * X / 100))
        v[f"top{X}"] = (cells, ranked.cell.values[n - m:], 5100 + 10 * k + i)
        v[f"bottom{X}"] = (cells, ranked.cell.values[:m], 5200 + 10 * k + i)
    for k, X in enumerate((10, 20, 75)):
        m = int(round(n * X / 100))
        v[f"random{X}"] = (cells, rng.choice(ranked.cell.values, m, replace=False), 5300 + 10 * k + i)
    ranked_e = df[df.dPCC_even.notna()].sort_values("dPCC_even")
    ranked_b = df[df.dPCC_best.notna()].sort_values("dPCC_best")
    for k, (name, X) in enumerate([("top", 10), ("top", 20), ("bottom", 75)]):
        for tag, rk, base_seed in (("cv", ranked_e, 5400), ("best", ranked_b, 5500)):
            m = int(round(len(rk) * X / 100))
            sel = rk.cell.values[len(rk) - m:] if name == "top" else rk.cell.values[:m]
            v[f"{name}{X}_{tag}"] = (cells, sel, base_seed + 10 * k + i)
    v["no_plastic_cv"] = (drop(df.cell[df.cls_cv == "plastic"]), None, 6500 + i)
    v["no_rigid_cv"] = (drop(df.cell[df.cls_cv == "rigid"]), None, 6600 + i)
    v["no_plastic"] = (drop(df.cell[df.cls == "plastic"]), None, 6000 + i)
    v["no_rigid"] = (drop(df.cell[df.cls == "rigid"]), None, 6100 + i)
    classed = df.cell[df.cls != ""].values
    v["no_random"] = (drop(rng.choice(classed, int((df.cls == "plastic").sum()), replace=False)), None, 6400 + i)
    v["no_plastic_v"] = (drop(df.cell[df.cls_v == "plastic"]), None, 6200 + i)
    v["no_rigid_v"] = (drop(df.cell[df.cls_v == "rigid"]), None, 6300 + i)
    rs = df.rate_sleep.rank(method="first").values
    low = rs <= len(df) // 2
    v["low_rate"] = (restrict(df.cell[low]), None, 7000 + i)
    v["high_rate"] = (restrict(df.cell[~low]), None, 7100 + i)
    gr = df.gain.rank(method="first").values

    v["high_gain"] = (restrict(df.cell[hi]), None, 8000 + i)
    v["low_gain"] = (restrict(df.cell[~hi]), None, 8100 + i)
    return v

def replay(name, keep=None, odd=False):
    R = [r for r, k in zip(VRES, keep if keep is not None else [True] * len(VRES)) if k]
    if odd:   # only the odd-numbered candidate events
        R = [{name: {q: w[r[name]["index"] % 2 == 1] for q, w in r[name].items()}} for r in R]
    pre = np.concatenate([r[name]["rZ"][r[name]["epoch"] == "PRE"] for r in R])
    post = np.concatenate([r[name]["rZ"][r[name]["epoch"] == "POST"] for r in R])
    p = stats.mannwhitneyu(post, pre, alternative="greater").pvalue
    sess = sum(np.nanmean(r[name]["rZ"][r[name]["epoch"] == "POST"]) > np.nanmean(r[name]["rZ"][r[name]["epoch"] == "PRE"])
               for r in R)
    # variant: each session's PRE mean subtracted from its PRE and POST scores
    pre_c = np.concatenate([r[name]["rZ"][r[name]["epoch"] == "PRE"] - r[name]["rZ"][r[name]["epoch"] == "PRE"].mean() for r in R])
    post_c = np.concatenate([r[name]["rZ"][r[name]["epoch"] == "POST"] - r[name]["rZ"][r[name]["epoch"] == "PRE"].mean() for r in R])
    pc = stats.mannwhitneyu(post_c, pre_c, alternative="greater").pvalue
    return dict(variant=name, n_PRE=len(pre), n_POST=len(post), mean_PRE=pre.mean(), mean_POST=post.mean(),

How the analysis differs from the paper's:

  • The supplementary Materials and Methods were not available. Place cells, candidate events (population bursts holding a ripple on one LFP channel), the decoder's details, the rZ shuffle, the PCC and its test, bursting, ripple gain and the field and in-field measures are common choices fixed before the analysis.
  • Immobility for MAZE candidate events is taken from the 2D head speed, running for the rate maps from the speed of the linearized position, both at 5 cm/s.
  • The sequence score is decoded with each direction's template on the linear tracks, the event taking the larger rZ, and with one template on the circular track. The circular sessions score lower in every epoch and supply a larger share of POST than of PRE events, so pooling all events, as the criteria fix it, hides the POST over PRE increase that every session shows and that the same test finds on each kind of track. The within-session variant recovers the paper's pattern for fig2d, fig2e and fig4e, and the linear-track sessions alone for fig2d and fig2e; for fig4d both give the criterion's partial pattern. The outcomes are judged on the pooled test.
  • The PCC test fixed in the criterion, a signed-rank test of a cell's contributions over events, has a false-positive rate of 56%, mostly on the linear tracks, where the contribution is taken on the template the event was selected for. This blocks the eight claims that rest on the selection of strongly contributing cells. A best-template variant of the contribution does better (21%) but is still far from 5%.
  • dPCC and the rigid and plastic classes are measured on the same events that are decoded again for fig2d and fig2e. A cross-validated control (cells chosen on even-numbered events, replay measured on odd-numbered ones) shows that, on the linear tracks, shuffling the top 10 to 20% of cells reduces replay out of sample without removing it, while removing plastic cells still removes it on the linear tracks and within sessions.

What was found about the dandiset:

  • The data hold what the paper's replay analyses need: sorted units with a cell type, positions, epochs, sleep states and the LFP, all on one clock (shown by decoding position, ripple-burst coincidence and the theta/delta ratio of the sleep states). No candidate events, ripples, place cell lists or PCC values are stored, so the paper's selections cannot be checked against it.
  • In versions 0.210812.1516 and 0.250624.0426 the position series store the sampling period (0.0256 s) as their rate; version 0.261005.2101 corrects it.
  • In Gatsby_08022013 the POST epoch ends at 30413.6 s while the spikes, LFP and states run to 32002 s.
  • On the circular track the animals ran toward decreasing linearized position; the description says the position increases clockwise but not which way they ran.
  • The description lists neck EMG and accelerometer signals; the files hold neither.
  • Electrode location is "unknown" for every channel; the units carry the hemisphere (lCA1 or rCA1).
