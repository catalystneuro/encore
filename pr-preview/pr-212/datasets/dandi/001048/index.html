  • fig3-not-heating

    Excitation by pure sine waves was not caused by heating; the adiabatic temperature rise was 2.5 C after 250 ms at 63 V/cm in NaCl, at most 1.24 C for the same stimulus in the low-conductivity solution, where it evoked sustained firing, and 4.1 C at 81 V/cm in NaCl.

    the maximum theoretical temperature rise (assuming no heat dissipation 43 ) was 2.5°C after 250 ms of near-threshold stimulation at 2 kHz, 63 V/cm, in the NaCl-based solution ( Figure 3A ). The same stimulation in the low-conductance solution elicited sustained neuron firing while the temperature rise would not exceed 1.24°C ... 81 V/cm in the NaCl solution, which would heat by 4.1°C in the adiabatic conditions. These observations prove that excitation by pure sine waves was not caused by heating

    Results, Decreased extracellular conductivity lowers excitation thresholds, paragraph 3; Figure 3A

    How it will be judged

    The rise is conductivity times E squared times duration over the volumetric heat capacity (no dissipation, reference 43). The paper does not give the heat capacity or whether E is the peak or the RMS field; readings (a) peak, (b) RMS, each with the heat capacity of water (4.18 J per cm3 per K, a standard choice). From the paper's values neither gives 2.5 C (peak about 3.9 C, RMS about 1.95 C), while the ratios 4.1/2.5 and 1.24/2.5 follow the scaling with E squared and conductivity. Reproduced when all three are within tolerance under a reading; blocked as methods_underspecified when none is but both ratios are within 10%; not reproduced when the ratios fail. That the Figure 3A neuron fired in NaGlu at 63 V/cm (O3) and showed firing in NaCl at 81 V/cm like that in NaGlu at 63 V/cm is reported where the dataset holds it.

  • fig4-orientation-and-poles

  • threshold-depolarization-estimate

    This threshold corresponds to a 0.68% ± 0.11% FluoVolt emission change (11.8 × 57.7/1,000) ... Then, a 0.68% emission change corresponds to a 6.8-mV depolarization at the AP threshold.
