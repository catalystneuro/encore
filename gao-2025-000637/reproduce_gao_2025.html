Gao, Ramachandran, Yu & He (2025) on dandiset 000637¶

Paper. Gao H, Ramachandran S, Yu K, He B (2025). Transcranial focused ultrasound modulates feedforward and feedback cortico-thalamo-cortical pathways by selectively activating excitatory neurons. J Neurosci 45(23):e2218242025, doi:10.1523/jneurosci.2218-24.2025.

Dataset. DANDI:000637, version 0.250829.1249 (doi:10.48324/dandi.000637/0.250829.1249), streamed. The paper cites no version of 000637 (its data statement names a different dandiset, 000946), so the most recent published version is pinned. The five published versions (0.250711.1756 to 0.250829.1249) are compared below: they hold the same files with the same digests, so the choice does not change the data.

Claims file. claims/000637/10.1523-jneurosci.2218-24.2025.yaml, written from the version of record (PMC12139594), which has no supplement and links no code.

What the dataset holds. 292 recordings from 25 rats, each with spike times of sorted units from one 32-channel probe in S1 and the trial start and stop times. The condition is coded only in the file's identifier. There are no spike waveforms, no POm, auditory cortex or CG1-targeted recordings, and of the duty-cycle series only the four conditions whose pulse duration is 100, 200 or 400 us, the three pulse durations the description lists.

Every claim of the paper is about units classified as RSUs or FSUs, and the paper classifies them from waveform shape (Methods, "Neuronal type classification"). Without waveforms none of the claims can be tested as the claims file states them, and most also need POm or AC units or conditions that are not here. All 31 claims are blocked. The notebook shows what the dataset does hold: the conditions, the checks of the metadata against the data, the trial clock, the counts of units under the paper's 2,000 spike rule, a check of the claims file's N2 reading on simulated units, and, as variants that decide no claim, the responses of all S1 units pooled across cell types.

Checks of the description against the data, from the output above:

  • The description says "Each recording has 500 trials"; the counts per file are printed above, and those with fewer than 500 are a metadata error.
  • The description calls 100, 200 and 400 us "ultrasound durations (UDs)" and says "The pulse duration was kept at 67ms". The paper uses the opposite terms (UD 67 ms; PD 200 us in the PRF series). The identifiers carry 67 in the ultrasound-duration place for every stimulated recording, consistent with the paper's terms; the description has them swapped. One recording uses 600 us, which the description does not list.
  • The last field of the identifier is read as the input voltage (5 V in the paper's Methods); recordings at 3.5, 5.5 and 7 V are present and are not described.
  • The units carry no electrode, waveform or quality column, and all electrode locations are "unknown": the units cannot be assigned to S1 layers, to channels, or to cell types.
  • The intertrial intervals are within the paper's 2.25 to 2.75 s, consistent with trial start being the tFUS onset, which the next check tests.
  • No file holds anything beyond the fields listed in the tree above: acquisition, processing, analysis, scratch and stimulus are empty, the Units table has spike_times and its index only (no waveform_mean or other dataset outside colnames), general holds only the institution, the subject and the electrodes, with no experiment_description, notes, stimulus or protocol field, the one electrode group has location "brain area", and every electrode has z = 0. Nothing in any file names a target (S1, POm, CG1), a sham session or a cell type.
  • The voltage field takes only the values printed above (5, 7, 35, 55), so reading a two-digit field as tenths of a volt affects only 35 and 55 (3.5 and 5.5 V).

Trial clock¶

The files do not say that a trial's start is the tFUS onset. For each unit with more than 2,000 spikes in a stimulated recording at 1500, 3000 or 4500 Hz (where the paper reports responses), the spike counts in the 67 ms windows after the trial start are compared with the 67 ms before it, at the true trial starts and at starts shifted by 1.2 s (half an intersonication interval). A response that appears at the true starts and not at the shifted ones shows the trials and spikes share a clock and that the start is the onset to within the window.

Result: trial clock. At the true trial starts the response is a clear contrast with the shifted starts (table above), so the trial start is taken as the tFUS onset. The shifted onsets are not a null, though: with onsets moved by 1.2 s every window is below the shifted pre-window. The time course over the whole trial (PSTH above) shows why: after the onset the rate rises in 0 to 67 ms (143% of the unit mean), dips at 67 to 134 ms, peaks again at 201 to 268 ms (147%) and then decays slowly, reaching the unit mean only about 1 s after the onset and settling at 91 to 94% from about 1.3 s to the next trial. The shifted pre-window (1,133 to 1,200 ms) falls on the end of that decay (about 96%), and the shifted windows (1,200 to 1,468 ms) a little later (93 to 95%), so the shifted windows sit below their pre-window because the post-stimulus elevation lasts more than a second. This is a slow after-effect of the stimulus, not a sign that trial start and onset are misaligned: the rise at 0 to 67 ms is confined to the first bin after the trial start. The 1.2 s shift is therefore a contrast, not a null with no stimulus effect; the conclusion that trial start is the tFUS onset rests on the contrast, which holds in every window (pooled over units) and, at 201 to 268 ms, the one window compared per rat, in 23 of 25 rats.

Recordings deposited more than once¶

Each unit is fingerprinted by its spike count and its first 20 spike times (to 0.1 ms). A fingerprint shared by units in two files, or two units of one file, would be a recording deposited twice.

def window_tests(df, label):
    rows = []
    for w in ["0-67", "67-134", "134-201", "201-268"]:
        x, b = df[f"rate_{w}"], df["rate_pre"]
        r = dict(set=label, window=w, n=len(df), mean_N1=df[f"n1_{w}"].mean(), sem_N1=df[f"n1_{w}"].sem(),
                 mean_N2=df[f"n2_{w}"].mean())
        for tail, alt in [("up", "greater"), ("down", "less")]:
            r[f"W2_{tail}_p"] = min(1.0, 4 * stats.wilcoxon(x, b, alternative=alt).pvalue)
            r[f"W1_{tail}_p"] = min(1.0, 4 * stats.mannwhitneyu(x, b, alternative=alt).pvalue)
        rows.append(r)
    return rows

N2 is strongly biased, and the sign of the bias depends on the firing rate: 18.8% at 3 Hz, 56 to 58% at 10 Hz, 116% at 30 Hz and 121% at 100 Hz. A per-trial ratio with a pre-stimulus count of 0 to a few spikes in 67 ms is dominated by trials where the window count is 0 (pulling the mean down) or where the pre-stimulus count is 1 (pulling it up). The dataset's S1 units, whose median pre-stimulus rate is 12 Hz, have mean N2 between 60 and 86% in every window but one (108% at 1500 Hz, 0 to 67 ms, where mean N1 is 231%), including the BASELINE recordings (59 to 64%) and windows where N1 is above 200%. Under reading R2 (normalized rates tested against 100%), N2 would report decreases in units with no response and mask increases, so a claim tested on it could be blocked or not reproduced for an artefactual reason. No claim is tested here, so this changes no outcome; it is asked of a reader in questions/000637.yaml.

Every claim is about RSUs and FSUs, which the paper separates by waveform shape; the dataset holds spike times only. Most claims also need POm or auditory cortex units, CG1-targeted or sham recordings, or duty cycles that are not in the dataset (the condition table above). The paper's own data statement names a different dandiset, 000946, whose subjects (20 rats, BH494 to BH594, unit-counts section) are not those of 000637 (BH401 to BH477). No recording of this dataset could be linked to a recording the paper analysed, and the dataset has about 3.6 times as many S1 units over 2,000 spikes in the PRF series at 200 us as the paper's S1 RSUs and FSUs together (4,642 against 1,297; unit-counts section), so these are not, or not only, the units the paper analysed.

What was found about the dataset:

  • The units have no waveforms, electrodes or quality metrics, and all electrode locations are "unknown". The full HDF5 tree of every file was listed: nothing outside spike times, trial times, the electrode table and the subject is stored, and no field names a target, a sham session or a cell type.
  • Trials per recording differ from the 500 the description states (counts in the data check).
  • The description swaps pulse duration and ultrasound duration, and does not list the 600 us recording or the 3.5, 5.5 and 7 V recordings; conditions are coded only in the identifier.
  • Three recordings named BASELINE are not described.
  • Trial starts share a clock with the spikes and behave as tFUS onsets: at 1500 to 4500 Hz, 74% of units fire more in 0 to 67 ms than in the 67 ms before, against 43% with onsets shifted by 1.2 s, and the delayed response is larger at the true onsets in 23 of 25 rats (trial clock section). The elevated rate after the onset lasts about 1 s, so the shifted onsets are a contrast, not a null.
  • Pooling all S1 units (a variant, not a claim), every PRF at 200 us raises the 0 to 67 ms rate over baseline under W2, and 300 to 4500 Hz raise the 201 to 268 ms rate; 30 Hz does not raise it.
  • The three BASELINE recordings (119 units over the threshold, against the paper's 84 S1 units in sham) show an increase over the pre-trial window at 134 to 268 ms under W2 (corrected p 6e-09 and 1.2e-05) and not under W1, of about 5% of the unit mean, a size their trace also reaches at times with no trial event. Nothing says they are sham sessions; if they were, the W2 result would weigh against fig5-sham-no-response.
  • No unit was found deposited twice.
