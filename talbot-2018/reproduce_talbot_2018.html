Talbot et al. (2018) on CRCNS hc-16: single cells, coordination and frame-specific firing¶

Paper: Talbot, Sparks, Dvorak, Curran, Alarcon and Fenton (2018), "Normal CA1 Place Fields but Discoordinated Network Discharge in a Fmr1-Null Mouse Model of Fragile X Syndrome", Neuron 97:684-697, doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2017.12.043.

Dataset: CRCNS hc-16 (Sparks, Talbot, Dvorak and Fenton), doi:10.6080/K01V5C44, read from the public bucket crcnsarchive at hc-16/live/data.zip, streamed with fsspec and zipfile.

Version: CRCNS data sets are not versioned, so the version is recorded as unversioned. The paper cites the data set's DOI only (Key Resources Table), and the bucket holds one copy of the data, under live/ (there is no old/), so that copy is the one read. The cell below reads the bucket's checksums.md5; the file read is data.zip, MD5 45b534dd6dbeb94db539bc429223eecb (data descriptor version 0.6, April 26, 2018). A later change of the zip would change that sum.

Population used throughout: the data set has no isolation measures (IsoIBG, IsoINN) and no waveforms, so the paper's well-isolated cells cannot be selected. Every cluster other than cluster 0 of a tetrode is used. This is the largest difference from the paper's method and bears on every tested claim. Two variants bear on it: a refractory filter (clusters with at most 0.5% of interspike intervals below 2 ms), a proxy for isolation, and single-cell measures on the code's 32 x 32 grid, which needs no spatial scale.

Checks of the data against the description, and of the files against each other:

  • The descriptor gives x and y in 0-255 pixels and times in 100 us units, but no spatial scale. The scale is taken from the extent of the tracked room-frame positions (0.1 to 99.9 percentiles) and the enclosure sizes of the STAR Methods (large box 50 cm, arena 40 cm). This is an assumption; the printed px/cm differ between box sessions (about 3.6 and 4.4), which fits two camera set-ups and is consistent with, not proof of, the assumed sizes.
  • The TS headers hold the sampling rate, the first timestamp and a track-to-map scale of 1.00; no isolation measure and no spatial scale in cm (printed above).
  • The descriptor's example header has a sampling interval of 58.3 samples/s; every file here has 30.0, matching the 30 frames/s of the STAR Methods.
  • The header's sampling rate and first timestamp do not give the times of the position samples: spikes lie up to 118 samples (about 4 s) after the nominal time t0 + k/rate of the sample they are filed under, as the rate drifts against the spike clock and frames are dropped. The helper estimates each sample's time from the spikes filed under it; after that the spikes lie 0 to 1 sample after their sample's time, as they should. Six spike trains have one backward step in their times (printed above).
  • The next cells check that the .times files repeat the spike times of the TS files, that the _0-15min and _15-30min files are parts of the full sessions (one position sample at the split is in neither part, so a spike filed under it is lost), that the arena-frame files of stationary sessions repeat the room frame, and whether any recording is deposited twice.

def info_shifted(s, shift_s):
    idx, shape = hc16.pixel_index(s, "Rm")
    occ = hc16.occupancy(s, idx, shape)
    k = int(round(shift_s * s.rate))
    out = []
    for u in s.cells:
        n = s.counts[u]
        s.counts[u] = np.roll(n, k)
        rm, o = hc16.rate_map(s, u, idx, shape, occ)
        s.counts[u] = n
        out.append(hc16.information(rm, o))
    return np.array(out)

Cell table and classification¶

One row per cluster and session, with the single-cell measures on unsmoothed 1.25 cm room-frame maps. Classes (STAR Methods, "Classification of Cell Types"; the waveform criterion cannot be applied): interneuron, rate above 2 AP/s; pyramidal, rate below 2 AP/s with an ISI histogram peak below 10 ms; place cell, a pyramidal cell with rate 0.1-2 AP/s, coherence above 0.4 and information above 0.4 bits/spike ("Characterization of Place Cells"). Clusters below 2 AP/s without bursts are left unclassified. A variant drops the burst criterion (rate alone).

"Standard recordings" (the session a cell is classified in) is open in the paper; the claims file names two readings. R1: each cell once, from its first stationary-environment session (the large box, or the first stationary arena session of a day). R2: every stationary session (large box, stationary arena 1 and 2) on its own, a cell counted per session. Sessions of a day share a cluster cutting file (the cl... part of the name), so a cluster name within a cut is taken to be the same cell across that day's sessions (an assumption: the files carry no other identifier).

Passes and overdispersion¶

STAR Methods, "Overdispersion": for each 5 s interval, exp is the sum over position samples of the time spent times the session's time-averaged rate at that pixel, z = (obs - exp)/sqrt(exp), and the interval is a pass when exp is at least the cell's average rate. The claims file reads this literally, a count against a rate in AP/s; the count expected at the average rate over the 5 s (exp at least the average rate x 5 s) is a variant, and which is meant is asked of a reader (questions/crcns-hc-16.yaml). Intervals are consecutive from the session start (the claims file's reading); a 1 s sliding step is a variant. Place cells of the stationary sessions (large box, stationary arena 1 and 2), each session on its own (R2); R1 (each cell once, from its first session) is a variant.

def od_passes(zz, label):
    a = zz[zz.genotype == "WT"].z.to_numpy(); b = zz[zz.genotype == "Fmr1-null"].z.to_numpy()
    F = a.var(ddof=1) / b.var(ddof=1)
    p_one = stats.f.sf(F, len(a) - 1, len(b) - 1)
    lev = stats.levene(a, b, center="mean")

How the analysis differs from the paper's:

  • The paper's cells are units with IsoIBG and IsoINN of at least 4 bits. The data set has no isolation measures and no waveforms, so every sorted cluster other than cluster 0 is used, and cells are classified by rate and bursting without waveform width. Table S2 lists fewer cells per session than the data set has clusters (for example 15 cells for M16 in stationary arena 1, against 50 clusters here), so the population here includes clusters the paper excluded. This bears on every tested claim. A refractory filter, a weak proxy for isolation, removes 70 of 774 clusters. Among the single-cell claims it removes the rate effect of figs3-nonspatial-properties-similar and, in R1, the significance of the interneuron difference, and leaves the other results as they are. Run on the pass and Kendall claims (R2), it leaves their results as they are. It was not run on fig4d-network-state-recurrence, kendall-quiet-states, locomotion-similar or the rotating arena claims.
  • The data set holds 8 WT and 7 Fmr1-null mice, the large box and the stationary and rotating arena. The small box, the ring, sleep recordings, LFP, slice data and avoidance sessions are not in it.
  • The spatial scale is assumed from the tracked extent and the enclosure sizes of the Methods. On the code's 32 x 32 grid, which needs no scale, 45 of 774 clusters change class, and the outcome of no single-cell claim changes. On the grid, under the literal pass rule, the results of the pass and Kendall claims (R2) are as on 1.25 cm pixels; under the count rule fig4b-covariance-40-passes meets its criterion. The grid was not run on fig4d-network-state-recurrence, kendall-quiet-states, locomotion-similar or the rotating arena claims (there Ipos is computed on the 32 x 32 grid, but the place cells are classified on 1.25 cm pixels).
  • The pass rule of the overdispersion claims is read literally, as the claims file does; it passes most 5 s intervals. The count expected at the mean rate over 5 s, a variant, changes the overdispersion-passes result in R2 (not in R1, and not overdispersion-cells), and with the 32 x 32 grid the fig4b-covariance-40-passes result; it is asked of a reader.
  • The session that classifies a cell is open (R1, R2; A, B for the rotating arena); the results under each are printed. A cluster name within a day's cut file is taken to be one cell. R2 counts the cells and pairs of stationary arena 1 and 2 of a day twice; R1 counts them once.

What was found about the data set:

  • It holds part of the paper's data: 15 of 21 mice, three of the four environments, no LFP, no sleep or rest recordings, no avoidance sessions, and no slice recordings.
  • No isolation measures, waveforms or cell classes are given, so the paper's cells cannot be selected; the files hold every cluster, including cluster 0 (unsorted spikes). The TS headers hold only the sampling rate, the first timestamp and a track-to-map scale of 1.00.
  • The descriptor gives no spatial scale for the 0-255 pixel positions, and the scale differs between box sessions (about 3.6 and 4.4 px/cm by extent).
  • The stated position sampling rate drifts against the spike clock (spikes up to 118 samples, about 4 s, after the nominal time of their position sample); spikes are filed under the right sample, so the files are consistent, but the nominal sample times are not usable as times.
  • Six spike trains have one backward step in their times (up to 1.4 s).
  • The 0-15 and 15-30 min files repeat the full sessions except one position sample at the split, which is in neither part, so a spike filed under it is lost; the .times files repeat the TS spike times.
  • The descriptor's example header gives 58.3 samples/s; every file has 30.0.
