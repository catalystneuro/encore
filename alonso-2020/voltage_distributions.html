Alonso & Marder (2020), membrane potential distributions, from Dryad m31b1h7¶

Paper: Alonso LM, Marder E (2020), eLife 9:e55470, doi:10.7554/eLife.55470; claims in claims/alonso-2020.yaml (version of record, main text, legends and Methods).

Dataset: Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.m31b1h7, version id 94811 (the only published version, so the one pinned), file alonso_marder_tempcomp_2019_root.zip (MD5 from Dryad's file list checked on load; SHA-256 printed below). The models are simulated with the port in pyloric.py, compared with the deposited Java in reproduce_alonso_2020.ipynb.

Method (Materials and methods, Membrane potential and current shares distributions): each temperature simulated from the deposited initial conditions for 30 s at dt = 0.001 ms; the last 10 s used; 5 x 10^5 samples per distribution, so V is kept every 20 steps (0.02 ms). Histograms in 0.1 mV bins (a plotting choice).

The Methods use 101 temperatures. At dt = 0.001 ms one temperature costs about 1500 simulated seconds at dt = 0.05 (50 times the steps), about 23 s of the runner's 4 CPUs, so 101 temperatures would take about 40 min. This notebook uses 26 temperatures, 10 to 25 degrees C in steps of 0.6 degrees C, for G7P2WE, and 6 (10, 13, 16, 19, 22, 25 degrees C, all on that grid) for 4X6W66 and MAXTTP.

fig2-lp-spike-amplitude-threshold¶

In the LP cell of the model of Figure 2, spike amplitude decreases and the spike threshold depolarizes as temperature rises from 10 to 25 degrees C.

G7P2WE, LP. Amplitude readings: (a) mean spike peak minus the mean trough before each spike (pyloric.spike_shapes); (b) the upper envelope, the highest V in the distribution. Threshold reading (a): V where dV/dt first exceeds 10 mV/ms after the trough before each spike. Threshold reading (b), where the distribution's spike region begins: neither the paper nor the claims file says how that point is located, so it is read as the upper edge of the densely occupied part of the distribution (pyloric.spike_region_start): the highest voltage below the -20 mV spike detection level whose occupancy, smoothed over 1 mV, is at least a fraction of the largest occupancy. Above it the cell passes only during spikes. The fraction is a choice; 2%, 5% and 10% are shown, 5% as the main one (an assumption).

Result: reproduced (resting on the assumed reading of where the distribution's spike region begins). In G7P2WE's LP cell the spike amplitude falls from 10 to 25 C under both readings (peak minus trough 56.9 to 26.2 mV; upper envelope 16.7 to -1.8 mV; Spearman -1.000 each). The threshold under reading (a) does not depolarize steadily (-27.8 to -27.5 mV, Spearman -0.197). Under reading (b) it rises: the upper edge of the densely occupied part of the distribution goes from -36.1 to -26.5 mV at 5% of the largest occupancy (Spearman 0.997), from -38.2 to -29.1 mV at 10% (0.996) and from -33.0 to -20.0 mV at 2% (0.996; at the -20 mV search limit at 2 of 26 temperatures). 26 of the Methods' 101 temperatures were run. The outcome rests on the rule for reading (b): LP fires 114 spikes in the analysed 10 s at 10 C and 433 at 25 C, and more spikes put more occupancy just above the densely occupied part of the distribution, which could raise this edge with no change in the per-spike threshold, which reading (a) finds flat.

The effect of temperature on the membrane potential differs across models; in three example models the peak voltage of spikes decreases, increases, or stays relatively constant with temperature, and cells can become more depolarized or more hyperpolarized.

G7P2WE (left), 4X6W66 (middle, 4?6W66 of the Methods) and MAXTTP (right); upper envelope (highest V) of every cell at 10 and 25 degrees C. Decrease or increase: a change of more than 5 mV in that direction in any cell; constant: within 5 mV in every cell (2 and 10 mV variants).
