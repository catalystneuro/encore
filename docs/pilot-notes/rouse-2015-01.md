# rouse-2015, Rouse et al. 2015

No claim reproduced, none partially, none not reproduced and 26 blocked, 25
for missing data and `table2-movement-time` as `metadata_insufficient`. The
first verification round agreed with 25 of the 26 outcomes as they stood and
the second with all 26; nothing was proposed after the second, so there was
no third round. It pins version 0.241029.1405, the only published version;
the paper cites none. The paper, Rouse and Schieber, "Spatiotemporal
distribution of location and object effects in reach-to-grasp kinematics"
(J Neurophysiol 114:3268-3282), tracks 36 markers on the arm and hand of
three monkeys (L, X and Y) reaching to and grasping four objects at eight
locations, computes 22 joint angles, and reports that location effects are
largest in the shoulder and wrist and object effects in the digits, that both
appear early and evolve concurrently, that object effects come to dominate
late in the reach, and that location and object can be decoded from groups of
angles. The claims were written from the version of record, which has no
supplement and links no code; the claims check corrected the statements or
criteria of ten claims and added `fig8-hand-shape-invariant-to-location` for
the abstract's finding on hand shape, for 26 claims, and left open the
Kruskal-Wallis bound, garbled in the extracted text and read as P < 0.001. Only three
claims could be run in part, the trial counts, the movement time and the 28
coaxial cylinder trials at 45 degrees; the other 23 need joint angles or
markers. The author's first run gave none reproduced, one not reproduced and
25 blocked: the median movement time of monkey L was 200 ms against 255 ms.
Round 1 found that the notebook took the dataset's `move_onset_time` for the
paper's onset without checking it, and asked for per-session movement times
next to the authors' 2016 EMG paper, cue to onset next to the J Neurosci 2016
value and EMG aligned on the onset; in reply these were added and the claim
moved to blocked as `metadata_insufficient`. Round 2 agreed with every outcome
and found sentences the outputs did not support (the onset called shown,
where the evidence supports very likely, the later event named as the paper's
own definition of onset, the first 53 minutes, eleven claims for fourteen and
a statement on dandiset 001209), which were corrected. The author asked a
reader nothing, so the answer and author-answers runs changed nothing. The
author's run took about 13 minutes (18:51 to 19:04 UTC); the notebook ran in
32 s at first and 69 s at the end.

The dandiset, released as the FALCON benchmark M1-A, holds no kinematic data:
no file holds a marker position or joint angle, only trial tables, 16 EMG
channels at 50 Hz, an evaluation mask and multiunit spike times, so 23 of the
26 claims cannot be tested. Only monkey L is in it; the description points to
dandiset 001209 for monkey X, a draft with no published version, which was
not used. Session L20120928 is there only in part: its held-in-calib file
holds 412 trials, all rewarded, numbered 201 to 673 and spanning 53 of the
session's 86 minutes, where the paper reports 768 trials and 717 successes,
with 61 numbers absent and nothing saying why. The event times are not
defined and lie on a 20 ms grid, and `move_onset_time` is very likely not the
paper's movement onset: in all four monkey L sessions the median movement
time (180 to 200 ms) is 52 to 58 ms shorter than the authors' own medians
(238 to 255 ms), the median cue to onset (300 to 320 ms) is longer than the
243 ms the authors give, and cue to contact agrees, as the task software's
center-release event marker would. `reward_time` follows contact by a median
of 0.10 s, where the task requires a 1,000 ms hold, and `start_time` and
`stop_time` are fixed windows 1 s around the cue and the reward. `tgt_obj`
does not name the grasped object: it runs in three blocks of 118, 159 and 135
trials, and EMG around contact decodes `obj_id` at 0.99 against 0.42 for
`tgt_obj`. The `obj_id` codes have no names; from the Fig. 1 legend codes 2,
1, 4 and 3 were named the perpendicular cylinder, coaxial cylinder, button and
sphere. In each held-in-calib file one trial is numbered 127 where 255 or 256
is expected, and in session 20120927 a second trial also carries 127. Each
held-in-minival file repeats the first two trials of its held-in-calib file,
with the EMG rescaled by a factor of 0.955 to 1.016. No questions are put to
the paper's authors.

The author's points on the protocol: it does not say whether a claim on three
monkeys is blocked or judged on the one present, and the movement time was
judged on L while the counts were blocked; it does not say whether counts on
part of a session are blocked or not reproduced, and blocked was chosen;
`with_labels` is defined for outside sources, and naming codes from the
paper's own figure was treated as a method detail; step 1.2 speaks of
identical recordings, and the copies with rescaled EMG were reported as
duplicates; the clock check is written for every stream, and EMG and spikes
were checked though no claim uses them; and it does not say whether blocked
claims get notebook cells, which the two count claims were given.
