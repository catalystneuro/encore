# 000223, Xue et al. 2022

Five claims reproduced, seven partially, one not reproduced and none blocked.
It pins version 0.260528.0906; the paper cites the dandiset without a version,
and its two published versions, 0.220823.0826 and 0.260528.0906, list the same
20 assets with the same blob ids. The paper, Xue, Buccino, Saseendran Kumar,
Hierlemann and Bartram, infers monosynaptic connections in rat cortical
cultures by imaging calcium in a dendritic spine of a target cell while
recording extracellular spiking on a high-density microelectrode array
(HD-MEA): sorted units are ranked by the Pearson R of their convolved spike
trains with the spine trace, and a connection is accepted by thresholds taken
from simulated surrogates. The claims were written from the accepted author
manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC7613561) with its one supplement, not the
version of record, and from the paper's code (starquakes/mea_spineca_mapping
at commit 99c2a47). All 13 claims could be tested: the data scale, the best-R
values of Table 1, the surrogate F-scores and optimal thresholds, the
acceptance of 9 of 20 spines and the comparisons that follow from it, the
spike contrast and OUT/IN network measures, and their correlations with
F-score in Figure 5. `table1-network-measures` is not reproduced, as spike
contrast matches for all 19 recordings but OUT/IN for at most 6 of 19, against
the 10 needed. The acceptance test gives 7 spines (18 of 20 decisions agree),
which carries into `best-r-accepted-vs-rejected` (U = 5, not within 12 ± 1.2)
and `best-r-not-predictive` (smallest accepted best-R 0.515, not about 0.2);
`table1-optimal-thresholds` matches max F for 15 of 19 recordings under the
Methods' classification and 17 under the code's, with optimal quantiles that
do not match; `mean-max-fscore` has an SD of 0.121, just below the 0.1224
bound; and `fig5d-out-in-vs-fscore` gives R = 0.585, below the bound of 0.605,
resting on the assumption that the noise region is the background square
nearest the spine, as region 1 gives 0.615. The p_thresh carried forward for
each recording is the one whose best-R is nearest Table 1, by the claims
file's rule, so a best-R match is a weak test and the later outcomes are
conditional on that choice. The author asked a reader no questions, so the
answer run and the following author run changed nothing. Round 1 agreed with
12 of 13 outcomes; it would have called fig5d reproduced, as region 1 and the
code's classification fall inside the tolerance, and the author kept partially
reproduced and recorded the disagreement. Its points led to the share of
misidentified surrogates reported per recording, the effect of the empty units
on max F measured, a count of the bursts the code's merging pass drops with a
variant that merges every chain, the second-best units and the R distributions
of rows 3 and 16 printed, the elephant version printed and a figure of the
background squares; the author's round-1 report gives totals of 6, 6 and 1,
counting fig5d as the verifier did. Round 2 agreed with all 13 outcomes but
found that the notebook, re-executed on another machine, printed numbers
different from the ones the text was written from, and asked for the fig5d
assumption to be removed, as on its numbers no region fell inside the
tolerance. The author traced the drift to burst edges set where lowess-smoothed
counts equal the integer baseline within 1e-9, rounded the counts to 9
decimals and regenerated every number from the new run; `table1-best-r` went
from 18 to 17 of 20 matches and lost its assumption, as swapping the tied rows
gives 16, 17 and 16, and region 1 was again inside the tolerance for fig5d, so
its assumption was kept and the disagreement recorded. As round 2 agreed with
every outcome, there was no third round, and no verifier read the numbers
after the fix. The author's run went from 03:27 to 04:45 UTC and nothing failed
in the final run; during development presigned S3 HEAD requests returned 403,
and the helper now uses the asset's public S3 URL. The notebook ran in 18.4
minutes at first and 19.3 minutes in the last run.

The background ROIs the paper used to estimate the recording noise for its
surrogates are not in the files, which hold only the spine and shaft ROIs, so
the noise estimate, and with it the acceptance thresholds and F-scores, cannot
be repeated exactly; three 6 by 6 pixel squares were placed near each spine by
a rule fixed before any R was computed, and in row 10 two of them sit on bright
processes (noise SD 126 and 96, against 39). Nothing in the files or the
dandiset metadata links a file to its row or culture in Table 1; subjects and
files can be matched by unit count except for two pairs with equal counts (the
two sub-3112 recordings, rows 14 and 15; sub-2748 and sub-2765, rows 16 and
20), and the file with two spines does not say which ROI pair is spine 9. Two
of the 20 files, sub-3112/sub-3112_ecephys+ophys.nwb and
sub-3112/sub-3112_ses-20210227T190233_ecephys+ophys.nwb, share one blob, so the
dandiset holds 19 recordings, as the paper reports. The ROIs are not labelled
as spine or shaft, nor paired, and their roles were inferred from the order the
paper's code uses and from their sizes; the stored `Dff` series has no
description and differs from the paper's dF/F by up to 0.23. The imaging plane
gives the indicator as "unknown" where the paper used jGCaMP7b, and the image
series has no description and the unit "n.a.". Two files (sub-2748 and
sub-3278) each hold one unit with no spikes, and without them the unit counts
equal Table 1's 58 and 100. The shaft trace lines up with the population spike
rate at zero lag in 19 of 20 spines and at one frame in the last. Methods 2.6
says bursts closer than 75 ms were merged into one, while the code's single
merging pass drops every burst after the second in such a chain, which here
drops bursts in 7 of 19 recordings, 18 in one of them. Four questions are put
to the paper's authors: which p_thresh and hyperactive-unit strategy each
recording used, as the code's default pair gives 14 of 20 best-R values within
10% of Table 1; where each spine's background ROI was and whether one noise
estimate was used per recording, as Table 1 gives spines 9 and 10 identical
thresholds; how OUT/IN was computed, as with any p_thresh from 96 to 99 it
matches for 10 of 19 recordings at best and for row 8 is at least 0.139
against 0.060; and what share of the ground-truth surrogates had a best unit
other than their own, which Methods 2.8 calls rare and which here is 6% to 44%
per recording.

The author's points on the protocol: it does not say whether a claims file's
main reading or any reading decides an outcome, and the main (Methods) reading
was let decide; it does not say how a choice made for one claim, such as the
noise region, carries into claims that depend on it; link claims do not cover
matching dataset files to the paper's own table, which here made the
spike-contrast check circular for four rows; it does not say how to judge a
match when the claims file's rule tunes a free parameter towards the values
being tested; the notebook's 18 minutes exceed the 10-minute aim; and the
elephant version was checked outside the notebook.
