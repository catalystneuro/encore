# huszar-2022, Huszar et al. 2022, second attempt

Seventeen claims reproduced, ten partially, seven not reproduced and ten
blocked, five for missing data and five as `too_large`; twelve outcomes rest
on an assumption. The first verification round agreed with 39 of the 44
outcomes; it disagreed on `sessions-durations`, which it read as partially
reproduced, and on the blocks of ed3, ed5, ed7 and ed10, which it found
partly testable. The second round agreed with 38 and disagreed on
`sessions-durations`, on ed10, whose dependency on the blocked
`ed2-waveform-validation` its criterion does not use, and on fig3c, fig3e,
ed6 and fig4d, which it called not attempted, not too large; nothing was
proposed after the second round, so there was no third. The attempt pins
version 0.261113.1035, the latest published version; the paper cites none.
The paper, Huszar, Zhang, Blockus and Buzsaki, "Preconfigured dynamics in
the hippocampus are guided by embryonic birthdate and rate of neurogenesis"
(Nature Neuroscience 25, 1201-1212), records CA1 units with silicon probes
in 17 mice whose pyramidal cells were labelled by in utero electroporation
at E13.5 to E16.5, and reports that a cell's birthdate shapes its firing,
bursting, ripple participation, theta modulation and spatial coding, that
pairs born on the same day (SBD) cofire more in ripples and theta cycles,
share place fields, remap together and converge on the same interneurons
more than pairs born on different days (DBD), and that assemblies and a
network model follow from it. The claims were written by a reader without
the data, from the author manuscript (PMC10807234) with its two supplements
and the code at commit 51320d4, which lacks the theta detectors, the
monosynaptic connection detector and the rate map builder; the claims check
made 9 corrections and added `ed10-waveform-matched-controls`, for 44
claims. Reproduced are the cohort and every row of Supplementary Table 1,
the maze and novel cohorts, the SPW-R duration, the unit and cell type counts (9,472 units, 7,411 pyramidal cells, 664
light-responsive), the labelled fraction, firing rate, SPW-R rate and
participation and wake theta depth by birthdate, SBD synchrony in 10 of 13
animals, SPW-R cofiring, the familiar and novel remapping together, and the
theta rate and lower novel rate of Figure 6. Not reproduced are
`sessions-durations` (two sessions hold no sleep), the REM shift of Figure 1f
(slope -1.36% per day, not significant), the depth control of Extended Data
Fig. 3, `maze-task`, the higher novel ratemap correlation of Figure 6c, the
gamma peak of spike transmission (29 to 42 ms) and the connection counts of
Figure 7f-g. The histology claims, the light-evoked waveform check and the
L-ratio controls are blocked for missing data, and the assembly claims and
the network model were not run: the first run reached its time limit before
the assembly analysis was built. The author's first run gave 18 reproduced,
9 partially, 6 not reproduced and 11 blocked. Round 1 found that the theta
channel broke the Methods' rule in 62 of 104 sessions and that its sharp
wave was taken without the conversion to microvolts, that the open fields
and one linear track were left out of the novel analyses, that only alpha of
the connection detector was varied, and that ed3, ed5, ed7 and ed10 were
partly testable; in reply the sharp wave was measured against a pre-ripple
baseline, the open fields were binned at an assumed 1 cm per lab unit with
other scales as variants, every assumed detail of the detector was varied
(38,402 to 50,756 connections against 31,932), ed3 was tested and moved to
not reproduced, the computable parts of the others were run for the record,
and `fig2c-theta-cofiring` moved to partially reproduced. Round 2 found 6
sessions counted as following the theta rule whose channel lay below the
layer centre, which left 72 that follow it, and sentences the outputs did
not support, all corrected, and asked for printed evidence of the open-field
scale, which was added. While choosing the version the author read the
release notes, which say the conversion fixes problems found by a
reproduction attempt; it looked no further, and the reconciliation judged
the attempt blind. Against `huszar-2022-01`, 22 outcomes changed, 21 of them
because the new version holds what the earlier one lacked and
`sessions-durations` because the criterion changed; of the earlier attempt's
15 findings, 10 are fixed, one partly fixed and four still present. The
author's run took about 4 h 10 min (04:31 to 08:39 UTC) and nothing failed;
the notebook ran in about 36 min at first, with peak memory 1.05 GB, 49.7
min after round 1 and 47.6 min at the end.

The dataset was converted by CatalystNeuro (contributors Garrett Flynn and
Cody Baker). It gives the paper's counts exactly: the mice with sex and
birthdate, every row of Supplementary Table 1, the cell types, the 664
light-responsive cells, the pair counts and the 184,230 possible
pyramidal-interneuron pairs. The wide-band signal around the light pulses is
in 11 raw files, 5 of them homecage-mouse sessions, so the light-evoked
waveforms cannot be formed; the units table has no L-ratio or other
isolation metric, so the paper's L-ratio controls cannot be repeated; and no
histology is deposited. Positions outside the familiar figure-eight are in
the lab's units with no scale, and the open fields, 7 of the 16 novel
explorations, have only 2D position, so their 1 cm bins rest on an assumed
scale and the Figure 6c outcome depends on it (at 2 or 0.5 cm per unit it
would be partially reproduced). Sessions e13_1f1_200305 and e13_1f1_200307
hold only wake in their sleep states, one Awake row each over the whole
recording, while the paper says every recording included sleep. In
e16_3m2_211211 the position series named for the linear_manu subsession lies
inside the openField_thomas epoch, and both were left out. In 25 files the
electrodes carry probe_side Front or Back with shanks 1 to 4 on each,
without saying whether these are two probes or two faces of one; only the
reading as faces gives the paper's pair counts. The two E13.5 mice the
paper leaves out for having a single light-responsive neuron per session
have 1 and 2 same-birthdate pairs in the files, and 27 of the 57 familiar
figure-eight sessions hold fewer than ten trials of one arm, where the
Methods say every session had at least ten. In the paper, the Methods'
92 sessions appear to be a miscount of the 96 the files hold, with the
paper's exact duration range; the Figure 1f legend's n exceed the REM-locked
counts of Figure 2d; and one of the three mice of the "separate" novel
cohort, e13_26m1, is also among the nine familiar-maze mice. The paper does
not say how the theta channel was chosen, what the monosynaptic detector's
lags, hollow fraction and correction were, or how the burst index ACG was
built, and the outcomes of Figures 1h, 2c and 7 turn on these. Questions put
to a reader ask about the `sessions-durations` criterion's missing case, the
Figure 1f legend's n and whether ed10 should depend on ed2.

The author's points on the protocol: the per-mouse counts of
`st1-animal-sessions` are listed in the table's row order while its mice
are named in birthdate order, and the row order was read from the table
image; the `sessions-durations` criterion has no partial outcome for all
but the sleep condition holding, and it was applied literally; no
`blocked_by` fits a claim skipped for time, and `other` was used for ed3;
the dependency rule blocked ed10 although part of it could be computed;
there is no rule for a monosynaptic count far from the paper's, and the
claims were tested under a stated assumption; and each edit to the entry,
even of text, forces a full run of the notebook.
