# hunt-2018, Hunt et al. 2018

Thirty-seven claims reproduced (one on an assumption), two partially, one not
reproduced and none blocked. The first verification round agreed with 39 of
the 40 outcomes as they stood, disagreeing on `figs5c-latency-order`, and the
second with all 40; nothing was proposed after the second, so there was no
third round. CRCNS data sets carry no version, so the record gives
`unversioned` and the notebook prints the MD5 sums of the files read. The
paper, Hunt, Malalasekera, de Berker, Miranda, Farmer, Behrens and Kennerley,
"Triple dissociation of attention and decision computations across prefrontal
cortex" (Nature Neuroscience 21, 1471-1481), records single units in ACC,
DLPFC and OFC of two monkeys, F and M, which uncover picture cues of reward
probability and magnitude one at a time before choosing between two options,
and reports that cue 1 activity encodes spatial attention in DLPFC, the
attended stimulus and its value in OFC and accept/reject and left/right value
in ACC, that OFC compares attended against stored value in subspaces of its
own, that an ACC belief-confirmation signal predicts reaction time and ramps
to choice, and single-neuron fractions in line with these. The claims were
written from the version of record (PMC6331040) and its supplement, without
the main figure legends, and without the MATLAB scripts, which ship in the
data set; the attempt follows the scripts where the claims file leaves a
detail open and runs their choices beside its readings. Every claim could be
tested. Reproduced are, among others, the session and trial counts (25 and
32 sessions, 9863 and 14251 trials, exact), the unit counts (189, 135 and 183
after the 1 Hz exclusion, with the stored `average_firing_rate`), the RSA
templates of Figure 4 under all four readings (spatial attention CPD 80.2% in
DLPFC, stimulus identity 35.8% in OFC), the t75 latencies (all nine equal
the paper's at a 1 ms step over -200 to 600 ms), the OFC subspace
correlations of Figure 5 (attended value r = 0.845) and the ACC belief
subspace of Figure 6. Three of them hold under a minority of readings, which
the criteria allow: `fig6e-action-subspace-ramp` under 12 of 72, among them
the scripts' own choices, `fig7a-ofc-value-difference-neurons` under 4 of 12
with no pairwise chi-square p below 0.05, and `fig8-spatial-value-dlpfc`
under 2 of 26. `chose-on-partial-information` (85.52% and 71.43%) rests on
the script's count of cues evaluated; the count of fixated pictures gives
54.23% and 42.56%. Partial are `figs1ab-cue-weights`, where M weights
probability and magnitude unequally (option ratio 1.11, attribute 1.13), and
`fig8-action-value-acc-dlpfc`, where the tests hold in five readings and the
script's but no reading has both fractions within 18 +/- 1.8% (the script's
gives 20.6% and 17.9%), while the authors' stored flags give 18.5% and 17.2%
and differ from those computed here in 2.1% of ACC units. Not reproduced is
`figs5c-latency-order`: the t75 point estimates of the nine claimed curves
follow the paper's order, but the simulated ranges overlap in OFC and ACC
under every reading. The author's first run gave 35 reproduced, 4 partially
and 1 not reproduced. The author asked a reader two questions; the answers
let `figs1cd-fewer-cues-more-accurate` take accuracy on the pictures
observed and `fig7a` hold at one significance level, and the author-answers
run moved them from not reproduced and partially reproduced to reproduced.
Round 1 put figs5c at not reproduced and found that the Methods' two-column
Figure 6 projection and the -200 ms action bin had not been run, that the
cross-correlation maps were computed within single epochs where the
criterion describes one map over the three cue epochs, that dropping units
87 and 653 leaves n below the paper's, that the script's binomial formula is
anti-conservative and that the "evaluated" count decides
chose-on-partial-information. In reply the projection, the bin, a variant
with the paper's n and the exact binomial test were added, the maps were
computed per epoch pair as the script does, with 8-connected clusters (they
had been 4-connected) and the single map as a variant, that outcome was
marked assumed, and two more questions went to a reader in
`questions/hunt-2018.yaml`. Round 2 agreed with all 40 and found sentences
the outputs did not support, on the t75 ranges, one Fisher p (1.3e-05 where
the paper gives 0.00013), the exact-test count for fig8-action and the figs5c
point estimates; in reply these were corrected, the single-map variant of
`fig5gj-cross-correlation-dynamics` was rerun with 10 other seeds and failed,
leaving the outcome on the script's separate maps, and the Figure 6 data were
checked against `info_gathering_ramp_to_choice.mat` by range requests. The
author's run took about 1 h 40 min (18:36 to about 20:15 UTC); the notebook
ran in about 13.3 min at first, with peak memory 4.4 GB, 383 s after the
author-answers run, 549 s after round 1 and 590 s at the end. One
development run failed on a lower-case subject letter, and the final runs
had no failures.

Units are selected by the `average_firing_rate` field of
`other_data/all_units_info.mat`, which the scripts use for the 1 Hz
exclusion and which is not documented; the whole-session rate selects 190,
136 and 182 units and the rate over the analysed trials 186, 137 and 179,
where the field gives the paper's 189, 135 and 183. Every figure script
drops unit 87 (DLPFC, 4.8 Hz) as a bad cell, and the Figure 5 and 7 scripts
also drop unit 653 (OFC, whose cue 2 T-statistics are not finite), so those
analyses use 134 and 182 units where the paper reports 135 and 183, and the
paper does not mention it. The Figure 6 script differs from the Methods: it
averages 100 random half splits drawn per unit, adds a constant and the mean
option/attribute response to the projection design, takes the action weights
at the bin centred 210 ms before the response and uses fixed reaction-time
bins the Methods do not give. Two thresholds are not in the text, |T| > 1.64
for value-difference neurons in Figure 7 and accuracy on the pictures seen,
averaged over sessions, for Supplementary Figure 1c, and the paper does not
define when a cue counts as "evaluated". The caption of Supplementary Table 1
says it counts units above 1 Hz, but its counts agree with the data in all
53 sessions when every unit is counted and in 26 of 53 when units below 1 Hz
are left out. The Results place the Figure 7a cue 3 tests on attribute
trials, but cite Figure 5d/e, which are option trials, and the script tests
the option-trial regressors. The unit files 681 to 724 (sessions 5, 11, 23 and 27 of Miles)
store the subject as "m" where all other units of Miles store "M", and the
descriptor, README and flowchart name the folder
`LTH_preprocessed_data` where the archive holds `LTH_processed_data.zip`. The
script that bins reaction times stores a bin holding one trial as a flat line
at that trial's mean, in 1 cue-locked and 14 response-locked ACC traces of the
first five random halves; every other trace matches the data here to within
1.4e-07. The data set holds 198 ACC, 156 DLPFC and 195 OFC units over 24 F and
29 M sessions, every unit file agrees with the units table and the spikes
line up with the trial events; the cue 1 regression matches the stored
T-statistics to within 3.3e-07 and the Figure 6 weights the stored ones to
within 2.6e-07, but the data set holds no stored RSA output to check the
Figure 4 analyses against. Questions are put to the paper's authors: whether
the two thresholds and the script's count of cues evaluated are the choices
behind the reported results.

The author's points on the protocol: the claims reader did not read the
paper's code because it ships in the data set, so several criteria list
readings that differ from what the code does, and the criteria's readings
were run with the code's choice added and judged by the criterion, except
neuron-counts, judged on the code's rate as a deviation; the criterion's rule
that one reading suffices conflicts with step 1.6, and the criterion was
followed with the reading counts in the notes; there is no rule for partial
when a criterion has no partial clause but holds in part; and reading 1.1 to
1.7 GB HDF5 files over range requests was too slow (about 0.6 s per trial),
so each file was read into memory, as `archives.md` permits.
