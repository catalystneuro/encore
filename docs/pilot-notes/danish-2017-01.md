# danish-2017, Danish et al. 2017

Five claims reproduced, eleven partially, five not reproduced and five
blocked, two for missing metadata, one for missing data and two for other
reasons; sixteen outcomes rest on an assumption. The first verification
round agreed with 20 of the 26 outcomes as they stood and the second with
25; the second round's one disagreement, `spectral-features-weak`, was
proposed after it as not reproduced in place of blocked, and a third round
reviewed that claim and agreed. CRCNS data sets carry no version, so the
record gives `unversioned`, and the verifier checked the archive's checksums
against those the notebook pins. The paper, Danish, Aronov and Fee in PLoS
ONE (12:e0169568), lesions the thalamic nucleus Uva in 7 adult zebra finches
and records multiunit activity (MUA) in Uva of freely singing birds, and
reports that the lesions abolish directed song while undirected song goes on
at similar rates with less stereotyped syllables, gaps and rhythm, and that
Uva activity rises during song, peaks before syllable onsets, dips before
offsets, leads song amplitude and is coherent with it near 10 Hz. The claims
were written from the version of record (PMC5472270); the paper links no
code, and the check left open which pre-lesion day the lesion measures use
and that the control-lesion claim, with 4 birds, cannot fail its Wilcoxon
test. Reproduced are the broader gaps after the lesion, the elevated MUA
during song (18 of 18 sites), the dip before syllable offsets (18.0 ms), the
correlation with amplitude (0.382 with MUA leading by 40.7 ms, paper 0.40 at
42) and the coherence peak (9.03 Hz, 0.789). Partial are the maturity index
(0.235 and 0.096, but only under a log-centred spectral form of the
attempt's own, while the literal reading does not fall), Gamma and
rhythmicity R (both fall in all 7 birds, with values outside tolerance), the
small-syllable peak (7 birds against 6), the introductory-note and call
latencies, the two consistency claims of Fig 5, the onset peak (17.0 ms
against 20 +- 2, a mean of 18.7 ms with b871's channel 7), the 10 Hz power peaks
and the bout-offset suppression (127 ms against about 200). Not reproduced
are the similar singing rate (lesioned birds sing less per hour, p = 0.031,
but only when the authors' selected segments are counted), the residual
rhythm peak (7 of 7 against 6), the weak spectral features (9 of 18
bird-feature pairs significant in single birds where the paper reports
none), the long-syllable claims of Fig 9 and the absence of a motif or bout
signal (one bird, p = 0.0012). Blocked are the loss of directed song, the
control lesions, the antidromic identification, the single-unit rates and
the GTE relation, which was not attempted. The author's first run gave 7
reproduced, 10 partially, 4 not reproduced and 5 blocked, with the
antidromic and spectral-feature claims not attempted for lack of time. The
author asked a reader nothing, so the answer and author-answers runs changed
nothing. Round 1 found that the maturity-index form matching the paper was
the author's own, that the channel was chosen on the modulation later
tested, that the fig9 nulls and the fig4b shuffle had bugs, that the
residual-peak test could not fail and that the singing-rate result was
fragile; in reply the nulls and shuffle were fixed, a local-maximum test
added and four outcomes changed. The first revision did not finish
executing its notebook: one full run crashed on a bug in a helper change
and a second was killed at the author's 47-minute limit, so the revised
notebook was executed afterwards (43 minutes). Round 2 found that the record
still described that notebook as not executed, that the fig3 spike count
subtracted each file's median trace and so removed the response it sought,
and several numbers that did not match the notebook; the count was dropped
and the numbers corrected. In the second revision a re-run took 63 minutes,
over the 60-minute limit, and two rhythm-null helpers were sped up without
changing their results. The author's notebook ran in about 33 minutes at
5.4 GB peak memory, 40 minutes after round 2 and 21 minutes at the end.

Each electrophysiology recording has three neural channels and neither the
data descriptor nor the files say which is the Uva electrode, so every
electrophysiology outcome rests on the channel with the strongest
syllable-locked activity (b871 6, Orange150 7, Uva4 3); Orange150's channels
5 and 6 are near copies of each other, and only Orange150's first-day
analysis file names a channel, 7. The data set holds 3 of the 5 recorded
birds, no control-lesion birds, no directed-song labels for the lesion
recordings and no sorted single units (EventTimes is empty in all five
analysis files), and the lesion folders hold one day before and one day
after the lesion per bird. The files hold no record of when the recorder
was running, so the recording hours were taken as the span from first to
last file, which ranges from 2.2 to 41.6 h. The syllable labels are not
described; from the data, a to h are motif syllables, "i" introductory
notes and "k" distance calls, and the descriptor says introductory notes and
calls are unselected, while segments labelled "i" and "k" are selected in
all three birds. The archive holds Bird_2-Orange150/2013-10-06.tar.gz, a
second copy of the folder Bird_2-Orange150/2013-10-06 (173 files, identical
MD5). One Uva4 recording (UVA4_d000020) has an invalid closing sample number
and time. The Uva4 spreadsheet tab is named "October 6, 2016", lists
recordings dated 2007-07-25 to 2007-07-27 and covers 56 of the analysis
file's 178 recordings, and the lesion folders hold undescribed files, among
them a SyllGaps.mat in one bird whose durations differ from the analysis
file's. Read literally, the Methods' "correlation of power spectra" gives a
maturity index of 0.32 to 0.62 and no decrease. Five questions are put to
the paper's authors: the form of the maturity index, which channel is the
Uva electrode, the scale of Gamma and the segmentation behind it, the
synthetic null for song rhythm, and the shuffled baseline of Figs 4 and 6.

The author's points on the protocol: archives.md covers zip files but CRCNS
ships tar.gz here, so each archive was streamed with urllib and tarfile; it
has no rule for choosing a data channel, so one was chosen and the outcomes
marked assumed; no block reason fits a claim not attempted for lack of time,
and `other` was used; it does not say what a count above the expected one
with zero tolerance gives, judged partial; and the time budget forced 200
null draws where the claims file asks for 1000, recorded as a deviation.
