# payne-2020, Payne et al. 2020

Eight claims reproduced, one partially, six not reproduced and 15 blocked, 14
for missing data and one as `metadata_insufficient`, plus one claim that also
uses outside values, `table1-comparable-to-published`, reproduced; eight
outcomes rest on an assumption. The first verification round agreed with all
31 outcomes and the second with all 31; nothing was proposed after the second,
so there was no third round. It pins version 1, the only published version
(doi:10.26275/kkmb-vun5). The paper, Payne, Wiedmann, Eiber, Wong, Senn,
Osborne, Keast and Fallon in Frontiers in Neuroscience (14, 619275), implants
a planar four-electrode array on the pelvic nerve of rats, records
electrically evoked compound action potentials (ECAPs) with three peaks, P1
to P3, under urethane anaesthesia and in awake rats at weeks 0 and 1, follows
electrode impedance, measures bladder pressure responses to stimulation by
cystometry in awake rats, and looks for injury in ganglion histology and
spinal c-Fos. The claims were written from the text of the version of record
(PMC7773906), without figure pages; the paper has no supplement and links no
code. Reproduced are the ECAPs in all five anaesthetised rats, the higher
threshold of P3 than P1 (unadjusted Dunn P = 0.034, n = 4, with a Friedman P
of 0.105), and the six Table 1 voiding values and their overlap with Wiedmann
et al. (2020), all from the authors' derivative table, the HFPO frequency from
7 rats, not 9. The impedance rise is partial: 4.192, 7.245 and 12.044 kOhm,
the paper's values, with no file for the 8-week value. Not reproduced are the
anaesthetised latencies (medians 1.60, 2.50 and 4.19 ms against 1.80, 3.48
and 3.77), conduction velocities and threshold order, the one rat with P1
above P2, the awake latencies (three peaks in at most 5 of 7 recordings) and
the colonic pressure response (one responder, 12-020, with 1.44 mmHg against
2.6). Every ECAP outcome rests on peak rules the paper does not give, and
band-passed noise alone passes the 0.05 uV two-recording test in 63 to 77% of
current steps at 0.2 to 0.7 uV SD, so the second round held that the
not-reproduced threshold order says little about the paper. Blocked are the
awake threshold increase, the electrode failures, the absence of faecal
expulsion and erection, the six stimulation-response claims and the six
histology, c-Fos and post-mortem claims. The author's first run gave the same
outcomes, and the author asked a reader nothing. Round 1 found that only one
peak rule had been run, that the 0.05 uV level lies inside the noise, that the
fig2c pass rests on leaving out 12-008, that partial tests of several blocked
claims were possible and that Table 1 rested on the derivative table without a
recomputation from the traces; in reply four peak rules were run through every
ECAP analysis, a synthetic noise test and three-noise-SD and 5 uV variants
were added, partial results were stated for the blocked claims and all six
Table 1 parameters were recomputed from the pressure traces, with no outcome
changed. Round 2 corrected its round-1 count to 16 tested and 15 blocked,
found five places where the text did not match the outputs, among them
statements resting on the dataset's R notebook output, which had not been
printed, and asked that `table1-hfpo-frequency` be marked assumed, all done.
The author's run took about 25 minutes (00:02 to 00:26 UTC) and nothing failed
in the final runs; the notebook ran in 29 s at first, with peak memory 0.74
GB, and in 56 s (0.9 GB) after round 1. During drafting three conclusion
sentences contradicted the printed numbers and were corrected.

The dataset holds none of the histology (ATF3, CGRP, synaptophysin), c-Fos
counts, post-mortem notes or behavioural observations behind Figures 4 and 5
and the safety claims, though files/README.txt lists summary derived data from
c-Fos activation mapping; stimulation trains are deposited for four rats (9
trains), where the paper stimulated eight, no file records whether urine was
released after a train, and the 8-week rats have no stimulation, so 13 claims
cannot be tested, and electrode-failures, with per-electrode impedances for 8
arrays (32 electrodes) of the 40 electrodes counted, makes 14. In the 32, one
electrode (18-273 E3) reads 252270 Ohm from day 14 and none is short. The
seven awake ECAP recordings are seven subject identifiers, four at day 0 and
three at day 7, with nothing linking a day-7 recording to a rat recorded at
day 0 (pool_id is empty), so the week 0 to week 1 comparison cannot be made;
the Methods give three awake rats and the Results four. The ECAP files are
sampled at 200 kHz with a 500 to 3000 Hz filter, where the Methods give 100
kHz and 300 to 5000 Hz; the acute files step the current on a logarithmic
scale up to 1.6 mA with no 0 mA trace, not 0 to 2 mA in 0.1 mA steps; and
several files stimulate E3-E4 and record E1-E2 (12-008, 12-050, 12-053,
12-056) or stimulate E2 to E1 (12-006, 12-007), where the Methods give
stimulation on E1-E2. Neither the ECAP files nor their schema give the
amplifier gain (the Methods give 10^2) or define time zero, and the schema
gives TimeUnits s where the files have ms. In the colonic recording of 15-758
the pressure rises by about 0.65 mmHg during the 10 s before each recorded
stimulus time over 33 trains, so the recorded times are one train after the
response, and the colonic stimulation in all three files (1.114 mA, 200 us, 4
or 5 s; 0.541 mA, 25 us, 15 Hz, 10 s) differs from the Methods (1 mA, 100 us,
10 Hz). The derivative cystometry-dataset.xlsx and the /Voids tables of the
same rats give different values for most cycles (peak pressure agrees within
0.5 mmHg in 4 of 27 cycles), and neither says how it was computed; the
derivative and the dataset's R notebook output from it do not give Table 1's
means (minimum pressure 11.74 against 10.3 mmHg; inter-void interval 6.66
against 7.6 min), though each lies within Table 1's CI, and it has HFPO
frequencies for 7 of the 9 rats. The per-array impedance file reproduces the
paper's impedance means, spreads and ranges exactly, from 12, 12 and 10
arrays, where the paper describes 38 electrodes in 10 arrays, and its spreads
are SEMs over arrays. The manifests of the derivative, code, docs and protocol
folders have no descriptions, the /Voids columns and the CL scale of the ECAP
stimuli are not defined, some cystometry waves carry an undescribed
AppliedOffset attribute, and the dataset lists 29 subjects where the paper
reports 24 rats. Three questions are put to the paper's authors: how P1, P2
and P3 were picked and at which stimulus level and time origin their
latencies were read; how the threshold amplitude was measured, whether the
0.05 uV level applies to the deposited traces in their units and whether
Dunn's P is adjusted; and which rat and which 13 responses give the 2.6 mmHg
colonic increase.

The author's points on the protocol: the "absent at 0 mA" criterion assumes 0
mA traces exist, and the lowest current was used as a deviation; the readings
rule does not say what to do when an author-chosen detection rule underlies
every reading, so the detection margin and artefact end were treated as extra
readings and all ECAP outcomes marked assumed; there is no rule for an
all-or-nothing criterion when one time point is absent, and impedance-increase
was judged partial; it is unclear whether to block or partially test a claim
with data for 32 of 40 electrodes, and electrode-failures was blocked with the
partial count in the notes; outside values printed in the paper itself were
given the paper's DOI as their source under `inputs`; the header lists
hashes for the non-binary files but not for the 33 HDF5 files read, where the
archive guidance asks for each file read; and `entry.py run` reused an earlier
executed copy after a markdown-only edit, so the first handover held old
conclusion text, and the run was repeated with `--fresh`.
