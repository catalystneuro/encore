# kondo-2025, Kondo et al. 2025

Fifteen claims reproduced, one partially, none not reproduced and six
blocked, two for missing data and four as `methods_underspecified`; no
outcome rests on an assumption. The first verification round agreed with 21
of the 22 outcomes as they stood and judged `fig3e-cluster-b-similar-rates`
blocked where the record had it reproduced; the author agreed and changed it.
The second round agreed with all 22, and nothing was proposed after it, so
there was no third round. It pins version 0.250705.0947: the paper cites no
version, and this one, the most recent, is the only one with the day-0
resting-state sessions of all 25 mice, which one claim needs. The paper,
Kondo, Sehara, Harukuni, Aoki et al., "A multimodal dataset linking
wide-field calcium imaging to behavior changes in mice during an operant
lever-pull task" (bioRxiv preprint, doi:10.1101/2025.02.03.631599), describes
25 VGluT1-Cre x Ai162 mice trained for 15 sessions on a tone-cued lever-pull
task, with wide-field imaging of 22 Allen CCF ROIs per hemisphere, body, face
and eye videos with DeepLabCut keypoints and a pupil fit, resting-state
sessions and a sensory-mapping session under anaesthesia. The claims were
written from the preprint's text (version 1), without figures, tables or a
PDF, and from the paper's code at pinned commits, which holds no analysis
behind Figures 3 to 6 or Figure S2b. Reproduced are the cohort, session and
frame counts, the training breaks, the rise of the response rate (median
0.556 in session 1 and 0.856 in session 15), the T_pull_final clusters of 7
and 18 mice, cluster A's early success, the absence of signal degradation,
the visual, tactile and auditory maps from the ROI series, the absence of
aberrant transients, the lever-tip correlation (0.981) and, in the mean over
sessions, the motor and somatosensory activity after reward and the drop in
variability after pull onset. The auditory map meets its criterion with
little evidence, as a random 9-ROI region would meet it with probability
0.61. `fig5f-forepaw-correlation` is partial: both correlations are within
tolerance, but 356 sessions qualify against an exact 354. Blocked are the
constant success rate and cluster B's similar rates, where the readings of
"constant" and "similar" disagree; the movement order and the areas tracking
the pupil, which the paper states for an unidentified example session and
which no reading supports over all sessions; and the landmark and DeepLabCut
validations, whose manual annotations are not deposited. The author asked a
reader nothing, so the answer and author-answers runs changed nothing. Round
1 found that the onset rule of the claims file gives onsets before 0 on
synthetic traces without movement, that F_B0 from 8 frames had unmeasured
noise, that the TTL channel names are wrong in six sessions, that the
auditory criterion has a high chance level and that the blue and UV stacks
had not been checked from the data; in reply a synthetic test and a 100 ms
variant of the rule were added, F_B0 was taken from 160 frames and checked
against four sessions read frame by frame, readings over the 19 sessions
with correct channel names were added, the chance level and a shifted-onset
comparison were printed, and the stacks were shown to fit their names.
Round 2 found statements the outputs did not fully support, among them the
external searches for the annotations, which no output showed; a notebook
cell now runs them, and two questions went to a reader in
`questions/kondo-2025.yaml`. The pixel-map readings of the sensory maps were
not run in the time limit. The author's run took about 2 hours, with the
notebook at 18 to 20 minutes; it ran in 1353 s after the author-answers run,
24 minutes after round 1 and about 38 minutes at the end. The harness left
an empty `.claude/.cc-writes` folder in the entry that the author was not
allowed to remove.

In all 495 NWB files ImagingPlane_blue states an excitation_lambda of 405 nm
and ImagingPlane_UV 470 nm, the reverse of their descriptions and the
Methods; the stack named blue is about three times as bright as the one
named UV and follows dFF_B, so the data fit the names. The manual
skull-landmark annotations (75 sessions) and the manual keypoint annotations
of the DeepLabCut validation frames are not in the dataset or in the four
BraiDyn-BC repositories, and the Matsuzaki lab data platform, which the
dandiset names as a variant form of the dataset, answers the request for the
folder's file list with HTTP 403, so it could not be searched. The
sensory-mapping trial tables code each stimulus as 1, 2 or 3 with no
description; in the six sessions of VG1-GC#51, VG1-GC#52, VG1-GC#53,
VG1-GC#54, VG1-GC#60 and VG1-GC#61 there is no stim_visual channel, the
stim_whisker channel carries the visual train and stim_auditory the tactile
stimulus, and there is no third stimulus. In the other 27 sessions the
trials run visual, auditory, tactile by channel name, where the Methods give
visual, tactile, auditory. Reward TTL onsets outnumber the trials labelled
success in 205 of 364 task sessions, so the success rate depends on which is
counted. The lever and the other sensor channels have unit "a.u." and
"no description", while the paper's code gives the lever in mm. The
down-sampled keypoint series keep a confidence array of the 100 Hz video
length next to 30 Hz data. The pupil diameters hold infinite values or
values of 1e5 pixels and more, above 5% of frames in 10 of 356 sessions. 357
task sessions have body keypoints, against 355 in the paper; leaving out the
two VG1-GC#119 sessions noted "face video is not available" would give the
paper's counts, but the paper does not say it did. The session-initial
T_pull follows the Methods' minus-100-ms rule in 219 of 332 pairs of
sessions, the trial tables give T_pull values of 0.0 s in 10783 of 48549
trials where the Methods give a 1 ms start, and raw tone TTLs exceed the
trials by up to 145 (VG1-GC#78 day 4). Four sensory-mapping sessions hold
36000 frames where the Methods give 27000, and the stacks are inside the NWB
files where the Data Records describe TIFF files. One break, of VG1-GC#85
between task sessions 4 and 5, lasts 16 days. No recording is deposited
twice. Three questions are put to the paper's authors: which session is the
Figure 6 example and how its statements were judged, and what test or bound
supports "constant" in Figure 3c and "similar" in Figure 3e.

The author's points on the protocol: "a claim that depends on a blocked
claim is blocked" does not separate depending on a claim's measure from
depending on its result, and both fig3e claims were tested; "reproduced
under any reading" conflicts with a criterion that blocks when its readings
differ; it does not say what the outcome is when an exact expected value
fails but the criterion's test passes, and partially_reproduced was chosen;
it has no way to flag a claims-file method that cannot discriminate, such as
the onset rule; and reading the raw image frames does not fit the time
limit, so the fluorescence was subsampled as a deviation and the pixel-map
reading was not run.
