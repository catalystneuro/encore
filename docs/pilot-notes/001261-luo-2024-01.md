# 001261, Luo et al. 2024

Seven claims reproduced, six partially, four not reproduced and 12 blocked,
all on the dataset. The first verification round agreed with 26 of the 29
outcomes as they stood and the second with all 29, and nothing was proposed
after the second, so there was no third round. It pins version 0.250917.2023,
the only published version; the paper cites none, and the draft has the same
152 files. The paper, Luo, Jiang, Desai et al., "Comparative Physiology and
Morphology of BLA-Projecting NBM/SI Cholinergic Neurons in Mouse and Macaque"
in J. Comp. Neurol., records BLA-projecting neurons of the NBM/SI in slices
with whole-cell current clamp, 48 cholinergic and 46 noncholinergic neurons
from 27 mice and 46 cholinergic neurons from 9 macaques, compares them on 18
electrophysiological features with rank-sum tests, UMAP and LDA, and compares
13 morphological features and Sholl counts from neurobiotin fills. The claims
were written from the version of record in Europe PMC (PMC11583843) with its
supplement, and from the paper's code, patch_clamp_analysis at commit 17a3265,
which extracts the features from one recording's current steps but holds no
code for the resting potential, the statistics, UMAP, LDA or the morphology.
The dandiset holds only the current-clamp recordings, so the 11 morphology
claims of Figures 4, 5C, 9 and 10 and Figures S4-1 and S9-1 are blocked as
`data_missing`, and the 18 electrophysiology claims were tested on 17
features, the resting potential (F1) not being recorded. Both feature counts
reproduce at the lower edge of their ranges: `fig2-mouse-c-nc-15-of-18` gives
13 of 17 (range 13 to 15), one of them the AHP width in the direction opposite
to the paper's, and `fig7-macaque-mouse-feature-count` 14 of 17 (range 14 to
16); with the 200 pA step for the adaptation index and the CV each falls one
below its range. `fig2d-mouse-adaptation` reproduces only on the step with the
most spikes (p = 0.011; 0.86 on the 200 pA step), the LDA classifies 98.8% of
the 85 mouse neurons with every feature, AHP amplitude has the largest LDA
weight, and the macaque input resistance is larger (593.2 against 355.5 MOhm).
`figs2-2-mouse-amplitude-cv-sag` reproduces on an assumption, a saturated -60
pA response being read as 1000 or more consecutive identical samples since the
code's test cannot fire as written; without the rule the sag p is 0.011, which
misses the figure's 0.01. `sample-sizes` is partial (45 distinct mouse
noncholinergic recordings against 46), as are the mouse maximum rate (the
noncholinergic mean, 34.8 Hz, just below its tolerance of 35.1 to 42.9 Hz),
the rheobase (p = 1.1e-3, just above the paper's 0.001), the macaque maximum
rate (21.47 Hz against a tolerance of 23.67 to 28.93 Hz), the macaque AHP
width (p = 0.17) and Figure S7-1 (sag p = 0.090, and the downstroke said to be
shared differs, p = 7.6e-3). `fig2d-mouse-ahp` is not reproduced: with the
Methods' window the AHP width is longer in noncholinergic neurons (298.9
against 183.1 ms, p = 4.1e-5), the opposite of the paper, while the code's
window gives all four mouse AHP means within tolerance. `fig2d-mouse-passive`
is not reproduced on tau from the -20 pA step (39.92 against 28.59 ms, p =
3.2e-3, both means above tolerance), where the code's step gives 34.88 against
22.04 ms; the mouse UMAP misclassifies a median of 4 or 8.5 neurons against 2;
and the macaque spike width gives p = 0.013 against the paper's 0.01.
`fig7c-macaque-mouse-umap-separation` is blocked as
`methods_underspecified`, the criterion's reading (b) holding and reading (a)
failing. The author's first run gave 4 reproduced, 11 partially, 3 not
reproduced and 12 blocked, and asked a reader nothing, so the answer and
author-answers runs found no questions and changed nothing. Round 1 disagreed
with fig2d-mouse-ahp, fig2d-mouse-passive and fig7-macaque-spike-width, whose
criteria a failed test does not leave partial, and found that taking the first
non-zero stimulus sample as the step onset put the analysis window on a -20 pA
test pulse in 8 files, which had made eight neurons look non-responsive and
given tau values up to 401 ms; it also judged the claims file's AHP window a
likely misreading of what the paper computed, and found the sag saturation
rule not implemented, the rank-sum test without tie correction and a leak in
the cross-validation. In reply the onset was taken from each sweep's longest
run of non-zero current, which moved 170 sweeps and made both feature counts
reproduced, the sag rule was implemented, the three outcomes were changed to
the verifier's, and the AHP window was put to a reader. Round 2 agreed with
every outcome, asked that the sag outcome be marked assumed and for a table of
AHP width against latency, and found sentences the outputs did not support
(steps said to reach 300 pA where a cell prints 1200 pA, amplifier fields
described but not printed, "in every sweep" of the 8 files, and a runtime of
"about two minutes" where the notebook printed 2.6 min), which were corrected;
the table showed that in 43 of 47 cholinergic and 35 of 43 noncholinergic
neurons the potential does not recross within the 100 ms window, so the width
falls with the latency of the first spike (Spearman rho -0.996 and -0.990), and
the span of F14 was put to a reader as a second question. The author's run
took about 50 minutes (12:50 to 13:38 UTC) and left two sandbox artefacts
uncommitted. The notebook ran in about 2 minutes at first, 129 s after the
answer, 100 s after round 1 and 117 s after round 2.

The morphology of the paper (13 features, Sholl counts, reconstructions of 31
and 44 mouse and 52 macaque neurons) is not in the dandiset, whose description
says it holds the physiology data, and the paper names no other archive. The
resting potential is not recorded, as every sweep carries the holding current;
63 of 152 original file names, kept as the NWB identifier, carry a value. The
recording CG608_FL_S4C7_B+G+_-63mV.mat is deposited twice with identical
traces, once as a mouse cholinergic and once as a mouse noncholinergic neuron,
its name saying G+, so the dandiset holds 45 distinct mouse noncholinergic
recordings, not the paper's 46; the macaque noncholinergic recording
NHP_20220420-03 of M#12 Shahruk is also deposited twice, under two session
names. In the macaque file "2023_03_06_0002 R+B+A2C1 RMP -78.abf" the voltage
is 20 times too large (baseline -1557 "mV"), while its conversion factor is 1.
Most sweeps hold a short -20 pA test pulse besides the step and nothing marks
which part of the stimulus is the step; in 8 files (six mouse cholinergic
neurons of November 2021 to February 2022 and two macaque neurons of M#11) the
pulse starts 0.7 s before the step, so an analysis taking the first non-zero
sample as the onset misses the step. The stimulus series are labelled amperes
but hold pA (values -100 to 1200), and the amplifier fields of every sweep of
every file hold one value each (bias current 1e-11 A, bridge balance 2e7 Ohm,
capacitance compensation 1e-12 F, gain 1), placeholders, though the bias
current was set per neuron. The current steps differ between files and from
the paper's description: increments up to 200 pA of 10 to 50 pA in most files,
5 pA in 24 files and 100 pA in one, not the 10 pA of the Fig. S2-1 legend; 23
files go above 200 pA (one to 1200 pA); and the steps last 500 or 1000 ms. The
files record neither the quality-control measures nor the slice of each
neuron, general/notes holding only a bregma position, and the file names carry
undocumented labels such as R+, B+, G+ and B+R- that do not follow
session_description consistently. Two files in the folder of mouse CG608 are
named for CG609, and the 46 macaque cholinergic files come from 6 of the 9
macaques, while the paper reports 46 cholinergic neurons from nine macaques.
The -60 pA responses of 8 neurons (seven mouse, one macaque) are clipped at
-100 or -105.5 mV for 1144 to 4793 samples. The paper's code measures the AHP
on the first step with two spikes where Methods 2.5 measure it after the first
spike within 100 ms, and the code's window gives all four mouse AHP means of
the Results within 10%; it fits tau on the most negative unsaturated step
between -50 and 0 pA where the Methods use the -20 pA step, and the code's step
gives the paper's mouse values (34.9 and 22.0 ms against 34.6 and 22.3).
Questions are put to the paper's authors: which window produced the reported
AHP values and over what span the AHP width is measured, how a saturated -60
pA response is recognised for the sag, whether "the maximal current step" of
Methods 2.5 is the 200 pA step or the step with the most spikes, how the UMAP
separations were judged, and where the resting potential was recorded and for
how many neurons.

The author's points on the protocol: several criteria do not cover a test that
holds only at a weaker p or one test holding while another reverses, and the
protocol's definition of partially reproduced was applied; streaming with
`remfile`, as `archives.md` says, took 11 minutes for the metadata alone, and
each file was read whole into memory over HTTP (22 s); step 1.2 says to count a
duplicate once but not which label to keep when the copies disagree, and the
label in the file name was used; `measured` needs one number per expected
value though the resting potential cannot be computed, and the file-name
subset was given; the protocol says nothing about excluding recordings that
are clearly broken; the clock check has no recipe for current clamp without a
trial table; the Methods-before-code rule decides outcomes even where the code
clearly matches the paper's numbers, as for the AHP, leaving a finding as the
only route; the UMAP and LDA criteria say F1 to F18 while F1 is missing, and 17
features were used; and the prompt's harness name, "Claude Code", differs from
the "Claude Code (GitHub Actions)" of the example record.
