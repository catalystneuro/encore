# souza-2026, Souza et al. 2026

Fifteen claims reproduced on the dandiset, twelve partially, five not
reproduced and none blocked; of the five layer claims, which also use the
CCFv3 25 um annotation volume, two reproduced, one partially and two not
reproduced; and the link claim `data-sessions-units` is partially reproduced.
Every one of the 38 outcomes rests on an assumption about which sessions and
units the paper used. The first verification round agreed with 33 of the 38
outcomes as they stood and the second with 37; it still disputed
`data-sessions-units`, which it would give as not reproduced, the outcome was
kept with the disagreement recorded, nothing was proposed after the second
round, and so there was no third. The dandiset is pinned at 0.240702.1725, the
version the paper cites and the only published one. The paper, Souza, Vieira,
Ventura et al., "Reward coding across mouse visual cortical areas" (Front.
Behav. Neurosci. 20:1944695, a Brief Research Report), reuses 103 sessions from
54 mice of the Allen Institute's Visual Behavior Neuropixels dataset and
reports a reward-modulated cohort of units in six visual areas, mostly
suppressed, with the suppressed fraction falling from VISp to VISam and higher
in narrow-spiking and layer 4 units, a lower population variability (CV) in the
cohort than in the other units, and six signed NMF archetypes of the
peri-reward response, mixed within units, with anticipatory profiles commonest
in VISp. The claims were written from a text extraction of the version of
record, with no PDF, so no figure was opened, and with no code, which the paper
offers on request; the claims check corrected eleven claims. Reproduced are the
block and reward checks, the suppressed fraction by area (87.1% in VISp to
53.6% in VISam), the waveform separation and the narrow-spiking claims, judged
on the units table's `waveform_duration`, the layer 4 suppression (higher in 71
of 86 sessions, median +0.118), the lower CV of the cohort (0.630 against
0.851) and its faster firing, the archetype peaks (with peaks as the maximum
absolute value) and the anticipatory gradient. The cohort is partial (10,822
units against 11,032, with 286 discordant units against 239), as are the
latency and lick timing, the session gradient, the modulation index, the
rate-matched CV, the CV correlations and their block excess, which hold only
when the 20 count-matched RW- draws are averaged before correlating, and the
lick-bout contrast. Not reproduced are the ambiguous waveform band, the layer
coverage (97.2% of tested units against 87.5%, and 97.4% in VISrl against
48.0%), the layer 4 controls, the pre to post-stimulus CV pattern (7 of 12
cells match), the archetype mixtures (a hard assignment explains 0.529 against
0.36), the area overlap (eta squared 0.0114 against 0.004) and the hits
against false alarms contrast. The author's first run gave 17 reproduced, 15
partially and 6 not reproduced. Round 1 disagreed on five claims, found that
the mixture on durations from the stored mean waveform does not split narrow
from broad units, that only one reading of the ISI-shuffle null had been run,
that `CA1` was read as layer 1 and that a stricter layer rule might fit the
paper; in reply the waveform row layout was checked, the whole-recording null
was run on 12 of the 104 sessions after a first revision run stopped at the
60-minute cell limit with it on all of them, the stricter rule was shown as a
variant and put to the authors, and `layer4-controls` moved to not reproduced.
GIN answered 403 Forbidden to the runner during the first revision, so the
notebook falls back to the Allen Institute's CCF files, and the final run
loaded both and found them identical. Round 2 asked that the averaging of the
RW- draws be listed as an assumption and found sentences the outputs did not
support, on the atlas provenance, a p range, two layer 4 values and the change
under the second null reading, which were corrected. The ISI-shuffle null uses
100 permutations per unit, not the paper's 1,000, which would take about nine
CPU hours. The notebook ran in 38 minutes at first, with peak memory 6.9 GB,
about 53 minutes after round 1 and 41 minutes at the end.

The paper does not say how its 103 sessions and 54,039 units were chosen from
the dandiset's 153 sessions from 81 mice (110,563 units in the six areas), and
neither reading in the claims file comes within 10%; the Sst-Cre and Vip-Cre
mice, with units of quality good and presence ratio at least 0.95, give 104
sessions, 54 mice and 54,045 units, and the session the paper left out cannot
be identified, since the smallest session holds 262 units. These give 15,592
hits against the paper's 16,330. The units table's `waveform_duration` and the
trough-to-peak duration measured on the stored mean waveform of the peak
channel agree only at r = 0.66; the mean waveforms are sampled at 30 kHz (82
samples), so their durations take 60 distinct values and a two-component
mixture on them does not separate narrow from broad units, while the stored
`waveform_duration` (151 values) does, and the files do not say how it was
computed. Upsampled to 200 points, the measured durations agree with the
stored ones within 0.02 ms for 97.5% of units, but 1,263 units differ by more
than 0.1 ms. 1,508 of the 54,045 analysed units have a CCF coordinate of 0 or
below, outside the brain, while still carrying an area label, and the files do
not say that 0 marks a missing registration. The CCF-derived area agrees with
the unit-table label for 66.0% of tested units, the disagreements mostly VISli
(4,513) and SSp-bfd (3,735). Blocks 0 and 5 are the active block and its
passive replay in all 104 sessions, with no licks or rewards in block 5; water
follows the nearest lick by 1.3 to 53.5 ms, and summed spiking rises after the
stimulus table's image changes (post to pre ratio 1.19 to 4.12, against 0.52
to 1.29 with the times shifted 0.25 s), so licks, rewards, stimuli and spikes
share a clock. In the paper, the Figure 1H legend classifies 2,308 narrow and
8,716 broad cohort units, 11,024, more than the 10,805 left after the 227
ambiguous ones, and the unmatched layer 4 excess is +0.148 in the rate-matching
control against +0.116 in the sentence before. Four questions are put to the
paper's authors: which sessions and units were analysed; how an archetype's
peak and a unit's ramp sign were defined; how a lick bout was defined and
whether the ISI-shuffle null spans the active block or the whole recording; and
whether layers were assigned only where the CCF structure is a visual area,
which gives 87.0% of tested units and 45.5% in VISrl, close to the paper's.

The author's points on the protocol: conclusions written into the notebook
from its own numbers make every text fix cost a full run, and the 38-minute
notebook was run four times; the five-seed rule cannot be met when the random
draws happen inside the costly per-session pass, and it was recorded as not
computed; it does not say whether fewer permutations, with a check, are
acceptable or the claim should be blocked as `too_large`, and 100 were used as
a deviation; outside inputs must be pinned by a DOI or a commit, but Allen's
CCF path carries no version and GIN's URL by commit returns 404 for annexed
files, so the file was pinned by the repository commit and a SHA-256; it does
not say whether the author may adopt another selection when a link claim's
readings do not fit, and one was adopted as an assumption; many criteria have
no partial rule, and the general definition was used; the rule for `measured`
does not cover the case where no reading matches, and the closest was
recorded; and the entry check reads `tifffile.imread` as reading a figure, so
`TiffFile.asarray()` was used.
