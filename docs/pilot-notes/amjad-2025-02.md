# amjad-2025, Amjad et al. 2025, second attempt

Seven claims reproduced, one partially, one not reproduced and four blocked,
three for missing data and one as `metadata_insufficient`, plus one claim
linking the dandiset to the paper's Zenodo deposit, reproduced (trial lists
agree for 99.82 to 100% of trials); both verification rounds agreed with
every outcome, so there was no third round. It pins version 0.261120.1306,
published after the first attempt with fixes to the problems that attempt
found. The paper describes microinvasive probes (s-µIPs) for recording
dopamine by FSCV and electrical activity in monkeys; the recording
parameters, the beta power and burst claims, the dopamine reward effect and
the isolation of a sorted unit reproduce, the FSCV waveform partially (its
lowest potential is -0.347 V against -0.4 V), and the redox peaks of Figure
3A do not: on the raw current the falling-sweep minimum is at 0.897 V and
the mean voltammogram is flat within noise. The insertion yields, the
recording duration after implant, the second dopamine channel of Figure 3A
and the reward-related spiking of Figure 5A are blocked. The dopamine
effect is weak (p = 0.0226, with three of four variants not significant and
a +10 s control also below 0.05), and within a block the reward and side
effects on beta power cannot be separated, as the side of the big reward is
fixed in each of 16 blocks. The attempt was not blind: the claims file's
opening comment and the release notes of the pinned version carried the
earlier results to the author. The only outcome that differs from
`amjad-2025-01` is the redox claim, blocked there, which the reconciliation
traces to the dataset change that put the raw FSCV scans on the session
clock. Round 1 asked for the feedback time after the value cue, the noise of
single trials, a per-block analysis and the first and last scan samples,
and found that the deciding redox extremes were noise and the dopamine
evidence weak; in reply these were added, the paper's order of the beta
power steps was run as a variant and put to a reader in
`questions/amjad-2025.yaml`. Round 2 found sentences without printed
support, two of which turned out to be false and were corrected, asked that
the limit of the block analysis be stated and raised the finding on implant
dates to major. The author's work took about 32 minutes (22:48 to 23:21
UTC); the notebook ran in 73 s at first and 93 s at the end, with peak
memory 3.35 to 4.4 GB.

The dataset was converted by CatalystNeuro (catalystneuro/schwerdt-lab-to-nwb).
The second FSCV channel of Figure 3A (c8ds) is not in the dataset; Monkey
P's session has no trials table and its 36 event codes have no names ('The
code map for this session is not available'), so its reward-related spiking
cannot be tested; and no implantation dates and no list of implanted
sensors are given, so the days post-implant and the insertion yields cannot
be checked. The events, trials tables, bipolar LFP, raw EPhys and raw FSCV
now share one clock, the events are stored once each, the LFP is in volts
and the raw FSCV reads as nA, all fixed since the first attempt. The raw
FSCV current shows no dopamine redox signature after large-reward cues
(change at 0.6 V of -0.014 +- 0.020 nA), and the stored iox shows no reward
difference, while the stored PCA dopamine of the same trials does; the PCA
weights are not in the dataset, and the data do not show whether this is an
error or a small signal. The trial-aligned FSCV series are stored with
'Units unknown' and cover only trials 0 to 733 of 1468 without saying so.
The trials tables hold no trial conditions, so they must be decoded from
the event codes, and codes 35 and 36 do not say that they mark large and
small reward. The stored high cut is 8000 Hz where the paper and the
description give 7500 Hz, the applied FSCV voltage runs from -0.347 to
1.340 V where the paper gives -0.4 to 1.3 V, Monkey P's site is cl3 in the
electrodes table, ch33 in the units and c33 in the paper, and its raw signal
sits at the +-1 mV input limit in 2.9 to 10.7% of samples. In the paper's
Zenodo deposit the example file of Figure 3B stores the time axis in the
field meant for the LFP. No questions are put to the paper's authors.

The author's points on the protocol: step 1.3 asks to show how versions
differ in a blind repeat, and only file sizes were listed; the link claim
was already in the claims file and was tested as written; it does not say
whether a blocked claim may have a tagged output cell, and one was added as
not deciding; the severity scale gives only a ceiling for problems the data
cannot settle; findings on the paper's own Zenodo deposit fit neither
category, and it was filed under the paper; `measured` was left out of
claims with no expected values; and a Zenodo file inspected while preparing
is cited only in the notes.
