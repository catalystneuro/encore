# daste-2025, Daste et al. 2025

Nineteen claims reproduced, one partially, nine not reproduced and seven
blocked, all seven as `methods_underspecified`; three outcomes,
`fig2e-reliability`, `figs10e-sparseness-slope-no-difference` and
`discussion-differences-grow`, rest on an assumption. The first verification
round agreed with 34 of the 36 outcomes as they stood and gave not reproduced
where the record had partially reproduced for `fig3de-concentration-decoding`
and `fig5f-stability`, which the author adopted; the second agreed with 34 of
36 and would have given blocked for `fig4ij-valence-correct-trials` and, in
part, for `fig2b-lifetime-sparseness-similar`, so the author proposed both
changes and a third round reviewed those two claims and agreed. It pins
version 0.260520.1603; the paper cites no version, and the two published
versions hold the same 61 blobs. The paper, Daste, Pierré et al.,
"Projection-specific Routing of Odor Information in the Olfactory Cortex", a
bioRxiv preprint, images anterior piriform cortex in mice through a GRIN lens
and compares neurons that project back to the olfactory bulb (OB-p) or to
mPFC (mPFC-p) with untagged neurons, in passive odor identity, concentration
and 30 Hz sniff-aligned experiments and in a Go/NoGo task over three days
with neurons tracked across sessions. The claims were written from version 1
of the preprint flattened from its JATS XML, with no PDF, in which Tables 2 to
8 survive as titles only and the equations for normalized fluorescence,
sparseness and mutual information are lost, and from the paper's code at
commit f478b34, which only loads and preprocesses the NWB files; the claims
check made 11 changes and left 3 points open, for 36 claims. Reproduced are,
among others, the 30 Hz counts (24, 27 and 424 neurons from 5 mice, exact),
the tracked-neuron counts (46 and 41), the higher proportion of OB-p neurons
responding, the earlier OB-p latency (3.58 against 4.65 s) and early peaks,
earlier and better early identity decoding by OB-p and late identity decoding
by mPFC-p, concentration tuning, the Go/NoGo identity decoding pattern,
generalized valence in any-p populations, the higher CV of OB-p neurons
(p = 0.0115, paper 0.0127) and transferability. Partial is
`dataset-neuron-counts` (the Go/NoGo OB-p mean is 103.3 against 95 +/- 2).
Not reproduced are mutual information, population sparseness, reliability,
concentration decoding (mPFC-p peaks at 53.6%, above the criterion's bound for
close to chance), pairwise concentration decoding, the learning effect on
performance (F = 1.571 against 4.375), valence timing (OB-p above chance in
act-late), generalized valence on correct trials and stability. The four
transformer and MLP claims are blocked because Tables 5, 6 and 8 are missing
from the text and the figure code is not released; `fig2h-identity-decoding-5s`
because the 0 to 5 s and 1 to 5 s windows disagree; `fig2b` because the
treatment of never-active neurons decides the outcome and the paper gives
none; and `fig4ij` because neither correct-trial table can be identified as
the paper's. The author's first run gave 18 reproduced, 3 partially, 9 not
reproduced and 6 blocked. The author asked a reader whether the CV test of
`fig5c-cv-higher-obp` allows the valence-by-phase reading; the answer amended
the criterion to odor by phase alone, and the author-answers run moved the
claim from blocked to reproduced. Round 1 also found that never-active neurons
were dropped under one treatment only, that two mPFC mice decoded no better in
the odor phase than at baseline and that the trial tables were unbalanced; in
reply both fill treatments, a per-session clock check, shuffled-label nulls, a
leave-one-mouse-out reading of `fig5e` and the OB-p minus mPFC-p difference by
day were added, and whether `discussion-differences-grow` tests a growing
difference went to a reader in `questions/daste-2025.yaml`. Round 2 found that
projection is confounded with mouse in the latency and early-peak results and
found sentences the outputs did not support, which were corrected. The
author's run took about 3 h (01:46 to 04:45 UTC); development bugs (a pingouin
column name, a pandas `.agg` name clash, folds with a single class) were fixed
and the final runs had no failures. The two notebooks ran in 23.5 and 9.9 min
at first, 1383 and 601 s after the author-answers run, 23.5 and 15.5 min after
round 1, 1390 and 621 s after round 2 and 1396 and 653 s at the end.
Untracked dotfiles and editor folders that appeared in the repository root
were left alone.

The Go/NoGo experiment holds 8 mice, 4 OB-tagged and 4 mPFC-tagged, where the
paper has 7 (4 OB-p, 3 mPFC-p); the extra mouse is presumably sub-668, the
only one without tracking identifiers, with 24 to 58 cells per session, and
its day 3 performance (90% correct) passes the paper's 60% exclusion
threshold, so nothing in the dandiset says why it is left out. The paper's
counts are matched only when planes without red cells are left out, as the
paper's code does; over all planes the identity experiment has 2328 untagged
neurons, against 1937 with the rule and 1978 in the paper, and in sub-164 and
sub-8 the plane without red cells nearly repeats plane 0 (mean-image r = 0.994
and 0.996), which the files do not mark. The imaging rate in the ImagingPlane
metadata is wrong in 21 files: 30 Hz (concentration session of sub-2400) and
50 or 100 Hz (all GNG_2023b sessions) where the plane timestamps give 4.51 to
4.52 Hz per plane. Four identity sessions (sub-8, sub-9, sub-163, sub-164,
recorded in 2020) have 80 trials of 30 s with odor onset about 10 s into the
trial, while the other six have 100 trials of 22 s with onset at 5.1 s, where
the paper describes 22 s trials with a 5 s baseline. Under the code's
criterion the 46 tracked OB-p neurons come from 3 OB-p mice (sub-664
contributes none), not 4. The Methods count 18 mice as 9 OB-p and 8 mPFC-p,
and the Figure S2B legend has 9 OB-p and 10 mPFC-p mice; the dandiset has 18
subjects, 10 OB-tagged and 8 mPFC-tagged. In the 30 Hz data sub-2456 and
sub-2457 are the only mPFC mice, and their untagged neurons are slow too
(medians 4.82 and 4.12 s, against 2.70 to 3.73 s in the OB mice), so the
latency difference follows the mouse at least as much as the projection;
these two mice also supply 61 of the 93 day 3 Go/NoGo mPFC-p cells. The NWB
outcome labels do not apply the 10 s relabeling of hits, which changes 0 to 3
trials per session. In the 30 Hz files the odor onset falls 1 to 10 ms after a
rising edge of the sniff signal in all 500 trials, the Go/NoGo onset frame
equals the files' stim_onset_frame in every trial, licks and rewards are timed
from the trial start, and no recording is deposited twice. Questions are put
to the paper's authors: whether Tables 2 to 8 and the analysis code can be
released; how many comparisons the Bonferroni correction of Figure 2E covers;
how neurons without a defined lifetime sparseness enter Figures 2B and S10E;
which mice form the Go/NoGo dataset and where the 0.7 s binarization window
sits; and how the correct-trial pseudo-populations of Figures 4I-J and S8E-G
were built.

The author's points on the protocol: step 1.6 says to adopt the
best-supported reading while the criteria of `fig2h` and `fig5c` block a claim
when readings disagree, and the criterion was followed; the protocol judges
absence on the paper's test alone while the `fig2h` criterion names several
readings, and the criterion was followed; it does not cover an outcome that
depends on the seed, and for `fig2j` a reversal in a development run was noted
without deciding the outcome; some criteria define only reproduced, and
partial outcomes were graded by the protocol's general definition; it does
not say whether conclusion markdown may be changed without re-running; it
covers datasets that hold part of the paper's data but not more, and the
extra mouse was left out as an assumption with results shown with it; it is
unclear whether method details read from the paper's code belong under
`inputs`, and the GitLab commit was listed; and the duplicate-recording check
could come before the first full run.
