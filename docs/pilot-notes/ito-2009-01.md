# 001641, Ito et al. 2009

Twenty-three claims reproduced, seven partially (two on an assumption), eight
not reproduced and eight blocked, seven as methods underspecified and one for
insufficient metadata. All 46 claims are on the dandiset. The first
verification round agreed with 36 of the 46 outcomes as they stood and the
second with all 46, and nothing was proposed after the second, so there was no
third round. It pins version 0.260922.0719, the only published version; the
paper cites none. The paper, Ito and Doya, "Validation of Decision-Making
Models and Analysis of Decision Variables in the Rat Basal Ganglia" in the
Journal of Neuroscience, records single units in the nucleus accumbens (NAc)
and ventral pallidum (VP) of six rats in a probabilistic free-choice task with
a choice cue (tone A) and a no-choice cue (tone B), and reports the history
dependence of the choices (Fig. 2), the fit of the local matching law, standard
Q, F-Q and DF-Q models and Markov models with constant and, through a particle
filter, time-varying parameters (Figs. 3 to 5, Tables 1 to 3), the neurons
coding state, action, reward and action values (Fig. 8) and their mutual
information (MI) with these variables (Fig. 9). The claims were written from
the version of record in PMC (PMC6666589) and the supplemental Methods, without
a PDF of the main text, so the figure panels were not read, and without the
display equations of the Methods, which are images missing from the text; the
paper links no code, and supplemental Data 1 to 3 were not available. The check
made eleven corrections and left three points open. Forty-five claims were
tested. Reproduced are the trial and block counts (39,175, 12,707 and 8329
choice, no-choice and error trials, exactly as reported), the reaction times
(72.1% under 1 s, median 0.754 s), five of the six Fig. 2 history claims,
`binary-prediction-accuracy` (67.7%), `fig4-strategy-tracking`,
`suppdata1-choice-vs-q-difference`, the unit counts (148 NAc and 66 VP) and
firing rates, the action and reward coding counts, `fig8b-qr-no-region-difference`,
`preference-balance`, `fig8c-ql-vp-higher`, `fig8c-regression-detects-more`,
`fig8-above-chance`, the state and action MI claims and
`action-information-outlasts-reward-peak`. Partial are `fig3-lsq-switching`,
whose order and switching hold while the DF-Q residual is outside its tolerance
under every reading, `fig5-variable-models-best` (0.5304, 0.5374 and 0.5369
against the paper's 0.5304, 0.5375 and 0.5368), `fig8b-action-value-psth`,
`fig8b-ql-vp-higher`, `nac-noreward-preference` (signed-rank p = 0.0032 against
the paper's p < 0.0001), `fig8d-policy` and
`fig9-action-value-information-low`; the two on an assumption read the
criterion's partial rule as holding when its condition holds under every
reading, although some readings alone would give reproduced. Not reproduced are
`tone-discrimination-errors`, which rests on the tone-B rate (0.97 to 1.11%
under every count against 0.73 +- 0.073), `table1-lsq-parameters` (8 of 14
values, 10 needed), `table2-ml-parameters` (6 to 8 of 16 under O1 (a), 8
needed under every reading),
`fig8b-action-command-coding` (15 and 14 units against 17 and 12),
`fig8b-nac-vp-same-event-coding`, where action command differs between regions
(p = 0.029; paper p = 0.19), `fig8c-regression-action-value` (6 of 12),
`fig8d-state-value-vp-higher` (2 of 6) and `fig9-reward-information`, where
reward MI is above threshold before the L or R entry in every reading. Seven
claims are blocked as methods underspecified because their readings give
different outcomes: `fig2b-two-back-history` (what the Mann-Whitney tests rank),
`fig5-markov-accuracy` (the training and test split), `fig5-constant-models`
(the local matching law's initial value, under the Methods' Qi(1) = 0 a
constant 0.5), `fig5-fq-dfq-beat-all-models` and `fig5-fq-dfq-no-difference`
(the split and the Q update form), `table3-parameter-averages` (the Q update
form) and `fig8b-state-coding` (whether error trials count, 80 and 33 units
exactly as reported without them, 83 and 37 with them).
`core-shell-no-difference` is blocked because no unit is assigned to the NAc
core or shell. The author's first run adopted the best-supported reading where
readings disagreed and gave 29 reproduced, 7 partially, 9 not reproduced and 1
blocked. The author asked no questions of a reader, so the answer and
author-answers runs changed nothing. Round 1 disagreed with ten outcomes,
seven of them claims whose readings disagree, which the claims file blocks, and
found that every 5000-particle run used one seed, that the sigma search used
1000 particles on a 4 x 4 grid with the optimum at its edge, that the local
matching law's headline score used an assumed Qi(1) = 0.5, that early exits
probably passed the hold test of the tone-A error count, and that the O13 (b)
estimate was taken before the update with the trial's reward; in reply eleven
outcomes changed, ten to the verifier's, and fig9-action-value-information-low
moved to partially reproduced once the O13 (b) estimate was corrected, six
filter seeds (under O1 (a) every seed finds an F-Q against DF-Q difference, p
0.0030 to 0.039, under O1 (b) none, p 0.13 to 0.98), a 5000-particle search on
a 6 x 5 grid, the fits at the paper's Table 1 and Table 2 values and a recount
keeping cue tones of at least 1 s (0.56% and 0.98%) were added, and two
questions for a reader, on the fig9-reward-information criterion and on the
table3 criterion's treatment of the Q update form, were filed in
`questions/001641.yaml`, after the reader's run. Round 2 agreed with all 46,
asked that the reading of the partial rule and the initial value Qi(1) = 0.5
be stated, and found sentences the outputs did not support (that the paper's
0.39% and 0.73% lie between the counts, which holds for 0.39% only, DF-Q
leading in 8 readings stated as all 12 with O1 (b) and O5 (b), that the cue
tone stops at an early exit stated as fact, "6 to 7% of the peak" where the
output gives 5.6 to 6.7%, and others), all corrected. The author's run took
about 1 h 45 min, one development run was wasted on a harness bug that re-ran
the 13-minute model fits, and nothing in the final runs failed; the notebook
ran in 1092 s at first, 658 s after the author-answers run, 2319 s after round
1 and 2386 s at the end. An untracked `scripts/` directory, which the author
did not create, was left alone.

The trial table's `result` column refers to a description that is in none of
the files; codes 1 to 5 occur (33,243, 8084, 18,639, 141 and 104 trials), and
while codes 1 and 3 can be read from the other columns, the error codes 2, 4
and 5 cannot be told apart with certainty, so the paper's error rates (0.39%
and 0.73%) could not be recounted with certainty: the tone-A rate is not
determined (0.27 to 0.81% under different counts), while every count of the
tone-B rate gives 0.97 to 1.11%. The cue tone probably stops when the rat
leaves the center hole early, which the files do not describe: some tone-A
tones last under 1 s (1st percentile 0.46 s) against the task's 1000 to 2000
ms, 96 of the 317 tone-A error trials with no L or R entry have a cue under 1 s,
and the center exit falls within 10 ms of the tone offset in these trials as in
most trials with a full cue, so early exits cannot be told from held cues by
the exit time. The no-reward tone of choice trials (500 Hz, 250 ms) is stored
in the `SpTone Err` and `ViTone Err` series, named as the error tone, together
with the error tone, and the two are told apart only by their duration (about
0.3 s and 2 s); the error tone lasts about 2 s (8299 tones over 1.5 s) where
the paper gives 1000 ms. Units carry only `Area` (NAc or VP) and an electrode
number, and the NAc core and shell assignment the paper uses (Fig. 6,
supplemental Data 3), the bundle positions and the per-session depth are not
in the dataset. 18 of the 70 files have no units table (11 of the 12 sessions
of rat 44, and seven others), and the files do not say whether no unit was
recorded or none was accepted; the units of the other 52 sessions match the
paper's counts. Three choice trials have an L or R entry before the
center-hole exit (reaction times of -19,829, -11,554 and -5.2 s), and one error
trial has no `state`. Of the paper itself, its total of 60,221 trials is 10
more than its parts, which sum to 60,211, the number of trials in the dataset,
and the Results say the constant-parameter Q models predict worse than the
first- or second-order Markov models while the reported F-Q and DF-Q (constant)
values (0.531) exceed the first-order Markov model (0.528), as the dataset does
(0.5306 and 0.5295 against 0.5283). Questions are put to the paper's authors:
the form of the Q update (equation 3, missing from the text), how the 70
sessions were divided into training and test sets (alternate sessions in file
order give exactly the 19,986 training choice trials reported), what initial
value gave the local matching law its 0.508, what the Mann-Whitney tests of
Fig. 2B and 2C rank, how sigma_alpha and sigma_kappa were searched and whether
alpha's random walk was bounded, and whether error trials were included in the
state-coding test of Fig. 8B.

The author's points on the protocol: the claims file's rule that disagreeing
readings block a claim conflicts with step 1.6, which allows adopting the
best-supported reading as an assumption, and the first run followed step 1.6;
some criteria, such as fig9-reward-information's, have no partial rule, so the
protocol's definition was used; the Table 1 criterion counts the F-Q Q0 as a
value to match although it cancels from the fit, and the count was kept as
written; the spike clock check could not be shown in 15 sessions with few
units, which were reported as consistent with the data, not shown; and a full
5000-particle sigma search under every reading did not fit the notebook's time
limit, so the search used 1000 particles on a 4 x 4 grid and was recorded as a
deviation.
