# 001642, Ito et al. 2015

Six claims reproduced, none partially, one not reproduced (on an assumption)
and 25 blocked, 13 for missing data and 12 as methods underspecified. All 32
claims are on the dandiset. The first verification round agreed with 17 of the
32 outcomes as they stood and the second with 31, disagreeing on
`neuron-counts`; the author proposed that change and the same change on the 12
claims that depend on it, and a third round, which reviewed only these 13,
agreed with all of them. It pins version 0.260205.0153, the only published
version; the draft has the same 87 assets and size, and the paper cites none.
The paper, Ito and Doya, "Distinct Neural Representation in the Dorsolateral,
Dorsomedial, and Ventral Parts of the Striatum during Fixed- and Free-Choice
Tasks" in the Journal of Neuroscience, records units from microwire bundles in
the dorsolateral (DLS), dorsomedial (DMS) and ventral striatum (VS) of seven
rats in a task with fixed-choice blocks (the left and right tones), free-choice
blocks (a choice tone) and extinction tests, and reports the choices and the
models fitted to them (Fig. 2), the activation of phasically active neurons
(PANs) through the trial phases (Fig. 4), their mutual information (MI) with
state, action and reward (Figs. 5, 9 and 10) and the neurons coding events,
action values and state values (Figs. 6 and 8). The claims were written from
the version of record in PMC (PMC4339358) without a PDF, so the figure panels
were not read, and without the display equations of the Methods, which are
images missing from the text; the paper has no supplement and links no code.
The check made fifteen corrections and left three points open. All 32 claims
were tested. Reproduced are `trial-counts` (75,348 trials from 7 rats under
reading b on the 86 sessions with units), `trials-per-block` (22.50 ± 8.62,
47.15 ± 20.34 and 41.05 ± 27.54), the three extinction and decision-tree claims
of Fig. 2B to F, and `actor-critic-near-chance` (0.5000 to 0.5026, all within
0.0508 of 0.5080), though the fits sit at or near a bound where the model gives
exactly 0.5, so the test cannot tell the model from one that does not learn.
Not reproduced is `fig2g-fq-variable-best`: the FQ model with time-varying
parameters (0.5758 and 0.5762 on the alternate and random split) scores below
the DFQ variable model and Markov 3 and 4, also over three further
particle-filter seeds and a 5 x 5 grid of drift SDs, on 86 sessions split 43
and 43 where the paper used 202, and with the update, choice rule, initial
values, bounds and particle-filter priors assumed. `neuron-counts` is blocked
for missing data: exclusion conditions 1, 2 and 5 need waveforms or histology,
and under reading (4i) the counts without them and without the duplicate check
(265, 224, 169 units; 213, 163, 143 rule PANs) are upper limits of the paper's
counts, none below the lower end of its tolerance. The 12 claims that depend on
it are blocked with it although their analyses ran, five of them reproduced and
seven not reproduced as tested, and three of them (`fig6-above-chance`,
`fig8f-state-value-vs`, `no-policy-coding`) depend directly on claims blocked as
methods underspecified, so their notes give both reasons. The other 12 are
blocked as methods underspecified because their readings disagree, among them
the two unit sets (the dandiset's `PANs` flag and the set rebuilt from the
Methods' rule), any bin or the first crossing for the Fig. 4 activation, and
the window axis and trials of the Fig. 8 regression: `fig4b-dms-phase5` holds
in 2 of 48 readings, `fig4b-vs-phase7` in all 24 any-bin readings and none of
the 24 first-crossing ones, `fig8e-action-value-dms` in 2 of 16 and
`fig10c-vs-no-fixed-free-difference` in 27 of 32. The author's first run
adopted the best-supported reading where readings disagreed and added the
Panzeri and Treves (1996) bias correction as a reading of the MI, giving 13
reproduced, 8 partially, 9 not reproduced and 2 blocked. The author asked no
questions of a reader, so the answer and author-answers runs changed nothing.
Round 1 disagreed with 15 outcomes, eleven of them claims whose readings
disagree, which the claims file blocks, with `fig5e-action-info-cue-offset-dls`,
which held only under the added correction, with `fig5g-reward-info`, whose
code tested the first crossing at or after E5, and with `neuron-counts`, and
found that the regression ran only on the deposited PANs, that shuffling labels
across all trials lets MI cross its threshold before the cue, that the Fig. 2G
fit rested on one particle-filter run per model and a coarse 3 x 3 grid, and
that `fig10a-dls-fixed-above-free` ordered the blocks by the sustained rise
where the criterion says first crossing; in reply fourteen outcomes changed to
the verifier's, the correction became a variant that decides no outcome,
`neuron-counts` was kept not reproduced with the disagreement recorded, the
units-table printout, the regression on the Methods-rule set, the further seeds
and the finer grid and a threshold from labels permuted within blocks were
added, and four questions for a reader were filed in `questions/001642.yaml`,
after the reader's run. Round 2 argued that none of the computable counts
limits the paper's counts and asked for the VS count after conditions (3) and
(4i) without the duplicate check, which is 169, not below 161.1; it asked that
`fig8f-state-value-vs` and `no-policy-coding` be marked as resting on the
assumed FQ model, and found sentences the outputs did not support (p 0.94 and
0.87 given as 0.18 and 0.94, a failing reading of `fig4c-activation-duration`
named as one that holds, ranges ending at 0.80 and starting at 0.27 for 0.82
and 0.26, and one other), all corrected, and two more questions for a reader
were filed. The author then found that `fig6-above-chance`,
`fig8f-state-value-vs` and `no-policy-coding` should have been blocked already,
which the third round said it had missed; it left open whether these three
should be blocked as methods underspecified, and as `blocked_by` takes one
value, data_missing was kept with both reasons in the notes. The schema does
not allow `assumed` on a blocked outcome, so the flag added in round 2 was
removed and the assumption stated in a deviation. The author's run took about 2
h 10 min (04:13 to 06:21 UTC); a few code errors (a session with no units, a
read-only array, an indexing bug) were fixed during development and nothing
failed in the final runs; the notebook ran in 1346 s at first, 887 s after the
author-answers run, 2058 s after round 1, 1347 s after round 2 and 2122 s at
the end, while its header says about half an hour. Untracked entries at the
repository root (`.bashrc`, `.idea`, `scripts` and others), which the author
did not create, were left alone.

The dandiset holds 859 units (297 DLS, 255 DMS, 215 VS, 92 CS), more than the
paper's 617 stable neurons, with no column saying which passed the paper's
stability, amplitude, histology and duplicate exclusions, and no waveforms,
amplitudes or recording depths, so exclusion conditions 1, 2 and 5 and the
waveform basis of the PAN class cannot be applied. Its `PANs` flag (204, 112,
138 in DLS, DMS, VS) is described as Prop(ISI > 1 s) > 0.4, but 174 units that
pass that rule are not flagged, so the flag rests on a further criterion that
is not described; 15 CS units are flagged, though the paper analyses only DLS,
DMS and VS, and 38 flagged units have no PETH above 1 Hz around the three
events. The trial table describes
`state` as 1 = Tone A (left), 2 = Tone B (right), 3 = Tone C (choice), while the
data show 2 = left tone, 3 = right tone and 1 = choice tone: state 2 is the
only state in single fixed-choice blocks with rule (50, 0), its right choices
are never rewarded (2669 trials), state 3 left choices are never rewarded
(2026), and state 1 is the only state in free-choice blocks. The paper's model
comparison used 202 behavioural sessions (101 training, 101 test), and the
dandiset holds 86 sessions with free-choice trials, so Fig. 2G can be run only
on these. There are 87 files for the paper's 86 recording sessions; LE115_14
holds behaviour (802 trials) and no units. Some event times fall outside the
task's stated ranges: cue durations from 0.009 s where the paper gives 1 to 2 s
(1st percentile 1.04 s), centre-poke durations before the cue from 0.026 s
where the paper gives 500 to 1000 ms (1st percentile 0.51 s), and 942 choice
trials lack one of the six events or have them out of order. Of the paper
itself, the Results cite Fig. 7F for state-value neurons, meaning Fig. 8F, the
Discussion cites Fig. 8C for action-value neurons, meaning Fig. 8E, E7 is
defined as 2 s after E8, and the regression is said to have six variables and
2^6 models for the eight terms listed. Questions are put to the paper's
authors: whether the MI was corrected for bias, and by the Panzeri and Treves
estimate or by shuffling, how the four firing levels were defined and how the
random events of the threshold were drawn; which smoothing and trials the Fig.
4 EASHs used and what reaching 0.5 during a phase means; over which bins the
reward information of Fig. 5G was compared; whether the action information of
Figs. 9 and 10 was pooled over blocks of a type or computed per block, and
whether the paired Mann-Whitney U test is the Wilcoxon signed-rank test; the
window axis, the seven events, the trials and the regressor subsets of Fig. 8;
the state window and window axis of Fig. 6; and the equations, initial values,
priors, parameter ranges and training and test split of Fig. 2G.

The author's points on the protocol: the claims file's opening comment blocks a
claim whose readings disagree while step 1.6 allows adopting the best-supported
reading, and the first run followed step 1.6; the protocol does not say whether
the author may add a reading the claims file lacks but the paper supports, so
the Panzeri and Treves correction was added as a reading of the MI with the
passage cited; it is unclear whether a claim should be partially reproduced or
blocked when the readings not adopted still disagree, and partially reproduced
was recorded where the claim holds at least in part under every reading; a
claim the dataset can test only in part (86 of 202 sessions for Fig. 2G) was
tested, with the shortfall recorded as a deviation and a data_missing finding;
and the notebook runs about 22 minutes against the protocol's aim of 10, with
100 shuffles per neuron and 5000-particle filters, and the full method was
kept.
