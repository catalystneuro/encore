# 001361, Sosa et al. 2025

35 claims reproduced (18 on an assumption), none partially, eight not
reproduced (seven on an assumption) and seven blocked, four as too large to run
and three for other reasons (not attempted in the run's time). All 50 claims are
on the dandiset. The first verification round agreed with 40 of the 50 outcomes
as they stood and the second with 49, disagreeing on
`ed4l-disappearing-decrease`; the author proposed that change, and a third
round, which reviewed only it, agreed. It pins version 0.251124.0550, not the
0.250406.0045 the paper cites, for the reason given below. The paper, Sosa,
Plitt and Giocomo, "A flexible hippocampal population code for experience
relative to reward" in Nature Neuroscience, images dorsal CA1 with two-photon
microscopy in 11 switch mice running a 450 cm virtual track over 14 days, with a
hidden reward zone that moves after trial 30 on seven switch days, and reports
reward-relative (RR) cells that remap with the reward, their growth with
experience, sequences, decoding, recruitment across days, a GLM and remap timing.
The claims were written from the version of record (Europe PMC, PMC12229899) and
the paper's code (GiocomoLab/Sosa_et_al_2024 at commit adf0f9e), whose
spatial-information test and cross-day matching run in an outside package,
TwoPUtils, that the repository does not include; the check corrected the header
and eight claims and left the readings of fig1h, fig4no and
ed5-teleport-fractions open. The time-warp claims use affinewarp as an outside
input, software only. Reproduced are, among others, the behaviour of fig1h
(under reading b, 11 of 11 mice), the place-cell fraction (48.7% against 48.5 +-
4.85), remapping above chance and its growth, the preserved sequences (72, 75
and 66 of 77 sessions), `ed6c-rr-shift-toward-reward` and
`fig6e-rr-prefer-reward` (8 of 11 mice under the code's reading, 7 under the
Methods'). Not reproduced are `ed2f-teleport-distance` (6.71% after the switch,
below the lower limit of 6.75% by about 3 cells), `fig4j-rr-overrepresent-reward`
(region to 90 cm after the zone start against 70 +- 10),
`ed4l-disappearing-decrease` (far from reward p = 0.069),
`fig5d-nontr-to-rr-increases` (p = 0.27), `fig5f-nonpc-to-rr-increases` (p =
0.076), `fig5i-rr-to-tr-decreases` (p = 0.087), `ed3d-single-field-increases`
(no slope significant) and `ed3fg-fields-remap-independently` (three days
significant, on two-field counts 2.5 and 4.2 times the paper's). The decoder
(fig3b, fig3c) and the GLM (glm-well-fit-cells, fig7de) are blocked as too large,
and the remap-timing claims (fig8e, ed10) and ed5-teleport-fractions as not
attempted in the run's time; the data for all seven are in the files. The
outcomes on an assumption rest on the place-cell test of TwoPUtils, this
attempt's own cross-day matching, the rule for omissions or the rule for place
fields. The author's first run gave 27 reproduced, 11 partially, 5 not
reproduced and 7 blocked, with 60% of cells place cells against the paper's
48.5%. The author asked no questions of a reader, so the reader's run, which
found no questions file, and the author-answers run changed nothing. Round 1
disagreed with ten outcomes and found the place-cell excess unexplained, the
mixed models unchecked for convergence (the fig5i fit had a Hessian that is not
positive definite), place fields found on binned data instead of the raw
activity the Methods name, and the cross-day matching run with no alternative;
in reply the author found TwoPUtils public, adopted its test (48.7% of cells
place cells), which changed eleven outcomes, printed convergence and
mouse-clustered OLS for each fit, found fields on the raw events, added two
matching variants and a listing of a day-14 file (no depth or matching data),
kept ed4l as partially reproduced with the disagreement recorded, and filed
four questions in `questions/001361.yaml`, giving 35, 1, 7 and 7. Round 2 asked
for the two parts of the new test to be run apart (position rolled without the
0.001 offset gives 56.4%, activity rolled with it 51.0%), noted that the
followed counts still exceed the paper's (a stricter matching that follows the
paper's 2,294 RR cells leaves fig5d at p = 0.55 and fig5f at p = 0.19), and
found sentences the outputs did not support (the masks' axes and the
`reward_zone` values, a zero at 180 cm, a run time of 41 minutes and a repeated
paragraph), all corrected, the `reward_zone` finding moving from 0 to 6 to 0 to
8. Round 3 noted that only the raw fraction was fitted for ed4l, not the logit
reading of fig4m, which was added as a question. The author's run took about 3
h 20 min, with two full runs of 38 and 39 minutes because the first run's
time-warp results did not repeat; on the way the ROI-mask row and column bug
broke the cross-day matching and affinewarp failed with scikit-learn 1.6, and
after round 1 one run failed on a dropped connection, so retries were added.
The notebook ran in 41 minutes after the author-answers run, about 41 after
round 1, 41.2 after round 2 and 48.1 after round 3.

In version 0.250406.0045, the version the paper cites, every file of a mouse
carries the behaviour of one and the same session: 152 files hold 11 distinct
behaviour series, and the behaviour's length equals the imaging length in only
11 files, so imaging and behaviour cannot be aligned in the other 141; version
0.251124.0550 has 152 distinct series of the imaging's length (to one frame in
10 files). The files' Deconvolved series (unit 'lumens', no description) is not
the OASIS on dF/F the paper analyses, correlating 0.47 per session (median, 0.37
to 0.70) with it, and the files hold no dF/F, so the signals were rebuilt from F
and neuropil with the code's parameters. The place-cell fraction turns on
details the Methods do not give: with the SI and shuffle of TwoPUtils, read at a
2026 commit, 48.7% of cells are place cells, with the Methods' formula alone and
activity rolled 59.9%, with an OASIS time constant of 1.0 s 46.3%, and the 0.001
offset, which is not scale-free, does more of the work than rolling position.
The files hold no cross-day ROI matching; the attempt's matching after a rigid
alignment matches 59% of cells (median), 55% with one threshold per mouse and
61% with the TwoPUtils rule, and the outcomes of Figure 5 and Extended Data Fig.
6 are the same under the three except fig5f (p = 0.032 under the TwoPUtils rule
only, from a fit that did not converge). Following the code's field rule gives
76 RR cells with two fields before and after on day 3 (paper 31) and 84 on day 8
(paper 20). The z-stack and per-ROI depth estimates and the three
fixed-condition mice are not in the dandiset, and the files do not say how the
image axes map onto the medio-lateral and antero-posterior axes. Omitted rewards
are not marked; taking unrewarded trials with licks in the zone as omissions
gives the paper's day counts per mouse exactly. In the ROI masks 'x' is
consistent with indexing image rows ('y' reaches 630 to 788, beyond the 512
rows, while 'x' stays at or below 508), and `reward_zone`, described as binary,
takes values 0 to 8 and rises in all 10,342 rewarded trials and in 52 of the
1,874 unrewarded ones. Questions are put to the paper's authors: which TwoPUtils
version and OASIS time constant were used, and on what scale the events were;
which matching rule and registration gave the paper's matches; and which samples
the s.d. of the field rule covers.

The author's points on the protocol: it has no rule for a cited version that is
broken, so the latest was pinned and recorded as a deviation on every claim and
as a finding; its only time-related block reason is `too_large`, so claims
skipped for time that were not too large were blocked as `other`; fixing every
random seed is not enough for affinewarp, whose search runs in parallel numba
threads, so numba was seeded and the warp run on one thread; it does not say
what happens when a criterion is met but a listed expected count is out of
tolerance, so each criterion was followed as written; it forbids numbers from
preparatory runs, so for fig6e and ed8k the notes say only that the earlier,
unseeded runs varied; it does not cover copying markdown-only edits into the
executed notebook without re-running; and its ten-minute target cannot be met
for a 92 GB dataset.
