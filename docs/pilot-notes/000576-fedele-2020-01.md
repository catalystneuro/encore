# 000576, Fedele et al. 2020

No claim reproduced, three partially, 12 not reproduced and three blocked on
the dandiset; with outside data, counted apart, one partially reproduced and
six blocked. The first verification round agreed with 20 of 25 outcomes and
the second with all 25, so there was no third round. It pins version
0.231010.1811, the only published version; the paper predates the dandiset
and cites none. The paper, Fedele, Tzovara et al. in NeuroImage, records
macroelectrode iEEG and microwire units in the 14 amygdalae of nine patients
with drug-resistant mesial temporal lobe epilepsy while they watch blocks of
dynamic fearful faces (AVERSIVE) and landscapes (NEUTRAL), and relates a
sustained delta power decrease and an onset increase in high gamma power
(HGP) to neuronal firing and to the BOLD signal of a pre-operative fMRI, with
the HGP modulation index (MI) separating amygdalae outside the seizure onset
zone (nSOZ) from those within it (SOZ). The claims were written from the
version of record, a PDF supplied by a person; the paper links no analysis
code, and the data descriptor (Fedele et al. 2021) was read for the layout of
the data only. The iEEG and spike claims were tested; the six claims that
need the BOLD time courses and the muHGP claim are blocked as `data_missing`,
and `fig3d-firing-correlations-modalities` was tested on its HGP part only.
`fig1b-delta-decrease` is blocked as `methods_underspecified`, as it holds in
6 of the 12 mixed-model readings: z-scoring over the whole 26 s trial finds
no delta cluster, while z-scoring against the 2 s baseline finds AVERSIVE
below NEUTRAL over 14.3 s of the 24 s block in the nSOZ amygdalae and 15.6 s
in all 14. No reading finds the HGP increase in the first 2 s (smallest
cluster p 0.056); the three HGP clusters that begin there are variants, and
all use a baseline other than the 1 s clip. The group test finds no HGP
cluster, so the MI claims rest on fixed windows of 0 to 2 s for HGP and 0 to
24 s for delta, taken from the Results' text, an assumption; under them no
reading gives all 28 Table 2 values within tolerance (at best 4 of 28), the
delta and HGP MIs correlate at r = -0.30 (p = 0.30) where the printed values
give -0.74, and `fig1f-hgp-more-specific-than-delta` is blocked as the
readings disagree. `table2-delta-reduced-12-of-14` is partially reproduced,
as the claims file's readings give 11, 11 and 12 and only the fixed-window
trial reading gives 10. Among the neuron claims, `neuron-count` is partially
reproduced (42 units, within 4.1 of 41, but in 8 amygdalae, not 9), the mean
firing rate variability is 0.033 plus or minus 0.690 Hz with 27 of 42 units
positive (p = 0.50), the SSI correlation is 0.26 (p = 0.11) and 0.40 against
0.78, the FRV coefficient of `fig4b-regression-firing-hgp` has p 0.07 to
0.65, and `fig3b-firing-hgp-no-condition-difference` is partially reproduced,
holding in 4 of 6 readings. The author's first run gave four reproduced,
three partially, ten not reproduced and eight blocked. The author asked a
reader no questions, so the answer run and the following author run changed
nothing. Round 1 agreed with 20 of 25 outcomes; it found that outcomes were
judged on readings the author had chosen (baseline z-scoring, the 1 s fig3b
reading and an added trialsd z-scoring), that the per-trial baseline SD rests
on about 2 independent values, that the per-amygdala window set MIs to 0
where it found no cluster, that SSI did not check microwire identity, and
that the fig3b smoothing mixed neighbouring blocks. Judging on the claims
file's three z-scorings, leaving such MIs undefined and smoothing each block
on its own moved `nsoz-delta-hgp-mi-inverse` from reproduced to not
reproduced, table2-delta-reduced-12-of-14 and fig3b from reproduced to
partially, fig1f from reproduced to blocked and fig4b from partially to not
reproduced; two questions to a reader, on the binomial interval and on the
printed Table 2 values of Fig. 1E, were put during the revision after the
answer step. Round 2 agreed with all 25; it asked that the outcomes resting
on the fixed windows be marked assumed, which all but fig1f now are, as the
record's schema allows the mark only on outcomes that are not blocked, asked
why fig3b is partial and not blocked, which the notes now say, and found
sentences the outputs did not support, among them four channel locations not
naming the amygdala where there are five and two HGP clusters in the first
2 s left out, which were corrected. The author's run took about 37 minutes
(21:07 to 21:44 UTC), with two small coding errors fixed during development.
The notebook ran in about 2.7 min at first and in 4.8 min in the last run.

The BOLD time courses of the clinical fMRI, on which six claims rest, are not
public: the dandiset holds none, the OpenNeuro version (ds003374, 1.1.1)
holds only iEEG, and the paper names no deposit. The continuous microwire
signals behind muHGP are not deposited either, only spike times and mean
waveforms, and each unit is assigned only to the bipolar macro channel of its
amygdala, with no microwire, so the spatial synchronisation index cannot be
computed as defined; a spike train test flags 4 of 116 testable pairs as
likely sharing a wire. The units are in 8 amygdalae of 5 patients (1 L and R,
2 L and R, 3 L, 4 L, 7 L and R), while the paper's Results give 9 amygdalae
and its Table 1 lists multi-unit activity for patients 5 and 8 and none for
7; three of the 8 (1 R, 2 R, 7 R, with 17 units) are SOZ amygdalae, though
Discussion 3.4 says there were no microwire recordings from SOZ amygdalae,
and their mean rate is 4.65 plus or minus 3.91 Hz against the data
descriptor's 1.66 plus or minus 2.65 Hz. Every unit's obs_intervals cover all
17 epochs, but the 16 units of subject 7 have no spike after 286 s and four
units of subject 2 none in epoch 1, at rates of 1 to 16 Hz elsewhere. Each
session is deposited twice, as `_ecephys.nwb` and `_ecephys+image.nwb`, with
identical iEEG, spikes and trials, so the 18 NWB files hold 9 recordings. The
iEEG is stated in volts, but its values (standard deviation 15 to 101, up to
1546) are microvolts. The trial table gives trial 1 as 0 to 26 s, including
its 2 s baseline clip, and trials 2 to 17 as their 24 s blocks only, and the
iEEG timestamps run without a gap from 0 to 442 s though the 17 epochs are
not contiguous (median step 12 to 36 at the boundaries against 1.4 to 1.9
within epochs). The ages of subjects 2 and 8 (39 and 24) differ from those in
the paper and the data descriptor (48 and 27), and subject 2's file holds the
same subject metadata as subject 7's. The right channel of subjects 1, 5 and
9 is located in the "Right Hippocampus, rostral hippocampus" and both
channels of subject 6 are "unknown", though their labels, the paper and the
data descriptor place them in the amygdala. Run on the paper's printed Table
2 values, the Fig. 1E tests find the DELTA MI also different between nSOZ
and SOZ amygdalae (rank-sum p = 0.035, t-test p = 0.013), the chi-square
test of Fig. 1F gives p = 0.0098 (0.053 with Yates' correction), not about
0.2, and no single binomial interval method gives all three printed
intervals. Three questions are put to the paper's authors: how the power
envelopes were z-scored within each trial, as a z-score over the whole trial
removes the sustained delta decrease and a z-score against the baseline keeps
it, over which window each amygdala's MI was computed, and on what time base
and over which span the Fig. 3B correlations of firing with HGP were
computed.

The author's points on the protocol: several criteria ask for a result under
every reading while the per-amygdala cluster reading leaves most values at 0
in these data, and outcomes were judged on the readings the data can carry;
for a claim of absence with several open details, judging on the paper's
test alone leaves those details open, and one reading was adopted as an
assumption; `measured` cannot mark an expected value that cannot be tested,
so the first expected value alone was given; neighbouring claims settle the
same open detail differently, as fig1b's criterion forces a block when
readings disagree while the others allow an assumption; and the rule against
bold conflicts with the bold headers of the example notebook.
