# 000637, Gao et al. 2025

No claims reproduced, none partially, none not reproduced and 31 blocked. The
first verification round agreed with all 31 outcomes and the second with all
31, so there was no third round. It pins version 0.250829.1249, the latest
published one; the paper cites no version of 000637, and all five published
versions hold the same files with the same digests. The paper, Gao,
Ramachandran, Yu and He in the Journal of Neuroscience, steers transcranial
focused ultrasound (tFUS) from a 128-element array to S1 in anesthetized rats,
records with 32-channel probes in S1 and POm (16 rats) or in S1 and auditory
cortex (AC, 12 rats, the auditory confound group), splits sorted units into
regular-spiking (RSU) and fast-spiking (FSU) units by k-means on their
waveforms, and compares their responses across PRF and duty-cycle series,
against sham and CG1 control sessions, and through the correlation of S1 with
POm and AC (dSTTC and STTC). The claims were written from the version of
record in PubMed Central (PMC12139594), with no supplement and no PDF; the
dSTTC equation is lost from the text, and the paper links no code. No claim
could be tested: all 31 are blocked as `data_missing`, as every one needs the
RSU and FSU split, and most also need POm or AC units, sham or CG1 sessions or
duty cycles the dandiset does not hold. `unit-counts` stays blocked because
its six POm counts cannot be computed, though the S1 totals alone rule out
reproduced (at most 9 of the 12 counts could match, below the criterion's 10).
The author's first run counted 30 claims, corrected to 31 after round 1. The
author asked a reader no questions, so the answer run and the following author
run changed nothing. Round 1 found the miscount, that the notebook did not ask
whether 000637 holds the paper's recordings or note that the S1 totals rule
out reproduced, that reading N2 of the shared spiking method is biased
downward for low-rate units (16 to 23% at 3 Hz on simulated units with no
response), that the onsets shifted by 1.2 s are not a null, and that the
pooled increase in the BASELINE recordings went undiscussed; it asked for the
full HDF5 tree and text fields of every file, which were added and show
nothing beyond spike times. In reply the N2 bias was put to a reader. Round 2
found that no cell printed the subjects of 000946, and printing them corrected
the range given for them from BH494 to BH535 to BH494 to BH594; it also found
a wrong explanation of which duty-cycle conditions are present and several
small ranges that did not match the printed output, all corrected. The
author's run took about 26 minutes (07:59 to 08:25 UTC) and nothing failed;
the notebook took 332 s, most of it streaming the 292 files, and 211 s in the
last run.

The Units tables hold spike times only, with no waveforms, waveform features,
electrode assignments or quality metrics, so the units cannot be classified as
regular- or fast-spiking as the paper does, and every finding of the paper is
about one or the other; the waveforms are not public anywhere else the attempt
could find. The dandiset holds one S1 probe per recording and no POm or AC
units, no recording identified as sham or as targeting CG1, and of the paper's
duty-cycle series only the four conditions whose pulse duration is 100, 200 or
400 us, the three pulse durations the description lists (3000 Hz at 30 and
60%, 30 Hz at 0.6%, 1500 Hz at 60%). The recordings cannot be linked to those
the paper analysed: the paper's data statement names dandiset 000946, whose
rats (BH494 to BH594, 20 rats) are not those of 000637 (BH401 to BH477), the
dandiset has 25 rats against the paper's 16 + 12, and 000637 has about 3.6
times as many S1 units over the paper's 2,000 spike threshold in the PRF
series at 200 us as the paper reports S1 RSUs and FSUs (4,642 against 1,297).
The description says each recording has 500 trials; 73 of the 292 files have
fewer (230 in 45 files, 425 in 13, 375 in 11, and 252 to 400 in four). The
description calls 100, 200 and 400 us the ultrasound durations and says the
pulse duration was kept at 67 ms, the reverse of the paper's terms and the
file identifiers, and a 600 us recording (BH446) and recordings at 3.5, 5.5 and
7 V are present and not described. The stimulation condition is coded only in
each file's identifier; the trials tables have start and stop times only, the
electrode locations are all 'unknown', the one electrode group's location is
'brain area', every electrode has z = 0, and all 292 files share the session
description "Neural Spikes for S1 tFUS". Three recordings named BASELINE
(BH458, BH460, BH461; 500 trials each) are not described; pooled over their
119 units, against the paper's 84 S1 units in sham, the rate rises at 134 to
268 ms under the signed-rank test, which would weigh against
`fig5-sham-no-response` if they were the paper's sham sessions. The trial
starts behave as tFUS onsets: 74% of units fire more in the 0 to 67 ms after
onset than in the 67 ms before, against 43% with onsets shifted by 1.2 s, 23
of 25 rats show the delayed response at 201 to 268 ms, and intertrial
intervals are 2.25 to 2.75 s, as the paper states. No unit was deposited
twice. Pooling all S1 units across cell types, a variant that decides nothing,
every PRF raises the 0 to 67 ms rate and 300 to 4500 Hz raise the 201 to 268
ms rate.

The author's points on the protocol: `data_missing` is reserved for data not
public anywhere else, and the paper's own deposit, 000946, is public but holds
other animals, so `data_missing` was used as the waveforms of 000637's units
are public nowhere; there is no outcome or finding kind for a target dataset
that may not be the paper's data, and it was recorded as a finding of kind
`other`; there is no guidance on tagging analyses that decide no claim, and
they were tagged `variant:...`; and the protocol requires one notebook section
per tested claim and is silent when none is tested.
