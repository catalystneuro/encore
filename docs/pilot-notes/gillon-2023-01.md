# 000037, Gillon et al. 2023

Twenty-two claims reproduced, one partially, one not reproduced and seven
blocked on the dandiset, with two more reproduced and one more blocked on the
dandiset together with the paper's code repository. It pins version
0.230426.0054, which the paper's reference 13 cites by DOI in the Crossref
record; version 0.240209.1623 has the same blobs plus a CHANGES file. The
paper, a data descriptor in Scientific Data that reports no statistical
analyses, describes two-photon calcium imaging of somata and distal apical
dendrites of L2/3 and L5 neurons in primary visual cortex of 13 mice over 50
sessions, in which Gabor sequences and visual flow are shown with occasional
inconsistent stimuli. The subjects and sessions, imaging depths and rates, ROI
counts and tracking, the Fig. 8 matching examples, the stimulus structure and
timing, behaviour, SNR, the grey screen transients of Fig. 9, file sizes and
the link to the Fig. 7a source data were tested. `qc-failures` is blocked as
`metadata_insufficient`, as no file holds a QC result,
`fig8-somata-less-order-dependent` as `data_missing`, as per-ordering matching
counts are published for 2 of 13 mice, and `fig5-example-responses`,
`fig7a-snr-stable`, `fig7b-signal-stable` and `fig7c-correlations-similar` as
`methods_underspecified`, because the criteria's readings disagree.
`table2-sessions` is not reproduced, as 43 of 50 sessions agree in every
recorded field against the 45 needed for partial, and
`gabor-sequence-structure` is partially reproduced, as 13 of 340000 images in
4 sessions fall outside 300 ms +/- 10%. The author put three questions to a
reader; the answer widened the L2/3 dendritic depth in `imaging-depths` to the
Methods' 50 to 75 um, which moved it from not reproduced to reproduced, and
added readings to `fig5-example-responses` and
`fig7c-high-correlation-fraction` without changing their outcomes. Rounds 1
and 2 each agreed with 33 of 35 outcomes: both would give
`fig5-consistent-vs-inconsistent` as partially reproduced and
`fig7c-high-correlation-fraction` as not reproduced (0.0178% pooled against
0.005 to 0.015%), which the author kept blocked because they depend on blocked
claims (protocol author step 1.5), recording what each claim's own test gives.
Round 2 found statements the outputs did not support, among them start times
called evening Pacific times, Supplementary Table 1's notes cited but not
printed and a run time of about seven minutes against 664 s printed; these
were corrected, no change was proposed, and there was no third round. The
first loader took 16 minutes on the chunked dendritic files, and streaming
each whole file into memory with parallel HTTP range requests brought the
notebook to 417 to 431 s, 695 s after the answers and about 11 minutes (670
and 665 s) in the last runs.

The session start times are stated with a Pacific offset (-07:00 or -08:00),
but their clock values are those Supplementary Table 1 gives as UTC, for all
50 sessions, so four sessions (412933 session 765427689, and 418779's three
sessions) start a day after Table 2's imaging date and the date of birth
implied by 418779's age is a day after Table 1's; read as UTC all 50 dates
agree, and the files hold no other wall-clock time to settle it. The three
sessions of mouse 433414 are recorded at a depth of 60 um where Table 2 and
Supplementary Table 1 give 75 um. The files hold no QC result, local session
number or stimulus seed, so the paper's 33 analysed sessions (local sessions 1
to 3 that passed QC, from 11 mice) can only be selected with Supplementary
Table 1, an outside table to which the dandiset's description points; none of
the 23 small datasets and attributes under general and at the top level of
each file mentions QC. The imaging plane is not recorded and follows from the
depth, the field of view in um is not recorded, and the visual flow square
size and position are given in pixels of the unwarped stimulus with no screen
geometry to convert them to the degrees the paper states, though their ratio,
6.09 per second, is within 3% of the paper's 50/8 = 6.25. The per-ordering ROI
matching counts behind Fig. 8 are published for two mice only, and the
paper's modified matching code is not linked. Other findings: the dF/F of the
28 dendritic sessions is stored gzip-compressed in small chunks (991 to 1994
frames by 5 to 26 ROIs) with the chunk index spread through the file, which
makes streaming it through remfile and h5py slow, where the 22 somatic files
store it contiguous; and in two sessions the response to grey screen
transitions does not exceed the 99th percentile of the responses at shifted
times (777496949, 15 ROIs, above 94.6% of them; 838633305, which failed QC and
whose experimental notes say the laser was set to 800 nm, while the file
states 910 nm), so their alignment to the trial table could not be shown,
though it is shown in the other 48. Every ROI's SNR is within 1% of the Fig.
7a source data (largest relative difference 3.4e-05), and no recording is
deposited twice.

The author's points on the protocol: the claims file gives no version to pin,
so the DOI was taken from the Crossref reference; the rule that blocks a claim
depending on a blocked claim also blocks dependents whose own test is fully
specified when the parent is blocked only on its decision rule; step 1.6
covers missing method details, not an ambiguous decision rule inside a
criterion; an author has no channel for a criterion stricter than the claim's
own statement; the gabor-sequence-structure criterion has no partial rule; the
several-readings rule covers the paper's text, not ambiguous metadata in the
dataset such as the time zone, which decides table2-sessions; streaming
through remfile and h5py was too slow for the chunked files; the `data`
categories have no slot for the paper's own supplementary table used to
select sessions; the notebook may not read local paths, so it cannot check
Supplementary Table 1 against Tables 1 and 2; and the suggested clock checks
do not fit this design, so the response at grey screen transitions was
compared against 500 time-shifted copies.
