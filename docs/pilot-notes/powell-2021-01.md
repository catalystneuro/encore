# powell-2021, Powell et al. 2021

Ten claims reproduced, four partially, four not reproduced and four
blocked, all four for missing data; one outcome,
`fig3-evoked-7-to-21-in-9-of-10`, rests on an assumption. The first
verification round agreed with 20 of the 22 outcomes as they stood and the
second with all 22; nothing was proposed after the second, so there was no
third round. The record pins version 3924718, the Zenodo record's own id,
as archives.md says. The paper, Powell, Haddad, Gorur-Shandilya and Marder
in eLife (10, e60454), records the pyloric (PD) and gastric mill (LG, DG)
rhythms of the isolated stomatogastric nervous system of Cancer borealis
across temperature, and reports that spontaneous gastric mill rhythms occur
from 11 to 30 C, that rhythms evoked by dpon stimulation from 7 to 21 C
speed up with temperature like the pyloric rhythm, with a Q10 of about 2,
that LG and PD stay integer-coupled and LG phase-locked to PD, and that DG
is neither. The claims were written from the version of record (PMC7889077)
and the paper's code (marderlab/gastric at the archived commit fa07d36 and
the later 7a82b1f); the claims check corrected the source paragraphs of
nine claims and the statements or criteria of six, and left three points
open. Reproduced are the evoked range (9 of 10 preparations), the decrease
of PD and LG periods in all 10, the PD and LG Q10s not differing, the
slowdown after stimulation, the Figure 5a example ratios (10.96 and 10.87),
LG to PD integer coupling, the absence of DG coupling, the ratio's
independence of temperature, and the absence of DG phase locking and of a
DG spiking drop. Not reproduced are the Q10 of about 2 (1 of 10
preparations under the Methods' formula), the ratio range (2.5th percentile
7.04 against 5), the drop of LG spiking near the PD burst end, which grows
shallower from 7 to 21 C, and the spontaneous against evoked comparison of
Figure 3, figure supplement 1 (only 901_080 similar of the three
testable). Partial are the coupling areas, LG phase locking, the burst start
counts (DG 3573 against 3228) and the constant LG phase (bin means 0.416 to
0.494). Blocked are the spontaneous rhythms of Figure 2 and the experiments
with the PD neurons hyperpolarized of Figure 4, figure supplement 2. The
evoked range outcome counts only bins held at least 120 s and passes
901_062, which was never brought to 21 C; requiring 21 C gives 8 of 10, and
counting every bin crossed gives 3 of 10. The author's first run gave 9
reproduced, 3 partially, 5 not reproduced and 5 blocked, and reported that
no outcome rested on an assumption. Round 1 disagreed on the evoked range
and the burst start counts and found that three of the four preparations
of the spontaneous against evoked comparison could be tested; in reply the
evoked range moved to reproduced and was marked assumed, that comparison
moved from blocked to not reproduced, the start counts to partial, a Q10
variant with measured temperatures was added, the events dropped at even
rounded temperatures were printed, and four questions were put to a reader
in `questions/powell-2021.yaml`. Round 2 found sentences the outputs did
not support (a logic error on the comparison, 0.37 for 0.361, a
per-preparation Q10 of 705 described as per bin, the README's run time) and
a sortedness check that always printed True; once fixed, it found the DG
spike times unsorted, a new finding. The author's run took about 55
minutes, ending at 15:57 UTC; three development runs failed (a wrong
metadata field, a column named `T` and a Zenodo HTTP 500, after which reads
were retried), and the notebook ran in 233 s in a clean environment and in
one to two minutes at the end.

The record holds spike annotations and raw data only for the ten evoked
preparations: the nine preparations with spontaneous rhythms (Haddad and
Marder 2018) and the nine with the pyloric pacemaker hyperpolarized are not
in it, although the paper's data statement names only this record, and a
search of Zenodo and DANDI found no deposit of them (Zenodo 5139650 holds
six ABF files of notebooks 845 and 857, with no spike annotations), so four
claims cannot be tested. 941_003.zip and 941_006.zip hold raw ABF
recordings of 11 and 12 June 2019 with no spike annotations or
description; they belong to none of the three sets the Methods describe,
and 941_006 is one of the four preparations of Figure 3, figure supplement
1. There is no zip of raw recordings for 901_005, 932_151 is dated 2 May
2019 where the Methods say 2017, and the zip of 901_062 holds 101 files
where evoked_data.mat stacks 58. The DG spike times are not sorted in nine
of the ten preparations (129 descents); the paper's getEvokedData.m sorts
PD and LG only, which changes the code's DG bursts by -2 to +6 per
preparation (3675 against 3667). The record has no README, its description
is the paper's abstract, the fields of evoked_data.mat are not described,
the preparation and file names are MATLAB categorical objects readable
outside MATLAB only by decoding the MCOS subsystem, T and time_offset are
empty, and Zenodo types the record as a journal article. The file matches
the paper's analysis: with the code's burst rule and computations the
reported numbers come out exactly (t = 1.27, p = 0.235; 3799 cycles and
Spearman p = 0.88; rho = -0.31, p = 0.46; 3816 LG burst starts). The code
departs from the Methods three times: its burst rule keeps bursts of at
least n + 1 spikes where the Methods say n; the reported Rayleigh ranges
are those of the later code's first 50 starts, not the Methods' first 40,
and the archived code's pooled sample gives z of 67 to 577; and its Q10 is
computed per burst, which gives the reported 8 of 10 and t-test where the
Methods' formula gives 1 to 3 of 10. Figure 3, figure supplement 1 counts
4 of 10 evoked preparations with spontaneous rhythms, but its script uses
941_006, which is not among the ten; 3 of the ten burst before their first
stimulation at 11 C, and 932_151 at 9.83 to 9.93 C may be the fourth. One
question is put to the paper's authors, which bins they counted for the
evoked range, and the reader questions ask whether the code's methods
should decide the Q10, the start counts and the phase locking, and which
bound decides LG to PD coupling.

The author's points on the protocol: the rule to stream data has no
exception for a survey, and one temporary copy of the 73 MB file was kept
while exploring; there is no guidance for a code variant that runs through
every claim, and the Methods rule was run as the main analysis with the
code's as a variant throughout; details the claims file leaves open were
run under both readings; archives.md has no Zenodo example; the `lock`
step did not write the `python` field until `all` ran; the protocol says
nothing on retries after a transient archive error; and it is awkward to
classify mismatches between the files and the paper, such as the 9 of 10
count, as about the data, which were filed as `other`, minor.
