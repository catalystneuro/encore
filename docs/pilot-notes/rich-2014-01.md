# rich-2014, Rich et al. 2014

Nineteen claims reproduced, three partially, four not reproduced and one
blocked. The first verification round agreed with 23 of the 27 outcomes as
they stood and the second with all 27; one change was proposed after the
second, so a third round was held on that claim alone, and it agreed. CRCNS
does not version its data sets, so the attempt records hc-31 as `unversioned`,
with DOI 10.6080/K0BV7DT2 and the MD5 of `resSave.mat` (167 MB), which matches
`checksums.md5`; the paper cites no version. The paper, Rich, Liaw and Lee,
"Large environments reveal the statistical structure governing hippocampal
representations" in Science, records 253 putative pyramidal cells in dorsal
CA1 of five rats exploring a novel 48 m track built in four sections, and
finds that the number of place fields per cell follows a gamma-Poisson
(negative binomial) model in which cells differ in their propensity to form
fields, so that only about 65% of cells are recruited at 48 m and 90% would be
at about 500 m, and that the propensity is related to firing rates in sleep
before the track. The claims were written from the version of record and its
supplement; the last page of the PDF supplied has a damaged content stream, so
Fig. 4 and its legend could not be read, and the paper links no analysis
code. Fields were recomputed from spikes and position by the Methods, with the
data set's own fields as a variant: 852 fields under the pass reading P-a,
against the data set's 887, of which they overlap 842. Reproduced are, among
others, `cell-counts` (63, 58, 24, 57 and 51, exact), `fig3b-overdispersion`,
`fig3b-negbin-fit` (r 0.563, p 0.143), `fig3c-recruitment-fraction` (64.0%),
`fig3c-extrapolation-500m` (472 m, while the printed parameters give 436 m and
the data set's fields 447 m), `fig3d-uniform-density` (KS 0.0315 against 0.03
+/- 0.003), `fig3e-memoryless-recruitment`, `fig4a-pre-sleep-rate` (r 0.450),
`fig4b-single-field-proportion` and `fig4c-active-subsets`, the last on the
assumption that the simulated environment sizes are spaced linearly (G-b).
Partial are `fig3c-lomax-recruitment` (KS 0.0600, outside 0.05 +/- 0.005, on
the assumption P-a), `figs15-higher-threshold` (38.3 to 44.0% of fields above
4.8 Hz, outside 50 +/- 5%) and `figs5-silent-cells-low-rate` (0.0141 to 0.0259
Hz against 0.006 and 0.01). Not reproduced are
`figs10-no-track-or-speed-influence` (3 of 12 track-angle bins outside the
shuffle's interval, binomial p 0.020, fragile to the binning),
`figs11-no-boundary-remapping` (p 0.026 to 0.045 at the boundary of sections 3
and 4, on P-a), `figs12-pairs-uncorrelated` (mean correlations of 0.0065,
0.0088 and 0.0281 at 48, 24 and 6 bins, above the null, though inside a null
that keeps each animal's field locations) and `figs14-field-properties` (field
size correlates with location or with the number of other fields under every
reading). `figs3-cluster-quality` is blocked as `data_missing`. P-a, a pass
that crosses the whole field, was assumed for three claims whose outcome the
other reading (any entry into the field) changes. The reader amended two
criteria: for fig4b the factor of 10 was not in the paper and became the width
the printed parameters give, less 10% (5.0 and 3.9), which moved it from not
reproduced to reproduced, and for fig4c the measure became the share of
environment pairs above a level (0.1 and 0.2). Round 1 disagreed on figs6,
figs10, figs11-no-boundary-remapping and fig4c, applying the claims file's
rule that the outcome is the one all readings give, and found that P-a dropped
every field at a section end; with an end allowance of 20 cm P-a went from 791
to 852 fields, `figs7a-mean-fields-per-animal` and fig3d moved from partially
reproduced to reproduced, and with a separate shuffle generator for each field
set figs10 failed under both pass readings. Round 2 agreed with all 27 and
asked that the allowance be varied: fig3d holds from 10 to 30 cm, with a margin
of 0.0015 at 20 cm, and fig3c-lomax is within tolerance only at 10 cm. It also
found the reasons given for logarithmic spacing in fig4c only partly apt; the
author rendered Fig. S18, whose sizes are drawn on linear axes from 0 to 50 m,
and proposed reproduced under linear spacing, which round 3 agreed with. In the
last revision the branch's attempt schema rejected the `about` field that the
newer `tools/entry.py` requires on `other` findings, so main's schema was put
in place for the checks only and the branch's restored, and a check on the
branch with its own schema fails on those three lines. The notebook ran in 2.8
min with 1.1 GB peak memory at first, 210 s after the answer, 266 s after
round 1 and 245 s at the end; `tools/entry.py all` took 5.8 min after round 2.

The L-ratio and isolation distance of every unit are NaN, in the unit's
overall cluster info and in each epoch's own (PRE, RUN1, RUN2 and RUN3), and
the data set holds only the spikes of the sorted units, with no unclustered
threshold crossings and no waveforms (the width and wave fields are empty), so
the cluster quality analysis of Fig. S3 cannot be repeated and the waveform
criterion for excluding interneurons cannot be checked; `csc_files.tar.gz`
holds one LFP channel per rat. The scale model of the track, which the paper
uses for the track angle and corners of Fig. S10, is not in the data set, and
the track was reconstructed from the animal's room position. The data set
holds the paper's own fields, which give the per-animal means of Fig. S7A
exactly and r 0.565, p 0.139, but its in-field rate is not described: on the
same field extents it averages 5.45 Hz, against 4.94 Hz for the mean of a
recomputed smoothed map and 5.39 Hz for spikes over occupancy. The readme gives
no units: linearized and room positions are in inches, open-field positions in
camera pixels with a per-animal unitsPerCm, speed in inches per microsecond
and spike times in microseconds; the direction column is 180 for outbound
travel, and the direction code of the fields (1 inbound, 2 outbound, 3 both) is
not described. The region of every unit of ANM228899 is N/A, so its CA1
localization cannot be checked. The data set's rate maps and fields use 1 inch
bins where the Methods give 1 cm. The section extents give sections of about
3.7, 6.1, 11.5 and 26.9 m, where the Methods give 3, 7, 12 and 26 m. With the
file's unitsPerCm the open field spans about 100 to 119 cm by 72 to 97 cm,
where the Methods describe an oval of about 40 x 100 cm, and one open-field
session lasts 9.2 min, against 10 to 25 min. Spikes and position share a
clock, as do the ripple periods, units look like the same cells across epochs,
and no unit is deposited twice. Questions are put to the paper's authors: what
counts as a pass through a field; how the corners of Fig. S10B were defined;
and how the 300 environment sizes of the active subset simulation were spaced
and what proportion of cells counts as distinguishing two environments.
Questions for a reader remain on whether the fig4c criterion's fitted equal
rate tests the paper's statement, on a stale note in the fig4b checks and on
the bin widths of figs10.

The author's points on the protocol: the prompt asks for a published version,
which CRCNS does not have, and archives.md was followed (`unversioned` with the
MD5); some criteria name no outcome between reproduced and not reproduced
(figs15, figs5), and the protocol's general definition of
`partially_reproduced` was used; some criteria say "under one reading" while
the claims file's opening says the outcome is the one all readings give, and
"under one reading" was let decide; "fails under all of them" is ambiguous for
a figs10 panel with one applicable test reading; the questions file format for
data sets outside DANDI is not given; and step 1.2 asks to show that a unit is
the same across sessions without a method, and the nearest mean peak amplitude
on the same tetrode was used.
