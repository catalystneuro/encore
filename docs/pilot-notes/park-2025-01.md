# 001266, Park et al. 2025

Four claims reproduced, none partially, 12 not reproduced and 34 blocked on
the dandiset, 29 for missing data and five as `methods_underspecified`. With
outside data (the paper's model code), counted apart: two reproduced and two
not reproduced. The link claim `source-data-index` is reproduced. The first
verification round agreed with 46 of the 55 outcomes as they stood and the
second with 54, and the second revision proposed one change, so a third round
reviewed it and agreed. It pins version 0.250710.0758; the paper cites no
version, and the two published versions have identical assets. The paper,
Park, Wong-Campos et al., "Dendritic excitations govern back-propagation via a
spike-rate accelerometer" in Nat Commun, images voltage with Optopatch-V in CA1
pyramidal neurons of acute hippocampal slices from mice, with patterned
optogenetic stimulation and in some cells patch clamp, and follows how
back-propagating action potentials (bAPs) and dendritic spikes (dSpikes)
depend on firing rate, with a NEURON model and a two-compartment Izhikevich
model; the dataset holds the figures' example recordings as raw camera movies
with DAQ channels, 84 files, and the paper's Source Data sheet "Raw data in
Dandi_Archive" serves as the index from figure panels to files. The claims
were written from the version of record (PMC11794848) with its Supplementary
Information; the paper links no analysis code, only the simulation code as
Supplementary Software. `fig2a-dspike-dichotomy`, `fig4a-step-example`,
`figs18g-ba-increases-calcium-spikes` and `figs2d-frame-rate-same-sensitivity`
(p = 0.14, 7 cells) reproduce, as do the model claims
`figs20ab-model-patch-vs-opto` and `figs21-izhikevich`, the latter on a line by
line Python port of the MATLAB script. Not reproduced are the length constant
of Fig. 2h (no reading gives 255 ± 10 µm; `measured` 1457.0), the dSpike maps,
motifs, third-bAP and predepolarization claims of Figs. 2 and 3, `fig3f`
(46 third bAPs against 54 ± 5.4), `fig4d`, `figs15`, `fig5a-plateau-collision`
(146 ms against 120 ± 12), `figs23` (206 ms against 270 to 330), `figs2`, and
the model claims `fig4f-g-model-accelerometer` and `figs20-model-robustness`,
where no run gives the soma motif F, S, S, F and the motif region of the 429
sweep runs never includes the Table 1 parameters. `fig2b`, `fig3d`, `figs17e`,
`fig5d-single-bap-no-plateau` and `fig1-denoising-variance` are blocked because
the readings disagree, and 29 claims resting on pooled samples are blocked as
`data_missing`. The author's first run gave 7 reproduced, 5 partially, 10 not
reproduced and 33 blocked, with `figs20-model-robustness` blocked as
`too_large` and `figs21-izhikevich` as not runnable. The author asked a reader
nothing at first, so the answer and author-answers runs changed nothing. Round
1 disagreed with nine outcomes, and in reply the five partial results moved to
not reproduced, all 429 sweep runs were run in parallel and the Izhikevich
script was ported, while `fig1` and `figs17e` moved from not reproduced to
blocked as their readings disagree; it also found that the dSpike threshold rule
turned a missing trough into zero dSpikes, which was changed to report the
count as undefined, and in reply a region overlay, amplitude histograms, the
488 nm drive per stimulus, a shared threshold for Fig. S18g, fig5b ratios for
10 cells, Table 1 model runs and a question to a reader on the somatic
threshold were added, while `fig3d` stayed reproduced on a 4 s.d. threshold and
`fig3i` stayed `data_missing`. Round 2 disagreed with `fig3d`, the 4 s.d.
threshold matching the legend's totals in none of the files, and found
sentences the outputs did not support (the 4 s.d. "legend totals", the
D4+soma dSpikes on the second bAP, the Ni2+ readings, the run time of the
simulations notebook and others), all corrected; a second question to a reader
was added for `figs15`, and the pixel weighting for `figs2` brought the
noise-equivalent voltage to 1.9 ± 0.6 mV at 1 kHz and 2.5 ± 0.5 mV at 2 kHz.
The second revision proposed `methods_underspecified` for `fig3d`, the filters
giving 32 and 31 of 54 against 35 ± 3.5 at 5 s.d., and round 3 agreed. The
author's run took about 2 h 55 min, finishing at 16:32 UTC, one full run
having had a bug in electrical spike counting that was fixed; the main notebook
took 26 minutes (it streams 41 GB) and the simulation notebook 2 minutes at
first, 1459 s and 99 s after the author-answers run, 17 and 36 minutes after
round 1, 26 and 34 after round 2 and about 27 and 35 at the end. In the first
revision the simulation work, done in a delegated run, held the session up for
almost two hours. The changes to `tools/build_site.py` and `tools/site_assets/`
were found already there and left untouched.

The dandiset holds the example recordings of the figures (the 83 rows of the
Source Data index, 84 files), not the pooled samples behind the paper's
statistics: the 17 neurons of Fig. 3, the 43 branches of Fig. 4b, the 28 cells
of Fig. 5b, the drug series of Figs. 6 and S16 to S18, the temperature series,
the patch recordings of Fig. S1 and the calibration series of Figs. S3 to S5,
so 29 of the 55 claims cannot be tested. The files hold only raw camera movies
and DAQ channels, with no pixel size, no structural (2P or HiLo) or eYFP
images, no stimulus masks and no regions of interest, so distances from the
soma, stimulus positions and the paper's user-drawn regions cannot be
recovered; the pixel size used (0.65 µm) is from the Methods. The paper's
Supplementary Software 1 does not run as published: run_morphology.py omits
the third argument ka_cutoff that FullCA1Model requires, cheriff.mod carries
non-ASCII characters that NEURON 9 rejects, and with these fixed the soma step
gives success, success, success, success, failure at 500 µm, not the failure,
success, success, failures of Fig. 4f; its phase-diagram sweeps give the motif
in 11, 17 and 0 cells, none at the Table 1 parameters, where the matrices
published in the same zip give 51, 48 and 88, and FullCA1Model registers a
per-step callback that outlives the model, so runs in one process slow down
and can affect each other. Every file is its own subject (81 subjects for 84
files), with date_of_birth 1900-01-01 and sex U, so animals and cells can be
told apart only through the file names. Three recordings are deposited twice
with identical sizes and first frames. Camera frames are 1.03 ms apart (971
Hz; 1.04 ms in one file) where the paper states 1 kHz, and 0.52 ms (1923 Hz)
where it states 2 kHz, the stated rates being rounded. The camera series has
"no description", the DAQ channels have short labels that differ between files
for the same signal (the EFS trigger is stim1, stim1 (distal), EC, EC_elec or
EC_stim), and the amplifier gains are given only in the dandiset description.
File names and figure legends name stimulus sites differently: the Fig. 2
legend's "D1 stim" is the file named D3, and the Fig. 3 legend's D2 is the
file named D4 (its 84 bAPs match the legend's 49 + 35). In the Fig. 2
recording 7 to 10 of the 54 trials have three somatic spikes, where the paper
says each stimulus evoked two, and most dSpikes ride on the third. A somatic
threshold of 5 robust s.d., the claims file's standard choice, misses spikes in
bursts (139 to 150 against the legend's 167 and 170 bAPs), and somatic
detection in the wide-field and drug files swings up to threefold with the
settings, with no patch channel to check it against. Questions are put to the
paper's authors: what somatic spike threshold, dSpike threshold and distal
region were used for the counts of Figs. 2 to 4 and S8 to S18, how dF_ref was
taken for bursts and how the length constant of Fig. 2h was fitted, over what
window the AUCs of Figs. 5 and 6 were integrated, how the noise-equivalent
voltage of Fig. S2 was defined and on which pixels, and which pixels and
preprocessing went into the PCA of Fig. S7b.

The author's points on the protocol: the claims file said its somatic
threshold "cannot change the outcome" when it does, and the threshold was
treated as an open detail with both values run; the claims file makes
disagreeing readings `methods_underspecified` while step 1.6 lets the author
adopt one, and the claims file was followed for its readings and step 1.6 for
added ones; no blocked reason fits a run short of time but not
computation-bound; `archives.md` says to read files with pynwb, while they were
read with h5py over remfile; and the protocol is silent on non-ASCII characters
in published code, which were stripped from mod-file comments and recorded as a
deviation.
