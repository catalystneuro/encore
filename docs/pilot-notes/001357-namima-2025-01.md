# 001357, Namima et al. 2025

Ten claims reproduced (one on an assumption), three partially, eight not
reproduced and two blocked, one for missing data and one as
`metadata_insufficient`. With outside data, counted apart: four blocked, three
for missing data and one as `methods_underspecified`. The first verification
round agreed with 25 of the 27 outcomes as they stood and the second with all
27, and nothing was proposed after the second, so there was no third round. It
pins version 0.261006.2007, the only published version; the paper cites none.
The paper, Namima, Kempkes, Zamarashkina, Owen and Pasupathy, "High-Density
Recording Reveals Sparse Clusters (But Not Columns) for Shape and Texture
Encoding in Macaque V4" in J Neurosci, records good units with Neuropixels
probes in dorsal V4 of two macaques, M1 and M2, during a shape test of 15
shapes at 8 rotations, dark and bright, a texture test of 40 textures, original
and contrast-reversed, and a position control, and relates the noise-corrected
tuning similarity of neurons on one penetration to their distance along the
probe (the intercept Isimilarity and slope Ssimilarity of each neuron's
regression line) to find sparse clusters of similarly tuned neurons and no
columns. The claims were written from the version of record, which has no
supplement; the paper links no analysis code, and the noise-corrected
correlation was written from the Methods and Pospisil and Bair (2021).
Reproduced are `methods-sessions-and-neurons` (30 and 44 shape sessions, 17 and
34 texture sessions, 1,869 and 985 responsive neurons), the M2p6 invariance
count (20 of 24, under reliability reading (a)), the M1p12 strong responses (28
and 26, the paper's numbers), the intercept-slope correlation (r = -0.768), the
contrast invariance (0.632, under reliability (b) with unreliable values counted
as 0), the sparse clusters across penetrations, the 20% asymptote (21.5%), the
smaller spread of penetration means at high yield, the recording density
correlation (0.257, under reading (a)) and, on the assumption that the aggregate
RF center is not file position 4, `position-optimal-consistent` (r = 0.325, 721
neurons against 723). Partial are `ex-m2p6-cluster` (13 neurons against 11, 17
missing and 12, 21 and 22 added), `ex-m2p6-pairs` (the bright similarity of
neurons 18 and 19 at -0.144 against -0.25) and `pop-within-vs-across-sessions`,
whose direction holds under every reading while no reading matches the four
means. Not reproduced are `ex-m1p12-no-cluster` (4 significant intercepts, 1
positive and 3 negative, where the criterion allows 2),
`pop-significant-isimilarity` (always a minority, but no reading within
tolerance, the closest giving 394 against 344), `pop-dark-bright-consistent`
(0.484 against 0.435), `orientation-auc-coarse-clusters`, the three texture
claims (the consistency of original and reversed textures holding more strongly
than reported, 0.706 against 0.464) and `position-examples-invariant`. Blocked
are the CSD claim, for the missing LF signal, the gyral penetrations, which the
files do not identify, the orientation and curvature claims, which need the
shape images and which stim_ID is which shape, and the simulation, whose
parameters the paper does not give. The author's first run gave 11 reproduced,
4 partially, 6 not reproduced and 6 blocked. The author asked a reader whether
Figure 11's n of 16 and 55 counts the neurons of the similarity matrices; the
reader answered that it does (the RF insets read 8 and 26), so the claim and
its 10% rule stand, and the author-answers run added a table of each candidate
session's counts and changed no outcome. Round 1 disagreed with two outcomes,
putting `ex-m1p12-no-cluster` and `pop-significant-isimilarity` at not
reproduced, and found that the paper's counts of neurons without a line (2
shape and 48 texture) largely rule out reliability reading (a), that five M1
texture files failing the clock check entered every texture count, that the
position center is the author's own heuristic, that the dataset's p09 label is
the likelier M2p9, that the bootstrap interval is not a confidence interval of
the corrected r and that the noise of simultaneously recorded neurons was taken
as independent; in reply both outcomes moved as the verifier gave them, a reader
was asked about the reliability reading, variants without the five files and
with shared noise subtracted (400 to 274 neurons) were added, no outcome
changing, and the center variants ran under both reliability readings, while
`methods_underspecified` was kept where the verifier would give `other` and the
claims file's rule kept p12 as M2p9. Round 2 agreed with all 27, asked that
`position-optimal-consistent` be marked as assumed, which was done, and found
sentences the outputs did not support (neurons without a line placed in small
penetrations, M2p28 said to fail both readings and whichever session is M2p9,
the paper's 1,847 matched to a different count, and horizontal bins of 0 to 500
um where 0 to 599 um is printed), all corrected; it also noted that the search
for the shapes had not been verified, and the second revision's new search found
the outlines of the 370 Pasupathy and Connor shapes at a pinned commit
(github.com/deanpospisil/v4cnn, commit b0532e9), leaving the shape claims
blocked since the dataset does not say which 15 were used or which stim_ID each
is. The author's run took about 1 h 20 min (06:52 to 08:10 UTC) and nothing
failed; the notebook ran in 457 s at first, 296 s after the author-answers run
and after round 1, and 416 s at the end. Untracked dotfiles in the repository
root, which the author did not create, were left alone.

The dandiset holds spike times of good units only: the LF signal behind the CSD
analysis, the RF-mapping experiment, the stimulus images and the penetrations'
grid coordinates are not in it (`acquisition` and `processing` are empty in all
159 files, and the electrode location is 'V4' for every channel), so the CSD,
feature-model and gyral-penetration findings cannot be tested. Shapes are coded
by stim_ID 0 to 14 and the position test's positions 0 to 4 without saying
which shape each code is or which position is the aggregate RF center, and the
position with the largest population response is position 1 in 12 files, 2 in
11, 4 in 5, 0 in 3 and 3 in 3, so the center cannot be read from the data
either. Spikes and presentations share a clock in 152 of 159 files; it could
not be established in five M1 texture files (2020-07-15, 07-19, 07-29, 07-30,
08-04), where 0% to 12% of units are tuned at the true onsets while 9% to 47%
fire more on stimuli than on blanks, which points to stimulus labels that do not
match the presentations, nor in the two single-unit files of 2021-05-29. The
files' `pen_ID` labels identify the example penetrations, giving the paper's
counts for M2p6 (24), M1p12 (42) and M2p28 (55), and the texture files' good
units sum to the paper's 1,345 exactly, where the shape files hold 2,296 against
2,311. The trial tables of all 34 position-test files give the session as
'...SL', the shape test's code, while their units tables and file names give
'...SP'. The shape files hold a third luminance level, 8 cd/m2 (the
background), in 73 of 74 files, which neither the paper nor the dandiset
description mentions, and the texture files hold a spectral-noise version the
paper does not use and do not store the rotation of each presentation. Unit
depth is the best channel's distance from the tip in 20 um steps, not the
template center of mass of the Methods, and it gives the paper's distances for
the example pairs (260 and 80 um) and spans (2,340 and 2,580 um). The Methods
say shape and texture tests were run in 34 M2 sessions, while 29 of the 34 M2
texture files share a day and penetration label with a shape file and 5 (p07,
p17, p40, p42, p53) are texture-only days, and the 2021-05-30 texture file is
labelled p40, as is the 2021-05-29 shape file. The position test has 34 files
against the paper's 33, one (2021-05-29, p40) holding a single unit, and the
shape test of that day is a penetration with one responsive neuron, where the
paper reports at least 6. Questions are put to the paper's authors: whether a
similarity is unreliable when the width or the half-width of its 95% bootstrap
interval exceeds 0.5 (1,729 against 1,805 shape neurons with a line, the paper
having 1,848, and 681 against 923 texture neurons, against 931), how unequal
repeats enter the bias correction, what the estimator gives when a neuron's
bias-corrected variance is not positive (the cause for 62 of the 64 shape
neurons without a line under the half-width reading, where the paper has 2),
and whether the similarity of simultaneously recorded neurons corrects for their
noise correlation, which lowers the count of significant positive Isimilarity
from 400 to 274 against the paper's 344.

The author's points on the protocol: pop-significant-isimilarity's criterion
defines only reproduced, and the author first recorded it as partially
reproduced, changed to not reproduced after round 1; a clock check by
stimulus-against-blank rates failed in many files because stimuli suppress some
units, so a per-unit ANOVA at true onsets against onsets shifted by -1.3 s was
used; the protocol says to ask a reader only when a misreading could change the
outcome, and the Figure 11 question, which changes half of the claim but not the
outcome, was asked anyway and said so; the bootstrap of a noise-corrected
estimator sits below the point estimate, so only the interval's width was used,
recorded as a deviation; and no pinned public copy of the shapes was found in
the first search, so those claims were blocked as `data_missing`, as the
protocol requires for an outside input that is not public.
