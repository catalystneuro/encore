# wang-2025, Wang et al. 2025

One claim reproduced, one partially, none not reproduced and 27 blocked, all
for missing data, plus one claim linking the dandiset to the authors' Zenodo
deposit, reproduced (all 16 areas of the deposit's area grouping are among the
dataset's annotations, and all 106 sessions of its Fig. 6 and 7 file match a
dataset session). The first verification round agreed with the link claim and
with `fig6e-marker-clustering`, would have given `data-scale` partially
reproduced, and gave `data_missing` in place of `too_large` as the block
reason; the author recounted the structures and changed the block reason on 27
claims, the second round agreed with all 30 outcomes, and nothing was proposed
after it, so there was no third round. The attempt pins 0.230822.0128, the
version the paper cites. The paper, Wang, Kurgyis, Chen et al., "Brain-wide
analysis reveals movement encoding structured across and within brain areas"
(Nature Neuroscience), predicts the activity of neurons recorded on Neuropixels
probes across the mouse brain from orofacial video, by tracked markers, a
16-dimensional autoencoder embedding and end-to-end networks, and reports that
the variance explained is highest in medulla and lowest in thalamus, changes
smoothly within thalamus, leads or follows movement by area and by depth in
ALM, and that choice can be decoded from uninstructed movements in a way
specific to each mouse. The claims were written from the version of record and
the Supplementary Information, and from the paper's code at commit 7d27624;
the deep-network repository could not be fetched, and the code of the choice
decoder of Figs. 6 and 7 is in neither repository. The claims check corrected
nine of the 29 claims. Reproduced is the scale of the data: 154,948 units with
`unit_quality` good, in 13 structures when each unit is counted once in its
major Allen division with ALM apart from other isocortex, more than 12 by one.
Partial is the Fig. 6e clustering of mice by single markers: a
Calinski-Harabasz score of 6.83 against a null of 1.03 +/- 0.42 (p < 0.001),
with 4 nose-type and 5 jaw-type mice, where the paper gives 12.57; all 72
variants give 3.52 to 7.91, and the same decoder gives a response-epoch AUC of
0.754 for the better of jaw and nose against the paper's 0.88, so the lower
score may come from the decoder and not from the data. The other 27 claims, on
Figs. 2 to 7, the Extended Data and the session-independent autoencoder, need
the embedding, the networks or the video decoder and are blocked. The author's
first run gave the same outcomes, with the blocked claims as `too_large`.
Round 1 found that the 17 areas of `data-scale` nest inside each other, that
only about a third of the authors' sessions have video in any version, that
the marker half of `fig6-marker-vs-embedding-decoding` could be run, that the
session AUC pooled the folds as an unstated choice and that the authors' 106
sessions should be the main set; in reply each unit was counted once in its
major division, the 106 sessions were crossed with the video files (34 with
video, 72 without), the marker half was computed, the fold-averaged AUC became
the main reading and the 106 sessions the main set. Round 2 found sentences
the outputs did not support (the read time of the video, 167 sessions where
166 are decoded, and the 105 sessions put in the Methods), which were
corrected, and asked that the weaker Fig. 6e score be read as weaker under this
decoder. After round 2 a cell was added that checks the frame spacing of the
flagged sessions from their frame counts; the first re-run failed for going 7 s
over the 60-minute limit, and with six workers in place of four the second
passed in 3589 s. The author's run took about 2 h 10 min (17:38 to about 19:46
UTC); the notebook ran in about 18 min at first, with at most 0.5 GB of
memory, and in 42 min after round 1.

The version the paper cites holds no video and no autoencoder embedding, the
input of most of the paper's analyses. The later version 0.231012.2129
(65.7 TB) adds the raw electrophysiology to 169 files and the raw video to 71
of them (28.9 TB), from 16 of the 28 mice; 34 of the 106 sessions the paper
used for Figs. 6 and 7 have video and 72 have none in any version, and the
authors' per-frame embeddings are in no pinned deposit. In five sessions, SC011
on 2019-02-24, 2019-02-25 and 2019-02-26 and SC022 on 2019-02-27 and
2019-02-28, the marker timestamps advance 3.4 ms per frame but the frames are
6.8 ms apart, rated critical: as deposited the tongue is visible at 6 to 13% of
the lick events, and stretching each trial's timestamps by 2 puts it at 99.9
to 100%; their trial videos hold 934 to 946 frames against 1864 to 1886 in the
other eight sessions of the same mice, the spacing is inferred from the frame
counts and the licks, and nothing in the files shows it. In SC026_080719 the
tongue is visible at 60% of lick events, with 539 trial videos for 630 trials,
so some videos are probably attached to the wrong trials. SC066_041321 has
markers for 7 trial videos of 550 trials, and SC065_050521 lacks them for 140
of its 354 correct trials. Only units with `classification` good carry a CCF
annotation, so 92,769 of the 154,948 `unit_quality` good units have no
location, and which label the paper's 50,000 neurons use is not said. The
marker timestamps step by exactly 3.4 ms, 294 Hz, where the paper states
300 Hz. The paper's 106 sessions come from 21 of the 28 mice, and the paper
does not say how they were chosen. The question put to the paper's authors is
which choice decoder Fig. 6 used, with which features, penalty selection and
time resolution, and how the AUC of a time bin was formed from the folds.

The author's points on the protocol: it does not say which fields a link claim
takes; it is silent on whether the author may change a claim's `data` or
`depends_on`; an outside list used only to select sessions fits no data
category; it does not say how to block claims whose input is only in an
unpinned later version; it does not say whether sessions that fail the clock
check are left out of an analysis; the fig6e criterion has no partial rule, and
the general definition was used; its severities for clock problems overlap; and
it asks for one full run while any edit forces another.
