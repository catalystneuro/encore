# temmar-2025, Temmar et al. 2025

17 claims reproduced, three partially, three not reproduced and one blocked.
The first verification round agreed with 23 of the 24 outcomes as they stood,
the second with all 24, and a third round was held for the one change proposed
after the second, which it accepted. It pins version 0.261026.1912, the latest
published; the paper cites none, and all three published versions list the same
asset ids. The paper, Temmar et al., "Long-term Intracortical Neural activity
and Kinematics (LINK)" in the NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks track,
presents 312 sessions on 303 days of spiking-band power (SBP) and threshold
crossings from 96 channels of two Utah arrays in one rhesus macaque, Monkey N,
doing a two finger group task, and describes how the signals, the channels'
tuning and the decoding accuracy of ridge regression (RR) and an LSTM change
over the dataset. The claims were written from the version of record with its
supplement and from the paper's code (chesteklab/LINK_dataset at 5e1c36a). Every
claim was tested except `fig5b-finetune`, blocked as `too_large`: the
continual fine-tuning needs 2,446,600 training iterations, about 18 hours at
round 2's fit times. The LSTM claims were run on 20 of the 312 sessions (every
16th), with 12 more for `fig4a-lstm-beats-rr`, and the multi-day training on 12
of the 283 day-0 sessions, for time. Reproduced are, among others,
`fig2a-sbp-decline` (-0.0338 per month), `fig2c-pr-decline` (-0.0708 per month),
`figs4-bank-slopes` (all four slopes equal to the printed digits) and
`fig4a-lstm-beats-rr` (32 of 32 sessions); `fig4d-cross-day-decay` holds under
one of four reading pairs, by margins of 0.017 and 0.003. Partial are
`dataset-contents` (81 files without an impedance column),
`fig2d-population-drift` (three quarters with an outlying spread where the rule
allows two, 2023Q2 at 25.5% from the median) and `suppc1-shuffle-baseline`
(LSTM -0.154 inside the tolerance, RR -0.223 outside it). Not reproduced are
`figs5-active-inactive-slopes` (an inactive slope of -5.36e-04 where the
supplement prints 0.54e-3), `fig3e-array-tuning` (medial channels tuned to
extension of both fingers and lateral ones to flexion, the reverse of Section
4.2, though the verifier reports that Figures 3C and 3E show the files'
pattern) and `fig5a-long-term-decay`, whose double-exponential fits from the
criterion's initial values are degenerate on 12 day-0 sessions, while fits
started from the values printed in the Figure 5A panel meet all three
conditions on point estimates whose initial-rate intervals include zero. The
author's first run gave 16 reproduced, five partially, two not reproduced and
one blocked, and asked a reader nothing, so the answer and author-answers runs
changed nothing. Round 1 disagreed only on figs5, which moved from partially
reproduced to not reproduced; in reply Figure 5A was also scored with
normalisation refit on each test session, fits from the panel's values were
added, the LSTM was trained on the 12 sessions with the highest RR R2, channel
47 was printed from the electrodes table, and five questions went to a reader
in `questions/temmar-2025.yaml`. Round 2 agreed with all 24 and found the LSTM
fit times wrong throughout (this run printed 207 to 223 s per fit), corrected
to 77 to 223 s, and the fine-tuning cost understated; in reply `window.ipynb`
fitted 24 of the 42 other window sessions, which moved `fig2a-irregular-shifts`
from partially reproduced to reproduced in round 3 (window median 0.641 inside
0.529 to 0.651), and a sixth question, on the shift rule, went to a reader. In
the round-1 revision the full run hit the 2-hour command limit and the
fine-tuning notebook ran 52 minutes without finishing, so it was removed; runs
started in the background were killed when their call returned, and the later
revisions ran the notebooks in two foreground stages. The author's run took
about 2 h 50 min; the notebooks ran in 10, 33 and 27 min at first and in 8, 34,
24 and 29 min at the end.

The files give the subject's date of birth but not the implant date, so the
paper's days 349 to 1591 after implant cannot be recovered from the dataset.
ThresholdCrossings is stored with unit volts and conversion 1.0 but holds
integer counts per 20 ms bin, while the stored SBP is already in microvolts
(the code's converter divides it by 0.25 and its analysis multiplies by 0.25).
In 81 of 312 files the electrodes table has no imp column at all, which
Appendix A.2's rule for sessions without a measurement within three weeks may
explain, but a column that comes and goes breaks code that reads every file the
same way. Velocity at bin t is position at t+1 minus position at t, a forward
difference that neither the files nor the paper state. 29 sessions have 72
pauses of 1.05 to 472.9 s that the files do not mark, and 998 trials are no
longer than the 750 ms hold. With the files' array labels, which agree with
Figure S1 (channel 47 medial, row 5, column 5), the lateral bank has the lowest
SBP and the shallowest slope as Figure S4 says, but the tuning by array is the
reverse of Section 4.2, so the paper's two statements cannot both hold on these
data. The supplement prints the inactive channels' slope as 0.54e-3 while
Section 4.1 says all categories decreased; the data give -0.536e-3. The code's
train_model passes the LSTM's noise arguments in swapped order, adding a bias
of SD 0.1 and white noise of SD 0.2, the reverse of Appendix C.1; the paper was
followed. The claims file's "February 2024" comes from Appendix B.1's
candidate days, while the data end on 2023-06-22. Six questions are put to a
reader: the sign of the inactive slope, Figure 3E against Section 4.2, the
impedance rule, the end date, the criterion's initial values for the
double-exponential fit, and whether the shift rule should be compared with a
baseline rate.

The author's points on the protocol: `tools/entry.py check` requires an
`about` field on `other` findings that the attempt schema rejects, and the two
findings were re-filed as `metadata_insufficient` and `paper_inconsistency`;
conclusion cells must quote the full run's numbers and editing them changes the
entry hash, so every notebook ran in full twice; "develop on a subset" says to
block as `too_large` while the protocol also accepts a subset as a deviation,
and the LSTM claims were run on pre-chosen subsets with only fig5b blocked;
changing `runtime_minutes` in `entry.yaml` after the run invalidates its reuse
stamp; and with no PDF renderer the supplement's Figure S1 could not be opened.
