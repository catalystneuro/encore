# brinke-2017, ten Brinke et al. 2017

Eighteen claims reproduced on the dataset and three more with recording depth
from the paper's Source data 1, 21 partially, six not reproduced, and five
blocked plus two more that need outside data, with a link claim, reproduced,
that matches Source data 1's cells to the dataset's by order (all 372 cells
agree on trial count, per-trial eyelid closure at US onset and baseline rate).
The first verification round agreed with 48 of the 56 outcomes as they stood;
the revision took the verifier's outcome on seven and kept
`fig2n-suppression-ds2` at partially reproduced, and the second round agreed
with all 56, including that one, so there was no third round. The dataset
is CRCNS cb-1, which has no versions; the attempt records it as `unversioned`
with its DOI (10.6080/K0B85697) and the file's MD5. The paper records
interpositus nucleus (IpN) cells in mice over delay eyeblink conditioning, 270
cells across training and 102 after full training, and relates CS-US
facilitation and suppression, CS-related pauses and the responses to the US to
the conditioned eyelid response. The trial-by-trial link between facilitation
and eyelid closure reproduces in both datasets, with mixed-model slopes of
0.393 and 0.627 % per Hz, as do `ds2-modulation-proportion`,
`us-pause-prevalence` and `fig6g-us-response-depth`. Not reproduced are
`fig1ijk-modulation-classes` (80, 23 and 167 cells under reading (a), against
60, 23 and 187), `modulation-absolute-not-rate-dependent`,
`fig2-s1-correlation-matrix-offsets`, `suppression-same-timing-as-facilitation`
and the two claims that suppression does not lead the eyelid (`fig2l`,
`fig2p`), where the Wilcoxon test is significant under every reading. Since
the modulation classes do not match, every claim that builds on them carries
the gap, and many are partial for it (42 facilitation cells with behavior
against the paper's 30 in `fig2b`). The optogenetics of Figure 3, the
Purkinje cell data and model (Figure 5-figure supplement 1, Figure 6-figure
supplement 1B,C) and the US-only trials of Figure 7 are blocked as
`data_missing`, and `fig5ej-cs-pause-cells-shallower` stays blocked because
Source data 1 has no depth for the second dataset. The four depth claims were
blocked in the author's first run (22 reproduced, 18 partially, 5 not
reproduced and 10 blocked); a reader's answer let them take depth from Source
data 1, and three then reproduced. Round 1 found that the lag estimator
recomputed a Pearson r at each lag and left many lags at the +/-100 ms edge,
that the CS transient readings were judged one claim at a time, and that the
0-212 ms window was not run in full for `fig2n` nor the 0-200 ms window in
every second-dataset claim; in reply the
cross-covariance of the two traces was adopted as an assumption (no outcome
changed with it), a table holding each CS reading fixed showed that none meets
all eight tests (the best meets five), and both windows were run. Round 2
quoted sentences the outputs did not support and found that
`us-peak-cooccurs-with-facilitation` still reported the count of this
attempt's own reading (a+); these were corrected, and it noted that
`cs-pause-cells-have-us-pause` reaches its expected count only under this
attempt's 50 ms rebound span, under which `fig4b`'s association fails. The
author's run took about 1 h 15 min; the notebook ran in about 6 min at first,
16 min after round 1, and `tools/entry.py all` took 316 s at the end.

The dataset holds no recording depth, which the paper uses in four of its
findings; Source data 1 on the eLife site gives it for the 270 first-dataset
cells, in the dataset's order, and as NaN for all 102 second-dataset cells,
though Figure 5J reports their depths. The US-only trials of 25
second-dataset recordings and the optogenetic sessions of three L7cre-Ai27
mice are not in the dataset, and nothing marks a trial's type. The per-trial
crAmplitude, described as eyelid closure at US onset, equals the eyelid
position 13 ms before the stated US onset (exactly in the second dataset, with
a median per-recording largest difference of 1.1% closure in the first). The
per-trial facilitation variable is not defined: in the second dataset it
equals the positive part of the spike density above the trial's baseline mean
over the whole 0-212 ms interval, minus the same over the baseline, and in the
first no reading reproduces it exactly. The stored ephysMean is not the mean
of the stored trials in 26 recordings (largest single difference 158 Hz). 282
of the 13027 deposited trials have a baseline eyelid at or past half closure
and a Grubbs test flags 402, though the Methods say such trials were removed;
Source data 1 lists exactly the deposited trials as valid. The first dataset
has 18 mouse IDs where the Results say 19 and the Methods 22, and the second
dataset's CS-US interval is 212 ms in the data and its description but 200 ms
in the paper's Results. Source data 1's per-cell facilitation and suppression
give 84 and 34 cells at the stated 5 Hz, against the paper's 60 and 23, and its
undocumented 'Mean US-Cluster' code gives the Figure 6B counts exactly; Source
data 1's own CR flags give 103 recordings with behavior, the paper's number,
where this attempt's rule gives 111. As no analysis code is linked, questions
are put to the paper's authors on the baseline correction behind the
modulation classes, the CS pause thresholds, the bootstrap null, the normality
test, the cross-correlation estimator and lag range, and the velocity measure,
whose first-difference reading gives two to four times the paper's values.

The author's points on the protocol: CRCNS has no versions while the prompt
asks for a pinned published version; it does not say whether the author may
add a reading the paper allows to those a criterion names; data public
elsewhere but marked `data: dataset` has no rule; some criteria ignore their
own expected values, so a claim can reproduce with numbers far off; it does
not say whether a dependent claim must use its parent's reading; the clock
check assumes a trial table where these traces are pre-cut around the CS; it
does not say what to do when only part of a claim can be tested; and no
finding kind fits a count mismatch between the data and the paper.
