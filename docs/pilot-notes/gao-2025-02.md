# 000946, Gao et al. 2025

No claims reproduced, none partially, two not reproduced and 31 blocked. The
first verification round agreed with 30 of the 33 outcomes and the second with
all 33, so there was no third round. It pins version 0.250829.1249, the
latest; the paper cites the dandiset without a version, and all six versions
list the same files. The paper, Gao, Ramachandran, Yu and He in the Journal of
Neuroscience, records sorted units in isoflurane-anesthetized rats with
32-channel probes in S1 and POm (16 rats) or in S1 and auditory cortex (AC, 12
rats) during transcranial focused ultrasound (tFUS) aimed at S1, types the S1
and POm units as regular spiking (RSU) or fast spiking (FSU) by k-means on the
initial phase and afterhyperpolarization of the spike waveform, and relates
their responses and the directional spike time tiling coefficient (dSTTC)
between areas to pulse repetition frequency (PRF), duty cycle and pulse
duration (PD), with sham and CG1-aimed controls. The claims were written from
the version of record in PubMed Central (PMC12139594), which has no
supplement, without a PDF, so the dSTTC equation is missing and was
reconstructed as reading D1; the paper links no code. Only the two unit count
claims and `fig11b-ac-responses` were tested: 28 claims are blocked as
`data_missing` for want of cell types, and `pathway-follows-local-prf` and
`auditory-confound-low-prf` as depending on blocked claims.
`fig4-unit-counts` and `fig6-unit-counts` are not reproduced: the k-means puts
every unit in one of its two clusters, so RSU + FSU is the number of units
with more than 2000 spikes under every typing reading, and POm has 352 units
(339 without the BH499 POM_S1BF recording) against 250 at 3000 Hz, 315
against 232 at 30 Hz and 1470 (1447) against 1019 over the five PRFs, while
one S1 selection matches at 3000 Hz and another at 30 Hz but none both.
`fig11b-ac-responses` is blocked as `methods_underspecified`, as the four
readings of normalization and baseline sample give different outcomes: N1
with B1 is partial, 4500 Hz also being above baseline during sonication
(108.0 +/- 1.5%), N1 with B2 is not reproduced, 300 Hz also being above
baseline (99.7%, corrected p = 6.29e-03), and N2 with either is not
reproduced (Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.0213 during sonication); with one rat left
out, N1 with B1 gives anything from reproduced (without BH537) to not
reproduced (without BH539 or BH547), and 300 Hz has the lowest mean in both
windows in all twelve runs. The author's first run gave fig11b as partially
reproduced on the assumption of N1 with B1 and both count claims as blocked.
The author asked a reader no questions, so the answer run and the following
author run changed nothing. Round 1 disagreed with the two count claims,
whose printed totals already rule out the reproduced and the partial outcome
under any split, and with fig11b, whose readings disagree and so fall under
the header's rule; it also found that N2 is biased low (49.8 to 61.7% at trial
starts shifted by 1 s, where N1 gives 94.3 to 99.0%), that under N1 the B1
sample is 100% for every unit, that the Kruskal-Wallis across PRFs mixes PRF
with rat, each PRF coming from 3 or 4 of 6 rats, and that the recordings with
suffixed identifiers were never checked. In reply the three were changed as
round 1 gave, the totals were printed by selection, rat and suffix, SEMs and
the leave-one-rat-out run were added, and two questions were put to a reader,
on whether N2 and B1 are readings of the paper. Round 2 agreed with all 33 and
found sentences the outputs did not support (22 claims in the README where
there are 18, the normalization readings said to disagree where N1 with B1
and N1 with B2 differ only in the baseline sample, the POm totals said to
exceed the paper's under every selection where leaving out BH511 and BH513
gives 227, 241 and 1052, a PD range printed only for AC recordings, single
recordings of S1BF where there are 3, 60 sessions given as all two-area
sessions where they are only those checked, and the BH500 and
data_collection counts printed nowhere), which were corrected; the totals at
other spike counts and every pair of POm rats whose removal fits were added,
and a third question was put to a reader, on the count criteria's "under one
reading" against the header's rule. The author's run finished its steps at
23:51 UTC, about 1 h 10 min into the window; a first survey of the files with
threads took 28 min, as h5py runs one call at a time across threads, and the
helper uses processes instead, and one early notebook run failed on a pandas
type error, which was fixed. The notebook ran in 258 s at first, 267 s after
the answer and 264 s after round 1.

No file holds spike waveforms or the paper's cell types: the units tables hold
spike_times and celltype_label only, acquisition, analysis and processing are
empty in all 1550 unit files, and celltype_label, described as label 1 = RSU,
label 2 = FSU, is 0 in all 1516 files that have it and absent from the other
34, so 30 of the 33 claims cannot be tested. 734 of the 1550 unit files are
second (or third) copies of another file, with identical spike times and
trial starts and the same identifier, under session start times up to 57 s
apart and different file names; 816 distinct recordings remain, and counting
the files as they are would double most unit counts. No recording is marked
as sham or as tFUS aimed at CG1, the controls of Figure 5, and the dataset has
10 rats with POm recordings and 7 with AC recordings, where the paper reports
16 and 12, 20 rats in all. Even so it holds more units than the paper: the AC
recordings at PD 200 us give 633 units with more than 2000 spikes (112, 89,
138, 167 and 127 at 30 to 4500 Hz) against Figure 11's 453 (83, about 59, 107,
104 and 100), and the POm totals exceed the paper's RSU + FSU counts by 36 to
44% under every selection of recordings by identifier. They come within 10%
only if two of the POm rats are left out (9 of the 45 pairs, for example BH511
and BH513, which nothing marks for exclusion) or if only units with more than
8000 spikes are counted (242, 230 and 1031), and with more than 4000 spikes
the AC count is 445, inside 453 +/- 45.3, so the paper's unit rule or spike
counting may differ from more than 2000 spikes over the whole file. The
seventh AC rat, BH532, has no PD 200 us recording, and the AC_1 and AC_2
files of BH538 are at 1500 Hz and PD 40 us. The dataset also holds recordings
of M1 and sessions with tFUS aimed at M1 (152 distinct recordings) or at POm
(65), and a few recordings labelled S1BF (3), VPM (1), S1_R, S1_1, M1_R (1
each) and AC_1, AC_2 (1 each), none of which the paper describes. In the 60
sessions at PD 200 us with tFUS at S1 that have files from two of S1, POm and
AC, both files have identical trial start times but different
session_start_time values (median 15.5 min, up to 3964 min apart). The
recorded area and the tFUS target are given only in free text in
general/notes, in two spellings, the electrodes table has location unknown
and the electrode group location is 'brain area'; five recordings of BH500
leave the area empty, and general/data_collection, which carries the PRF and
PD, is empty in 33 recordings (BH494 25, BH499 6, BH500 2), where the
identifier gives them and agrees in the 782 recordings that give both. The
arrays of the 370 STTC files have no dimension for the nine dt values their
descriptions list (for example 10 x 499 x 20 x 9) and name no units. Firing
changes after the trial starts and not when they are shifted by 1 s, so the
trial times share the spike clock. A question is put to the paper's authors:
whether the normalized rate was the ratio of mean rates over trials or a
per-trial ratio averaged over trials, and what the baseline sample of the post
hoc Wilcoxon test was.

The author's points on the protocol: it does not address a dataset where one
missing variable, the cell type, blocks nearly every claim, and each claim was
blocked as `data_missing` with untyped results shown as variants that decide
nothing; step 1.6 says to run every reading, but one of the claims file's
readings is biased by construction, and it was run, its bias shown against
shifted trial starts and the other reading adopted as an assumption in the
first run; there is no rule for a dataset holding experiments the paper does
not describe, and an `other` finding was recorded; no header field is named
for the reason the version was chosen, and it went in the notebook header;
and untracked files at the repository root (`.bashrc`, `scripts` and others)
are sandbox device mounts created before the run, which were left alone and
which the workflow should not commit.
