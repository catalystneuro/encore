# pelot-2020, Pelot et al. 2020

Seven claims reproduced on the dataset, plus ten that also use the human and
pig datasets; none partially on the dataset alone, plus two with those
inputs; none not reproduced, plus two; and one blocked. Twelve claims on the
human and pig data only, ten reproduced and two partially, and the link claim
`rat-sample-set`, reproduced, are counted apart. The first verification round
agreed with all 35 outcomes as they stood and the second with 34, and with
`fascicles-larger-cervical` only with qualification; the author proposed it
as partially reproduced, so a third round reviewed that one claim and agreed.
The paper compares the morphology of the vagus nerve in human, pig and rat at
the cervical and subdiaphragmatic levels: nerve and fascicle diameters,
fascicle numbers, tissue proportions and perineurium thickness. SPARC:60
(version 5) holds the rat traces of nine left cervical and nine anterior
subdiaphragmatic samples from 10 rats; the human and pig data were taken from
SPARC:65 (version 6), SPARC:64 (version 4) and SPARC:89 (version 1), which
the paper's supplement cites, and the literature values from Supplement 4,
read from Europe PMC (PMC7672126) because the supplement was not under
`paper/`. Reproduced are the rat diameters, rat nerves about ten times
smaller than human and pig nerves, the similar human and pig diameters, the
pig fascicle numbers, the species and level effects on fascicle diameter, the
perineurium fits (R2 0.749, 0.298 and 0.765 for 73, 284 and 22 fascicles),
the species order of perineurium thickness and the Sunderland reanalysis
(2.750%, R2 0.510). `fascicles-larger-cervical` is partial: cervical
fascicles are larger in all three species, but the pig contrast has p = 0.055
(paper 0.0009). The two literature comparisons are partial on readings of
Figure 7, and `peanut-fascicle-counts` is partial, with 24 pig cervical
peanut fascicles where the legend says 25. Not reproduced are
`perineurium-no-level-difference` (human p = 2.4e-07, pig p = 3.1e-09, rat
p = 0.87) and `no-age-trend` (3 of 26 uncorrected correlations with
p < 0.05, all in pigs; none after a Holm correction). `rat-carotid-sheath-bundles`
is blocked: the nine cervical micrographs are deposited, but only the vagus
is traced. The mixed models are fitted by REML with unbounded variance
components and tested with Kenward-Roger on the observed information, an
assumption that gives the paper's F and p for all nine effects it reports
(nerve diameter 577.0, 11.01 and 2.4455 against 577.1, 11.01 and 2.445);
with the variance bounded at zero the nerve diameter interaction becomes
significant (p = 0.024). The author's first run gave 29 reproduced, 3
partially, 2 not reproduced and 1 blocked. Round 1 found that the paper's
numbers follow its code's first-trace rule for the rat fascicle, that the
pig contrast's degrees of freedom were implausible, that Supplement 6 was not
opened and that the micrographs are in the archive; in reply Kenward-Roger
tests on the expected information were added, Supplement 6 and the
micrographs were shown, and the blocked claim moved from `data_missing` to
`other`, while the Methods' largest fascicle stayed the main rule. Round 2
showed that the paper's implied degrees of freedom did not support that test,
and the test on the observed information, which does, moved
`fascicles-larger-cervical` to partially reproduced. The R cross-check of
the Kenward-Roger code that round 1 suggested could not be run, as R is not
installed. Three questions are put to a reader in `questions/pelot-2020.yaml`:
whether `no-age-trend` should correct for its 26 tests, which rat fascicle
rule the fascicle diameter criteria take, and how to read the carotid sheath
criterion, which contradicts itself for this dataset. The author's run took
about 1 h 30 min (02:20 to 03:47 UTC); three notebook runs failed before the
passing one, on a `90+` age format in SPARC:65, a helper function in a
markdown cell and a key error in the final cell. The notebook ran in 264 s
at first and in 240, 228 and 245 s after the revisions.

The derivative folder's `RatVNMorphology_OutputMetrics.mat`, described in
the manifest as the rat output metrics, is an older output (written
2020-03-03) that holds 54 samples of all three species; its rat values are
not those of the traces or the paper, with cervical nerve diameters up to
8.5 um larger (R22-3, 245.2 against 236.7 um), while the code folder's file
of the same name (2020-09-05) matches the traces exactly. That file keeps
only 7 of the 22 rat perineurium thicknesses, so the thicknesses behind the
rat fit can be recovered only by measuring the traces again. The Methods use
the largest fascicle of each rat sample, but the code takes the first inner
perineurium trace, which in R22-1 is 39.35 um against a largest fascicle of
105.76 um and in R23-1 142.15 against 147.47 um; the paper's rat
subdiaphragmatic fascicle diameter, 0.121 +/- 0.032 mm, and its fascicle
diameter F values (8.989, 51.88, 5.273) rest on these, and with the largest
fascicles they are 0.129 +/- 0.014 mm and F = 9.59, 46.5 and 4.93. The
Discussion's rat ratios (0.32 and 0.68) are reproduced only with all
fascicles and the connective tissue boundary of the multifascicular samples
(0.317 and 0.678); with the largest fascicle they are 0.270 and 0.525. The
readme gives the rats' ages as 65 to 268 days, where `subjects.xlsx` and the
paper give 75 to 268; `samples.xlsx` does not say the subdiaphragmatic
samples are the anterior trunk, which only the readme and the code's file
names give; and R17-1 has a NerveMask though it is monofascicular, with
nothing saying whether it is meant to be used. In the input datasets the
C54-3 NerveMask of SPARC:65 is inverted, its complement giving the code
output's 1615 um, and SPARC:64's output file has no fascicle below 23.7 um,
where the paper gives an MT minimum of 5 um. Questions are put to the
paper's authors: which JMP settings were used and how the pig p of 0.0009
was obtained, which would need about 25 degrees of freedom where the test
gives 3.74; what test supports no level difference in the perineurium
relationship; and what test is behind no trends with age.

The author's points on the protocol: a finding about an input dataset has
nowhere to go, as findings are shown on the attempt dataset's page; the
check treats `tifffile.imread(` as reading a figure when the TIFFs are the
data; the paper's sister datasets are not in `papers/datasets/pelot-2020.yaml`,
so they went under `inputs`; `paper/` lacked the public supplement; the rule
to put SHA-256 sums in the notebook header conflicts with computing them;
"REML as the standard choice" does not settle bounded against unbounded
variance components; the peanut count criterion has no partial clause; and
the check does not accept `data_missing` for `elsewhere` claims when the
outside input is missing.
