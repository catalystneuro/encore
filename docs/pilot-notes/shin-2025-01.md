# 000248, Shin et al. 2025

Fourteen claims reproduced, none partially, three not reproduced and 23
blocked, all on the dataset. It pins version 0.240502.2344, its only published
version, as the paper cites none. The paper, Shin, Ogando, Abdeladim, ...
Lecoq and Adesnik in Nature Neuroscience, reports that illusory contour (IC)
images are represented in mouse visual cortex by IC-encoders that complete
the pattern recurrently, from Neuropixels recordings in V1, LM, RL, AL, PM and
AM, 2p imaging, mesoscope imaging and 2p holographic optogenetics. The claims
were written from the version of record in Europe PMC without the
Supplementary Information or figures, and from the first author's public code
(github.com/hs13/IC_paper_code at commit 50e04eaf), as the Code Ocean capsule
the paper names returned HTTP 403. The 18 Neuropixels claims were tested: the
unit counts, IC-encoder and segment responder counts, receptive fields, fixed
gaze, pupil and gaze, cross-correlograms and decoding. The 21 claims on 2p,
mesoscope and holography data are blocked as `data_missing`, and
`supp3-decoder-types` is blocked as `too_large`, as the network decoder is
estimated at about 18 hours on one core against the 30-minute notebook limit;
the quadratic and RBF SVMs, which did run, agree with the linear SVM in V1, LM
and AL. `fig1e-npx-exclusive-center` is not reproduced, with 44 exclusively
center-responsive units under reading (a) and 52 under reading (b) against
18 +/- 1.8, and `fig1f-npx-ic-ire-responsive` and `fig1g-npx-orientation-match`,
which rest on that selection, are not reproduced either.
`fig2d-npx-ccg-hva-input` is blocked as `methods_underspecified`, as 7 of 8
combinations meet the criterion and in the eighth the medians are 1 and 1,
though IC-encoders have the larger mean in all 8 (tie-corrected p from 1.0e-05
to 3.9e-04). The author's run ran out of time: two commands without a time
limit hung for about an hour each, the notebook was executed only after the
cutoff, and all 18 Neuropixels claims were recorded as blocked (`too_large`).
The author asked a reader no questions, so the answer run and the following
author run changed nothing. Round 1 agreed with the 22 blocked outcomes and
gave its own outcome for each Neuropixels claim from the outputs, which the
author took, except that `ed2b-npx-fixed-gaze` was set to reproduced under an
added coverage rule, `fig2d-npx-ccg-hva-input` to reproduced by a tie-break
and `fig3d-npx-areas-inference` to partially reproduced under decoder reading
(b). Round 2 agreed with 39 of 40, disagreeing on the fig2d tie-break, and
found statements the outputs did not support, among them the fig1e step
counts, stale network timings and an ed7a note that left out RL and LM, which
were corrected. A third round, on two proposed changes, agreed that fig2d is
blocked and that fig3d is reproduced, with a reservation. Some outcomes rest
on weak ground: `fig3d-npx-areas-inference` meets the criterion in five of six
runs and is decided by the majority of seeds, a rule chosen after the first
seed's result, and the statement's set of areas (PM significant, AM not)
appears in only one run; `ed2b-npx-fixed-gaze` gives the paper's 9 sessions
only when inverse-patch trials count (8 with standard trials only), and its
unit count may be partly coincidental, as fig1e gives 44 units against 18; and
`ed6b-npx-v1-group-counts` matches 87 and 636 exactly only with the units
labelled noise kept. The author's run went from 00:36 to about 05:12 UTC; in
the revisions the decoding moved into four further notebooks, each under the
30-minute limit, and the checks notebook takes 26.6 minutes.

The dandiset holds only the 12 Neuropixels sessions (78 files, no imaging),
and the 2p imaging, mesoscope and holography recordings behind Figures 1h to
1k, 2b, 2c, 3e and 4 to 6 are in no public archive the paper names, so 21 of
its findings cannot be tested. The units tables hold units whose quality is
labelled noise (539 of 3,026 units in sub-633229, 322 of the V1 units across
sessions), while the Methods say all units not classified as noise were
packaged into the NWB files. The pupil position is stated in meters but its
values are camera pixels, with no conversion to visual degrees, so the
Methods' 8 degree fixed-gaze criterion cannot be applied and the code's 20
pixels was used. The Methods' Animals list 14 Neuropixels mice (10 Sst, 4 PV),
where the files hold 12 sessions from 12 mice (9 Sst, 3 Pvalb), as the Results
and legends state. The Methods give 30 Hz for the eye video, where the eye
timestamps are 16.7 ms apart (60 Hz), as the paper's code assumes. The IC
block tables give each image only as a frame index (0 to 21), with the names
taken from the paper's code, and the last eye tracking timestamp of each
session with eye tracking is NaN. Three questions are put to the paper's
authors: how the SVM hyperparameters were set, as the significance of PM and
AM in Fig. 3d depends on it and on the cross-validation partition; whether
inverse-patch trials count towards the fixed-gaze coverage of Extended Data
Fig. 2; and whether the 87 IC-encoders and 636 segment responders of Extended
Data Fig. 6b include the units labelled noise.

The author's points on the protocol: the check in `tools/entry.py` can hang
without a message, as its long-literal regex backtracks on a two-line integer
list; the protocol sets no time limits on commands, and a slow SVM run and the
hanging check cost about two hours; the many SVM fits do not fit in a
30-minute notebook, so the inner C search was first limited to V1; and no
blocked reason fits claims not reviewed in time, so `too_large` was used.
