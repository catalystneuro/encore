# 001535, Natraj et al. 2025

One claim reproduced (on an assumption), none partially, none not reproduced
and 51 blocked, 49 for missing data and two for insufficient metadata. All 52
claims are on the dandiset. Both verification rounds agreed with all 52
outcomes as they stood, and nothing was proposed after the second, so there was
no third round. It pins version 0.260608.0617, the latest published version,
which holds the same single asset as 0.250714.1218; the paper cites none. The
paper, Natraj, Seko, Abiri, Miao, Yan, Graham, Tu-Chan, Chang and Ganguly,
"Sampling representational plasticity of simple imagined movements across days
enables long-term neuroprosthetic control" in Cell, records ECoG from two
participants with tetraparesis and anarthria after brainstem stroke, B1
(128 channels) and B3 (253 channels), and reports the somatotopy of imagined
movements (Fig. 1), daily initialized 7-action discrete control of a virtual
arm with MLP decoders on 200 ms band power (Figs. 2 to 4), plug-and-play
control with a fixed decoder (Fig. 5), and continuous control of a virtual arm
and real-world Kinova Jaco tasks in B1 with a biLSTM decoder (Figs. 6 and 7).
The claims were written from the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central
(PMC11932800), not the version of record, with the supplement read only as its
legends, and from the paper's OSF code (doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/TBDWG), which holds
the real-time BCI code and no offline analysis; the check made 15 corrections
and left three points open. Only `rnn-training-data-amount` could be tested.
Under reading (a), the whole stored trial, the 9737 trials last 837.4 min
against the paper's 14 h 48 min (888 +/- 88.8 min), and 814.3 min without
target ids 8 and 9, but the match holds only if the 1886 robot trials (297.3
min) are the "practice with the Kinova Jaco in the real-world using the MLP"
that the Methods count; the 7851 virtual trials alone last 540.0 min. Reading
(b), the active portion only, cannot be run as the file has no task states; if
the first 0.5 s of every virtual trial were outside it, the total would be
771.9 min, a lower bound and not an estimate. The other 51 claims are blocked:
49 because B3, the Figure 1 task, the band-power features, the online decoder
outputs, the kinematics and the task outcomes are not in the dandiset, and
`fig4b-day-differences` and `figs3-spatial-pooling` because no trial has a day
label and no electrodes table places the channels on the grid. For
`figs3-spatial-pooling` a variant pools the stored high gamma 2x2 and lowers
the median per-channel variance to 0.499 of the raw value with channels in
row-major order (z = 7.05) and 0.538 in column-major order (z = 7.12), and
under all 200 random layouts (ratios 0.457 to 0.533, z 7.40 to 8.05), against
the paper's z = 8.721, so it shows only that averaging lowers variance and
cannot test the grid. The author asked no questions of a reader, so the
reader's run, which found no questions file, and the author-answers run changed
nothing. Round 1 agreed with every outcome but said `figs3-spatial-pooling`
should be blocked as metadata_insufficient and its variant run, and found that
the robot-trial question decides `rnn-training-data-amount`, that reading (b)
was left unbounded, that targets 8 and 9 were unexplained and that the
duplicate check finds only exact copies; in reply the block reason changed, the
variant was added, the notebook quoted both Methods passages on the robot
trials and gave durations per context and target, trial-id order against
context, the high-gamma time course at trial start and end and a full listing
of the acquisition group, and a question was filed for a reader in
`questions/001535.yaml` on whether the totals with and without the robot trials,
and readings (a) and (b), should have to agree. Round 2 agreed with all 52,
noted that the paper's next sentences ("Having created the RNN-based decoder
from the virtual environment data, we then fine-tuned...") favour leaving the
robot trials out and that the criterion's one-reading rule is inconsistent with
the all-readings rule the check applied to other claims, asked for 771.9 min to
be called a worst case and for `fig4i-boundary-convergence` to be blocked as
data_missing, and found that the size of the pooling effect does depend on the
layout (0.538 lies above every random layout's); all were taken up, and the
question for a reader now names both points. The author's run took about 1 h 55
min (23:46 to 01:42 UTC), of which an hour was lost when a `grep` with a long
backtracking pattern on the paper's text hung until the tool timeout; the
notebook ran in 437 s at first, 494 s after the author-answers run, 495 s
after round 1 and 608 s at the end. Untracked files at the repository root
(`.bashrc`, `scripts` and others), which the author did not create, were left
alone.

The dandiset holds one NWB file, for participant B1 only: 9737 trials of 256
features at 100 Hz, the biLSTM training input, with a target id and a virtual
or robot flag; participant B3, the Figure 1 imagined-movement task, the delta,
beta and high-gamma band-power features of the MLP decoders, the online decoder
outputs, the ReFit-KF and IBID velocities and the robot task outcomes behind
Figures 1 to 7 are not in it, so 51 of the paper's 52 findings cannot be
tested. No trial carries a day, session, block, or open-loop or closed-loop
label, the trial table's start_time and stop_time are placeholders (the trial
id and id plus one, described as DUMMY), and session_start_time is one date for
all trials, so trials collected over many days cannot be grouped by day or
context. The is_robot flag does not say whether a robot trial is the
real-world practice with the MLP decoder or the later fine-tuning data of the
RNN, which decides whether the stored total (837.4 min) matches the paper's 14
h 48 min (540.0 min without the robot trials); in trial-id order every robot
trial follows every virtual trial, and nothing says whether the ids follow the
order of recording. The target ids 1 to 9 carry no names or directions, while
the Methods name seven actions and the OSF code numbers them in two different
orders, and the dandiset description speaks of 7 directional targets where the
trial table has 9, ids 8 and 9 (132 trials each) only in virtual-environment
trials. The 256 features are not described column by column; columns 0 to 127
are almost always positive and 128 to 255 signed, which fits high gamma then
low frequency, and each block has an L2 norm of 1 per trial, which the units
("arbitrary") do not state, and there is no electrodes table, so the channels
cannot be placed on the 8x16 grid. The subject's age is P38Y where the paper
gives B1 as 41 years old, and neither says at what date the age applies. No
trial is deposited twice, though the check finds only exact copies, and the
target is decoded from trial-mean features at 0.72 with the true labels, 0.17
to 0.18 with labels shifted by one trial and 0.13 shuffled. Of the paper
itself, the Results report the same robust regression slope, z and p (b =
0.258, z = 6.474, p = 1.847e-10) for B1 (Figure 4F) and B3 (Figure 4G), and
with the 48 and 18 trials the paper states for Figure S7I a Mann Whitney z
cannot exceed about 6.2, below the reported 6.967. Questions are put to the
paper's authors: whether the 9737 trials are the data the biLSTM was trained
on, whether the robot trials are the real-world practice with the MLP or the
later fine-tuning of the RNN, and whether each stored trial is the whole trial
or only its active portion.

The author's points on the protocol: `archives.md` says to read files with
pynwb, but listing the 9737 series that way took 164 s, so the file was read
with h5py and remfile by the same paths; the clock check of step 2 assumes event
times or continuous sessions, which this file lacks, so the target was decoded
with true, shifted and shuffled labels instead, which cannot check timing
within a trial; many claims fail for two reasons at once while the protocol
asks for one, so `data_missing` was given where better metadata could not
supply the data and the other reason named in the notes; `assumed` is defined
for method choices while the open point here is what the trials are, and the
outcome was still marked `assumed: true`; and reading (b) cannot be run, so the
outcome was left to reading (a) under the criterion's one-reading rule.
