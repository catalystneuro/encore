# pelot-2020, Pelot et al. 2020, second attempt

Thirty-one claims reproduced, one partially and three not reproduced on the
datasets, with none blocked; the two literature comparisons, which also use
the literature values of Figure 7, are partial and counted in brackets. The
five link claims and `sunderland-reanalysis`, whose 777 values the claims
file marks as elsewhere though they are deposited in SPARC:65, reproduced
and are counted apart. The first verification round agreed with all 43
outcomes as they stood and the second with all 43, and nothing was proposed
after the second, so there was no third round. The paper, Pelot et al. in
Frontiers in Neuroscience, compares the morphology of the vagus nerve in
human, pig and rat at the cervical and subdiaphragmatic levels: nerve and
fascicle diameters, fascicle numbers, tissue proportions and perineurium
thickness. Unlike `pelot-2020-01`, which tested SPARC:60 and read the others
as inputs, this attempt rests on all seven datasets at their latest
versions (SPARC:65 at version 8, not 6), under a claims file rewritten for
pilot-18 with 43 claims, and was blind to the earlier attempt. The claims
were written from the version of record in Europe PMC (PMC7672126), without
the supplement or Eqs. 1 to 4, which were lost when the text was flattened;
the claims check made seven corrections. Reproduced are the nerve diameter
mixed model (species F = 587.3, level F = 11.03, interaction F = 2.45
against 577.1, 11.01 and 2.445, with the subject variance unbounded and
negative), the rat nerves about ten times smaller, the fascicle numbers and
diameters, the tissue proportions, the perineurium fits and species order,
and the Sunderland reanalysis. `fascicles-larger-cervical` reproduced with
Kenward-Roger degrees of freedom (pig p = 0.012, rat 0.044, paper 0.0009
and 0.0234), but the rat contrast is near 0.05 and with containment degrees
of freedom it is 0.089. Partial are `peanut-fascicle-counts` (24 pig
cervical peanut fascicles against 25) and the two literature comparisons (9
of 12 parts for human and pig, 1 of 3 rat cervical means). Not reproduced
are `perineurium-no-level-difference` (separate fits differ by level in
humans and pigs, F = 19.2 and 21.0, p < 0.0001; with sample clustering the
human difference remains and the pig one does not), `no-age-trend` (3 of 27
Pearson tests with p < 0.05, all pig, none after correction) and
`human-subdiaphragmatic-fatty-tissue` (fat in 29 of 32 human micrographs
but also in 13 of 45 pig micrographs, where the criterion allows one).
Fourteen outcomes, among them the readings made by eye of fat, cell bodies
and carotid sheath bundles, rest on an assumption. The author's first run
gave 36 reproduced, 2 partially, 3 not reproduced and 2 blocked. Round 1
found that `DataPlotting_v4.m` hard-codes the Figure 7 literature values,
that the paper's rat values follow the code's first fascicle ring, that
Kenward-Roger, the claims file's method, had not been run, and that the
images behind the readings by eye were too small to check; in reply the two
literature comparisons moved from blocked to partial, Kenward-Roger was
implemented in `mixed.py`, and at full resolution R24-3 holds six bundles,
which moved `rat-carotid-sheath-bundles` from partial to reproduced. Round
2 found sentences the outputs did not support, which were corrected, asked
that finding 2 be rated critical, which it now is, and asked for a question
to a reader on the fat criterion, now in `questions/pelot-2020.yaml`. The
reconciliation finds all nine earlier findings still present, and traces
the two outcomes that differ to whether Kenward-Roger uses the observed or
the expected information (`fascicles-larger-cervical`, pig p = 0.055 before
and 0.012 now) and to the rewritten carotid sheath criterion. The author's
run took about 1 h 4 min (03:21 to about 04:25 UTC) and no step failed;
the notebook ran in about 350 s at first, with peak memory 1.15 GB, and in
456 s with 1.29 GB at the end.

The deposited output metrics file
(`CadaverVNMorphology_OutputMetrics.mat` in SPARC:65, identical to
`RatVNMorphology_OutputMetrics.mat` in SPARC:60) gives pig fascicle
diameters about 1.40 times those measured from the pig masks of SPARC:64
and reported in the paper (1.36 to 1.61 per fascicle, so not a scale
factor), and its pig fascicle counts are those of the version 1 masks in
all 18 samples, which differ from version 4 in 11; nothing in the files
says which values are current, and its human values match the masks
exactly. The rat traces of SPARC:60 changed between version 1 and version
2 with a changelog that says only 'initial dataset creation': the outer
perineurium boundaries moved inward, the effective nerve diameters of the
15 monofascicular samples are 0.3 to 3.5% smaller cervically and 0.2 to
8.8% subdiaphragmatically, and the rat perineurium fit gives R2 0.57 with
version 1 and 0.77, the paper's value, with version 5; the output metrics
file matches version 1. The Methods take the largest rat fascicle, but
`DataPlotting_v4.m` keeps the inner trace of the first ring, which in
R22-1 is 39.3 um against 105.8 um, and the paper's rat subdiaphragmatic
fascicle diameter (0.121 +/- 0.032 mm) follows the code (121.1 +/- 32.3
um), not the Methods (129.1 +/- 14.0 um). For the three multifascicular
rat samples the output file gives the connective tissue boundary as the
nerve diameter, and the Discussion's rat ratio of 0.68 is reproduced only
with that boundary (0.678), while the Results' means give 0.52. 18 of the
212 masks read are stored as TIFF MinIsWhite, so readers that return the
raw bits give them inverted; SPARC:65 holds a second, undescribed nerve
trace, `NerveMask_AllTissue.tif`, for four samples; and
`RatVNMorphology_OutputMetrics.mat` keeps 7 rat perineurium thicknesses
where the traces give 22 rings. Smaller errors: the readmes of SPARC:60 and
SPARC:16 give the rats' ages as 65 to 268 days, where the subjects sheets
give 75; in SPARC:61 C48-2 is filed under a sample with no row in
samples.xlsx and copies of the C51-3 and C63-1 derivatives sit in other
folders; in SPARC:89 the P9-3 scale bar is byte-identical to P11-1's;
samples.xlsx of SPARC:60 does not say the subdiaphragmatic samples are the
anterior trunk; and R17-1 has a NerveMask though it holds one perineurial
ring. Emulating the deposited MATLAB code reproduces the human output
metrics exactly, and the current masks reproduce the paper's reported
means, SDs, counts and fits. Questions are put to the paper's authors:
which JMP settings gave the mixed model tests, what test supports no level
difference in the perineurium fits, whether Supplement 6 tested age
trends, and the rules for counting carotid sheath bundles and calling
adipose tissue.

The author's points on the protocol: the pinned SPARC:60 version 5 is not
the one the deposited outputs match, and it was kept, with version 1 shown
as a variant; claims judged by eye conflict with computing every result
and embedding no numbers, so the readings were stored in the helper, the
images shown and the outcomes marked assumed; the claims file marks the
Sunderland data as elsewhere though they are deposited in SPARC:65, where
the claim was tested; and JMP's Kenward-Roger method was not available, so
Satterthwaite was used at first with containment degrees of freedom as a
variant.
