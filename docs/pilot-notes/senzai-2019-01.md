# 000166, Senzai et al. 2019

Nine claims reproduced, nine partially, 23 not reproduced and five blocked on
the dataset; `fig3ab-alpha-analogy`, which compares the mouse rhythm with
published monkey figures (Bollimunta et al. 2008), is counted apart and blocked
as `methods_underspecified`, as the claims file records. It pins version
0.250624.0434; the paper cites no version, and both published versions hold
the same 19 assets. The paper, Senzai, Fernandez-Ruiz and Buzsaki in Neuron,
records V1 of 19 mice with 64-site linear probes during sleep and waking,
anchors the laminar patterns to current source density (CSD) landmarks of the
DOWN-UP transition, divides depth by gamma coherence clusters and gamma ICs,
groups units into eight layer clusters by their coupling to those ICs, and
compares firing, rhythms and monosynaptic connections across layers and brain
states, with a few DOWN state-active neurons. The claims were written from the
version of record as served by Europe PMC (PMC6367010) with its two
supplements; the paper links no code of its own, and the lab's buzcode
toolbox, which its Key Resources Table links, was read at commit e615663. The unit counts, waveform and CCG
classes, optotagging, connection counts and strengths, the gamma ICA and
coherence clusters, LFP power and cross-frequency coupling by layer, the 3-6 Hz
rhythm, the eight unit clusters and every layer claim built on them, and the
DOWN state-active neurons were tested. `fig1f-sink-b-deeper`,
`fig1f-other-landmarks-same-depth` and `figs2d-visual-offset-rebound` are
blocked as `data_missing`, as the flash times are in 2 of the 13 stimulated
mice, and `fig1g-landmark-distances-consistent` and
`figs8c-lognormal-strength` as `methods_underspecified`: the paper places
landmarks a, b, d and e by eye, and a rule taking the extreme CSD above and
below the MUA peak c places all five in only 15 or 16 mice, with adjacent
distances ranging by 220 to 620 um against less than 110 um. The other laminar
claims take depth in sites relative to c, an assumption, and 29 outcomes are
marked as assumed. `fig7-connection-counts` (1,295 excitatory and 578
inhibitory connections against 525 and 323), `fig4a-ccg-classification` (673
and 187 CCG-based E and I cells against 347 and 100) and `fig5b-eight-clusters`
(the silhouette favours 2 to 6 clusters, never 8) are not reproduced, and the
layer claims then use the eight-cluster solution the criterion asks for. The
author's first run tested 7 claims and blocked the other 40, 34 of them as
inherited from fig1g or as placing sites by its landmarks. Round 1 agreed with
12 of 47 outcomes: it found that most of those criteria need no landmark but c,
and that the channel order was unchecked. The author removed the 34 blocks and
ran the claims, checked that the columns are in depth order and added CSD maps
for every mouse, the IC reading for figs7, every group of every file and the
connection counts per state; the verifier would give
`figs7-strongest-3-6hz-coupling` not reproduced, and the author kept partially
reproduced, as 3-6 Hz is strongest under the adopted distant-site reading, and
recorded the disagreement. No questions were put to a reader, so the answer
run and the following author run changed nothing. Round 2 agreed with all 47,
so there was no third round, and found statements the outputs did not
support, among them deep coupling above 100 Hz in fig3d, where the deep index
peaks above 100 Hz in only 5 of 18 mice, and two of three within-layer
fractions called outside where all three are, which were corrected. It found
that `fig2b-six-coherence-clusters` is reproduced only under a reading that
can hardly fail, as each boundary ranges across mice by 31 to 40 sites (620 to
800 um), which was put to a reader during the revision, after the answer step;
that the deepest IC counts both as volume conducted and as main IC 6, spread
over depth in 9 of 18 mice; and that `slow-gamma-superficial-wake` passes on
the state order alone, while 30-60 Hz power in cluster 1 lies below the site
mean (median -7.06 uV^2/Hz). The author's run went from 03:59 to 05:18 UTC; a
survey that walked every object of all 19 files timed out after 30 minutes and
lost its output, the first survey crashed because unit columns differ between
files, and two helper bugs were fixed before the final runs. The notebook ran
in about 3 minutes at first, and the entry in 22 minutes after round 1 and 21
minutes in the last run.

The electrode table gives no depth or position (x, y and z are NaN, location
"unknown", impedance -1), only rel_y (-1 to -64) implies the site order, and
the units carry no waveform, layer, depth, landmark or cluster label, so every
laminar analysis of the paper must be rebuilt from the raw signal and LFP; the
best-correlated site in the 100-500 Hz LFP is an adjacent one for 50 to 63 of
the 64 sites in each file, consistent with the columns being in depth order.
The 520 nm flash times are in 2 of the 13 stimulated mice, as the `Visual
laser` tables of YMV05 and YMV08 (498 intervals of 0.4 s each), and those
tables also hold the optogenetic pulses (1,488 and 2,715 of their intervals
start within 5 ms of a `Laser diode` pulse), with one YMV05 interval lasting
49.6 s. The files give 11 PV-Cre::Ai32 and 3 VGAT-Cre::Ai32 mice where the
STAR Methods give 5 and 9, so the optotagged mice cannot be split as the paper
split them; pooled over both lines, 80 units are tagged and 62 (78%) are I
cells, against the paper's 67 and 54 (81%). The units' `max_channel` holds the
electrode table `id` of the peak site, a hardware channel number, matching it
for 1,157 of 1,255 units, which its description does not say. The `cell_type`
labels (Pyramidal Cell 836, Narrow Interneuron 277, Wide Interneuron 223,
Unknown 15, down state 10) follow a scheme the paper does not describe, and
198 "Wide Interneuron" units have a waveform E trough-to-peak latency; the 10
"down state" units are in 6 mice, where the paper reports 5 neurons in 4 mice.
The `synaptic_effect` labels (444 Excitatory, 145 Inhibitory) do not match the
paper's 347 and 100 CCG-based cells, and their rule is not given. YMV03 has no
unit columns but quality, YMV19 has no sleep-state table, and YMV07, a
CaMKII-Cre::Ai35 mouse, has 41,022 laser pulses of 20 ms at one level, a
protocol the paper does not describe. Five questions are put to the paper's
authors: how landmarks a, b, d and e were chosen on the DOWN-UP CSD map; which
LFP site gives the phases of the spike-LFP coupling of Figure S7; whether
further selections were applied in the monosynaptic detection, as the STAR
Methods rule finds about twice the paper's counts and the pair count of
102,332 matches no count of pairs of the 1,477 units; how the eight unit
clusters were obtained, on which features and over which range of cluster
numbers; and which components count as volume conducted in the 0.34 of gamma
variance and how the six main ICs were chosen among the ten.

The author's points on the protocol: the dependency rule does not say what to
do when a claim depends on a blocked claim but uses only the part the dataset
can provide, and "blocked for the same reason" was then applied to the 30
claims depending on fig1g; it does not cover claims whose criteria place sites
by a blocked claim's landmarks but list no `depends_on`; it allows blocking
after the analysis has run only for chance levels, and the same reasoning was
used for the landmark rule; the schema requires numbers in `measured` where
figs7's result is an order of bands; an inherited block hides a time limit,
as reading the LFP alone was estimated at 46 to 56 minutes; and
docs/archives.md should warn that walking a whole file with h5py over remfile
takes about 6 minutes per file.
