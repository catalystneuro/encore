# mills-2026, Mills et al. 2026

Two claims reproduced, five partially, five not reproduced and 32 blocked,
29 for missing data and 3 as `metadata_insufficient`; the link claim
`fig2-recorded-neurons` did not reproduce, and the three snRNA-seq claims on
the data at GEO GSE211437 (`data: elsewhere`) are blocked as not_runnable and
counted apart. Three outcomes, `figs13-imaging-mice-discriminate`,
`figs15-no-context-encoding` and `figs10-no-reward-relationship`, rest on an
assumption. The first verification round agreed with every tested outcome
and disagreed with the block on four claims; in reply
`fig2o-region-differs-cs-reward` and `figs10-no-reward-relationship` moved to
partially reproduced, and the other two stayed blocked with the comparison
printed for the record. The second round agreed with all 48, and nothing was
proposed after it, so there was no third round. It pins 0.261103.1719, the
only published version, whose draft has the same assets. The paper, Mills,
Lee, Howe, Li et al., "Amygdalostriatal transition zone neurons encode
sustained cue responses to guide defensive behaviors" in Neuron, records
units in the amygdalostriatal transition zone (ASt), tail of the striatum
(TS) and central amygdala (CeA) of mice in a two-tone discrimination task
with a reward cue and a shock cue, images Drd1a+ and Drd2+ ASt neurons with
miniscopes, and relates them to freezing and darting, with snRNA-seq,
RNAscope and optogenetics. The claims were written from the author-accepted
manuscript (PMC13592531) with the legends of Figures S1 to S17, and from the
paper's code at commit 1fee26d, which holds five Python analyses of the
electrophysiology and none of the MATLAB code for Figure 2, the imaging or
the behavior scoring; the claims check corrected several criteria and added
two claims, for 48. Reproduced are the divergence of the CS-shock and
CS-reward trajectories (7.36 against a largest baseline distance of 2.87)
and the discrimination of the imaging mice (paired Drd1a-Cre p = 0.0057,
Drd2-Cre p = 0.046), which comes from darting alone. Partial are the
sustained-only CS-shock response of Figure S9 (the slow group has 112
neurons against 96), the single-trial divergence (paired half only), the
Drd2+ CS-shock difference (p = 5.6e-10 across 1189 and 625 ROIs, not
significant across mice), the ASt reward response of Figure 2O and the
reward-latency test of Figure S10. Not reproduced are the CS decoding in
paired mice (Mann-Whitney p = 0.026 against the criterion's 0.01), the
absence of a Drd1a+ difference and of a Drd2+ CS-reward difference, the
Drd2-rich clusters of Figure 5M under all eight readings, and the absence of
context encoding. Blocked are the region and paired against unpaired
comparisons, the GLM, the US session, and all RNAscope and optogenetic
claims. The author's run took about 1 h 50 min (09:03 to 10:52 UTC); the
first imaging load failed on files that store `starting_time` and `rate` in
place of timestamps, and a first calcium baseline gave NaNs because imaged
clips start about 9.6 s before the cue, both fixed. The notebook ran in 18.8
min with peak memory 2.7 GB.

The 13 Neuropixels files (5 paired and 4 unpaired ASt mice, 4 CeA mice) hold
spikes but no trial events and no behavior, though the Methods say events
were recorded by TTL at 10 kHz, and their units have no region: every
electrode of a probe carries one label (ASt or CeA) although the probes span
3.8 mm. So no unpaired ephys mouse, no TS neuron and most CeA neurons can be
analysed, and the files give 1515 paired ASt units, 885 CeA and 633 unpaired
against the paper's 193, 145 and 71. In the Open Ephys files the SLEAP poses
are not on the clock of the spikes and the trial table, and nothing says so:
the lag is constant within 0.8 s in four 2018 sessions and changes by 22 to
529 s in the other eight, so behavior cannot be placed against spikes. Two
recordings are deposited twice: sub-6197 has two Open Ephys files with
identical spikes and events, and of the two sub-J933 imaging files with
identical traces, the one named sub-J933-Disc4_ses-J933-Disc4 has 55 port
entries in place of 193, 236 s off the video's clock. The imaging files hold
every CNMF-E ROI (2036 paired and 1233 unpaired) with no column marking the
1110 curated neurons the paper analysed, so counts are 2.4 to 4.0 times the
paper's. The paper says its raw and processed data are in the dandiset, but
it holds no RNAscope data, no optogenetic behavior and no unpredicted-US
session, which leaves fifteen claims untestable. The Open Ephys files do not
state the group; the poses are in pixels with no scale; imaging frames are
1/15 s apart where the Methods say 15 frames/s downsampled by two; and in the
imaging poses the largest frame-to-frame displacement is below 0.5 px in
19.6% of frames and above 1.6 px in the rest, so the paper's freezing rule
counts only frames that repeat the previous pose. Questions are put to the
paper's authors: how the Figure S9 slow trials were selected, what the
Figure 5M soft normalization is, how total displacement is computed, and
whether the curated imaging neurons and the Neuropixels regions and events
are recorded anywhere.

The author's points on the protocol: a link claim whose criterion fails
while part of its data is usable, here 10 of the 15 paired ASt mice, had its
dependents tested on that part; a claim with one testable and one untestable
half has no outcome that fits; the claims reader wrote a link claim, which
the protocol gives to the author; the severity table rates a recording
deposited twice as critical even when the file names make it plain; and the
protocol does not say whether data elsewhere that need software the locked
environment cannot install should be fetched first.
