# 000715, Yemini et al. 2021

One claim reproduced, three partially, three not reproduced and two blocked on
the dataset, with one more reproduced and one more blocked that also need
outside data. The first verification round reviewed the 16 tested or blocked
claims it was given and agreed with 13, the second agreed with 31 of the 32
outcomes, and a third round, held on the two changes of outcome proposed after
the second, agreed with both. It pins version 0.241009.1514, the published
version. The paper, Yemini, Lin, Nejatbakhsh et al., "NeuroPAL: A Multicolor
Atlas for Whole-Brain Neuronal Identification in C. elegans" in Cell, builds a
statistical atlas of neuron positions and colors from the heads and tails of
ten young-adult NeuroPAL worms (otIs669), uses it to identify neurons
semi-automatically, chooses reporter colorings that minimize color violations
between neighbors, and images whole-brain activity in strain OH16230. The
claims were written from the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central
(PMC10494711) with Methods S1 and S2, without the version of record, and from
the paper's code, CELL_ID at commit d9f5df9 and Optimal_Coloring at 990c597.
The dandiset holds only the ten heads, so every claim was tested on the head
alone and no tail part could be. `fig2c-ollr-more-variable-than-olll`
reproduces (OLLR's standard deviation 2.14 times OLLL's along the major axis),
and `fig2cd-variability-differs`, `ms1-uncertain-first-faster` and
`ms1-detection-accuracy` are partial, the head half holding and the tail half
untestable, detection accuracy being 71.0% against 72 to 88% with precision
83.4% and recall 82.5% inside 81 to 99%. All but the detection claim rest on an assumption for
Algorithm 4 of Methods S1, which as written does not converge on these data,
the atlas shrinking along its minor axes; it was kept in the initial worm's
frame and stopped after 20 alternations, with variants shown.
`fig7bc-identification-accuracy` is not reproduced: Algorithm 5 gives 39.05 to
51.80% and the CELL_ID alignment 55.00 to 63.25% against 77.4%; started from
four rotations about the long axis (not the paper's method) Algorithm 5 gives
at most 77.38%, missing the bound by 0.02 points, and aligning each worm by its
true correspondences gives 77.72 to 80.22%. `fig7b-eight-labels-above-90` (43.0%)
and `ms1-position-only-accuracy` (20.4 to 27.1% against 45%) are not reproduced.
`ms1-calibration` is blocked as `methods_underspecified`, its two readings
disagreeing (Spearman rho 0.51 to 0.70, but a mean absolute difference of 27.8
to 49.2 points against a bound of 10), and `fig7bc-ventral-ganglion-lowest` as
`data_missing`, as it compares every tail ganglion with the head's ventral
ganglion. `ms2-neuropal-color-violations` reproduces at 3.64%, inside 3.5 +/-
0.35, after a reader amended the claim so that the code's mean shortfall below
the margin decides it in place of the Methods share of neighboring pairs, which
is 21.50%. `fig7de-optimal-coloring-violations` is blocked as
`methods_underspecified`: the 39-reporter value is inside the tolerance under
every reading, but the paper gives no sparsity for the 3-reporter solution, and
with 7,500 runs the best value is 6.95% at sparsity 1500 and 8.38% at 2000,
below 9.45 to 11.55, and 9.90% at 3000, inside it. Of the 21 claims on data
elsewhere, five were tested on the paper's Zenodo record:
`fig6d-ase-nacl-asymmetry` reproduces (t = 3.82, p = 0.00046) and
`fig6ef-nacl-novel-interneurons` reproduces on an assumption about Storey's
pi0, `figs1h-avf-cyclic-activity` is not reproduced (median peak rate 0.197
per second against 0.27 to 0.33 Hz), and `fig6de-asj-ric-stimulus-specific`
and `tables6-tail-pvq-and-post-stimulus` are blocked as
`methods_underspecified`, their outcomes depending on whether both members of a
pair of similar neurons were removed or one. The other 16 are blocked as
`other`: six because the data are in no public archive, five because Table S4
was not supplied to the run, and five because the claims file gives no
criterion and they were not attempted in the time. The author's first run gave
fig2cd and ms1-uncertain-first-faster as reproduced, ms2 as not reproduced on
the Methods share with a question to a reader, fig7de as blocked as
`not_runnable`, the reporter table being a MATLAB table object, fig6de as not
reproduced and tables6 as partial. The reader amended the ms2 claim, and the
following author run reran ms2 and, reading the table with mat-io and porting
the solver, gave fig7de as partial. Round 1 disagreed with fig2cd and
ms1-uncertain-first-faster, which it gave as partial for want of the tails, and
with fig6de, and found that the identification failures cluster in the worms
imaged in dorsal-ventral view, that CELL_ID's rotation search does nothing,
that fig7de was run in full at only one sparsity and that the helper called
removing both members of a pair a choice that removes few traces where it
removes 647 of 3,550 head traces; in reply the three were changed as round 1
gave, the orientation was measured and the rotated-start variant added. Round 2
disagreed with tables6, which the notebook's own rule for disagreeing readings
blocks as it blocks fig6de, found that fig7de turns on the sparsity, and found
sentences the outputs did not support, which were corrected; the changes of
tables6 and fig7de to blocked were proposed and round 3 agreed. The author's
run took about 1 h 40 min of the 5 h, and two failures during development, the
CELL_ID alignment rejecting every candidate as a mirror image and NaN samples
in the Zenodo traces, were fixed before the final run. The notebooks ran in
991 s and 198 s at first, 666 s, 118 s and 1132 s after the answer, and about
96 minutes in all, four notebooks, after round 3.

The dandiset holds only the heads of the ten worms; their tails, used for the
paper's tail atlas and tail accuracies, are on the paper's Zenodo record, as is
the whole-brain activity of Figure 6, and nine of the claims tested here need
the tails in part or in full. The files record no orientation, and the five
heads imaged in dorsal-ventral view (subject names ending DV) are stored
rotated by 92.6 to 102.7 degrees about the long axis relative to the atlas,
against 1.8 to 12.9 degrees for the left-right heads, which only the subject
names hint at; Algorithm 5's identity start gives 9.6% on average on the DV
heads and 68.5% on the LR heads. The files carry no per-neuron colors, so
colors were recomputed from the volumes, and do not say whether the stored
voxel indices are 0- or 1-based; comparison with the Zenodo annotations shows
each stored voxel is the Zenodo position made 0-based and rounded. Every file
gives strain OH16230, a spinning disk confocal, a worm "in microfluidic chip",
birth on 2021-01-05 and imaging on 2021-01-07, but the files are the heads of
the paper's ten otIs669 (OH15262) atlas worms imaged on a Zeiss LSM 880: the
volumes of 7_YAaLR and 76_YAaDV are identical, voxel for voxel, to the Zenodo
volumes of those worms, and the ten identifiers match. The third channel is
labelled GFP-GCaMP, with GFP emission settings, but its mean is 17,000 to
21,000 in every file and the Zenodo copies mark it as the DIC (transmitted
light) channel; the worms carry no GCaMP. 0 to 6 of the 189 to 196 stored
neurons per file have an empty name, with no description of what they are,
and names are stored as a ragged array of single characters. The dandiset
metadata name no related publication, and the description and the files'
related_publications give the paper's title without a DOI. The paper's code
also differs from its description: CELL_ID's local alignment re-runs the
principal-axis alignment after each rotation, which undoes it, so its search
over 26 angles and 2 signs yields 4 distinct starts, and the 3.5% NeuroPAL
color violation is the mean shortfall that find_optimal_colors.m computes, not
the share of neighboring pairs that Methods S2 defines (21.5% and 3.47% on the
paper's own data.mat). Questions are put to the paper's authors: how the atlas
of Algorithm 4 was kept from shrinking and after how many alternations it was
stopped; how held-out worms were oriented before identification in Figure
7B-C; which sparsity and how many runs gave the 10.5% solution of Figure 7E;
which measure of agreement between confidence and accuracy was meant; and, on
the activity data, whether both or one of a pair of similar neurons were
removed, which window defines the post-stimulus period, and which five worms
and which peak rule underlie Figure S1H.

The author's points on the protocol: a claim whose criterion is half testable,
head present and tail missing, has no rule, and the testable half was judged
with a deviation and no `measured`; the checker rejects `data_missing` for
`elsewhere` claims whose data are unpublished, so `other` was used; tail data
assigned to the dataset are public on Zenodo, which conflicts with the
definition of `data_missing`, and a claim cannot be split between dataset and
elsewhere; step 1.6 covers methods the paper leaves open, not a stated method
that fails on the data, and the fix to Algorithm 4 was treated as an
assumption; the rule of Methods before code gave a main measure for ms2 that
contradicts the paper's own number, and a reader was asked; `elsewhere` claims
whose criterion reads "Not tested on the dandiset" have no criterion, and only
those whose test the STAR Methods define were tested; the clock check does not
apply to static images; the protocol does not cover using outside Zenodo
volumes only to check what the dandiset's files are, without a link claim; and
no `blocked_by` reason fits a claim left for lack of time when the computation
is not what stopped it, so `other` was used.
