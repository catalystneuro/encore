# timme-2016, Timme et al. 2016

Seven claims reproduced, four partially, three not reproduced and none
blocked; seven outcomes, fig4ac-te-lognormal, fig4bd-out-tail-longer, the
three Figure 5A and 5B claims, fig6-information-gain-not-rising and
fig7c-degree-modified-rule-matches, rest on an assumption. The first
verification round agreed with 12 of the 14 outcomes as they stood and the
second with all 14; nothing was proposed after the second, so there was no
third round. CRCNS data sets carry no version, so the record gives
`unversioned` and the notebook checks the streamed archive against its MD5.
The paper, Timme, Ito, Myroshnychenko, Nigam, Shimono, Yeh, Hottowy, Litke
and Beggs, "High-Degree Neurons Feed Cortical Computations" (PLoS Comput
Biol 12(5), e1004858), records spontaneous spiking in 25 organotypic
cultures of mouse somatosensory cortex on a 512-electrode array, builds
transfer entropy (TE) networks at two time scales, 1.6 to 6.4 ms and 3.5 to
14 ms, measures the synergy of neuron triples by partial information
decomposition (PID), and reports heavy-tailed degree distributions, that
synergy rises with a transmitter's out-degree but not with a receiver's
in-degree, and that in a feedforward model a degree-modified Hebbian
rewiring rule matches these correlations and raises neuron-to-neuron
communication. The claims were written from flattened text that had lost
all 24 equations; the author read them from the version of record's JATS
XML, and took the TE procedure (bins, a 7-bin jitter, 5000 surrogates, the
sub-network rule) from the paper's reference [44], Timme et al. 2014, both
listed as inputs. Reproduced are the recording counts (25 recordings, 7735
neurons, 309.4 per recording, 2.132 Hz), the heavy-tailed degrees (10.2%
and 14.3% of neurons above the random 99.9th percentile), the longer
out-degree tail, the positive out-degree correlations (42 of 49 under
sub-networks and Pearson), the information gain (negative exponents for the
normalized gain), the Table 1 PID examples and the Figure 7B connectivity
patterns. Partial are the log-normal weights (R2 0.882 to 0.957, three of
eight fits below 0.9), the in-degree correlations (18 of 49 positive, a
significant negative skew at p = 0.043), the correlations beyond the null
(98% for out-degree, 88% for in-degree) and the model match, where the
degree-modified rule matches and is closest but the firing-rate rule does
not. Not reproduced are the pooled trends of Figure 5C and 5D, where synergy
does not rise with out-degree under any reading, the conditioned mutual
information of Figure 7D (random 0.00230 bits highest, degree-modified
0.00153 lowest) and the larger model of S1 Fig. The author's first run gave
8 reproduced, 3 partially and 3 not reproduced. The author asked a reader
nothing, so the answer and author-answers runs changed nothing. Round 1
found that the jitter test called a pair significant when every surrogate
tied with the real TE (25 of 40 independent Poisson pairs at 0.03 and 2 Hz),
which left the log-normal fits at R2 0.08 to 0.61, and that only one
past-bin reading, one sub-network reading and the full networks for Figure
6 had been run; in reply ties were counted as exceeding, with the strict
rule as a variant, both readings were run, short sub-network draws were
redrawn, and fig5ab-correlations-beyond-null moved from reproduced to
partially reproduced. Round 2 held the tie-counting test still too liberal
and found three sentences the outputs did not support, on the run time,
the number of correlations (49, not 50) and the figures quoted from [44];
in reply the connections expected by chance and a stricter-threshold
variant were added and the text corrected. An early full TE pass ran into
the one-hour command timeout and printed nothing, which cost about an hour.
The notebook ran in 28.8 min at first, 30.2 min after the author-answers
run, 52.4 min after round 1 and 54.6 min at the end.

The paper analysed the TE networks of [44], and the networks, the TE values
and the sub-networks are not in the data set, which holds spike times only,
so every network result here rests on networks rebuilt from the spikes,
which may differ from the paper's. How the rebuilt test counts ties decides
much of it: [44] takes the share of surrogates larger than the real TE, and
taken literally that rule calls 10.8% of pairs on circularly shifted trains
significant against the 0.1% of p < 0.001, and gives networks about 95 times
denser than chance; counting ties as exceeding gives 0.32%, still about 8%
to 30% of the significant connections of the five tested recordings
expected by chance (pooled 15.8%), and no stricter count of the 5000
surrogates reaches 0.1%. Under that rule degree rises with firing rate
(median Spearman 0.75 and 0.81), so a high-degree neuron here is mostly a
high-rate neuron. The spike times otherwise match the paper: 25 recordings,
7735 neurons, every one with at least 100 spikes, so the deposit already
applies the paper's threshold, and no recording is deposited twice.
DataSet19 (2013-01-06-1) lasts 2887498 ms (48 min) where the paper describes
60 minute recordings, and the first ten recordings (2012) have no spike in
their first 8 s. Within 5000 draws four recordings give fewer than 500
sub-networks at 1.6 to 6.4 ms (DataSet2 6, DataSet5 0, DataSet10 380,
DataSet17 148). Questions are put to the paper's authors: which bins form
the TE past state, as [44]'s text implies bins t-1 and t-2 and its delay
windows bins t-2 and t-3, and the log-normal fit and the information gain in
bits differ between them; how the jitter test counted ties; whether Figures
4 and 5 used the sub-networks or the full networks, with Pearson or
Spearman correlations; which 40 of the 50 correlations Figure 5 uses; and
how the model's synergy and mutual information were computed. A reader is
asked in `questions/timme-2016.yaml` whether the claims file's PID
redundancy misreads Eq 8; the two forms give the same verdicts.

The author's points on the protocol: it does not say whether fetching the
version of record for equations lost from the text counts as the paper, and
it was fetched and listed as an input; the method lives in a cited earlier
paper, and its details were taken from [44] with the passages cited; there
is no way to approximate a method too expensive to run in full, and the
surrogate counts of pairs whose jitter moves more than 50 relevant spikes
were drawn from a normal distribution as a deviation; the time rule does not
cover a part of the method exceeding the notebook's 60-minute limit, and
full-network triples, receivers and input combinations were sampled; the
clock check assumes trial tables or other streams and does not apply to
spike times alone; CRCNS has no versions where the prompt asked for a pinned
one, and archives.md was followed; the markdown-only Result cells were
inserted into the executed copy without re-running; and keeping the first
of several matching readings gave 2.132 Hz where the second, 2.103 Hz, is
closer to the paper's 2.10.
