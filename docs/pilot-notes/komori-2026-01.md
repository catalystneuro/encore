# komori-2026, Komori et al. 2026

No claim reproduced, one partially, five not reproduced and eighteen blocked,
all eighteen for missing data. All six tested outcomes rest on assumptions, and
`rest-decoding-above-null` is marked seed sensitive. The verification rounds
did not agree with every outcome. Round 1 gave `rest-decoding-above-null` and
`rest-ic-count-and-window` as not reproduced where the record had partially
reproduced, and agreed with the other 22; round 2 agreed with 23 and gave
`rest-shap-medial-anterior-first` as partially reproduced where the record has
not reproduced, a disagreement the author recorded and did not take. After
round 2 the author proposed not reproduced for `rest-ic-count-and-window`, and
a third round, on that claim only, agreed. It pins version 0.250705.0947. The
paper, Komori, Mizusaki, Tomita, Yoneda, Matoba and Morita, "Mesoscopic
cortical activities associated with pupil-linked perceptions inferred via
explainable machine learning" (bioRxiv preprint), decodes pupil dilation from
wide-field calcium imaging of mouse cortex with a GRU on independent components
(ICs) and explains the models with SHAP, in its own recordings with hindpaw
electrical stimulation, visual pattern change and a visual oddball task, and in
resting data reused from DANDI:001425. Only the six resting-state claims can be
tested; the 18 claims on the stimulation experiments are blocked, as those
recordings are available only on request. The paper's code covers the
stimulation experiments only, so the resting analysis was rebuilt from the
Methods, with readings R1 to R10 named in the claims file: three mice
(VG1-GC#51, 52, 53) and their first three resting sessions were assumed, ICs
were kept by a compactness rule in place of the paper's visual inspection, the
GRU was written in PyTorch and integrated gradients replace Deep SHAP. All 25
mice (about three hours of streaming) were not run. The resting decoder is above
its null models in two of the 16 combinations run under the epoch selection
rule, the stronger at 0.696 against 0.514 (p = 0.0014) and at 4 of 5 seeds, and
every mean model AUC is below 0.738, the lower end of 0.82 +/- 0.082; under the
main combination it is 0.625 against 0.648 (p = 0.259). Loss and accuracy are
better than null together in none of the 16 combinations, the 0.1 Hz pattern
holds in one of four combinations run at the cutoffs, 10 ICs and 1.5 s are never
highest, the 10-IC maximum VIF is 21.55 (2.97 with an intercept, 10.32 on
training samples) against 9.0, and no kept IC matches the medial anterior IC of
the SHAP claim (IC 18 has 0.266 of its positive weight in the band against
0.494 in MOs). The author's first `entry.py all` was killed by the 60-minute
tool timeout while the second notebook ran, and that notebook was executed
alone with nbconvert; the notebooks ran in 31 and 37 minutes at first and 43
and 55 minutes after round 2. Round 1 found that the conclusions had been
written from an earlier run and did not match the executed numbers, that the
epoch selection rule (R5 b) was run only at one seed although the paper says
such sessions were excluded from all datasets, that the readings were never
combined, that the null model was close to the model by construction (the
shuffle changes 12.8% of the training labels) and that the VIF definition might
differ from the paper's; in reply the author read the paper's VIF code, adopted
its VIF without an intercept, ran R5 b as a main reading with every combination
of R2, R3, R7 and R8, and put the R5 point to a reader in
`questions/komori-2026.yaml`. Round 2 found again that the text did not describe
the executed run, as the workflow's re-run on another machine gave other ICs.
The cause was that the Methods divide the SVD components by their singular
values, which leaves the 30th to 31st eigenvalue ratio at 1.00000000 so that
the dimensions JADE keeps are set by rounding, and that JADE stopped at 100
sweeps; the components now keep their singular values as in the paper's code,
JADE runs to convergence and the kernels are pinned, and a fresh run gave the
same outputs. The final run, on an AMD Zen 5 processor, crashed in OpenBLAS
until the L2 cache size was given to it, and gave the same decomposition but
some GRU fits different from the round-2 run, with no outcome changed.

The paper's own recordings with somatosensory, visual pattern and visual
oddball stimulation are not in the dataset, so 18 of its 24 claims cannot be
tested on public data; the dandiset's 33 sensory-stim files are of
anaesthetised mice given a left-eye flash, right whisker-pad vibration and
white noise, with widefield only and no pupil. The deposited pupil diameter
(`processing/downsampled/pupil_tracking/diameter`) holds unflagged failed
detections: in VG1-GC#51 day0 and VG1-GC#53 day1 its maximum is 2.88e21 and
1.05e11 pixels, with 148 and 57 frames above three times the median, besides
768 and 269 missing values, and after cleaning VG1-GC#53 day1 has no positive
label. The imaging-plane metadata give 405 nm excitation for
ImagingPlane_blue and 470 nm for ImagingPlane_UV, while the values say the
reverse (dF/F SD 0.050 and 0.028 in `widefield_blue` against 0.012 and 0.014 in
`widefield_UV`, two sessions). Six of the 98 resting-state files (VG1-GC#73
day7; VG1-GC#99, 105, 109, 116 and 124 day0) are widefield-only and hold no
pupil or other behaviour. Every resting session read holds 18,000 frames per
channel at 29.95 Hz over 600 s, the pupil and the deposited dF/F are on the
imaging timestamps, the pupil follows mean cortical activity by 1.1 to 2.3 s in
seven of nine sessions, and no recording is deposited twice. Of the 9 files
used, 6 are the same assets in v0.250514.0602 and the other 3 were replaced and
not compared. In the paper, the Methods assess the VIF on validation data while
the code computes it on training features and without an intercept, which
decides the VIF claim, and the Methods divide the SVD components by their
singular values where the code does not; the session counts differ between
text and legends (143 against 109 selected visual pattern sessions, 79 against
105 oddball sessions), and the Results give the grating as rotating clockwise
where the Methods say counterclockwise. Four questions are put to the paper's
authors: which mice and sessions were used, how the resting samples and labels
were formed and whether the session exclusion, the hemodynamic correction and
the 0.1 Hz high-pass apply to resting data, which ICs were kept and how 'every
fifth session' applies to 600 s recordings, and whether the resting-state code
will be published.

The author's points on the protocol: the Result cells must be written after the
run but any edit invalidates the run stamp, and running twice does not fit with
40-minute notebooks, so the final text was copied into the executed copies;
`entry.py check` rejects index options in `requirements.in`, so the PyTorch CPU
index could not be used and the environment is 5.7 GB; "run every reading" gives
64 or more combinations here without saying whether one change at a time is
enough; the five-seed rule is costly for ML claims with many variants; it says
nothing on results that differ between software environments; the Bash tool's
60-minute default timeout matches the notebook limit, so `entry.py all` on two
notebooks is killed; two criteria were ambiguous, the 10-IC VIF maximum, read
strictly, and SHAP precedence when an IC has no onset, where both onsets were
required; and there is no finding kind for a paper whose own data are not
public when the dataset is reused, which was recorded as `other`, `about: paper`.
