# okuno-2025, Okuno et al. 2025

No claim reproduced on the dandiset alone, as every claim on the dandiset also
uses outside inputs; of these 19, five reproduced, one partially, two not
reproduced and 11 blocked, all 11 for missing data. The 11 claims on the
connectomes alone (`data: elsewhere`) are counted apart: five reproduced, two
partially, one not reproduced and three blocked. The eight tested outcomes on
the dandiset rest on an assumption. The first verification round agreed with
28 of the 30 outcomes and held that fig4h-reciprocal-distances and fig5-wagn
had been blocked without a reason; the second agreed with all 30, and nothing
was proposed after it, so there was no third round. It pins version
0.240106.0043, the version the paper cites and the only published one. The
paper, Okuno, Woodward, Okano and Hata, "Functional connectivity, structural
connectivity, and inter-individual variability in Drosophila melanogaster"
(eLife reviewed preprint 107990 v1), reuses the whole-brain two-photon calcium
imaging of Brezovec et al. in 9 flies walking on a ball, compares functional
connectivity (FC) between brain regions with structural connectivity (SC) from
the FlyEM hemibrain and FlyWire connectomes, and reports that polynomial
detrending with tCompCor (Poly-tCompCor) and spatial smoothing give the best
FC-SC agreement, which falls with the logarithm of the ROI count, that FlyEM
agrees better than FlyWire, that agreement rises with a region's share of
cholinergic output neurons and falls with its GABAergic share, that SC from
unsegregated or reciprocal synapses agrees better than random synapse samples
and SC from segregated synapses worse, and that walking activates LAL, FB, IPS
and GNG and suppresses WED and AVLP. The claims were written from the
publisher's HTML, with no figure readable, and from the paper's MATLAB code at
commit 4b92add, which does not hold the NWB-to-NIfTI step or the SPM12 and ANTs
registration; the claims check corrected several Figure 2 settings to the
code, made the synapse selections exact and added criteria to fig3c and
fig5-wagn, and left open the Figure 2 ROI counts, an index slip in one of the
code's rank-sum tests and the values shown only in figures. The nine claims of
Figures 1 and 2 and the Discussion are blocked because the SCKm and DistKm ROI
atlases are not published, and fig3d-confidence-threshold and
figs4-1-flyem-permutation because the FlyEM SC they need is not. Reproduced on
the dandiset are fig3e-flyem-higher-most-regions (55 of 63 regions), the three
permutation tests (fig4f under reading (a) only; neuron SC z = +23.5, -4.8 and
+24.1) and fig5b-walking-activation; fig3d-flyem-vs-flywire is partial (FlyEM
r = 0.606 above FlyWire 0.566, both below tolerance), and
fig3g-transmitter-correlation (ACh r = -0.040, GABA r = -0.096) and
fig5b-walking-suppression (no voxel below -3.242) are not reproduced. These
outcomes rest on an antspy SyN registration of the mean green image without the
SPM12 motion and slice-timing correction the claims file requires, and on no
left-right flip. The paper's own deposited outputs give its numbers (FlyWire
R(7) = 0.684, ACh r = 0.395 and GABA r = -0.252 through the same transmitter
table, two negative GLM clusters), so the shortfalls lie in the FC computed
here. fig3e is not robust: every registration gave 53 to 56 regions against a
bound of 52.2, 47 with left and right swapped and 51 without nuisance removal,
and the permutation results hold only with Poly-tCompCor. On the connectomes,
from the paper's deposited tables, fig3c, figs3-1a, fig3f, fig4h and fig5-wagn
(reading (b)) reproduced; fig3a is partial, its FlyEM confidence-0 count not
being in the deposit, and fig4b too, as 138,144 of the 139,255 neurons carry a
PPSSI, which went to a reader in `questions/okuno-2025.yaml`;
figs4-1ij-segregation-index-biased is not reproduced (FlyWire mean 0.363); and
fig4a and fig4c are blocked, their FlyWire halves holding and the FlyEM DBSCAN
not run, with figs3-2cd not runnable. The author's first run gave 7 reproduced,
3 partially and 3 not reproduced, the rest blocked, five connectome claims as
not attempted. The author asked a reader nothing, so the answer run changed
nothing, but the author-answers run found that re-executing the unchanged
notebook moved its numbers, as the registration did not repeat, and rewrote
them. Round 1 found that the motion and slice-timing correction was missing and
not stated, that the orientation was not settled, that the permutation outcomes
were fragile, that the GLM's departures from the code were untested and that
five connectome claims had deposited data; in reply the missing correction was
stated (a notebook with it went over the 60-minute cell limit twice), a second
registration and the paper's deposited permutation values and GLM map were
added, fig4h and fig5-wagn moved to reproduced and figs4-1ij to not reproduced.
In that revision `entry.py all` failed on a syntax error, so the main notebook
was run with nbconvert and its `result.json` entry set by hand. Round 2 found
that the notebook text and the record quoted an earlier run; antspy 0.6.3
ignores the `random_seed` argument, so the registration had never been seeded.
It was then seeded through ants.config with ITK on one thread, two runs gave
identical output, each fly's own voxel size was used, a motion check was added
and every number was rewritten. The author's run took about 3 h (05:09 to 08:04
UTC); the main notebook ran in about 18 min at first, with peak memory 4.6 GB,
and 1202 s after the author-answers run.

The dataset was converted by CatalystNeuro (catalystneuro/clandinin-lab-to-nwb).
The files do not state the orientation of the functional volumes; dorsoventral
and anteroposterior can be read against the template, but left and right
cannot, and with them swapped fig3e and the synapse parts of fig4e and fig4i
would no longer be reproduced, while the evidence conflicts (the paper's
per-region FlyWire values correlate with this attempt's at r = 0.661 as
registered and 0.407 swapped, the walking t map with the paper's at r = 0.359
as registered and 0.394 mirrored). The raw series is not motion corrected: a
rigid in-plane shift from the tdTomato channel has a median of 0.6 to 3.2 um and
a 95th percentile of 2.8 to 6.1 um per fly, against an in-plane voxel of about
2.6 um. TwoPhotonSeriesFunctionalGreen and Red store data as (time, 256, 128,
49) while their `dimension` attribute is [128, 256, 49], and the processed
FDA-space series has grid_spacing [1, 1, 1] in unit 'unknown', so its voxel size
is not given; the native spacing is 2.55 to 2.61 by 2.61 to 2.68 by 5 um. The
FicTrac speed, in the files' name order, reproduces the per-volume speed and
walking regressor stored in the paper's code to within 1e-5 for all nine flies,
with the code's 104.654892 ms offset, which confirms the subject order and the
alignment of behaviour to imaging, and fly-100, the one the code leaves out, has
the lowest coverage after registration (9 hemibrain ROIs partly outside the
field of view, AL(L) 55% inside), consistent with the paper's reason. The files
hold 9 flies of 3384 volumes at 1.8797 Hz with 50 Hz FicTrac speed, and no
recording is deposited twice. Outside the dandiset, the SCKm and DistKm atlases
are in neither the code repository nor either version of the Zenodo record,
which hold their SC connect lists but not the voxel-to-ROI assignment; the
FlyEM SC at confidence thresholds other than 0.8, the FlyEM permutation SC
matrices, the FlyEM synapses in the template and the JRC2018F-to-FDA transform
are not published either. The paper's deposited second-level GLM reproduces the
code README's result exactly (maximum t 14.71, one positive cluster of 416,801
voxels, negative clusters of 406 and 260 voxels). A question is put to the
paper's authors: which SPM12 realignment and slice-timing settings, ANTs
registration settings and NIfTI voxel size were used.

The author's points on the protocol: "fix every random seed" could not be met
for the ANTs registration, which was said in the notebook and record (round 2
traced this to antspy ignoring the seed); conclusion markdown was edited after
execution, the same way in the executed copy; the companion notebook was run
with nbconvert, not `entry.py run`, so `result.json` listed only the main
notebook; it does not say whether the paper's own derived tables count as the
data for `data: elsewhere` claims, and they were used and recorded as a
deviation; the time rule covers only claims cut off at the deadline, and
`other` was used for claims not attempted earlier; there was no clear place for
a link claim, and subject identity was checked through the behaviour regressor
instead; and where the claims file's M3 and the code it cites disagree on when
tCompCor voxels are ranked, the code was followed and a deviation recorded.
