# garud-2026, Garud et al. 2026

Three claims reproduced on the dandiset, two partially, none not reproduced
and none blocked; the 13 claims on data in other archives are counted apart:
of the 12 on the Krausz et al. (2023) rat data on Mendeley Data, ten
reproduced and two partially, and the claim on the Brown et al. (2026) data
is blocked as `methods_underspecified`. Two outcomes on the Krausz data,
`fig2gh-stationary-dip` and `fig3-recovered-value-increases`, rest on an
assumption. Both verification rounds agreed with all 18 outcomes, and
nothing was proposed after the second, so there was no third round. It pins
version 0.250828.1728, which the paper cites; version 0.250903.1802 holds
identical assets. The paper, Garud and Morris, "Continuous Temporal
Difference Learning as a Unifying Theory of Dopamine Function" (bioRxiv,
posted April 8, 2026), records no data of its own: it reuses NAc dLight
photometry of 10 freely moving rats approaching reward ports on a hexagonal
maze (Krausz et al. 2023; Figures 2, 3 and 5) and of 18 head-fixed mice in
Pavlovian conditioning with short and long ITIs (Floeder et al. 2025,
DANDI:001552; Figure 4). It reports that in the rats dopamine ramps toward
the goal, more steeply for high reward and on fast approaches, dips when the
rats are stationary, gives a recovered value function that increases toward
the goal, and is fitted best by a Gaussian continuous-time TD model, and
that in the mice dopamine settles below baseline late in the long ITI, a dip
that deepens with training while anticipatory licking rises. The claims were
written from the extracted text of the preprint, without figures or the
supplement, and the code on OSF (osf.io/g3e2d) is not public (its API
answered 401), so the claim on Supplementary Figure S2 is blocked. On the
dandiset the subject and session counts, the higher lick rate in short-ITI
sessions (2.393 against 1.600 Hz, p = 0.00067) and the rise of lick rate
with training (r 0.648) reproduce. `fig4b-long-iti-dip-below-baseline` is
partial: the late long-ITI ΔF/F is below zero in 18 of 18 mice under all
eight readings, but the short-ITI trace is below zero (-0.0067) before the
next cue; both corrections regress the 405 nm signal over the whole session,
so each session's mean ΔF/F is zero by construction, and against the level
5 to 0 s before the cue, outside the criterion, there is no dip (0.0003,
p = 0.32). `fig4d-dip-deepens-with-training` is partial: the slope on
session number is negative under all 16 readings and below p = 0.05 under
10, but no reading gives both r within tolerance of -0.29 and p below
0.001. On the Krausz data the ramp, reward, early and late, velocity and
coupling claims reproduce, `fig2f-coupling-grows-near-goal` and
`fig2gh-stationary-dip` with the paper's values (r = -0.494; t(9) = -4.274),
the latter only with all rows off the ports as the "non-approach"
observations; the recovered value increases toward the goal in 7 of 10 rats
against 8, and the Gaussian profile wins in 9 against 8, with mean R² not
above 0.8 for all four shapes. Round 1 found that Figure 3 had not been run
on the row set that matched Figure 2G-H, that grids limited the spatial
scales of the fits, that ΔF/F is centred by construction, and smaller
points; in reply Figure 3 was run on both row sets (7 of 10 either way), λ
was allowed below zero and σ up to 10,000 hexes, which moved the
`fig5d-tonic-term-and-gaussian-contribute` values from 1170.1 and 695.6 to
1201.0 and 665.0, still within tolerance, the ITI measured from reward was
adopted, all 144 photometry-only files were opened, and the Krausz et al.
code showed the reward values to be probabilities in percent. Round 2 found
sentences the outputs did not support, which were corrected, and asked for
the pre-cue baseline, which was added with two questions for a reader in
`questions/garud-2026.yaml`. In that revision the Figure 4D mixed model was
found to stop at a lower optimum in some runs; it is now fitted with three
optimizers and the best fit kept, which moved one reading from p = 0.014 to
0.00064. The author's run took about 1 h 11 min (09:30 to 10:41 UTC); the
notebook ran in 246 s at first, with peak memory around 0.4 GB, 418 s after
round 1 and 688 s at the end. During development Mendeley and the DANDI S3
redirect rejected `urllib` requests (403), and `requests` was used instead.

The photometry and the event log are on different clocks and the files do
not say so: every photometry series states a start of 0, while the
photometry_ttl2 channel, described in every file only as "raw photometry
channel ttl2", has rising edges on the cue onsets, and aligned by it the
photometry starts 3.6 to 251 s before the event log's zero; every session
was aligned here by fitting TTL2 edges to cue times. Two recordings are
deposited twice under different days, sub-JF-dLight-ramp2-F2 Day3 and Day4
(381,921 samples) and sub-JF-dLight-ramp2-F4 Day5 and Day6 (330,189
samples), with identical photometry, cues and licks; each was counted once,
at the first day. The 144 files of the 9 JF-VR-FP mice hold only raw
photometry and TTL channels, with no event log, task parameters, behaviour
or processing module, and nothing in the dandiset says which experiment
they belong to. 64 of the 144 JF-FP photometry series state a rate of
120481.9 Hz for signals sampled at 120.3 to 120.5 Hz, a factor of about
1000; the other files state 130 Hz, which the TTL2 fit confirms. The files
hold no ΔF/F of the depositors (processing/ holds only the task
parameters); the TTL channels, the eventFlag series and the lick code in
use (code 5, "Lick3 onset") are not explained, and the stretches where the
470 nm signal is near zero (about 2950 samples, some 23 s, in
JF-dLight-ramp2-M4 Day9 and JF-dLight-ramp2-M5 Day9) are not marked as
dropouts. The sessions run in blocks of 8 long-ITI then 8 short-ITI
sessions (JF-FP) or 7 or 8 long, 8 short and 7 or 8 long (JF-dLight-ramp2),
with median inter-cue intervals of 53.7 and 16.6 s, where the paper
describes them as alternating across days. The paper gives the ITIs as
about 8 and 55 s in the Results and about 15 and 45 s in the Methods (from
reward to the next cue the medians are 7.57 and 44.72 s), and the Krausz et
al. destination reward as > 80 and < 20 µL in the Figure 2B legend and
≥ 80 and ≤ 20 µL in the Methods, where the table's values (10, 20, 50, 80
and 90) are reward probabilities in percent in the Krausz et al. code.
Questions are put to the paper's authors: how the 405 nm signal was
regressed out and ΔF/F formed, where the ITI is measured from, what an
observation is in the 20-observation rule, how sessions are numbered and
how r was computed for Figure 4; and which rows are the "non-approach"
observations of Figure 2G-H, and the minimum count, approach axis and
profile forms of the value recovery.

The author's points on the protocol: its link-claim rules do not cover
claims on outside data that this paper's authors did not deposit and that
need no link to the dandiset, and these were tested on the pinned Mendeley
DOI, listed under `inputs`, with no link claim; some criteria state "under
one reading" for reproduced but not for partial, and any reading was
applied; it does not say whether a duplicate deposit is dropped before
sessions are numbered, and the later copy was dropped; the rule on
developing on a subset fits poorly when the whole run takes 4 minutes, and
3 sessions were timed before everything was run; and the `about: data` rule
felt forced for a finding that sets the reusing paper's description against
the data (the alternating sessions), which was recorded as about the data
with severity none.
