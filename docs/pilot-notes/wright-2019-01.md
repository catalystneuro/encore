# wright-2019, Wright et al. 2019

Thirty-two claims reproduced and seven partially; none was not reproduced or
blocked. Nine outcomes rest on an assumption: `fig1d-interval-suppression`,
`fig1s1a-nose-poke-rates`, `fig5bc-onset-beta-bias`,
`fig5d-onset-all-intervals`, `fig5s1-regressor-combinations`,
`fig5e-onset-tuning-curve`, `fig5gh-ramping-beta-bias`,
`fig5s1-ramping-regressor-combinations` and `fig6ef-beta-correlations`. The
first verification round agreed with all 39 outcomes, and so did the second;
nothing was proposed after the second, so there was no third round. CRCNS pag-1
is unversioned. The paper, Wright and McDannald, "Ventrolateral periaqueductal
gray neurons prioritize threat probability over fear output" (eLife 8, e45013),
records 245 vlPAG single units in six rats over 88 sessions of fear
discrimination, with danger, uncertainty and safety cues predicting shock with
probability 1.00, 0.375 and 0.00 while the rats nose poke for food, and reports
an Onset population of 29 neurons and a Ramping population of 14 that
discriminate the cues, and single-trial regressions in which both signal threat
probability over fear output, measured as suppression ratios. The claims were
written from the version of record served by Europe PMC, without a PDF, so
values printed only inside figures could not be read; the paper's only code
link, the lab website, returned 404, and every method came from the text. The
reader wrote 38 claims, and the claims check made seven changes and added
`fig5s1-ramping-regressor-combinations`, for 39. Reproduced are, among others,
the cue suppression ratios of `fig1c-total-suppression` (0.768, 0.502 and -0.002
under the first listed reading, 0.799, 0.531 and 0.030 under the reading the
Methods regression table fixes, against 0.80, 0.53 and 0.03), the clusters of
`fig1e-two-clusters` (197 and 48 against 199 and 46), `onset-population` (29
neurons from 5 rats) and `ramping-population` (14 from 4 rats), the population
ANOVAs and t-tests of Figure 2, the single-unit patterns of Figure 4, every
Onset regression claim of Figure 5 (regressor F(2,56) = 7.16, the paper's value,
in `fig5d-onset-all-intervals`), `fig5i-ramping-all-intervals` (means 0.577,
0.120 and -0.026 under the table's reading, paper 0.58, 0.12 and -0.03) and the
post-cue claims of Figure 6A to 6D. Partial are fig1s1a, where baseline poke
rates differ across trial types with the 20 s window (F = 3.47, p = 0.017) and
not with the 10 s one (p = 0.066); `fig2e-departure-times`, where danger departs
from safety before uncertainty under every reading but no reading puts all four
times within tolerance (2.7, 4.5, 5.1 and 6.4 s against 2.8, 5.8, 5.7 and 6.5 by
window start); `fig2s1-single-trial`, where Ramping firing exceeds baseline at 6
of 10 safety positions where the criterion allows 5;
`fig3-onset-ramping-independent`, with 3 Onset-Ramping pairs in two sessions
against four in four sessions and no negative beta;
`fig4efg-ramping-single-unit-biases`, whose panel E correlation has p = 0.11;
fig5s1-ramping, where total fear alone also differs from zero under all 18
readings; and fig6ef, partial only through the criterion's partial branch, with
Onset 3 of 20 and Ramping 0 at p < 0.05 against the paper's 13 and 4. The
author's first run had the same counts, with 12 outcomes marked assumed. The
answer run answered the author's one question by giving interval fear and total
fear separate poke baseline windows in the claims file and adding 0/0 set to 1
as a reading; the author-answers run changed no outcome, moved the measured
values of fig1c and fig5i to the first listed reading, and took the assumption
off `fig5s2-binary-regressor`, `fig5f-ramping-last-interval` and
`fig5j-ramping-tuning-curve`. Round 1 held that baseline equality in fig1s1a
holds only with the window the data check does not favour, that fig6ef is close
to not reproduced, that the near-threshold units behind the missing fourth pair
were not shown, that fig2s1 printed no verdict, that Z-scoring forces the Figure
4 correlations negative, that the session-rate reading ignored gaps in the spike
trains, and that two terms of the mixed ANOVA were unchecked; in reply a
within-neuron shuffle null, a table of units with screen p from 0.017 to 0.05,
the criterion's verdict for fig2s1, a session rate over the stored windows and a
synthetic check of the mixed ANOVA were added, and fig6ef was kept partial with
its fourfold miss stated. Round 2 found that the Summary overstated the match to
the paper's printed precision, that the fig2e note was wrong about window ends,
that nothing printed showed the script's interval ratios using the 20 s
baseline, and two miscounted notes; all were corrected. The author's run took
about 38 minutes; the notebook ran in 95 s with 0.36 GB peak memory, 110 s after
the author-answers run, 150 s after round 1 and 122 s after round 2, and nothing
failed.

The data set holds the 245 units of the 88 sessions from six rats, with spike
times, events and waveforms, so every claim could be tested. Neither the paper
nor the data description states the nose poke baselines of the suppression
ratios or how a 0/0 ratio is handled; the paper's printed regression input is
reproduced on one unit (rf03092316 SPK12a) only with a 2 s baseline for interval
fear, a 20 s baseline for total fear and 0/0 set to 1, and other choices change
the outcomes of fig1d and fig1s1a. The data description says the cube's
poke.base is the poke rate 2 s before the cue, but the data set's cubeScript.m
computes it over the 20 s before onset and uses it for every suppression ratio,
including the interval ratios, where the paper's interval fear uses 2 s; the
script also rounds the 2 s baseline firing rate down to an integer, which would
make the Onset screen 31 neurons. The spike trains hold spikes only from 20 s
before each cue onset to 31 to 34 s after it (34 of 12,261,110 spikes fall
outside), with gaps of about 141 s, which the description does not say, so the
files cannot give a whole-session rate. The data set does not mark which units
the paper placed in each population, and the paper's lists are in figures only;
with populations matching the paper's counts only three Onset-Ramping pairs
share a session against the paper's four. Five pairs of units in the same
session share 66 to 90% of the spikes of one unit within 0.5 ms and may be one
neuron sorted twice; the paper counts all 245 units, and one of them, dr02092716
SPK04a, is an Onset neuron. The paper and the description give a mean ITI of 3.5
min, but cue onsets are 132 to 195 s apart (mean about 2.7 min) in all 88
sessions. Interval Z firing has mean zero over each neuron's 32 trials, so the
cross-neuron correlations of Figure 4A, 4B, 4E and 4F are negative even with
trial labels shuffled (median r -0.49 to -0.70), and the observed -0.45 to -0.55
lie within that range. The claims also record that the Onset danger against
uncertainty test is p = 9.70x10-5 in the Results and 9.70x10-4 in the Figure 2B
legend, and that the mean danger suppression ratio is 0.80 in the Results and
0.83 in a supplement legend. Questions are put to the paper's authors: which
poke baseline windows and 0/0 rule were used throughout and which baseline
Figure 1, figure supplement 1A plots; which shuffle gave the chance levels of
Figure 4D and 4H, where permuting within neurons reproduces the claims and
permuting each cue across neurons does not; and which firing measure and time
point give the departure times of Figure 2E.

The author's points on the protocol: it does not say whether an author may add a
reading that the paper's own table fixes but the claims file does not list;
recording the first listed reading in `measured` sits badly with such an added
reading; some criteria give no rule for a mixed result, such as a wrong count
with the effect present; it ranks code the paper links but says nothing of code
deposited only in the data set; and counting each recording once does not fit
unit pairs that overlap without being identical.
