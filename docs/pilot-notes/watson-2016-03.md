# watson-2016 again, Watson et al. 2016 (third attempt)

Twenty-seven claims reproduced, ten partially, sixteen not reproduced and
none blocked. The first verification round disagreed on 13 claims and the
second agreed with all 53 outcomes; nothing was proposed after the second, so
there was no third round. The attempt pins DANDI:000041 at 0.261104.0300, the
latest published version, chosen because its re-conversion corrected the
units, cell types and gain the analysis uses; CRCNS:fcx-1 is unversioned, and
the notebook prints the MD5 of each file read. The paper, Watson et al.,
"Network Homeostasis and State Dynamics of Neocortical Sleep" (Neuron),
records units and LFP in the frontal cortex of rats across WAKE and SLEEP, and
reports that sleep homogenizes the firing rate distribution, fast cells
decreasing and slow cells increasing their rates over sleep and within nonREM
episodes and packets, with UP/DOWN state, spindle and band power dynamics, and
that the sub-states between packets (WAKE, MAs, REM) change rates in
different ways. The claims were written from the accepted author manuscript
(PMC4873379) with its supplement, without a PDF of the main paper, so values
printed only inside Figs. 2 to 5 could not be read; the paper links no code.
The new claims file, 53 claims covering both datasets, replaced the
per-dataset file of 41 the earlier attempts used. Every claim was tested on
both datasets except `fig5c-wake-movement`, tested on CRCNS only because DANDI
has no motion or EMG signal. Reproduced are, among others, the dataset counts,
the state durations, the UP/DOWN durations, the decline of the population rate
over sleep, the first to last packet slope (0.872 [0.840, 0.904]), the sextile
changes over sleep and from WAKE before to WAKE after it, and the trends within
nonREM. Not reproduced are the MA statistics, the rate-group correlations of
Fig. 2F, the spindle and UP/DOWN trends over sleep, the within-WAKE and REM
sextile trends of Fig. S5, the unchanged interneurons of Fig. S2D and most of
the sub-state effects of Fig. 5 (MA to MA across a packet, REM between
packets, REM duration and theta, delta change, sigma before an MA and WAKE
movement). The author's first run judged a claim reproduced only when it held
on both datasets and gave 18 reproduced, 21 partially and 14 not; round 1
found that rule was not in the claims file, that the burst index dropped every
interval crossing a 1 s grid boundary, that short bins of reading c were
empty, that the deposited MAs were never located and that the sextiles were
open to regression to the mean; in reply outcomes were judged on CRCNS, which
holds all 27 sessions and the lab's own intervals and detections, intervals
were counted from spike times, bins were fixed, a table of where the MAs lie
and held-out sextile rankings and reverse slopes were added, and five
claims-file readings went to a reader in the new `questions/watson-2016.yaml`.
Round 2 found sentences the outputs did not support (the best DANDI reading
of `fig2a-median-rates`, "rank-sum tests are not significant" for
`post-sleep-wake-lower`, the DANDI directions of `fig4e-within-up-sextiles`,
the summary's omission of the WAKE-SLEEP duration) and an `assumed` mark on
`fig2de-fast-before-slow` with no assumption behind it, all corrected. The
answer and author-answers runs changed nothing, as no questions file existed
then. The reconciliation with watson-2016-02 is not blind: the author-answers
run printed a report quoting one line of the earlier record and listed the
earlier entry's path without opening it. 13 of the 37 shared claims differ,
mostly from the rewritten claims file, and none of the 10 earlier findings is
fixed. The author's session ran from 05:00 to about 07:00 UTC and nothing
failed; the notebook ran in 24.6 min at first, with peak memory 1.6 GB, about
19 min after round 1 and 1513 s at the end, streaming 88.9 GB from CRCNS with
peaks of 2.62 GB in the notebook and 5.13 GB in the largest loading worker,
and in the reconciliation run 1285 s, with 491 s for the new
`earlier_findings.ipynb`.

The dataset was converted by CatalystNeuro (catalystneuro/buzsaki-lab-to-nwb).
DANDI:000041 holds only Awake, Non-REM and REM epochs: the paper's analysis
units (WAKE, SLEEP and WAKE-SLEEP cycles, packets, MAs, WAKE interruptions,
nonREM and REM episodes), its UP/DOWN states and spindles, and the motion and
EMG signals, all in CRCNS:fcx-1, are not in it, and its only ElectricalSeries
is the 1250 Hz LFP; built from the epochs by Table S1's rules they give 47
cycles against 51, one session without a cycle, 181 MAs in cycles against
434, a median packet of 144 s against 111 s and a median REM episode of 64 s
against 98 s. It holds 22 of the 27 sessions, without rat B22 and the ACC
probes of Dino 061914 and 062014, so 10 of 11 rats, 850 of 995 pE and 95 of
126 pI cells, and Dino-061914 has no LFP. In 7 DANDI sessions whose spike
trains are identical to the CRCNS stable units the cell_type labels differ
from the lab's (BWRat20-101513 has 63 pE and 3 pI against 60 and 6), and
Dino-072314 and Dino-072414 hold 10 and 15 units more than the CRCNS stable
set, with no column marking the stable units. The DANDI release notes say the
files were re-converted to fix problems found by a reproduction attempt. On
CRCNS, the deposited intervals hold 2130 MAs, of which 434 lie within the
SLEEP of the 51 cycles (median 11.0 s, 0.18 per minute, 3.7% of SLEEP), 26 in
a scored WAKE and 1670 outside any scored SLEEP or WAKE, against the paper's
790 (median 15.9 s, 0.32 per minute, 9.1%), and which MAs the paper counted is
not stated. The files hold 51 WAKE-SLEEP cycles where the paper reports 54;
345 WakeInterruptions within SLEEP are shorter than their 40 s minimum, and
one packet in BWRat17_121912 lasts 14 s, under Table S1's 20 s; cycles overlap
in Rizzo_022715 and Splinter_020515; and a scored WAKE follows the SLEEP of
only 23 of the 51 cycles, so the paper's 5 min post-sleep WAKE windows cannot
be located from the scored intervals for most cycles. The motion field, said
to be z-scored, is non-negative in every session with per-session means from
0.553 to 4076, and BWRat19_032513 holds 158 NaN values; RecordingFileIntervals
is [0, inf] in the three B22 sessions; and the UP/DOWN file is named
_UPDOWNINtervals.mat, not as the description gives it. The ACC and mPFC
folders of Dino 061914 and 062014 each hold an .eeg of the same size with no
shared spike train, consistent with one recording deposited twice, which the
paper counts as separate sessions. Each session is one tar.gz of 0.94 to
6.12 GB, and in 18 of the 27 the LFP comes before a spike, cell or state file,
so reading those needs the whole archive streamed. In the paper, spindle
incidence is 1.94 per minute in the Results and 2.94 in Table S1, which the
deposited spindles match, and Table S1's WAKE-SLEEP duration of 5918 s
compares with 4214 s in the data, while every other duration matches exactly.
Questions are put to the paper's authors: which MAs make the 790; the band,
smoothing and scale of the low-gamma rule for UP/DOWN detection; the sampling
unit of the correlations against normalized time; the burst index
definition; the autocorrelogram decay model; how triplets are formed; whether
motion was z-scored and which movement measure was used; and how WAKE sextiles
are computed for cells recorded over several cycles.

The author's points on the protocol: it has no rule for combining two
datasets into one outcome per claim; `measured` takes one set of values, so
the CRCNS values were recorded; docs/archives.md describes CRCNS zips, but
fcx-1 holds tar.gz files, in 14 of which the LFP comes before the metadata,
so each tar was streamed once and the channel count inferred where the .xml
came later, matching it in all 10 such cases; composite criteria do not say
when a part "holds"; the suggested clock check does not fit continuous
sessions without trials, and a contrast at UP onsets was used instead; and
delegating one long caching command to a subagent gave no parallelism.
