Reproducing Kaku, Liu et al. (2025) from dandiset 001619¶

Paper. Kaku H, Liu LD, Gao R, West S, Liao S-M, Finkelstein A, Kleinfeld D, Thomas A, Tipparaju SL, Svoboda K, Li N (2025). A brainstem map of orofacial rhythms. bioRxiv, doi:10.1101/2025.01.27.635041. The claims file was written from the preprint, version 1 (January 27, 2025), its Methods and the Extended Data legends, and from the paper's analysis code (github.com/NuoLiLabBCM/KakuEtAl2025 at commit fbc0abe).

Dandiset. DANDI:001619, version 0.251027.1953. The paper cites no version (its data statement says the data will be deposited upon publication; the dandiset was created later). This is the only published version, and it is the most recent; the draft has the same 335 assets and size. It holds the brainstem Neuropixels recordings of the multi-directional licking task: one NWB file per probe insertion, with units, quality metrics, CCF positions, DeepLabCut tracking and the airflow (breathing) trace, per trial.

What is not in it. The cued licking task with ALM, latSC and brainstem photostimulation, the rat recordings, the raw videos, and the premotor tracing data are not in the dandiset.

Outside input. Region names in the files are Allen CCF names; their acronyms and parent regions come from the Allen structure tree, read from the iblatlas repository at a pinned commit (kaku.ONTOLOGY_URL). It maps names only. The regions the CCF does not label (preBotzinger complex, parts of IRN, the areas next to AMB and NTS) are built from the Allen CCFv3 2017 annotation volume at 25 um (kaku.CCF_ANNOTATION_URL).

Revision after round 1 of verification. Lick frequencies within bouts take only pairs inside one bout; the regions above are built from the annotation volume, not from labelled units; the 3D tracks are read at each session's trial offset; the peak-difference claims carry a random-split null; Fig 4e units use the 40 fastest and 40 slowest cycles as the claims file says, with the code's selection beside them; and ed1, ed1bc, fig1l, fig4d, ed7 and ed8i show the outputs the verifier asked for.

Every file stores each unit's spikes in units/spike_times with spike_times_index holding the offset where the unit starts. In NWB the index holds where each unit ends, so a standard reader (pynwb) gives each unit the spikes of the unit before it. The per-unit, per-trial spike counts (unit and trial pairs) agree with the start-offset reading in every file, and spikes are read that way here. In the files listed above the histology table (region and CCF position) has more rows than the units, so its rows cannot be matched to units with certainty; like the paper's code, the rows are taken by position. Spike times are relative to the trial start; every trial starts at time 0 in the trial table, so the trials cannot be placed on a session clock. The behaviour of the two probes of a session is the same recording deposited twice: it is read once per session. The age field (for example P273W, 273 weeks) is not plausible for these mice. The stimulus field says photostim, but the files hold no photostimulation or cue times.

def within_trial_freq(times, bout=None):
    """Inverse intervals between consecutive onsets in the same trial; with bout, both onsets in the

def null_report(key, data):
    """Random-split null (verifier, round 1). The fast and slow windows differ, so a peak between their
    starts gives a positive difference with no effect. Per session, the difference for 20 random splits of
    the same cycles into groups of the halves' sizes (kaku.random_split); the session's null value is
    their mean. Data against null, paired over the sessions that have both."""
    rows = []
    for r, (_, s) in zip(res, sess.iterrows()):
        p = r[key] or {}
        nul = np.asarray(p.get("diff_null", []), float)
        rows.append({"subject": s.subject, "session": s.session, "diff": p.get("diff", np.nan),
                     "null": np.nanmean(nul) if np.isfinite(nul).any() else np.nan,
                     "null_first": nul[np.isfinite(nul)][0] if np.isfinite(nul).any() else np.nan,
                     "null_pos": np.mean(nul[np.isfinite(nul)] > 0) if np.isfinite(nul).any() else np.nan})
    N = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    both = N.dropna(subset=["diff", "null"])
    t0 = stats.ttest_1samp(N.null_first.dropna(), 0)
    tp = stats.ttest_rel(both["diff"], both["null"])
    print(f"Null, one random split per session: {N.null_first.notna().sum()} sessions, mean {N.null_first.mean() * 1000:.1f} ms, p = {t0.pvalue:.2e} (t-test against 0)")
    print(f"Null, mean of 20 splits per session: mean {N.null.mean() * 1000:.1f} ms; share of splits above 0, median over sessions {N.null_pos.median():.2f}")
    print(f"Data minus null over {len(both)} sessions: mean {(both['diff'] - both['null']).mean() * 1000:.1f} ms, paired t-test p = {tp.pvalue:.2e}; "
          f"data above null in {int((both['diff'] > both['null']).sum())}")
    mm = both.groupby("subject")[["diff", "null"]].mean()
    tm = stats.ttest_rel(mm["diff"], mm["null"]) if len(mm) > 2 else None
    print(f"Mice: data above null in {int((mm['diff'] > mm['null']).sum())} of {len(mm)}; paired t-test p = {tm.pvalue if tm else np.nan:.2e}")
    return N

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper's code (process_mat.py) is ported to the streamed NWB files and its choices are kept. The tuning curves of the 1000 resamples per unit are fitted by a grid search where the code uses curve_fit (the measured MI uses curve_fit; on 300 random units the two agree to a median of 0.0015 in MI, and on 24 tuned units the code's own significance scheme gives the same decision with either fit). Regions the CCF does not label are built from the CCF annotation volume as the claims file's O3 words them; fig2f and ed8bc reproduce with AMB's whole extent and do not with AMB's extent taken per AP step, so both are blocked. For Fig 4e the licking units' 40 fastest and 40 slowest cycles are used, as the claims file says; the code takes the 40 nearest the median. The peak-difference measures of Figs 1g and 4e give a positive difference under a random split of the cycles (15.7 ms and 47.5 to 54.7 ms), because the slow window starts later than the fast one; Figs 1i and 4f do not (2.7 ms and 1.5 to 8.8 ms). What was found about the dataset. The spike index of every file holds start offsets, not end offsets, so standard NWB readers misassign spikes; 19 files have more histology rows than units; the behaviour of both probes of a session is the same recording deposited twice; trials carry no session times; the age field (P2xxW) and the photostim stimulus field do not fit the data; the left/right location text disagrees with the side ccf_x gives; the 3D tracking has no stated axes, and in 19 sessions its trial index is one below the DLC trial number. The cued licking task, the photostimulation experiments, the rat recordings and the raw videos are not in the dandiset, and it holds 335 insertions where the paper reports 339. In six subjects (DL029 to DL034) the airflow peaks 20 to 30 ms later against the tongue than in the others, and fewer inspirations fall inside a protrusion; the files do not say how the airflow and video clocks were aligned.
