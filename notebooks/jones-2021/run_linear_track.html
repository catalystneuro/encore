Aery Jones et al. (2021) from dandiset 000165: linear track (run) sessions¶

Paper. Aery Jones EA, Rao A, Zilberter M, Djukic B, Bant JS, Gillespie AK, Koutsodendris N, Nelson M, Yoon SY, Huang K, Yuan H, Gill TM, Huang Y, Frank LM (2021). Dentate gyrus and CA3 GABAergic interneurons bidirectionally modulate signatures of internal and external drive to CA1. Cell Reports 37, 110159. doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2021.110159. The claims file (claims/jones-2021.yaml) was written from the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC9069800) with its main supplement; Table S7 was not available to the reader.

Dandiset. DANDI:000165, version 0.211118.1526 (doi:10.48324/dandi.000165/0.211118.1526). The paper links the draft, not a version. This is the only published version, and the draft has the same 572 assets and size, so the most recent published version is pinned. Files are streamed with remfile and h5py; nothing is written to disk.

Notebooks. rest_home_cage.ipynb tests the claims on the home cage (rest) sessions; run_linear_track.ipynb tests those on the linear track (run) sessions; csd_cohort1.ipynb tests the CSD claim on the single-shank home cage sessions of cohort 1. Methods are in the helper jones2021.py, whose docstring says where each parameter comes from.

Common method (Method M of the claims file). For each feature, a linear mixed model feature ~ treatment + (treatment|animal), fitted by maximum likelihood, with an F test of the treatment coefficient (CNO minus vehicle); the model is fitted exactly from per-mouse, per-treatment sufficient statistics (cell counts, means and sums of squares), which gives the same likelihood as fitting every observation. The genotype test is the likelihood ratio of feature ~ treatment + (treatment|animal) against feature ~ treatment:genotype + (treatment:genotype|animal) on the pooled data, with 9 degrees of freedom as Table S3 gives. Two readings of the log transform are run: (a) log when the within-cell residuals have skewness above 1 (assumption: the Methods log right-skewed features without a rule); (b) log for every feature (the code). Significance is Holm-Bonferroni at 0.05 over the paper's family (24 rest or 36 run comparisons per group or pair), with comparisons not computed here ranked after those computed.

Checks of the data:

  • 170 linear track files from 30 mice (8 PV-Cre, 7 SST-Cre, 7 PV-Cre/SST-Cre, 8 empty vector), all on the 4-shank probe, as the paper's cohort 3. Sessions last about 25 min; the Methods say 30 min.

  • LFP values stay within about +-12500 file units on the track. As in the home cage notebook, the unit attribute says microvolts.

Clock checks. Over 1 s bins of the whole session, the log theta/delta power ratio at the CA1 SWR site correlates with speed at zero lag (mean r = 0.134, positive in 141 of 170 sessions) and hardly with speed shifted by 30 s or more (mean 0.009); 122 of 170 sessions have their maximum within 2 s of zero lag (median 0 s). Theta power alone peaks with speed 2 to 3 s later (mean 0.125 at +3 s against 0.030 at 0 s), consistent with theta rising before running. Every file gives the LFP a starting_time of 0 s and its position timestamps start at 0 s. Position and LFP are therefore consistent with one clock to within about 2 s, the resolution of this check, and the run periods are taken as deposited. CA1 MUA rate also follows speed, with its maximum at +1 to +2 s (mean 0.070 at +2 s), so the spikes are on the position clock to within a few seconds. A finer spike-clock check, CA1 MUA around SWRs in still periods of the track, gives a clear peak in only 24 of 170 sessions (median lag -0.245 s, 5 within 20 ms), so the sub-second spike offset on the track is not established for most sessions; run MUA (fig5b) is a rate over 100 ms bins wholly in run, which such an offset changes only at the edges of run periods.

Fit check (per-peak features)¶

The frequency features have one observation per oscillation peak, up to millions per group, and their F tests use the residual degrees of freedom (n - 2), as the code does. Two checks of these results. First, every model is refitted with a Nelder-Mead search continuing from the best of the three L-BFGS-B starts (polish=True): with this many observations the likelihood is large, its finite-difference gradient is noisy, and L-BFGS-B can stop short of the maximum. Second, for the frequency features the fitted random-effect standard deviations, the same F statistic referred to the number of mice (F(1, mice - 1)), and a t test of the per-mouse mean change.
