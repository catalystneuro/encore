Reproducing See et al. (2018) from CRCNS ac-3¶

Paper. See JZ, Atencio CA, Sohal VS, Schreiner CE (2018). Coordinated neuronal ensembles in primary auditory cortical columns. eLife 7:e35587, doi:10.7554/eLife.35587. The claims file (claims/see-2018.yaml) was written from the version of record (PMC6017807) with its figure legends, Materials and methods, and Supplementary file 1.

Data set. CRCNS ac-3 (See, Atencio & Schreiner 2018), read from the public bucket crcnsarchive under ac-3/live/, zip files opened in place. CRCNS data sets are not versioned, so the version is recorded as unversioned, the only one published; the paper cites the data set by its DOI and no version. The files read have these MD5 sums in the data set's checksums.md5: Data.zip 3140d6178de9c044932901795a4cc817, Stimulus.zip ce43beade196882598cecddff5520d7b (the notebook prints the listing it read). The data descriptor is version 0.6 (March 28, 2018).

What the data set holds. 16 recordings (site1 to site16), 8 with the DMR (rn1) and 8 with the RN (rn16), 655 units, the 10-min stimuli and their triggers: the 32-channel columnar recordings behind Figures 1 to 7, 10 and 11C to F. It does not hold the 50 repeats of the 5-s segments (Figure 9 and its supplements), the 15 repeats of the 10-min RN (Figure 11A, B and the 'repeat' CRs of Figure 11E, F) or the 64-channel recordings with spontaneous and evoked epochs (Figure 8).

The descriptor's counts and units hold. There are 16 files, 8 per stimulus, and the stim field of every unit matches its file name. The 655 units match the paper's total. Every fs is 20 kHz. The triggers come every 333.3 ms (6666 or 6667 samples), which is the parameter files' taxis of 667 columns of 0.5 ms. Each stimulus matrix has exactly 667 columns per trigger of its files (1795 for rn1, 1799 for rn16), so it covers about 598 s, the "10-min" stimulus. The envelope runs from -20 to 20 dB, the 40 dB modulation depth of the Methods, over 64 carriers from 500 Hz to 40 kHz. Spike times are in ms, and sorted except in two units (site 4 unit 17 and site 7 unit 21), whose few steps back are under 0.1 ms and which also repeat spike times; no measure here depends on the order. They run from about 0 to 622 s, and 96 to 98% of the spikes fall within the stimulus. position is a pair: a lateral offset of -50, 0 or 50 um (the three shanks' columns of the a1x32-poly3) and a depth of 153 to 662 um below the surface, 150 um above the tip at the deepest. Sites 13 and 14 have one first trigger at 1.02 s where the others have 1.00 s. Site pairs (1, 2), (3, 4) and so on share a tip depth, so they may be one penetration recorded with both stimuli; the next cell tests whether any unit appears in two files.

# Clock check: the STRF of each unit with its spikes on the stimulus clock against the same spikes
# shifted by 30 s (circularly within the stimulus). A shared clock gives a structured STRF only when
# aligned, with its peak at the 10 to 30 ms latency of AI (Methods, Electrophysiology).
rng_clock = np.random.default_rng(11)
rows = []
for s in cne.SITES:
    stim = cne.STIM[(s["stim"], cne.STRF_BIN_MS)]
    dur = cne.stim_duration_ms(s)
    for k, u in enumerate(s["units"]):
        t = u["stim_ms"][np.isfinite(u["stim_ms"])]
        out = {"site": s["site"], "unit": k}
        for label, tt in (("aligned", t), ("shifted_30s", (t + 30000) % dur)):
            st = cne.sta(stim, cne.segment_index(tt, cne.STRF_BIN_MS, stim.shape[1], cne.STRF_LAGS), cne.STRF_LAGS)
            out[f"ptd_{label}"] = float(st.max() - st.min())
            prof = np.abs(st - st.mean()).max(axis=0)
            out[f"peak_lag_ms_{label}"] = (cne.STRF_LAGS - 1 - int(prof.argmax())) * cne.STRF_BIN_MS + cne.STRF_BIN_MS / 2
        rows.append(out)
clock = pd.DataFrame(rows)
clock["ratio"] = clock.ptd_aligned / clock.ptd_shifted_30s
summary = clock.groupby("site").agg(units=("unit", "size"), median_ptd_ratio=("ratio", "median"),
                                    frac_ratio_above_1_5=("ratio", lambda r: (r > 1.5).mean()),
                                    median_peak_lag_aligned=("peak_lag_ms_aligned", "median"),
                                    median_peak_lag_shifted=("peak_lag_ms_shifted_30s", "median"))
print(summary.round(2).to_string())

In all 16 recordings the STRFs are stronger with the spikes on the stimulus clock than with the same spikes shifted by 30 s (median PTD ratio 1.03 to 2.79 per recording, 1.40 over 655 units). In the aligned STRFs the peak falls at a median lag of 12.5 to 32.5 ms per recording, within or near the 10 to 30 ms latency of AI. In the shifted STRFs it falls at 42.5 to 62.5 ms, the middle of the window, where a peak with no structure lands. For the 289 units with a ratio above 1.5 the median peak lag is 12.5 ms. The triggers and spike times therefore share a clock, and the mapping of 667 stimulus columns to each trigger interval is consistent with the data. The check is weak in sites 9, 15 and 13 (median ratios 1.03 to 1.13; 2 to 17% of units above 1.5), where few units have strong STRFs.

def r2(x, y, log=False):
    x, y = np.asarray(x, float), np.asarray(y, float)
    ok = np.isfinite(x) & np.isfinite(y)
    if log:
        ok &= (x > 0) & (y > 0)
        x, y = np.log10(x), np.log10(y)
    f = stats.linregress(x[ok], y[ok])
    return f.rvalue ** 2, f.slope, int(ok.sum())

Models (Figure 11C, D)¶

200 runs of each model per recording, as the Methods. Shuffled: each 10-ms train shifted circularly. PR: each spike assigned to a random time bin of the same projection class (15 equal classes over the full range of the unit's STRF projections), on (a) 10-ms or (b) 5-ms bins, the latter summed to 10 ms. DG: binary 5-ms trains (counts above one set to one) fitted for means and pairwise covariances (cne.fit_dg, a Python port of the method of CorBinian's findLatentGaussian01, not the MATLAB code itself), sampled and summed to 10 ms. Membership in model runs uses the recording's thresholds from the shuffles of the real data, as the shuffled IC weights follow 1.5/sqrt(n) and do not depend on the data (printed above): an assumption, to fit the time limit. The check after the models shows how much the DG fit was changed to make its latent correlation matrix positive definite, and how well a sampled run matches the real 5-ms rates and covariances.

How the analysis differs from the paper's:

  • The detection follows the Methods, but the Marcenko-Pastur bound, the fastICA settings, IC sign, the activity projection and the event definition are this attempt's readings (claims file and cne.py). The number of cNEs matches the paper in total (104) and in its dependence on the number of units. The cNEs are smaller (4.6 against 7.4 members), so the membership threshold or IC scaling probably differs from the authors'. The membership claims (fig7b, fig7c, fig7f, fig7-negative-weights) and the pair counts depend on it.
  • PWC sharpness, as the half-count width of a raw histogram over +-50 or +-100 ms, includes the flat base and cannot give the paper's 2 ms; neither can a baseline subtracted from it (6 to 16 ms). The paper's lag range or baseline handling is not stated. PWCs use the spikes inside the stimulus period.
  • STRFs use 5-ms bins and 20 lags (and 1 ms with 50 lags for the temporal profiles), from the data set's sample code by the first author; the paper gives no STRF parameters. The MI projection bins are a standard choice.
  • Fewer iterations than the paper, for time: 200 model runs for the CRs (paper 1000). Membership in model runs uses the real data's thresholds. The random groups number 500 per cNE, as the paper.
  • The STRF MI is computed on the same events as the STRF, as the Methods describe; with the STRF from held-out events (a check) it is about a tenth to a fifth as large under event reading (a) (fig5a), and the cNE MI exceeds the member MI far more clearly (fig10c).
  • Arrays are float64, and activity is rounded to 1e-9 before it is compared with a threshold, so that the results do not depend on the machine; the ICA of the shuffles and of the model runs is float32, for time.
  • The DG model is a Python port of the CorBinian fit (bisection on each pair's latent correlation), not the MATLAB code.
