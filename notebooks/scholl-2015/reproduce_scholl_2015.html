Scholl et al. (2015) on CRCNS pvc-10¶

Paper: Scholl, Pattadkal, Dilly, Priebe and Zemelman (2015), "Local Integration Accounts for Weak Selectivity of Mouse Neocortical Parvalbumin Interneurons", Neuron 87:424-436, doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2015.06.030. Claims file: claims/scholl-2015.yaml, written from the version of record (main text, Experimental Procedures and Supplemental Information legends).

Data set: CRCNS pvc-10, "Disparity selectivity in mouse V1 measured using two-photon calcium imaging", doi:10.6080/K0GT5K3V, read anonymously from the public S3 bucket crcnsarchive.

Version. CRCNS data sets are not versioned, so the version is recorded as unversioned. The data set as published is the folder pvc-10/live/, the one its file list (live/filelist.txt) and its live/checksums.md5 describe; every file there is listed with an MD5 sum. The bucket also holds, under the same prefix, a folder pvc-10/original/to_remove/ with processed per-cell MATLAB structs that neither list describes. This attempt uses them, for the reasons given in the data check: they are public and under the data set's own identifier, each file read is identified by the ETag the listing gives (printed below), and for the 9 cells checked on one plane the stored traces equal the mask means of the published movie. Every tested claim rests on them, and on the assumption that their cells are the paper's.

Outcome in brief. The published folder holds the raw movies, stimulus tables and plane shifts of 6 PV-Cre;Ai14 mice, and no cell masks, PV+/PV- labels or per-cell measures. The unpublished structs hold per-cell responses, DSI, preferences and positions for PV+ and PV- cells of mice 8, 9 and 10, and PV+ cells of mouse 7. Seven claims are blocked (histology, CaMKIIa mice, neuropil patches, and the counts of all identified cells, none of which is deposited). Eighteen are tested on 3 of the 6 animals (4 for the PV+ side of Figure 3). The cells are taken as the authors' binocmonocinds lists, an assumption the data check explains; most outcomes that rest on it change under another cell set, and the notebook shows them all.

Stimulus tables¶

Each stim file has two columns: the frame at which a stimulus starts and its condition (1 blank, 2 to 9 the binocular phases 0 to 315 deg, 10 ipsilateral, 11 contralateral). The paper says 6 to 7 repeats per focal plane, a 1.5 to 2 s stimulus followed by a 3 s blank, and imaging at 30 Hz.

The processed structs in original/to_remove/¶

The bucket holds, under the data set's own prefix pvc-10/, a folder original/to_remove/ that the published file list and checksums do not describe. Its MATLAB structs hold per-cell data of the kind the descriptor promises ("fluorescence traces, peak responses per condition, ... disparity selectivity index, preferred relative disparity, the position of the cell"), which the published folder live/ lacks.

Decision. These files are read and used for the claims they can test, for mice 8, 9 and 10 (both populations) and mouse 7 (PV+ only). The basis: they are public, in the archive's own bucket under the data set's identifier, and each file read is identified by the ETag of the S3 listing, printed below (for a single-part upload, the MD5 of its content), so a later change is detectable. They are not part of the data set as published: they are in neither live/filelist.txt nor live/checksums.md5, the folder's name says they are to be removed, and nothing documents them. Every outcome that uses them says so, and rests on the assumption, checked as far as the data allow below, that their cells are the paper's cells. The checks below are: what one entry is, whether the per-trial responses come from the published movies, how the stored indices are computed, and which cells the structs hold.

The clock of the stimulus table, on the example plane¶

The descriptor's example plane (mouse5, depth 260 um) is streamed whole, the mean intensity in each of the 50 example masks is taken per frame (minus the offset of -160 that the data set's examplecode.m subtracts), and dF/F per trial is computed with Fo the 10 frames before onset (a standard choice; see the helper). If the stimulus table shares the movie's clock, stimulus trials should differ from blank trials in a window after onset, and the difference should fall when the onsets are moved or the condition labels shuffled. This is a check of the clock only. The window is centred on the peak of the average over all masks (the plane), where the paper's plain reading is per cell; a per-mask window is shown as a variant. Either way the window is placed on the same trials it then measures, so the stimulus-minus-blank value is biased upward; the shuffled labels and the onset shifts, which use the same window, are the comparison. The data set gives no frame rate: the window is 15 frames (0.5 s at the paper's 30 Hz), and 13 frames (0.5 s at the 26.2 frames/s the onset intervals imply for this mouse) is shown; the conflict between the two rates is not resolved here and is recorded as a finding. This checks one plane of 58; the others are not checked, except mouse 8 depth 205 through the structs above.

How the analysis differs from the paper's:

  • The per-cell data come from structs outside the published folder, for mice 8 to 10 (and the PV+ cells of mouse 7), against the paper's 6 mice. Counts of cells in the analyses are therefore about half the paper's or less (for example 18 PV+ cells at 50 um against 86), which bears on every test of significance.
  • The visually responsive cells are taken as the binocmonocinds lists (an assumption); the Methods' criterion is also applied with and without a neuropil test, and the outcomes that differ under those cell sets are marked as assumed. The lists decide every outcome; the other sets are shown as the sensitivity to this choice.
  • ODI uses blank-subtracted responses with negatives set to zero, the form the stored DSI takes.
  • The local analysis runs the claims file's open readings; reading 1a (individual on population) gives major-axis slopes with bootstrap SEs far above 1 in most cases, as population averages vary little, while 1b gives slopes of the size the paper reports.

What was found about the data set, from the outputs above:

  • The published folder holds raw movies (green and red), stimulus tables and plane shifts for 58 planes of 6 mice, and 50 example masks for one plane. It holds none of the per-cell data that its descriptor says it contains, and no masks or PV+/PV- labels for the paper's cells.
  • The folder original/to_remove/, not in the file list or checksums, holds those per-cell data as MATLAB structs, one entry per cell: for mice 8, 9 and 10 (PV+ and PV-) and mouse 7 (PV+ only); the three mouse 7 PV- files are truncated, and mice 5 and 6 have index lists only. Their Ftrace equals the mask mean of the published movie exactly (9 cells of mouse 8, depth 205), and their trial counts per condition equal the stim files' repeats or one fewer. The stored DSI is the vector strength of blank-subtracted responses with negatives set to zero for all 578 cells; the stored responsiveness index matches its recomputation from peaks for 279 of 578 cells only. The structs hold only cells already selected (every PV+ cell passes the binocular index), so the paper's counts of identified cells cannot be checked. Nothing documents the structs, the binocmonocinds lists or their fields; the mouse 7 plane shift is stored as (-70, 157) where stats.mat gives (157, -70).
  • The CaMKIIa-nls-tdTomato mice, the neuropil patches and the anti-PV histology are not in the data set; the structs hold one neuropil region per plane.
  • The descriptor gives frames of 255 by 456 pixels; the files hold 256 by 455, as the paper says.
  • No frame rate is given. At the paper's 30 Hz, the onset intervals give cycles of 3.93 s in mouse 5 and 4.37 to 4.43 s in mice 7 to 10, against the 4.5 s and 5 s of the stimulus plus a 3 s blank in the Methods; either the frame rate or the blank differs in five of six mice.
  • In four planes the last stimulus cycle runs past the last frame. Eight planes have fewer than the 6 to 7 repeats the paper states (all six planes of mouse 10 have 4 or 5), and mouse 10 has a plane at 125 um, above the Methods' 150 um.
  • No recording was found deposited twice.
  • On the example plane, stimulus trials differ from blank trials after onset, and the difference falls when onsets are moved, so the stimulus table is consistent with the movie's clock there.
