Reproducing Huszár et al. (2022) from DANDI:000552¶

Paper. Huszár R, Zhang Y, Blockus H, Buzsáki G (2022). Preconfigured dynamics in the hippocampus are guided by embryonic birthdate and rate of neurogenesis. Nature Neuroscience 25, 1201-1212. doi:10.1038/s41593-022-01138-x.

Claims. claims/huszar-2022.yaml, written from the author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC10807234) with its Reporting Summary and Supplementary Information (Supplementary Tables 1 to 5), and the paper's code (github.com/rhuszar/optotagCA1_final, commit 51320d4). Method details the claims file lacks are taken from the Methods and legends of that text, cited beside the code in huszar.py.

Dataset. DANDI:000552, version 0.261113.1035 (doi:10.48324/dandi.000552/0.261113.1035), the most recent published version. The paper cites no version (it was published before the dandiset). The earlier published version, 0.230630.2304, lacks what the claims need: its release notes say it had no light-responsive labels, no light pulses, units for only some sessions and LFP in only 13 files, while 0.261113.1035 holds all of these for the 104 sessions. It therefore also matches the claims file's description better. Only the 104 processed files are read, streamed from the archive; the 11 raw wide-band files are not.

This notebook streams every processed file and takes about 47 minutes, most of it reading two LFP channels per session for the theta analyses.

The files describe 17 mice and 104 sessions, one file per session with no duplicates by unit, ripple and pulse counts. Every session has units with cell types, light pulses, ripples and LFP at 1,250 Hz; 101 sessions have wake, non-REM and REM states, one has no REM, and two (e13_1f1_200305 and e13_1f1_200307) have wake only. In the five sessions checked, light-responsive cells fire 2.7 to 15 times more in the 10 ms after the pulse onsets than 1 s later, pyramidal cells fire 4.5 to 6.3 times faster in the ripples than in windows shifted by 1 s, and in the four maze sessions among them the median spatial information falls from between 0.56 and 1.07 bits/s to between 0.13 and 0.19 bits/s when spikes are shifted by 30 s against the position. Spikes, pulses, ripples and position are therefore consistent with one clock in those sessions; other sessions were not checked this way. The theta section adds the same contrast for the LFP.

The units table has the same 21 columns in every file and none is an isolation metric (no L-ratio). waveform_mean holds each unit's mean waveform on every channel (72 samples by 64 or 128 channels). The 25 files with 128 channels (9 mice) mark their electrodes Front or Back (probe_side): two faces of the same four shanks, with the same x positions on both faces. The two wake-only sessions hold a single sleep-state row each, Awake over the whole recording (414.7 and 331.5 min), with 1,942 and 1,247 ripples detected, so they were scored but carry no sleep.

def by_bd(df, col, claim, test="kw", n_seeds=5):
    groups = [df[df.birthdate == b][col].dropna().values for b in h.BIRTHDATES]
    med = [float(np.median(g)) for g in groups]
    print(f"{col}: n = {[len(g) for g in groups]}, medians = {np.round(med, 4).tolist()}, ranges = "
          f"{[(round(float(g.min()), 3), round(float(g.max()), 3)) for g in groups]}")
    ps = []
    for k in SEEDS_PLUS[:n_seeds]:
        f = h.boot_kw if test == "kw" else h.boot_anova
        st, p = f(groups, rng=np.random.default_rng(seed(claim) + k))
        ps.append(p)
    print(f"  bootstrap {'Kruskal-Wallis H' if test=='kw' else 'ANOVA F'} = {st:.3f}; p under the claim's seed and +1000..+4000: {ps}")
    return med, ps

Theta¶

Two LFP channels per session are read in full (h.theta_session), over the 96 homecage-mouse sessions and the 8 sessions of the two novel-only mice. The main one follows the Methods' rule, a channel with a positive sharp wave (above the centre of the pyramidal layer). The sharp wave of each channel is its mean LFP 0 to 50 ms after the ripple peaks minus its mean 100 to 50 ms before them, in microvolts, over the ripples of homecage non-REM sleep (all ripples in the sessions with 20 or fewer of them, printed below). Among the channels of the centre's shank with a positive sharp wave and larger rel_y than the centre (above it), the one with the largest ripple power is taken, on another shank when that shank has none, and the channel next to the centre with larger rel_y when no shank has one (assumption, pick_theta_channel; how the paper picked among qualifying channels is not said). Both halves of the Methods' phrase are required: in the first revision a positive channel below the centre also qualified, which 6 sessions used (verifier, round 2). The second channel is the pyramidal layer centre itself, run as a variant, and each theta claim is also shown on the sessions whose channel follows the rule.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The theta channel follows the Methods' rule (a channel with a positive sharp wave, measured against a pre-ripple baseline, above the centre of the pyramidal layer) in 72 of 104 sessions; in the other 32 no shank has a channel above its centre with a positive sharp wave, and the channel next to the centre with larger rel_y is used. Six sessions with 20 or fewer ripples in homecage non-REM sleep take the sharp wave over all their ripples. Every theta claim is also shown on the sessions whose channel follows the rule, and every one but fig7fg-convergence-cofiring on the layer centre channel. The monosynaptic detector follows the Methods' description with assumed lags, kernel, Bonferroni count and minimums, and finds 38,402 to 50,756 connections under its variants, 20% to 59% more than the paper's 31,932. Open fields are binned assuming one lab unit is 1 cm, with 2, 0.5 and 0.85 cm per unit as variants. Depth for the depth control is taken from the sharp-wave reversal of each shank face, and from the ripple-power centre where no reversal is found. Rate maps use a constant-velocity Kalman filter whose settings the paper does not give. The bootstrap post hoc test is replaced by Tukey's test on the six distance and birthdate groups.

What was found about the dataset. The files give the paper's counts of mice, sessions, units, cell types and light-responsive cells exactly, and the paper's per-session labelled fractions. Two sessions of e13_1f1 hold wake only in their sleep states. In e16_3m2_211211 the position series named for the linear_manu subsession lies inside the openField_thomas subsession. Open-field positions have no spatial scale, and the lab-unit positions of the other novel environments use different scales (0 to 1 in five series, 0.85 to 0.93 cm per unit in three, from their simultaneous linearized positions). The units table has no L-ratio. Mice e13_1f1 and e13_1m1, which the paper says had a single light-responsive cell per session, have 1 and 2 same-birthdate pairs in the files. 27 of 57 familiar-maze sessions hold fewer than ten trials of one type, in the trials table itself.
