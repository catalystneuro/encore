The dandiset has three neurons (subject ids, all recorded on 2024-08-06) where the paper reports eight. Each session is 1,200 images of 16 x 16 pixels, about 389 ms; the stimulus timing is not in the files and is taken from the Methods (50 ms before the stimulus, 250 ms during).

The traces are inverted, as Di-8-ANEPPS emission falls with depolarization (Methods). The check below compares the mean inverted dF before the stated onset, during the stimulus and after it, over all sessions: a response confined to the stated window supports the timing.

figs1-di8-anepps-control¶

With Di-8-ANEPPS instead of FluoVolt, in eight neurons, near-threshold stimulation elicited one or a few delayed APs and stronger fields induced sustained firing ending in sustained depolarization, as with FluoVolt.

Criterion: traces inverted; AP detection readings (a) a peak above 5% dF, (b) a rise of at least 5% within 5 ms, (c) a peak above 5 SD of the pre-stimulus trace. Reproduced when, under every reading, every neuron fires, first-AP latency falls with field in more than half, and the highest field ends in depolarization as in fig3c-sustained-firing-2s in more than half. Taken here (standard choices, as the criterion does not fix them): latency falls when the Spearman correlation of field and first-AP latency over a neuron's sessions with APs is negative; the highest field ends in depolarization when its sessions have no APs in the last 100 ms of the stimulus and mean dF above 5% there (the window of the claims file's block test for 250-ms stimuli, fig2-modulated-locks-to-beats). AP detection is the helper's: a 1-ms running mean, and a peak of reading (a) or (c) must stand above its surroundings by the detection level. The first run's detector is a variant.

# Variants. (1) The first run's detector (no smoothing, prominence 2.5% for readings a and c). (2) Reading (c)
# without APs in the first 5 ms of the stimulus, which may be an onset artefact. (3) The level of
# "ends in depolarization" taken as 5 SD of the neuron's pre-stimulus trace in place of the FluoVolt 5%
# (the claims file's 5% is a FluoVolt level; see the question to a reader in the attempt record).
def per_neuron(tab, level_col=None):
    rows = []
    for r in "abc":
        for neuron, sub in tab.groupby("neuron"):
            fired = sub[sub[f"{r}_aps"] > 0]
            rho = stats.spearmanr(fired.amplitude_v, fired[f"{r}_latency_ms"]).correlation if fired.amplitude_v.nunique() > 1 else np.nan
            top = sub[sub.amplitude_v == sub.amplitude_v.max()]
            level = 5 if level_col is None else 5 * top[level_col].mean()
            rows.append({"reading": r, "neuron": neuron, "fires": len(fired) > 0, "latency_falls": bool(rho < 0),
                         "level": level, "ends_depolarized": bool((top[f"{r}_last100"] == 0).all() and top.mean_dF_last100.mean() > level)})
    out = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    s = out.groupby("reading").agg(all_fire=("fires", "all"), latency_falls=("latency_falls", "sum"),
                                   ends_depolarized=("ends_depolarized", "sum"), neurons=("neuron", "count"))
    s["holds"] = s.all_fire & (s.latency_falls > s.neurons / 2) & (s.ends_depolarized > s.neurons / 2)
    return out, s
