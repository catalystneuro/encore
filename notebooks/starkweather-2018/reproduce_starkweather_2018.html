Reproducing Starkweather, Gershman & Uchida (2018) from CRCNS vta-1¶

Paper: Starkweather CK, Gershman SJ, Uchida N (2018). The Medial Prefrontal Cortex Shapes Dopamine Reward Prediction Errors under State Uncertainty. Neuron 98, 616-629. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2018.03.036

Dataset: CRCNS vta-1, doi:10.6080/K01J97X6, streamed from the public S3 bucket crcnsarchive (vta-1/live/data.zip).

Version: CRCNS does not version its data sets, so the version is unversioned, the only one there is (the paper cites this DOI, its Data Availability). The data are pinned by the MD5 of the file read, data.zip c5d2a9f8aaf699cbdd2bda1bace22e7e, as given in the data set's checksums.md5; the cell below checks the bytes read against it, so a later change of the file is detected. The data description read is version 0.6 (March 28, 2018).

Claims: claims/starkweather-2018.yaml, written from the version of record with its STAR Methods and Supplemental Information, and checked by a second reader. The belief state model claims use the paper's code, https://github.com/cstarkweather/Starkweather2018 at commit d667730, ported to Python in belief_td.py (MATLAB cannot be run here).

def frac_near(x, y, tol=0.5):
    """Fraction of spikes in x within tol ms of a spike in y."""
    k = np.searchsorted(y, x)
    near = np.minimum(np.abs(x - y[np.clip(k, 0, len(y) - 1)]), np.abs(x - y[np.clip(k - 1, 0, len(y) - 1)]))
    return (near <= tol).mean()

Data check. data.zip holds 173 members: the 172 neuron files and the directory entry data/, so no lick, free water or mPFC file is skipped by the loader. The 172 files are the four groups of the paper's Figure 4 (42, 42, 41 and 47 neurons) in 77 sessions, recovered from identical odor onset arrays; no session mixes drugs or tasks. They hold only spikes, odor and reward times and trial types, in ms. The reward delays of every trial type match the data description (odor A 1.2 to 2.8 s in steps of 0.2 s, odor B 1.2 s, odor C 2.8 s; none on omission and odor D trials). 21 file trials (9 session trials, in 8 sessions) are rewarded but have no trial type, at delays off the 0.2 s grid (1471 to 2421 ms); they are left out. By group they are 5 (task 1 saline), 1 (task 1 SalB), 1 (task 2 saline) and 14 (task 2 SalB) neuron trials. Kept as odor A trials at the nearest grid ISI, they change no Figure 4 ANOVA's conclusion (the largest changes are task 2 SalB pre-reward 0 to 200 ms, F 7.72 to 7.59, and task 2 SalB post-reward, F 0.70 to 0.68, p 0.69 to 0.71) and no slope count of Figure 5 (the reading file-untyped-kept below). The sessions hold 137 to 231 trials, below the paper's 150 to 300 in some, and their trials span 23.8 to 38.3 min, which fits files already cut to the 40 minutes the paper analyses; the stricter 40-minute reading drops 55 of 34430 neuron trials. Spikes and events share a clock: the auROC of rewarded odors against odor D is 0.496 to 0.513 in the windows before odor onset and 0.645 in the 250 ms after it, and rewarded against omission trials differ at the usual reward time (auROC 0.87 and 0.86) but not 2 s earlier (0.50 and 0.48). No two files hold the same spike train, but three pairs of task 1 SalB files share 65 to 99.7% of their spikes within 0.5 ms (chance, with one train shifted by 1 s, at most 1.5%): each pair is very likely one neuron deposited twice. The paper's counts include all six files, so the analyses below use every file, and the task 1 SalB claims also show a variant counting each pair once (39 neurons).

def session_signs(measure):
    """Per session, slope of the session's neuron-averaged response on ISI."""
    rows = []

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The files hold no recording onset, so the 40-minute
window is read two ways (every trial in the file, or odor onsets within 40 min of the session's first
spike); every outcome is the same under both. The paper does not say whether baseline is subtracted
trial by trial; that reading recovers the paper's slope counts exactly and is adopted, and the two
claims on significant slopes rest on it. The model is a Python port of the paper's MATLAB code, with
numpy's random numbers in place of MATLAB's, so single simulations differ from the authors'.


Two blocked ANOVAs (anova-time-task-drug-interaction, anova-slopes-task-drug) need the free water
response. Their other terms are run without it, as a deviation that decides no outcome: the interaction
terms do not show the paper's pattern on either reading, and on unnormalized slopes task is significant
and task 1 x drug is not, as in the paper, while task 2 x drug gives p = 0.066 (paper 0.032). The timing
impaired model (fig7ijk) passes its criterion, but its omission dips are reduced to 25 to 43% of the
intact ones, still below zero, so "abolished" holds only in the criterion's weaker sense; this is asked of
a reader.


What was found about the dataset. The data reproduce the paper's statistics closely: most F values agree to two digits, and
the p values to their order of magnitude, about half of them to two digits. The deposit holds only the light-identified dopamine neurons: the licks, free
water deliveries, mPFC recordings, unidentified VTA neurons and the no-KORD control neurons that
twelve of the paper's claims use are not in it. No file names its mouse or session; sessions can be
recovered from shared odor onsets, mice cannot, so the per-mouse inset of Figure 5 cannot be made.
Three pairs of task 1 SalB files share most of their spikes and are very likely one neuron each,
deposited twice; the paper's count of 42 includes them, and counting each once changes no outcome.
21 file trials have a reward but no trial type; keeping them changes no result. The printed counts of Figure 5F (SalB 9 significant
negative slopes) give Fisher p = 0.037, while the data give 8 and p = 0.0405, the paper's 0.041.
