Reproducing Goncalves et al. (2013) from CRCNS ssc-8¶

Paper. Goncalves JT, Anstey JE, Golshani P, Portera-Cailliau C (2013). Circuit level defects in the developing neocortex of Fragile X mice. Nature Neuroscience 16:903-909, doi:10.1038/nn.3415. The claims file (claims/crcns-ssc-8/10.1038-nn.3415.yaml) was written from the version of record in PubMed Central (PMC3695061): main text and Online Methods, without the figure legends or the supplement.

Data set. CRCNS ssc-8, doi:10.6080/K0X63K3X, read from the public bucket crcnsarchive under ssc-8/live/. CRCNS data sets are not versioned, and the paper cites no version (it predates the deposit of 2017); the only published state is the one in ssc-8/live/, data descriptor version 0.7 (13 October 2017), so that is the state used. It is recorded as unversioned and pinned by the MD5 sums of the data set's checksums.md5, which the next cell checks against the bytes read: firingrates_and_distances.mat ae1b48123540ecfd1bc9675c1eaa9a3d and Ca2+_imaging_TIFFs.tar.gz 75bf460dbe90c625637d1a1af3a25ca3 (the archive is streamed once, and its members are hashed one by one, as the archive's own MD5 needs the whole compressed stream).

What the data set holds. For each of 53 unanesthetized mice (WT and Fmr1 -/-, three ages), the estimated firing rate of each neuron in each frame, the cell-to-cell distances in microns, and the raw movie. The whole-cell recordings, the EEG, the isoflurane imaging and the 15.6 Hz imaging of the paper are not in it.

Readings shared by the claims (claims file, header). The trace is the deposited estimated firing rate (reading T-a); reading T-b, the low-pass filtered dF/F, needs the cells to be segmented anew from the raw movies, and the data set holds no contours, so it cannot be run here. The correlation is the zero-lag normalised cross-correlation without mean subtraction (C-b); Spearman (C-a) is a variant only, and Pearson is the paper's own variant (claim pearson-genotype). Each mouse's value is the mean over its pairs.

The descriptor says 6 to 13 animals per group, 40 to 149 neurons per animal, 3.9 Hz and about 7 to 8 min per recording. The counts per group (6 to 13) agree. The neuron counts run to 198 (two WT P9-11 recordings have 164 and 198), so the stated range is wrong. Most recordings have 2160 frames, which is 9.2 min at 3.9 Hz, longer than the stated 7 to 8 min; the range is printed above. The descriptor gives no unit for the estimated rates. They are 0 in about 90% of frames, with non-zero values from about 0.5 upwards, and the mean per recording is 0.19 to 0.74, close to the Hz the paper reports (0.30 Hz in WT at P14-16, Fig. 6); per frame they would be 3.9 times that (about 1.2 Hz). The values do not settle the unit (cell above): they are continuous (about 1.06 million distinct values in 1.18 million), and they fall near multiples of 1 or of 3.9 no more often than continuous values would, as deconvolved estimates need not be whole spikes. So U-a rests only on the means lying near the paper's Hz values, which are among the values tested, and that argument is partly circular. They are read as Hz below (reading U-a), and the claims on rates and on peaks of synchrony also show U-b. The distance matrices are symmetric with zero diagonals, and match the neuron counts.

The movies are in one folder per group (the folders are named "4Hz", where the Methods and the descriptor give 3.9 Hz). Each movie Movie_k.tif has exactly as many frames as the k-th rate matrix of its group. The population rate follows the movie's high-passed brightness, best when the brightness lags the rate by one or two frames, as a calcium signal lags the spikes it is deconvolved from. Every rate matrix correlates better with its own movie at lag 0 than with any of the 52 other movies, and than with its own movie when the rates are shifted by 50 to 400 frames (counts above). So the rate matrices and the movies share one frame clock, and the k-th entry of each group is Movie_k.tif; this is shown for every recording, though the margin is small for some (KO P30-40 entry 0). Frames are 128 x 256 pixels in 50 movies (one each of 357, 404 and 512 wide), where the Methods give 512 x 128 at 3.9 Hz. Most recordings are 2160 frames, three times 720, which at 4 Hz is three 3-min movies, consistent with the Methods' "several 3-min calcium movies were concatenated" and with the folder names; the brightness steps at multiples of 720 frames, larger than elsewhere, fit this. The TIFFs carry no time stamps, so the absolute frame rate cannot be checked; 3.9 Hz (Methods) is used, with 4 Hz as a variant where a rate per second is computed. No movie or cell trace is deposited twice.

def fig1d(meas):
    d = corr[(corr.measure == meas) & (corr.genotype == "WT") & corr.age.isin(["P9P11", "P14P16"])]
    long = d.melt(id_vars=["age", "index"], value_vars=BIN_NAMES, var_name="bin", value_name="r").dropna()
    long["mouse"] = long.age + "_" + long["index"].astype(str)
    # A-a: ordinary two-way ANOVA
    fit = ols("r ~ C(age) * C(bin)", data=long).fit()
    aov = sm.stats.anova_lm(fit, typ=2)
    mse, dfr = fit.mse_resid, fit.df_resid
    out = []
    for b in BIN_NAMES:
        x = long[(long.bin == b) & (long.age == "P9P11")].r.to_numpy()
        y = long[(long.bin == b) & (long.age == "P14P16")].r.to_numpy()
        diff = x.mean() - y.mean()
        t_a = diff / np.sqrt(mse * (1 / len(x) + 1 / len(y)))
        p_a = min(1.0, 3 * 2 * stats.t.sf(abs(t_a), dfr))
        p_b = min(1.0, 3 * stats.ttest_ind(x, y).pvalue)
        out.append({"bin": b, "n_P9-11": len(x), "n_P14-16": len(y), "mean_P9-11": x.mean(), "mean_P14-16": y.mean(),
                    "difference": diff, "p_bonf_A-a": p_a, "p_bonf_A-b": p_b})
    out = pd.DataFrame(out)
    # A-b: split-plot ANOVA on mice with all three bins
    full = long.groupby("mouse").filter(lambda g: len(g) == 3)
    return out, aov, full


def split_plot(full):
    # between factor age, within factor bin, subject mouse
    gm = full.r.mean()
    a_levels, b_levels = full.age.unique(), BIN_NAMES
    ss_age = sum(len(g) * (g.r.mean() - gm) ** 2 for _, g in full.groupby("age"))
    ss_subj = sum(3 * (g.r.mean() - gm) ** 2 for _, g in full.groupby("mouse"))
    ss_bin = sum(len(g) * (g.r.mean() - gm) ** 2 for _, g in full.groupby("bin"))
    ss_cells = sum(len(g) * (g.r.mean() - gm) ** 2 for _, g in full.groupby(["age", "bin"]))
    ss_ab = ss_cells - ss_age - ss_bin
    ss_tot = ((full.r - gm) ** 2).sum()
    n_subj = full.mouse.nunique()
    df_age, df_sw = len(a_levels) - 1, n_subj - len(a_levels)
    ss_sw = ss_subj - ss_age
    ss_err = ss_tot - ss_subj - ss_bin - ss_ab
    df_bin, df_ab, df_err = 2, 2 * df_age, 2 * df_sw
    f_age = (ss_age / df_age) / (ss_sw / df_sw)
    f_bin = (ss_bin / df_bin) / (ss_err / df_err)
    f_ab = (ss_ab / df_ab) / (ss_err / df_err)
    return pd.DataFrame({"F": [f_age, f_bin, f_ab], "df1": [df_age, df_bin, df_ab], "df2": [df_sw, df_err, df_err],
                         "p": [stats.f.sf(f_age, df_age, df_sw), stats.f.sf(f_bin, df_bin, df_err), stats.f.sf(f_ab, df_ab, df_err)]},
                        index=["age", "bin", "age x bin"])


def judge_1d(out, col):
    within_tol = all(within(d, e, t) for d, e, t in zip(out.difference, EXP_1D, TOL_1D))
    p_ok = out[col].iloc[0] < 0.05 and out[col].iloc[1] < 0.05 and out[col].iloc[2] >= 0.05
    falls = out.difference.iloc[0] > out.difference.iloc[1] > out.difference.iloc[2]
    return within_tol, p_ok, falls

def pooled(meas, exp, tol):
    rows = []
    for pairs, col in [("P-a (<100 um)", "<100"), ("P-b (all pairs)", "all")]:
        v = values(meas, col)
        test = ssc8.two_by_three(v)["genotype"]
        for avg in ["M-a", "M-b"]:
            r = {"pairs": pairs, "average": avg}
            for g in ssc8.GENOTYPES:
                if avg == "M-a":
                    x = np.concatenate([v[(g, a)] for a in ssc8.AGES])
                    r[f"{g}_mean"], r[f"{g}_sem"] = x.mean(), ssc8.sem(x)
                else:
                    r[f"{g}_mean"] = np.mean([v[(g, a)].mean() for a in ssc8.AGES])
                    r[f"{g}_sem"] = np.sqrt(sum(ssc8.sem(v[(g, a)]) ** 2 for a in ssc8.AGES)) / 3
            r["F"], r["df2"], r["p"] = test["F"], test["df2"], test["p"]
            r["KO higher"] = r["KO_mean"] > r["WT_mean"]
            r["WT within"] = within(r["WT_mean"], exp["WT"], tol["WT"])
            r["KO within"] = within(r["KO_mean"], exp["KO"], tol["KO"])
            rows.append(r)
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

# Seed sensitivity of the closest reading, R-b/U-b with Q-a. The threshold is the 95th percentile

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The data set holds 53 mice, not the paper's 56, in groups of different sizes (two of six match), so every group statistic is on a different sample. The trace is the deposited estimated rate; the paper's correlation may have used the filtered dF/F (reading T-b), which cannot be rebuilt as no cell contours are deposited. The correlation measure of the paper (its ref. 15) is not given; the zero-lag normalised cross-correlation is the claims file's reading, and it matches the paper's P14-16 and P30-40 WT values. The way the 2 x 3 unequal-variance ANOVA was fitted is not given; the Welch-James form used here reduces to the Welch test for one-df contrasts. For peaks of synchrony, what counts as an active cell and how the traces were shuffled are not given; all readings were run, with "at least one spike" read under both rate units (six in all), and the outcomes do not depend on them. For fig2c the closest reading (R-b/U-b, Q-a) gives p = 0.051 to 0.056 with 1000 shuffles and p < 0.05 in 3 of 40 seeded draws of 100 shuffles, so that outcome is near the boundary.
