  • climate-trends-cannot-explain-wing-increase

    The directions of the climate effects and of the climate trends do not fit a role for climate in the long-term increase in wing length. Breeding temperature, autumn migration temperature and breeding precipitation have risen through time while their association with relative wing length is negative.

    While colder breeding and autumn migration temperatures are associated with longer wings, the direction of the temperature-wing length relationships and the long-term trends in temperature are inconsistent with a direct role for increasing temperatures in driving multi-decadal increases in wing length (electronic supplementary material, table S8). This is similarly true for reductions in breeding season precipitation

    Discussion, Long-term increases in wing length are not driven by climate or by changes in body size, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    From the results of the claims this depends on. Reproduced when the breeding temperature and breeding precipitation trends (Table S8) are positive with intervals excluding 0 and their significant effects on the allometric intercept (HY and autumn AHY for temperature, all three models for precipitation) are negative, and the same holds for autumn migration temperature against its spring AHY intercept effect; the product of trend and effect is then negative, predicting shorter wings over time.
