  • figs1e-if-curves

    Superficial and deep CA1 PCs do not differ in firing frequency as a function of injected current (two-way ANOVA, p > 0.4; 8 sPCs, 9 dPCs).

    There were no differences between dPCs and sPCs in terms of their firing frequency as a function of intracellularly injected current (Figure S1E)

    Results, PVBCs Preferentially Innervate Subpopulations of CA1 PCs, paragraph 2; Figure S1E and legend

    How it will be judged

    From the Supplemental Experimental Procedures 2B, steps of 1 s from 0 to +550 pA in 50 pA increments, cells held at -70 mV; the data set's steps from 0 to +550 pA are used. How firing frequency is computed is not stated. Readings: (a) spikes during the 1 s step divided by 1 s; (b) (spikes - 1) over the time from first to last spike, the form the Figure S3E legend implies for the model (31.06 Hz for 30 spikes). Spike detection is a standard choice (upward crossing of 0 mV) that cannot change the outcome. A claim of absence, judged on the paper's test as written, a two-way ANOVA of firing frequency on group (sPC, dPC) and current step: reproduced when the group effect is not significant (p >= 0.05), not reproduced when it is; the effect size is reported either way, and a repeated-measures ANOVA is shown as a variant. The expected values, read from the figure, are checked as well under either reading.

    There were no differences between dPCs and sPCs in terms of their firing frequency as a function of intracellularly injected current (Figure S1E) and in input resistance (Figure S1F)

    Results, PVBCs Preferentially Innervate Subpopulations of CA1 PCs, paragraph 2; Figure S1F; Table S1

    How it will be judged

    From the Supplemental Experimental Procedures 2B, voltage responses to 1 s steps from -100 to +100 pA in 50 pA increments, cells held at -70 mV, measured at steady state 0.8 to 1.0 s after step onset. How resistance is computed from these responses is not stated. Readings: (a) the slope of a linear fit of steady-state voltage against current over the five steps; (b) the mean of the change from the pre-step baseline divided by the current, over the four non-zero steps. Per cell, then mean per group. Reproduced when, under one reading, both group means are within tolerance and the groups do not differ by the paper's rule (two-tailed unpaired t test, Mann-Whitney if Shapiro-Wilk fails, p >= 0.05). Partially reproduced when the groups do not differ but a mean falls outside tolerance.

  • figs1g-resting-potential

    How it will be judged

    The paper does not say how the resting potential was measured. It is read as the membrane potential with no injected current: the pre-step baseline of sweeps whose current channel shows zero holding current. Table S1's footnote says the biological PCs were given an unrecorded tonic current during the sweeps to rest at -84.6 mV (corrected), and the Methods say cells were held at -70 mV; if the files show a holding current, or no current channel and a baseline at the holding level, the sweeps do not hold the resting potential and the claim cannot be tested on them. Otherwise reproduced when sPCs are more depolarized than dPCs by the paper's rule (two-tailed unpaired t test, Mann-Whitney if Shapiro-Wilk fails, p < 0.05) and both means are within tolerance; partially reproduced when the difference holds but a mean falls outside.

  • figs1h-sag

    Superficial PCs show larger sag potentials than deep PCs in response to large hyperpolarizing current pulses (about 8.7 against 5.6 mV).

    as reported before (Jarsky et al., 2008), larger sag potentials in response to large hyperpolarizing current pulses (Figure S1H)

    Results, PVBCs Preferentially Innervate Subpopulations of CA1 PCs, paragraph 2; Figure S1H

    How it will be judged

    Sag amplitude is the difference between the peak hyperpolarization during the step and the steady state; the paper does not define the windows or the step. Steady state is taken as the mean at 0.8 to 1.0 s, the window the Supplemental Experimental Procedures 2B use for input resistance, and the peak as the most negative voltage in the step (a standard choice). The step is open; readings (a) -400 pA, the largest step of the protocol (Figure S3C plots sag against current down to -400 pA, and its biological values there are of the size of Figure S1H), (b) -300 pA, (c) -200 pA. Reproduced when, under one reading, sPC sag exceeds dPC sag by the paper's rule (two-tailed unpaired t test, Mann-Whitney if Shapiro-Wilk fails, p < 0.05) and both means are within tolerance; partially reproduced when the difference holds under a reading but no reading puts both means within tolerance.

  • tables1-rheobase

    The experimental superficial and deep PCs have the same rheobase, +150 pA, at the 50 pA resolution of the step protocol.

    How it will be judged

    Rheobase is the smallest step that evokes firing, excluding the tonic current (Table S1 footnote); steps are on a 50 pA grid, so a 10% tolerance admits only the reported step and the tolerance is set to 0. How a group value is formed is not stated. Readings: (a) the median of per-cell rheobases; (b) the mean of per-cell rheobases rounded to the nearest 50 pA step. The first step at which the group-mean firing of Figure S1E is above zero is not a reading, since that figure shows both group means at about 1 Hz at +100 pA. Reproduced when, under one reading, both groups give +150 pA; partially reproduced when they give the same step but not +150 pA.

    How it will be judged

Reproduced when, under one reading, there is no firing from 0 to +300 pA and the four values are within tolerance; partially reproduced when the onset is between +300 and +400 pA but some value is outside tolerance.
