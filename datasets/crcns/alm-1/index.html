  • fig1d-photoinhibition-ipsilateral-bias

    Unilateral photoinhibition of ALM during the delay epoch biases licking to the side of the inhibited hemisphere; inhibiting left ALM raises performance on lick left trials and lowers it on lick right trials, and inhibiting right ALM does the reverse.

    Unilateral photoinhibition (Methods) of anterior lateral motor cortex (ALM, centred at 2.5 mm anterior, 1.5 mm lateral), limited to the delay epoch, produced an ipsilateral bias ... Inhibiting left ALM caused a tendency to lick the left port, resulting in increased performance in 'lick left' trials and decreased performance in 'lick right' trials

    Results, ALM is required for movement planning, paragraph 2; Figure 1d

    How it will be judged

    As in Methods, Behavioural data analyses. Performance is the fraction correct, lick early trials excluded, computed separately for lick right and lick left trials. The change is photoinhibition minus control performance. Its P value is the fraction of 10^4 bootstrap rounds, resampling with replacement animals, sessions within animal and trials within session, that give a change of the opposite sign (one-tailed). Reproduced when, for delay epoch inhibition of each hemisphere, the contralateral trial type loses performance and the ipsilateral gains it, each at P < 0.05; partially reproduced when this holds for one hemisphere or for the contralateral loss only.

  • ed2-error-trials-predict-choice

    On error trials, when mice lick opposite to the instructed direction, ALM activity predicts the licking direction; most selective neurons switch their trial type preference.

    On error trials, when mice licked in the opposite direction to the instruction provided by object location (Fig. 1a), a majority of ALM neurons switched their trial type preference to predict the licking direction, as indicated by the negative correlations

    Results, ALM contains neurons with bilateral selectivity, paragraph 1; Extended Data Fig. 2b, c

    How it will be judged

    As in ED Fig. 2c. For each of the 912 selective neurons and each epoch (sample, delay, response), selectivity is the firing rate on lick right minus lick left trials, computed on correct trials and on error trials, with error trials labelled by the instructed trial type (pole position), so that negative correlation means activity follows the lick. Pearson's r between correct and error selectivity across neurons. Neurons without error trials of both types are left out; a minimum number of error trials is not given and is open (readings, at least 1, or at least 5, of each type). The paper gives r but no test of it; a two-sided test of Pearson's r at P < 0.05 is a standard choice. The "majority" is the fraction of neurons whose error selectivity has the sign opposite to their correct selectivity. Reproduced when r is negative with P < 0.05 and that fraction exceeds one half in all three epochs under one reading; partially reproduced for one or two epochs.
