  • fig7a-olm-sag

    O-LM interneurons recorded in CXCR4-EGFP slices show a sag in their voltage response to hyperpolarizing current pulses (pulses of -100 and +50 pA in Figure 7A), and the ChR2-expressing O-LM cell of Figure 7D has a similar firing pattern.

    Firingpatternsoftheaboveshowncells.Noticethepresenceofasaginthehyperpolarizingresponse. ... Notice the similarity with the firing patterns shown in A.

    Figure 7A and 7D legends

    How it will be judged

    The paper shows the sag in example traces and does not define a measure, so the measure is open. Taken from the legend, the -100 pA step is the hyperpolarizing response to examine. Two standard readings, neither of them the paper's, are run. (a) A visible sag, read as a depolarizing relaxation from the peak hyperpolarization during the -100 pA step to the mean voltage over its last 100 ms larger than twice the SD of the pre-step baseline voltage. (b) A sag ratio, (V_end - V_min) / (V_baseline - V_min), of at least 0.1, a common convention. The paper does not name its cells, so which cell of the data set is shown in Figure 7A or 7D is open; the similarity of the 7D firing pattern to those in 7A is read as the sag, the one feature the legends point to, and every cell is judged alike. Reproduced when every cell shows a sag, partially reproduced when most do, not reproduced otherwise. The outcome is the one both readings give; if they differ, both results are shown and the author adopts the reading the paper best supports, as an assumption. The outcome rests on the assumed measure either way.
