Most of the paper's main findings hold up in this data: Aplysia pedal ganglion activity forms a low-dimensional periodic orbit, sequential bouts fall on the same manifold, and single neurons vary widely in participation. Convergence within one oscillation period did not hold up, as only 12 of 30 responses reached high recurrence that fast. Motor output was decoded in 8 of the 9 responses with nerve recordings, not all nine. The stable spiral held for the grand mean but not in every preparation, and the caudo-lateral location of strongly participating neurons could not be tested, as the data do not give the array's orientation. Several outcomes depend on a kernel width and dimension count the Methods leave unclear.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Paper inconsistentThe paper gives 120 to 180 neurons per recorded population and 1131 neurons in all over 10 preparations; ten preparations of at least 120 would hold at least 1200. The data set holds 75 to 160 neurons per preparation, 1131 in all, as its description says.
  • Description missingCoordinates.mat gives each neuron's x and y on a 0 to 50 scale without a unit, and neither the files, plot_maps.m nor the data description say which directions of the array are rostral, caudal, medial or lateral, so the paper's caudo-lateral quadrant cannot be located.
  • OtherThe P10 spike times are on a grid of 0.614 ms (1629.7 Hz), which agrees with the data description's 1630 Hz sampling; the paper says 1600 Hz.
  • Identifier missingThe P10 files of preparation 2 are named Jun0414da0Np10.mat while its spike and coordinate files are Jun0414B; they can be matched only by the first seven characters, as the paper's code does.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. The Methods give the Gaussian width as the median ISI divided by 12 (the extracted text; possibly the square root of 12), but the code uses the median ISI itself, computed as a running median over the recordings of the same bout number read so far. With median ISI / 12 or / sqrt(12) the recordings need 21 to 76 PCs for 80% of the variance, not the paper's 4 to 8. Which width was used for the published figures, and was the median meant to be per recording?
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. The Methods say the projection kept the dimensions needed for at least 80% of the variance; the recurrence and participation code keeps those whose cumulative variance is at most 80% (one fewer), while the manifold code keeps the smallest number reaching 80%. Which rule produced Figures 2 to 5? The linear-model period of Figure 4C is 10.45 s under the first and 12.2 to 13.6 s under the second.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. For removing divergent periods within two oscillation cycles of the end, is the cycle the mean or the median recurrence time of the dominant peak, and is the rule applied to all divergent periods (Methods) or only to those that never return (code)? The counts change from 16 to 21 kept periods with these choices.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. The permutation test of the weighted Spearman correlation (Tracking changes in periodicity) is not described beyond 10000 permutations; the code permutes the pooled time and period values with 100000 permutations and tests one side. Which test gave Figure 5B?
  • Published code disagreesFigure 6D reports N = 173 forecast windows. The Methods' windowing (40 s fit, 10 s forecast, 1 s steps from 33 s) gives 40 windows per response here; the code's windowing (training on 50% of the series, 0.2 s steps) gives 174 per response, close to 173, so the figure appears to use the code's windowing, not the Methods'.
  • Published code disagreesIn What_is_Participation.m the rate changes are ordered (1,2), (1,3), (2,3) and the participation changes (1,2), (2,3), (1,3), so two of three pairs are mismatched in the correlation behind Figure 7, figure supplement 1D. With matched pairs the mean R-squared is 0.239, with the code's pairing 0.205.
  • Published code disagreesLocalLinearDynamics.m finds the contiguous neighbourhood of a point from abs(currixs - ixPt), the time indices minus the point's position within the neighbourhood, so gaps among neighbours earlier than that position are not detected and the fitted run can extend past them. The Methods describe a run contiguous in time.

Reproduction Spiral attractor in the Aplysia pedal ganglion (Bruno, Frost and Humphries 2017)

Tests the findings of Bruno, Frost and Humphries (2017) on CRCNS data set apl-1, which holds the spike times of 30 voltage-sensitive dye recordings of the pedal ganglion (three evoked bouts of fictive locomotion in each of 10 preparations), the P10 nerve spikes of nine of them and the neurons' positions on the photodiode array. It ports the paper's recurrence analysis, local linear models, manifold comparisons, P10 decoding, participation measures and ensemble typing from the paper's MATLAB code, under every reading of the kernel width and the number of dimensions. Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • Chronux mtspectrumpt is ported to Python (apl.mtspectrumpt) with its defaults, tapers [3 5], the time grid from the first to the last spike, the mean rate subtracted; it was not checked against MATLAB output.fig1d-slow-oscillation
  • The kernel width is read four ways (median ISI / sqrt(12), / 12, the median ISI of the recording, and the code's running median over the recordings of the same bout number), each with both count rules, as the criterion lists.fig2b-dimensions
  • Recurrence follows Analyse_by_Recurrence_Points.m (points from 35 s to 10 s before the end, the first contiguous run within theta dropped, bins of 1 s on 0 to 50 s, peaks of at least 100 points), as the claims file's opening comment gives it.fig2ef-periodic-recurrence
  • A window counts as coalesced when at least 90% of its points recur, as the Methods say (Testing convergence to the same manifold); the code's PrctWnone below 10 is the strict form.   • The similarity matrix is the correlation of the post-onset spike-density functions, as the criterion says; the code takes it from its clustering toolbox's convolved trains, which use the same kernel width.fig3f-pairwise-similarity
