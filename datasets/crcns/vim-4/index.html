  • fig1-csi-s2

    In subject S2 the four presence states (neither, humans only, vehicles only, both) are significantly separated in the attention-task state space, with a cluster separation index of 0.75 for attend-humans, 0.69 for attend-vehicles and 0.67 for passive viewing.

    The cluster separation index (CSI) is 0.75 for attend-humans, 0.69 for attend-vehicles, and 0.67 for passive ( p < 1e-5, permutation test), all indicating significant separation of the clusters

    Figure 1 legend

    How it will be judged

    State space as in the code (see the opening comment), fitted on S2's three conditions stacked. For each condition, the CSI as the Methods define it (Quantification of State Space Results, paragraph 1), a multivariate normal (sample mean and covariance) fitted to the TRs of each of the four presence states in the 3-dimensional state space, the JSD of every pair estimated as in the code's JSD function, and the CSI the mean of the six JSDs. Significance by permutation as in the Methods (paragraph 2), "permuting the state labels, and performing the full analysis using the permuted labels". The number of permutations is open (a p below 1e-5 needs at least 1e5); use at least 1000 and report p as (k+1)/(n+1). Which labels are permuted is open; the readings are (a) the human and vehicle presence labels shuffled together across TRs, the state space refitted each time, and (b) the same with the labels circularly shifted within each condition, which keeps their autocorrelation. Reproduced when all three CSIs are within tolerance and each exceeds every null value under a reading. The Monte Carlo JSD varies with the seed; report the mean over 10 seeds.

    The states are distinctly separated with a CSI of 0.66 ± 0.09 (mean ± std across subjects) for attend-humans, 0.74 ± 0.03 for attend-vehicles, and 0.53 ± 0.24 for passive viewing ( p < 1e-5; permutation test)

    How it will be judged

The CSI and permutation test as in fig1-csi-s2, per subject, on the five subjects the data set holds (S4 is not released); the mean and standard deviation are taken over them. The paper does not say how the group p is formed; the readings are (a) every subject's CSI significant and (b) the mean CSI exceeding the mean of the subjects' null CSIs at every permutation. Reproduced when the three means are within tolerance and the CSIs are significant under a reading. The means over the same five subjects from the per-subject figure values (0.64, 0.73, 0.51) are reported as a second reference, since the paper's own per-subject values do not average to the printed means.

  • att-passive-lower

    Attention increases state separation; the CSI under passive viewing is significantly lower than under the attentive conditions in five of the six subjects.

    Furthermore, we found that the CSI in the passive condition was significantly lower than the CSI in the attentive conditions for five out of the six subjects ( p < 1e-5; permutation test)

    Results, The Voxel-Based State Space Method Recovers a State Space for Variables in the Attention Task, paragraph 2; Figure 1 legend; Discussion, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    CSIs as in fig1-csi-s2. The paper does not give the form of this test. The readings are (a) passive against each attentive condition, both differences significant; (b) passive against the mean of the two attentive CSIs. The null in either is formed by permuting the attention-condition labels between passive and attentive TRs (the presence labels kept), either (i) refitting the state space each time, as the Methods' "full analysis" says, or (ii) keeping the fitted positions and reassigning TRs to conditions; p is one-sided (passive lower). A subject counts at the paper's threshold, p < 1e-5, which needs at least 1e5 permutations; where fewer can be run (at least 1000), a subject counts when its difference exceeds every null value, the bound reached is reported, and the count at p < 0.05 is shown as a variant. The data set holds five of the six subjects. Reproduced when the passive CSI is significantly lower in at least 4 of the 5 under a reading, which is what five of six allows; not reproduced otherwise. From the figure values, S1 (passive 0.64 against attend-humans 0.51) and S6 (0.61 against 0.60) fail reading (a) on the raw values, so the per-reading counts are reported.

  • att-classification

    Held-out TRs of the attention task are classified into the 12 labels (four presence states by three attention conditions) from their position in the learned state space at 48.4 +/- 7.6% across subjects, well above the chance of 8.3%.

    Average performance across subjects is 48.4 ± 7.6% ( p < 1e-5, permutation test, chance = 8.3%)

    Results, The Voxel-Based State Space Method Recovers a State Space for Variables in the Attention Task, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    Tolerance is the paper's stated spread across subjects. As in the Methods (Quantification of State Space Results, paragraph 2), per subject, 10-fold cross-validation over the stacked TRs of the three conditions; in each fold the state space (code's method) is fitted on the training 90%, the held-out TRs are projected onto its directions, and each is assigned to the nearest of the 12 training clusters; accuracy is averaged over folds and then over the five subjects in the data set. Open, with readings run each. Folds as (a) contiguous tenths of the stacked series, or (b) random TRs. Nearest cluster by (a) Euclidean distance to the cluster mean, or (b) the highest density under the multivariate normal fitted to the cluster, as for the CSI. Held-out positions are z-scored with the training means and standard deviations (the code z-scores the positions it fits; a standard choice for held-out data). The null permutes the 12 labels of the training TRs and repeats the full analysis, at least 1000 times. Reproduced when the mean is within tolerance and above every null mean under a reading; partially reproduced when it is significant but outside tolerance under all readings.

  • att-weights-cortex

    The human-presence dimension of the attention-task state space has its weights in face and body areas (FFA, OFA, EBA), MT+, LO, the frontal operculum and the inferior frontal sulcus; the vehicle-presence dimension in RSC, PPA, OPA, IPS, FEF and around the inferior frontal sulcus.

    The “human presence” dimension projects heavily onto FFA ( Kanwisher et al., 1997 ), OFA ( Gauthier et al., 2000 ), and EBA ( Downing et al., 2001 ) ... In contrast, the “vehicle presence” dimension projects strongly onto RSC ( Maguire, 2001 ; Epstein, 2008 ), PPA ( Epstein et al., 1999 ), and OPA ( Dilks et al., 2013 )

    Results, The Voxel-Based State Space Method Recovers a State Space for Variables in the Attention Task, paragraph 4; Figure 2; Supplementary Figure 3

    How it will be judged

    The ROIs were delineated per subject from functional localizer and retinotopic mapping data (Methods, Preprocessing and Visualization, paragraph 2), which the paper does not deposit and the data set's description does not list; the author decides whether an outside ROI source (for example an fsaverage atlas through the data set's mappers) can be used. Which weights are mapped is open; the readings are (a) the OLS weights B, (b) the denoised weights B_L, and (c) the state directions Q_S, each sign-oriented so that the dimension's own state has a positive mean position. Figure 2 and Supplementary Figure 3 threshold voxels by the prediction performance of a semantic encoding model at uncorrected p < 0.05. The data set holds the semantic labels and the preprocessed BOLD, so the threshold has two readings, (i) no threshold and (ii) voxels whose cross-validated prediction by the WordNet model of the deconvolution (Methods, Deconvolution, FIR delays 1 to 4, ridge with 10-fold CV) is significant at uncorrected p < 0.05; the test of the prediction is open (correlation of predicted and measured responses, against zero or by permutation). Per subject and ROI, the mean weight. Reproduced when, in at least 4 of the 5 subjects, the mean human-presence weight is positive and above the mean vehicle-presence weight in FFA, OFA and EBA, and the reverse holds in RSC, PPA and OPA, under a reading. The other named regions are reported, including those the Figure 2 legend adds (SEF for the human-presence dimension, FO for the vehicle-presence dimension).

  • game-csi

    In the video game task the behavioural states are distinctly and significantly separated in the 9-dimensional state space, with CSI 0.72 in subject S1 and 0.62 in S7.

    The states are distinctly separated with a CSI of 0.72 in subject S1 and 0.62 in subject S7 ( p < 1e-5, permutation test)

    Results, The Voxel-Based State Space Method Recovers a Behavioral State Space for the Open-Ended Video Game Task, paragraph 2; Figure 3A

    How it will be judged

    State space by the code's method on each subject's BOLD data (no deconvolution, as the Methods say) with the nine state columns as task variables. CSI as in fig1-csi-s2, in the 9-dimensional space. The clusters are open, and the states are not exclusive. Readings are (a) one cluster per state, a TR entering the cluster of every state it has; (b) one cluster per state from TRs with exactly one state; (c) the three groups of Figure 3A (safe explore, engage close, all other TRs). A state with no TRs (flash in S7) or too few to fit a covariance is left out and the count reported. Permutations as in fig1-csi-s2, shuffling the rows of the state matrix. Reproduced when both CSIs are within tolerance and above every null value under a reading.

  • game-mutual-negative-projection

    The mutually exclusive states engage close and safe explore each project negatively onto the other's dimension of the video game state space.

    For example, “engaging close” and “safe explore” each project negatively to the dimension associated with the other state ( Figure 3A ) ( p < 1e-5, permutation test)

    Results, The Voxel-Based State Space Method Recovers a Behavioral State Space for the Open-Ended Video Game Task, paragraph 3; Figure 3A legend

    How it will be judged

    State space as in game-csi. The code has no video game analysis, and two details that change the dimensions are open. The sign of each dimension, with readings (a) its own state's TRs have a positive mean position (by analogy with the code's plotting, which flips an axis by cluster means), (b) the diagonal of R made positive, so that the dimension points along the state's denoised weight vector. The order of the nine variables entering the QR, since each column of Q is orthogonalized against the earlier ones, with readings (i) the order of Table 1 (dead, round start, safe explore, unsafe explore, kill, engage close, engage far, flash, run away) and (ii) the order of the columns of the data set's state matrix. The measure is the mean position of engage-close TRs on the safe-explore dimension and of safe-explore TRs on the engage-close dimension. The null permutes the rows of the state matrix and refits, at least 1000 times, one-sided for a negative mean. Reproduced when both means are negative and significant at p < 0.05 in both subjects; partially reproduced in one subject.

Reproduced when both values are within tolerance and above every null value under a reading; partially reproduced when significant but outside tolerance.

  • game-weights-two-groups

    The video game state dimensions fall into two groups, dead, round start, safe explore, unsafe explore and flash in TPJ, precuneus and prefrontal cortex (default-mode areas), and kill, engage close, engage far and run away in motor, premotor and supplementary motor areas, IPS, FEF and SEF; the weights are largely consistent across the two subjects.

    They appear to fall into two general categories. The dimensions for “safe explore,” “round start,” “flash,” “dead,” and “unsafe explore” project on to the TPJ, precuneus, and prefrontal cortex. The dimensions for “run away,” “engage close,” “kill,” and “engage far” project on to the motor, pre-motor, and supplementary motors areas, and also in IPS, FEF, and SEF ... The weights for each dimension are largely consistent across the two subjects

    Results, The Voxel-Based State Space Method Recovers a Behavioral State Space for the Open-Ended Video Game Task, paragraph 5; Figure 4; Supplementary Figures 4 and 5

    How it will be judged

    The paper groups the dimensions by the regions their weights project onto, read from the maps (Figure 4, Supplementary Figures 4 and 5); it gives no quantitative grouping method. The regions need definitions the data set does not list (see att-weights-cortex); the author decides whether an outside source can be used (functional ROIs for IPS, FEF and SEF; an fsaverage atlas, through the data set's mappers, for TPJ, precuneus, prefrontal, motor, premotor and supplementary motor cortex). Weights with the readings of att-weights-cortex, the Q_S reading also under the order readings of game-mutual-negative-projection. Figure 4 thresholds voxels by "an encoding model" at Bonferroni-corrected p < 0.05, the model not named; readings are (i) no threshold and (ii) voxels whose cross-validated prediction by the nine-state OLS model is significant at that level. Per subject, dimension and region, the mean weight. Reproduced when, in both subjects, every dimension of the first group (dead, round start, safe explore, unsafe explore, flash; no flash in S7) has a positive mean weight in TPJ, precuneus and prefrontal cortex and a higher one there than in the second group's regions, and every dimension of the second group (kill, engage close, engage far, run away) the reverse in motor, premotor and supplementary motor cortex, IPS, FEF and SEF, allowing one dimension per subject to fail, under a reading; not reproduced otherwise. As a variant not taken from the paper and not deciding the outcome, the Pearson correlation across voxels between the weight maps of every pair of dimensions, with average-linkage clustering on one minus correlation cut into two groups, is reported. The paper gives no measure of "largely consistent across the two subjects"; the correlation of each dimension's map between subjects on fsaverage is reported, and the consistency part is said to hold when its median over shared dimensions is positive, a reading of the reader's, stated as such.
