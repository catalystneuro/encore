  • figs2-neurons-tested

    The tetrodotoxin experiment was replicated in several neurons, with vehicle controls; the representative traces of Figure S2 come from at least 4 independent replications in different cells.

    Representative traces of the optical membrane potentials were selected from at least 4 and usually 8-15 independent replications in different cells

    Figure S2 legend; Methods, Quantification and statistical analysis

    How it will be judged

    Neurons are counted from the dataset's subjects, sessions or cell identifiers, each recording counted once (author, step 1), and split into TTX and vehicle by the dataset's session metadata. The paper does not say whether the 4 replications count TTX neurons alone; readings (a) at least 4 TTX neurons, (b) at least 4 neurons over TTX and vehicle together. As a reported number read two ways, reproduced when either reading holds (protocol, step 3). The tolerance is 0 on a lower bound. The number of vehicle neurons is reported; the legend gives no count and the figure shows one, so at least one vehicle neuron is expected and its absence is reported as a finding.

A further check, from the paper's "seconds or longer", is that F0 of the next session of the same neuron (20 to 60 s later) is within 10% (a standard choice) of the F0 of the session before; it is reported, not judged. Windows are standard choices; the claim is tested on the Figure S2 neurons, while the paper's sentence cites Figures 1 and 3.

    single APs emerged near the peak of each beat, so the firing rate equaled the modulation rate ... the maximum number of APs evoked by five beats was limited to ten. Unlike pure sine waves, modulated stimuli did not trigger sustained firing or sustained depolarization

  • fig3-not-heating

    Excitation by pure sine waves was not caused by heating; the adiabatic temperature rise was 2.5 C after 250 ms at 63 V/cm in NaCl solution, at most 1.24 C for the same stimulus in the low-conductivity solution, and 4.1 C at 81 V/cm in NaCl.

    the maximum theoretical temperature rise (assuming no heat dissipation 43 ) was 2.5°C after 250 ms of near-threshold stimulation at 2 kHz, 63 V/cm, in the NaCl-based solution ... These observations prove that excitation by pure sine waves was not caused by heating

    Results, Decreased extracellular conductivity lowers excitation thresholds, paragraph 2; Figure 3A

    How it will be judged

    The rise is sigma E^2 t over the volumetric heat capacity (reference 43, cited for the adiabatic assumption). The paper does not give the heat capacity or whether E is the peak or the RMS field; readings (a) peak, (b) RMS, each with the heat capacity of water (4.18 J per cm3 per K, a standard choice). Reproduced when all three are within tolerance under a reading; since these are calculated numbers read two ways, that reading is reported. Computed from the paper's own values (1.64 S/m, 63 V/cm, 0.25 s), neither reading gives 2.5 C (peak about 3.9 C, RMS about 1.95 C), while the ratios 4.1/2.5 and 1.24/2.5 follow the formula's scaling with E^2 and with conductivity. The heat capacity or field definition the paper used is therefore not known; when no reading matches the absolute values but the two ratios are within 10%, the claim is blocked as methods_underspecified, and it is not reproduced only when the ratios fail as well. That the Figure 3A neuron fired at 63 V/cm in NaGlu as at 81 V/cm in NaCl is tested as in fig3b-low-conductivity-halves-threshold.
