From the paper NeuroPAL: A Multicolor Atlas for Whole-Brain Neuronal Identification in C. elegans · claims file · checked 2026-10-30: ready

  • fig6-sample-sizes

    Whole-brain activity was recorded in 21 heads (189 identified neurons) and, separately, 21 tails (42 neurons), with a median of 18 animals per neuron type.

    We recorded 21 worm heads (189 neurons) and, separately, 21 worm tails (42 neurons), with a median representation of 18 animals per neuron type

    Results, Whole-Brain Activity Imaging of Gustatory and Olfactory Responses, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Counts of OH16230 recordings. Animal counts are exact (tolerance 0, as they are whole counts of animals the paper states). The Figure 6 and S2 legends speak of correlations "between 189 neurons", which supports reading (a). The paper does not say whether "189 neurons" counts neurons with left and right separate or neuron names over all animals; the readings are (a) the number of distinct neuron names identified in at least one head; (b) the same, after the STAR Methods removals (near-duplicate traces, N of 3 or fewer). "Neuron type" is read as (a) a neuron name, left and right separate, or (b) a class, left and right pooled, with the median over types of the number of animals in which the type was identified. Reproduced when, under one reading, every value is within tolerance.

  • fig6-all-heads-responsive

    21 head recordings showed the published responses of the primary sensory neurons, ASE excitation to NaCl and AWC inhibition to both odors, in the left or the right neuron, and were kept for analysis.

    Worms were marked as stimulus responsive if either their left or right neuron showed the published response to their corresponding stimuli (ASE excitation for NaCl and AWC inhibition for odors) ... 21 heads responded to all three stimuli

    STAR Methods, Analysis of brainwide imaging data

    How it will be judged

    The paper says only that ASE and AWC responses were "reviewed", and does not say how a single neuron's response was judged in one animal, which had one pulse of each stimulus. The readings are (a) the mean dF/F0 during the 10 s pulse exceeds (ASE) or falls below (AWC) the mean over the 10 s before; (b) the same difference exceeds two standard deviations of the 10 s before. The paper does not say how many heads were recorded before this selection. Reproduced when, under one reading, 21 head recordings in the dataset pass for all three stimuli (all of them when the dataset holds only the 21 heads the paper kept).

  • fig6d-ase-nacl

    NaCl evokes left/right asymmetric responses in the two ASE neurons, and ASER shows an increase in calcium upon NaCl presentation.

    NaCl evoked stereotyped left/right (L/R) asymmetric responses in the two ASE neurons. However, ASER exhibited an increase in [Ca 2+ ] upon NaCl presentation

    Results, Whole-Brain Activity Imaging of Gustatory and Olfactory Responses, paragraph 2; Figure 6D

    How it will be judged

    Per the STAR Methods, ASER's mean dF/F0 during the NaCl pulse against the identical 10 s before, two-tailed paired t-test over head recordings; reproduced in part when the mean is higher with q of 0.05 or less. For the asymmetry, a two-tailed unpaired t-test of ASEL against ASER. The measure the asymmetry test compares is open; the readings are (a) the response, mean during the pulse minus mean before; (b) the mean during the pulse. The FDR family is open; the readings are (a) all neuron and stimulus tests of the heads together; (b) each stimulus apart. Reproduced when ASER is excited and the asymmetry test gives p of 0.05 or less under one pair of readings; partially reproduced when only one of the two holds.

  • fig6-nacl-unknown-interneurons

    NaCl evokes significant responses in six interneurons with no previously known function, AIN, AVF, AVH, AVJ, I2 and MI.

    In particular, we detected significant NaCl responses in six interneurons with no previously known function ... AIN, AVF, AVH, AVJ, I2, and MI

    Results, Whole-Brain Activity Imaging of Gustatory and Olfactory Responses, paragraph 2; Figures 6C to 6F

    How it will be judged

    Per the STAR Methods, for each class (left and right pooled) the mean dF/F0 during the NaCl pulse against the identical period before, two-tailed paired t-test, significant at p or q of 0.05 or less. The figure legend says significant responses are those with "p or q" of 0.05 or less, so the readings are (a) q from Storey FDR over all head tests of the three stimuli; (b) q over the NaCl tests only; (c) uncorrected p. Reproduced when all six classes are significant under one reading; partially reproduced for three to five.

  • fig6c-odors-more-inhibitory

    Both odors evoke responses in many sensory and interneurons throughout the brain, with more inhibitory (decreasing calcium) activity than NaCl.

    We found that odors also evoked responses in a large set of both sensory and interneurons, throughout the brain, but exhibited more inhibitory activity (decreasing [Ca 2+ ]) than seen for NaCl

    Results, Whole-Brain Activity Imaging of Gustatory and Olfactory Responses, paragraph 2; Figure 6C

    How it will be judged

    The paper gives no measure for "more inhibitory". Figure 6C shows peak activity before and during the stimulus per head class. The readings are (a) the number of head classes with a significant decrease (paired t-test as in fig6-nacl-unknown-interneurons, q of 0.05 or less) and (b) the fraction of head classes whose mean response (during minus before) is negative. Reproduced when, under one reading, each odor exceeds NaCl.

    In the tail, PVQ is significantly excited by 2,3-pentanedione, and a mix of sensory and interneurons show significant post-stimulus responses to all three stimuli, including LUA, PVN, PVQ, PVR and PVW.

    In the tail, the PVQ interneurons were significantly excited upon presentation of 2,3-pentanedione, while a mix of sensory and interneurons exhibited significant post-stimulus responses ... to all three stimuli ...   • figs1h-avf-cyclic

    In many animals the AVF interneurons show robust cyclical activity of about 0.3 Hz for the whole 4 min recording, whatever the stimulus, similar to the crawling frequency of freely moving worms of this strain.

    in many animals, the pair of AVF interneurons displayed robust cyclical activity for the entire 4-min recording, irrespective of delivery of gustatory or olfactory stimuli. The frequency of this activity was ~0.3 Hz

    Results, Whole-Brain Activity Imaging of Gustatory and Olfactory Responses, paragraph 3; Figure S1H

    How it will be judged

    Per the Figure S1H legend, AVF traces median filtered at 0.5 s, and the frequency counted as the number of peaks per 4 min interval; the legend shows traces from five OH16230 worms, and the Results say "in many animals". How peaks are detected, and which animals count as cyclical, are open. The readings for peaks are (a) local maxima of the filtered trace with prominence of at least one standard deviation of the trace; (b) the same with half a standard deviation. The readings for animals are (a) all head recordings with AVF identified; (b) those whose AVF trace has its largest spectral peak between 0.1 and 1 Hz. Reproduced when, under one pair of readings, the median frequency over AVF neurons is within tolerance. The crawling frequency (Figure S1G, Table S2) is from a behavioral assay the dandiset is not described as holding, and is not tested.

  • fig6gi-stimulus-specific-correlations

    Pairwise correlations between neurons are similar across individuals for a stimulus, but each stimulus generates its own brainwide correlation pattern, even the two attractive odors.

    We found similar stimulus-specific neuronal correlations across individuals, but each stimulus generated its own brainwide correlation pattern ... Even the two attractive odors produced distinct sets of neuronal correlations among and between sensory and interneurons

    Results, Whole-Brain Neuronal Dynamics and Connectivity, paragraph 1; Figures 6G to 6I and S2C to S2E

    How it will be judged

    Per the Figure 6 legend, Pearson correlations between head neurons in each animal over the 30 s following stimulus onset, averaged across animals. The paper gives no test of "similar" or "distinct". The readings are (a) for each pair of animals, the correlation between their correlation matrices over shared neuron pairs, same stimulus against different stimuli, compared by a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test over animal pairs; (b) split the animals in two halves at random 100 times, and compare the correlation of the two halves' mean matrices for the same stimulus against that for different stimuli. Reproduced when, under one reading, same-stimulus similarity exceeds different-stimulus similarity for each of the three stimulus pairs (p < 0.05 for reading a; in at least 95 of 100 splits for reading b); partially reproduced when it holds for NaCl against the odors but not between the two odors.

  • figs2a-pca-trajectories

    The average trajectories of whole-brain activity in the space of its first three principal components, over the 10 s stimulus and 20 s after, show similarities but remain distinguishable between the three stimuli.

    Average phase trajectories (N = 21 animal heads) of whole-brain neuronal activity in response to stimuli (10 s of stimulus application and 20 s thereafter) ... The average stimulus trajectories exhibit similarities but remain distinguishable

    Figure S2A and S2B; Results, Whole-Brain Neuronal Dynamics and Connectivity, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    The paper does not say how the principal components are computed or how "distinguishable" is judged. The PCA is read as (a) on the matrix of class-averaged traces over the whole recording, or (b) on the stimulus windows only, with neurons missing from an animal left out. A trajectory is distinguishable from another when the two trajectories' mean distance exceeds twice the mean standard error of the mean over the 21 heads (the mesh of Figure S2A). Reproduced when, under one reading, all three pairs are distinguishable.

  • fig6jk-connectome-correlation

    Functional correlations correspond little to the anatomical connectome; the R2 of the Pearson correlation between absolute functional correlations and synapse counts is 2.8% (head) and 1.7% (tail) for electrical synapses, and 0.5% (head) and 1.5% (tail) for chemical synapses.

    We found low Pearson correlation between these functional and structural matrices. For electrical connectivity, R 2 = 2.8% in the head and R 2 = 1.7% in the tail, and for chemical connectivity, R 2 = 0.5% in the head and R 2 = 1.5% in the tail

    Results, Whole-Brain Neuronal Dynamics and Connectivity, paragraph 2; Figures 6J and 6K

    How it will be judged

    Per the Results, the absolute value of the functional correlation matrix against the synapse count matrix of Cook et al. 2019 (hermaphrodite), Pearson R2. Open details and their readings. The functional matrix is (a) the per-animal correlation over the whole recording averaged over animals (the variations the paper lists include limiting to stimulus periods, so the default is read as the whole recording); (b) per-animal neuron pairs pooled. Neuron pairs are (a) all off-diagonal pairs of identified neurons, those without synapses counted as zero; (b) only pairs with at least one synapse. Electrical counts are made symmetric; chemical counts are (a) summed over both directions; (b) the larger direction. Reproduced when, under one combination of readings, all four values are within tolerance; partially reproduced when all four R2 values are below 5% but not within tolerance.

  • fig6-connectome-variations

    Variations of the comparison (thresholding for low sampling, ranked correlation, log-scaled synapse counts, stimulus or non-stimulus periods only) do not noticeably improve the correlation between functional activity and the connectome.

    We tried multiple variations in our calculations (e.g., thresholding for low sampling, using ranked correlation metrics, log-scaling synaptic counts, and limiting functional activity to only stimulus or non-stimulus delivery periods) but these did not noticeably improve correlation between functional activity and the structural connectome

    Results, Whole-Brain Neuronal Dynamics and Connectivity, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    The paper gives no threshold for "noticeably" and the values are in Table S6, not read. Each variation is run on the reading of fig6jk-connectome-correlation that matched (the first listed if none did); thresholding for low sampling keeps pairs seen in more than 5, 10 or 15 animals, the thresholds of Figure S2C to S2E. That legend writes "N > 5, 10, and 15 neurons", while the STAR Methods (Statistical analysis) say N is the number of worms; the worm reading is used, as a neuron count does not define a sampling threshold for a pair. Ranked correlation is Spearman, a standard choice the paper does not name. The 3.1% bound for "noticeably" is a choice of this file, not the paper's. Reproduced when no variation gives an R2 above 3.1%, the largest R2 the paper reports widened by 10%.
