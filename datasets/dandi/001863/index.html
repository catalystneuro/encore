  • ✓1 reproduced

In this data, most of the paper's behavioral and sniffing findings held up, but its reward-port findings could not be tested and its speed findings did not reproduce. DOI lowered accuracy, reduced trials completed (mainly in females) and lengthened odor trials. It also raised sniff frequency in every trial period, and head twitches rose toward the odor end of the arena. Lower movement speed on DOI did not reach significance (p = 0.063 within trials, 0.079 in the intertrial interval). The reward-port durations of Figure 2 are blocked because the dataset has no port entries or exits. The tracking has no frame times, and the within-trial speed and head-twitch results depend on the paper's assumed clock.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe reward-port entries and exits behind Figure 2 (reward-port duration after correct and incorrect trials, nine claims) are not in the dandiset; no event series, column or table holds them, and the code reads them from pre-computed files that are not public.
  • Metadata errorThe pose series carry no frame times; their stated rate is an estimate made at conversion (frames over the last trial's end time, 72.7 to 85.3 frames/s), and with it the tracking does not line up with the trial table. A linear clock fitted per session to the nose's movement over trials does (median 80.47 frames/s in the later cohort and 73.71 in the first, offsets of -10.5 to 14.0 s); 30 of 44 sessions have a good fit (the nose moves 100 px or more over a trial, offsets in the two halves of the session within 1 s of each other, and the implied video covers the trials and ends at most 2 min after the last one). The fit is weakest in the two sessions of mouse 2197 (27 and 79 px), and the 71.0 frames/s fit of 2204 DOI would end the video 311 s after the last trial. The HTR times are on the video's frame clock at 80 frames/s (frame t x 80): the nose moves most within 1 s of them in all 22 DOI sessions, against 8 when they are placed by the fitted trial clock.
  • Published code disagreesThe paper's code (fig3_cleaned.Rmd and fig5_cleaned.Rmd) places tracking frame i at i / 80 s and compares HTR video times directly with trial times. On these data that clock does not align frames with trials (median nose displacement over a trial about 0 px against hundreds with a fitted clock). With the fitted clock the within-trial speed difference (Figure 3C) and the within-trial HTR preference for x vanish, and HTRs are about a third as frequent within trials as in the ITI, against the paper's Figure 5F.
  • Description missingThe series processing/behavior/task_event_timestamps is the head-twitch times (by the conversion script's source folders and the paper's code), but the files call it only "Event times (s) from Time Stamps Task" with data all 1.
  • Metadata errorIn the 27 later-cohort sessions the HTR timestamps came as HH:MM:SS:FF video timecodes whose last field is a frame count at 80 frames/s (never above 79); the conversion stored it as hundredths of a second (FF / 100 against FF / 80), so the stored times are early by up to 0.2 s.
  • Published code disagreesfig5_cleaned.Rmd reads the last field of the later cohort's HTR timecodes as thousandths of a second, while it is a frame count at 80 frames/s, making the HTR times early by up to 0.9 s; the outcomes here do not change with the reading.
  • Metadata errorThe subject sex of the saline session of mouse 2182 (sub-2182_ses-2182-31) is female, while its DOI session and the paper's code give male.
  • Description missingThe trial columns are described only by their names (odor_control, side, outcome, iti_time, false_alarm, repoke); the codes are not explained. The session notes show side 2 is left and side 1 right in all 34 later-cohort sessions, and the code gives odor_control 0 as an odor trial and 3 as an omission trial.
  • OtherIn the five first-cohort mice 347 trials have a set ITI of 16 to 25 s, while the Methods give a variable ITI of 1 to 15 s.
  • Selection not documentedThe paper's code drops four sniff sessions as faulty (2195 DOI, 2198 DOI, 2198 saline, 2213 saline), without a stated reason; the dandiset keeps them without a flag, though their descriptions show many rows dropped for a missing exhalation (34772 of 35633 in 2198 DOI, 33144 of 34034 in 2198 saline, 2405 of 7577 in 2213 saline).   • Paper inconsistentThe Results name the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for the speed comparisons of Figure 3C, 3D and its supplement, while the Methods and the code use the paired signed-rank test; the two give different p-values here (for example 0.51 against 0.063 for Figure 3C).

  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors, on fig4supp-ks-every-mouse.
