    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The field map is computed here by a two-dimensional finite-difference solve (40 um grid, electrodes as 1.5 mm discs of fixed potential, insulating wall of a 35 mm dish centred on the image centre), in place of the paper's Sim4Life model (Methods 4.3, "1.6 S/m solution to a depth of 3.5 mm", field "in the plane 1 um above the glass"). The needles span the solution from the glass up (Methods 4.2, "lowered ... until the tips touched the glass bottom"), so the field does not vary with height. Electrode positions are measured in the image, and the scale is set from the 1.5 mm needle diameter (Methods 4.2); it gives 0.654 um per pixel, the camera's 0.65.
    • Assumed that E1, the electrode pulsed in Figure 4A, is the lower-right base electrode of the file's image (base_a). The file does not record it, and the paper's text does not place it (the Figure 4 legend says only that the panels are shown "with electrode positions labeled"). The published panel A has the apex at the top and E1 at the lower right, as the deposited image has the apex at the top, but that is not shown in the notebook, and the deposited images do not keep one orientation (the paired-pulse image has its apex at the bottom). base_a is also the base electrode with the most uptake (with E1 = base_b the grounded electrode would show 2.5 times the uptake of the pulsed one), so the assignment rests on the uptake. The notebook shows both assignments.
    • The single-pulse file is taken as the image of Figure 4A from its name, the dandiset's title (the Figure 4 legend) and its layout (three electrodes in a triangle, uptake around one base electrode). Its session description gives another exposure (four co-directional paired-pulse trains at 770 kHz, 10 kV/cm, "the electric field direction stayed the same in each pair"). It does not say which electrodes were energized; that the field came from one base electrode is an inference from the uptake around one of them.
    • The ROI grid (within 6 mm of the image centre, leaving out ROIs within 150 um of a footprint edge) and the neighbourhood (0 to 500 um from the footprint edge) are choices the claims file does not fix; the notebook shows 5 and 6 mm, 150 and 400 um, and 250, 500 and 1000 um, and the outcome is the same under all of them. The Spearman test is also run on ROIs thinned to 1 and 2 mm spacing, as contiguous ROIs are spatially dependent.
    • The amplitude is taken as 4 kV (Section 2.3); the file's description says 2 kV. A rank correlation and an order do not depend on the amplitude.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Assumed that objects bright in the MemBrite channel are not part of the electroporated monolayer, and their pixels are left out before the maximum is taken (0.54% of the region at 10 times the dish median, several objects). MemBrite stains all cell membranes about evenly (dish median 257); the object under the literal maximum is about 20 times brighter in it, larger than a cell and out of focus. The rule (MemBrite above 10 times the dish median, grown by 30 um) is a choice; 5 and 20 times are shown and give the same, as do two maxima robust to single objects (99.5th percentile, median filter). The paper gives no measure for this finding.
    • The 200 um mean is taken over pixels outside the footprints and inside 6 mm of the image centre only (normalised convolution), so that the dark footprints and image corners do not enter it. The median filter is not masked.
    • The criterion's further toward the base than across is measured from the apex centre, as the reach along the bisecting line toward the base against the largest distance across it. The extents along and across the line are shown too. They agree with it at a quarter maximum and in the 99.5th-percentile and median-filter versions, but not at half maximum in the versions without bright objects, on which the outcome rests. There the extent along the line is smaller than across it (2446 against 2592 um at 10 and 20 times, 2446 against 2473 um at 5 times), and the region is a blob around the apex footprint about as wide as it is long. The pass at half maximum depends on the reach measure.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds only the two whole-dish images of Figure 4. The ROI measurements of Figure 2 (n = 3 at 1 Hz and at 833 kHz), the square-array experiments of Figure 3 (n = 6 per group) and the strobe imaging of Figure 5, on which the paper's quantitative findings rest, are not deposited, nor is the field simulation of Methods 4.3 that gives each ROI its angle. Nine of the twelve claims cannot be tested.
  • File format errorEach image is stored as an ImageSeries with data of shape (22323, 22323, 2) and a rate of 2 Hz. NWB reads the first axis of an ImageSeries as time, so a standard reader sees 22323 frames of 22323 x 2 pixels; the data are one stitched image of 22323 x 22323 pixels in two channels.
  • Description missingNo pixel size, channel names or intensity unit (unit "n.a.") are stored. The channel order comes only from the free-text description, and the scale only from the needle diameter in the paper (0.654 um per pixel, which matches the camera and objective named in the description). The file does not say which base electrode was pulsed in the single-pulse image (E1 in Figure 4A).
  • Metadata errorThe session description of Single-pulse_trains describes another experiment ("4 co-directional paired-pulse (600 + 600 ns) trains ... 770 kHz, amplitude 10 kV/cm"), the settings of Figure 3, while its image shows a triangular array with the single-pulse pattern of Figure 4A.
  • Metadata errorThe session description of Paired-pulse_trains gives an amplitude of 2 kV; the paper gives 4 kV for both Figure 4 images (Section 2.3). With the array measured in the image, a field model gives 4.2 to 9.7 kV/cm on the bisecting line at 4 kV, close to the 5 to 10 kV/cm of Section 2.1, and half that at 2 kV.
  • Metadata errorThe image description says the tiles came from a six-well plate, where the paper used 35 mm glass-bottom dishes; the subject age is P1D for a cell line, and each subject is a condition (Single-pulse_trains, Paired-pulse_trains), not an animal or culture.
  • Methods not availableQuestions for the paper's authors. (1) Is the deposited file Single-pulse_trains the image of Figure 4A, despite its session description (four co-directional pairs at 770 kHz), and which of its base electrodes is E1, the one pulsed? This attempt took it as Figure 4A and E1 as the lower-right base electrode (base_a), which has the most uptake. Compared outside the entry with the published figure, the file did not match Figure 4A in fine detail, while the paired-pulse file matched Figure 4B turned by 180 degrees. If Figure 4A's image is another file, can it be deposited? (2) How was the agreement of uptake with the field map (Figure 4A) and the comet-shaped region (Figure 4B,C) judged? The paper gives no measure; this attempt used rank correlation, the order of electrode neighbourhoods, and thresholds of half and a quarter of a maximum taken after leaving out objects bright in the membrane stain, likely debris.
  • OtherThe brightest YP in the paired-pulse image lies on one object about 105 um across, 2.5 mm from the apex electrode, bright in both channels (median 14577 in YO-PRO-1 and 5266 in MemBrite, against about 305 and 255 in the monolayer around it) and out of focus, likely debris above the monolayer. A maximum taken over the image without leaving it out is about four times the brightest uptake of the monolayer.
