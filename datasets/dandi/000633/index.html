From the paper Control of the Electroporation Efficiency of Nanosecond Pulses by Swinging the Electric Field Vector Direction · claims file · checked 2026-10-23: ready

  • fig2-cancellation-at-180

    At a 180 degree change of field direction, trains of paired pulses electroporate less than trains of single pulses, about 2-fold less at 1 Hz and about 20-fold less at 833 kHz, so cancellation is stronger at the higher rate.

    Maximum cancellation was expectedly observed at the 180° vector change, reducing YP uptake compared to single-pulse trains 2-fold at 1 Hz and as much as 20-fold at 833 kHz

    Results, Section 2.1, paragraph 2; Figure 2

    How it will be judged

    As in Section 2.1 and the Figure 2 legend, YP uptake per ROI under paired pulses as % of single pulses in the same ROI, mean over the n = 3 experiments, with each ROI's angle taken from the field simulation of Methods 4.3 (an outside input, not deposited). Open details, each a reading to run. (1) Spontaneous uptake measured in the electrode footprints (Methods 4.4) is (a) subtracted from each ROI before the ratio, or (b) not subtracted. (2) The 180 degree value is (a) the ROI nearest the base, or (b) the value at 180 degrees read from a fit of the curve (the paper fitted in Grapher 16 but does not name the function). Reproduced when both folds fall within tolerance under one reading.

  • fig2-crossover-angle

    As the angle falls from 180 degrees, cancellation weakens, and paired-pulse trains equal single-pulse trains at about 160 degrees at 1 Hz and about 130 degrees at 833 kHz.

    Cancellation weakened as the angle was reduced, and at about 160° (1 Hz) or 130° (833 kHz), the effects of paired-pulse trains became the same as those of single-pulse ones

    Results, Section 2.1, paragraph 2; Figure 2

    How it will be judged

    The curves of claim fig2-cancellation-at-180, under its readings. The crossing is the angle where the mean curve crosses 100%, found by (a) linear interpolation between the two adjacent ROIs (a standard choice the paper does not fix) or (b) the fitted curve. Reproduced when both angles fall within tolerance under one reading.

  • fig2-facilitation-plateau

    Below the crossover, paired pulses facilitate electroporation, up to about three times the single-pulse effect at 90 to 100 degrees at both rates, and the effect then stays on a plateau down to 0 degrees.

    Further reduction of the angle enhanced electroporation, up to about three-fold at 90–100° for both nsEP frequencies, followed by a plateau all the way to 0°

    Results, Section 2.1, paragraph 2; Figure 2; Discussion, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    The curves of claim fig2-cancellation-at-180, under its readings. The value at 90 to 100 degrees is (a) the mean of the ROIs whose angle lies in 90 to 100 degrees or (b) the ROI nearest 95 degrees. The paper gives no test or bound for the plateau; it is read as the mean over the ROIs below 90 degrees lying within the same tolerance (270 to 330%) at each rate, a choice that uses the default 10% tolerance of the three-fold value. Reproduced when both values and both plateaus hold under one reading; partially reproduced when the three-fold values hold and the plateau does not.

  • fig2-60-fold-co-counter

    At 833 kHz co-directional pulse pairs electroporate about 60 times more than counter-directional ones.

    The profound, 60-fold difference in electroporation efficiency of co- and counter-directional nsEPs at 833 kHz

    Results, Section 2.3, paragraph 1; Figure 2

    How it will be judged

    The 833 kHz curve of claim fig2-cancellation-at-180, under its readings. The paper does not say which co-directional value is meant; the readings are the ratio to the 180 degree value of (a) the plateau value (claim fig2-facilitation-plateau) or (b) the ROI nearest 0 degrees. Reproduced when the ratio is within tolerance under one reading.

  • fig3-1hz-co-cross-same

    At 1 Hz, paired pulses that keep their direction and pairs whose second pulse turns by 90 degrees electroporate the cell monolayer equally.

    Consistently with the measurements in Figure 2 , co- and cross-directional pulses at 1 Hz had the same electroporation efficiency

    Results, Section 2.2, paragraph 1; Figure 3

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. YP uptake in the 200 x 200 um ROI at the array center (Methods 4.4), n = 6 per group, co-directional against cross-directional at 1 Hz with the paper's two-tailed Student's t-test (Figure 3 legend), read as the unpaired test with equal variances, which is what the name denotes; the paper reports no pairing of samples. The p-values are printed in Figure 3 only and were not read. Spontaneous uptake measured in the electrode footprints is subtracted, as Methods 4.4 says it was measured to account for it; the unsubtracted values are shown as a variant only. The threshold is not stated; the conventional p < 0.05 is used. Reproduced when the test finds no difference; the difference of means is reported either way.

  • fig3-770khz-co-enhanced

    Increasing the pulse frequency to 770 kHz enhanced electroporation by the co-directional pulses because of the temporal summation of the induced membrane potential

  • fig4a-single-pulse-follows-field

    Under a train of five single pulses applied to one base electrode (833 kHz, 4 kV), YP uptake is distributed as the electric field strength map.

    In control cell monolayers exposed to a train of five single pulses applied to one of the base electrodes (833 kHz, 4 kV), the distribution of YP fluorescence ( Figure 4 A) simply followed the electric field strength map

    Results, Section 2.3, paragraph 1; Figure 4A

    How it will be judged

    The paper shows this as an image beside the simulated field map (Methods 4.3, Figure 7B), which is not deposited and whose values the text does not give; the comparison needs that map as an outside input, computed as Methods 4.3 states (Sim4Life, 1.6 S/m solution 3.5 mm deep, 1 V on the energized electrode, field in the plane 1 um above the glass, scaled to 4 kV). The paper names no measure of agreement. Readings, each to run. Background-subtracted YP (footprint background, Methods 4.4) in 200 x 200 um ROIs over the image, compared with the field in the same ROIs by (a) Spearman rank correlation or (b) the order of the three electrode neighbourhoods by YP matching their order by field. Both measures are choices, as the paper names none. Reproduced when both readings agree with the map (correlation positive with p < 0.05, and the same order), not reproduced when both fail, and methods_underspecified when they disagree. Without the map the claim is blocked.

  • fig4bc-paired-pulse-focal-apex

    Under five paired pulses applied to the two base electrodes in alternation (833 kHz, 4 kV), electroporation is strong near the grounded apex electrode and weak or absent near the base electrodes, despite the strong field there.

    energizing two base electrodes in alternation (five paired pulses, 600 + 600 ns, 833 kHz, 4 kV) produced a strong effect near the apex electrode (ground) but weak or no effect near the base electrodes, despite the strong electric field there

    Results, Section 2.3, paragraph 1; Figure 4B,C; Discussion, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    The paper shows this as images only (Figure 4B and the 3D rendering in 4C) and gives no measure. From Methods 4.4, YP intensity in 200 x 200 um ROIs with the footprint background subtracted. How near "near" is, is open; the readings are ROIs centered (a) 100 um and (b) 500 um outside the edge of each electrode footprint (1.5 mm needles), on the line from that electrode toward the array center. Reproduced when, under both readings, the YP near the apex in the paired-pulse image exceeds that near each base electrode, and the ratio of apex to base YP is higher in the paired-pulse image than in the single-pulse image of Figure 4A (the contrast the paper draws between the panels); not reproduced when either fails under both readings; the readings disagreeing makes the claim methods_underspecified. The values near each electrode in both images are reported.

  • fig4-comet-shape

    Under paired pulses the electroporated cells form a comet-shaped region pointing from the apex electrode towards the base.

    The electroporated cells formed a comet-shaped region pointing from the apex electrode towards the base

    Discussion, paragraph 2; Figure 4B,C

    How it will be judged

    Shown in images only, with no measure. The paper does not say what counts as electroporated; the readings for the electroporated region are background-subtracted YP above (a) half and (b) a quarter of its maximum in the paired-pulse image, after smoothing over 200 x 200 um (the ROI size of Methods 4.4). Reproduced when, under both readings, the region touches the apex footprint and extends further along the bisecting line toward the base than across it.

  • fig5-base-sawtooth

    Cells placed between the base electrodes displayed sawtooth-like changes in membrane potential in synchrony with the applied pulses ... Depolarization at the cathode-facing pole of the cell manifested as a ~10% emission increase, and hyperpolarization at the anode-facing pole caused a ~10% emission decrease

    Results, Section 2.4, paragraph 2; Figure 5

    How it will be judged

    As in Methods 4.5 and the Figure 5 legend, emission at the two opposite poles along the field gradient, normalized to whole-cell emission, mean of 5 cells over the 220 time points. The peak is (a) the largest excursion over the train or (b) the mean of the per-pulse peaks (the paper does not say). Reproduced when both magnitudes are within tolerance under one reading and the sign of the change alternates with each pulse.

  • fig5-apex-larger-polarization

    Cells near the apex reach a polarization of about 25% FluoVolt emission change, more than twice that between the base electrodes, which follows the envelope of the train and not its individual pulses.

    In stark contrast, the cells near the apex reached a much higher polarization (~25% change in FluoVolt emission), which followed the envelope of the pulse train but not the individual pulses of it

    Results, Section 2.4, paragraph 2; Figure 5

    How it will be judged

    As in claim fig5-base-sawtooth, with its readings. Reproduced when the apex value is within tolerance, exceeds twice the base value, and the apex trace keeps one sign through the train.
