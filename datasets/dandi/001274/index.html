From the paper Excitation and polarization of isolated neurons by high-frequency sine waves for temporal interference stimulation · claims file · checked 2026-10-22: ready

  • fig1-pure-sine-excites-all

    Unmodulated 250-ms sine waves at 2 kHz and at 20 kHz excited every neuron tested, never as an on-off response.

    Pure sine waves at 2 and even 20 kHz excited 100% of neurons. It was never an "on-off" response

    Results, Stimulation by 2- and 20-kHz unmodulated sine waves, paragraph 1; Figure 1A to 1C

    How it will be judged

    Per neuron, excited when at least one session with an unmodulated 2- or 20-kHz sine wave has an AP during the 250-ms stimulus. The Methods give no detection rule beyond "brief emission upticks" in neurons whose APs peak above 5% dF and last about 10 ms (Recording of the optical APs); readings (a) a peak of dF above 5% over F0, (b) a rise of at least 5% dF within 5 ms, which also counts APs riding on a depolarization. Reproduced when, under each reading, at least 90% of neurons tested with each frequency are excited; blocked as methods_underspecified when the readings disagree.

  • fig1-onset-delay

    Near threshold, isolated APs appear 100 to 200 ms after the sine wave onset, and stronger fields shorten this delay and evoke sustained firing.

    at near-threshold electric field strengths, isolated APs emerged with a delay of 100-200 ms after the sine wave onset ... Increasing the field strength reduced this delay and evoked sustained firing

    Results, Stimulation by 2- and 20-kHz unmodulated sine waves, paragraph 1; Figure 1A to 1C

    How it will be judged

    AP detection as in fig1-pure-sine-excites-all, both readings. Per neuron and frequency, the near-threshold session is the session of lowest field with at least one AP. Reproduced when the median first-AP latency of those sessions lies within 90 to 220 ms (the range widened by 10% of each bound) and, per neuron, the first-AP latency falls with field (Spearman correlation below zero in more than half of the neurons with at least three sessions with APs). The paper gives no test for either part; the median, the Spearman sign and the majority are standard choices.

  • fig1-max-rate-ceiling

    The maximum firing rate increases linearly with field strength until it reaches a ceiling of 60 to 90 Hz.

    The maximum firing rate increased linearly until reaching a ceiling of 60-90 Hz

    Results, Stimulation by 2- and 20-kHz unmodulated sine waves, paragraph 1; Figure 1 inset

    How it will be judged

    The maximum firing rate of a session is the inverse of the interval between the peaks of its first two APs (Methods, Recording of the optical APs); AP detection as in fig1-pure-sine-excites-all, both readings. The ceiling of a neuron is its highest maximum firing rate over sessions with unmodulated sine waves. Reproduced when the median ceiling over neurons lies within 54 to 99 Hz (60 to 90 Hz widened by 10% of each bound). The rise below the ceiling is reported as the slope of a linear fit of rate on field over the sessions before the ceiling; the inset shows three neurons only.

  • fig1-excitation-block

    At the highest field strengths, APs coalesce into low-amplitude oscillations on a continuous depolarization, which can flatten and leave the membrane depolarized for the rest of the sine wave (an excitation block).

    At the highest field strengths, multiple APs coalesced into low-amplitude oscillations on top of a continuous depolarization. These oscillations could flatten after several cycles, leaving the membrane depolarized for the rest of the sine wave

    Results, Stimulation by 2- and 20-kHz unmodulated sine waves, paragraph 1; Figure 1A to 1C

    How it will be judged

    The paper gives no measure. Per neuron, the session of highest field with an unmodulated sine wave is compared with the near-threshold session (as in fig1-onset-delay). Reproduced when, in more than half of the neurons, the mean dF over the last 100 ms of the stimulus in the highest-field session exceeds the pre-stimulus F0 by more than 5% dF (the Methods' AP selection level) and exceeds that of the near-threshold session. The windows are a standard choice; the mean over the whole second half of the stimulus is shown as a variant.

  • fig2d-20khz-twofold-threshold

    All excitation thresholds at 20 kHz were about twice those at 2 kHz, for modulated and unmodulated sine waves and for 1 and 5 APs.

    All thresholds at 20 kHz were about 2-fold higher than at 2 kHz ( p < 0.0001, two-tailed unpaired t test)

    Results, Excitation thresholds are similar or lower for pure 2- and 20-kHz sine waves than those for modulated sine waves, paragraph 1; Figure 2D

    How it will be judged

    The threshold of a neuron for a stimulus is the lowest field of a session that evoked at least 1 (or at least 5) APs during the 250-ms stimulus (Figure 2D legend; Methods, 5% to 10% amplitude steps); AP detection as in fig1-pure-sine-excites-all, both readings. For each of the four groups (unmodulated and modulated, 1 and 5 APs) the ratio of mean 20-kHz to mean 2-kHz threshold, and a two-tailed unpaired t test of the two (Methods, Quantification and statistical analysis). The paper gives one p for "all thresholds", so which comparison it is open; readings (a) one test per group, each p < 0.0001, (b) one test pooling the four groups, 20 kHz against 2 kHz. Reproduced when each ratio is within tolerance and the test gives p < 0.0001 under both readings of the test; blocked as methods_underspecified when the readings disagree. The field per session is taken from the dataset's metadata; the paper gives the conversion from volts to V/cm only as the simulated profile of Figure S4C, with no number in the text, so a session without a recorded field is open.
