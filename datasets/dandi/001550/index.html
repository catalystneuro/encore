Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe dandiset holds only the behaviour. The electrophysiology behind Figures 1d to 1f, 3h and 5d (3,495 units from 7 rats) is not in it; it is public only in the paper's GitHub repository, as one MATLAB file per unit with binned spike counts, without raw data or spike sorting output.
  • Identifier missingNothing links the paper's units to dandiset sessions. Matched by trial sequence, 3,269 of 3,495 units fall in exactly one dandiset session when gaps are allowed (2,371 without gaps), from 112 sessions, where the paper reports 199; 226 units match no session. Searching every TaskSwitch protocol gives 3,349 units from 115 sessions, and 146 unmatched.
  • Metadata errorEvery optogenetic epoch gives a wavelength of 473 nm; Extended Data Figure 4 says 450 nm, and the conversion's documentation says the value was corrected to 450.
  • Description missingOptogenetic epochs are present for four rats (P116, P124, P127, P131), where the paper reports three; nothing in the dandiset or the paper says which three were analysed.
  • Metadata errorThe dates of birth give ages of 3.0 to 39.1 months at the sessions the paper's selection keeps (18 rats younger than 5.4 months at some), where the Methods state 6 to 24 months. Either the dates of birth or the stated ages are wrong. The dates of birth put 25 rats at 1.6 to 2.7 months at their first session; P189's (2018-02-21) puts it at 14.1 months on the day P187, born 2019-02-21, starts, and may be a year early.
  • File format errorThirteen task sessions (1 to 3 trials each) lack HistorySection_result_history or a pulse column, and 95 of the other 15,400 lack HistorySection_hit_history. Where both outcome columns exist they agree on every trial.
  • Data missingOne session of the paper's tables with 27 valid trials, P189 TaskSwitch6-210516a, is not in the dandiset; the conversion's notes list it among aborted sessions left out.
  • OtherSix task sessions of P187 and P189 (TaskSwitch6, 2021) are in the dandiset but not in the paper's tables; five of them pass the Methods selection, adding 1,442 and 609 trials to those rats.
  • OtherHistorySection_gammafreq_history, a signed strength, is stored as uint8 or int16 in 570 files; in all of them its values equal the float gamma_freq, so nothing was lost.
  • Selection not documentedThe paper's code also requires a per-session `good` flag that the Methods do not describe and the dandiset does not hold, and caps relative weights at 1; neither changes the selection except for one P059 session.
  • Published code disagreesThe Methods describe ridge regression with the regulariser chosen by cross-validation; the code adds a fixed penalty of 200 on squared differences of neighbouring weights. The code also uses looser selections (0.65 correct and 0.6 index) for the recorded rats in Figure 5d and for Extended Data Figure 1e than the Methods' 0.7.
  • Published code disagreesThe paper's own code (ExtDataFigure5ag.m), applied to its own fitted per-context kernels, gives angles of 1.1 to 8.0 degrees between the context choice axes (mean 4.7), not the reported average of 1.6 degrees. The permutation test of each angle against 0 that the Methods describe is not in the code.
  • Paper inconsistentThe Methods say 3,495 units were recorded from 5 rats in FOF and list FOF counts that sum to 3,285; the 3,495 include the 210 mPFC units. The main text calls a converging differential behavioural kernel a slope index above 0, while the Methods and the code give converging kernels negative indices, as the data have.

Reproduction Context-dependent evidence accumulation in rats (Pagan et al. 2025)

Tests the findings of Pagan, Tang, Aoi, Pillow, Mante, Sussillo and Brody (2025) on dandiset 001550, which holds the behaviour of 26 rats trained on a task that switches between accumulating location and frequency evidence. It links the dandiset to the paper's published behavioural tables, checks the session statistics and selection, fits the behavioural pulse kernels and their slope indices, tests the optogenetic inactivation, and, with the paper's neural data from GitHub, the link between behavioural and neural slope indices.
