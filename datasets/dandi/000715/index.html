  • fig2cd-variability-differs

    In a probabilistic atlas built from 10 young-adult NeuroPAL worms, the positional variability of cell bodies differs across neuron types.

    This map revealed different positional variability across neuron types

    Results, Variability in Neuronal Cell Body Positions, paragraph 2; Figures 2C and 2D

    How it will be judged

    The atlas is fit on all 10 worms, head and tail, as in the shared method above; a neuron's positional variability is the standard deviation of its atlas positional covariance along each of the three principal axes of the atlas means (the axes Methods S1 Figure 6.1 plots, major and minor axes of position). The paper gives no test or threshold. The one difference it quantifies is OLLR against OLLL, over a factor of two (claim fig2c-ollr-more-variable-than-olll), so the claim is reproduced when, along at least one axis, the largest per-neuron standard deviation is more than twice the smallest within the head and within the tail. The distribution of per-neuron standard deviations is reported.

  • fig2c-ollr-more-variable-than-olll

    In the head atlas, OLLR shows over twice the positional variability of OLLL along the anterior-posterior axis.

    OLLR exhibits over twice the positional variability of OLLL in its anterior-posterior axis

    Figure 2C legend

    How it will be judged

    Head atlas fit on all 10 worms as in the shared method. The anterior-posterior axis is the major axis of the atlas means (Methods S1 Figure 6.1 legend; the CELL_ID code aligns positions to it by principal components, AutoId.major_axis_align). "Positional variability" can be read as (a) the standard deviation of the neuron's positional distribution along that axis, (b) its variance, or (c) the anterior-posterior extent of the 50% contour within the 2-D plane Figure 2 slices (left-right or dorsal-ventral view, the conditional distribution in that plane). Reproduced when the OLLR to OLLL ratio exceeds 2 under any reading; all three are reported.

  • fig7bc-identification-accuracy

    Unsupervised identification of neurons against the statistical atlas of NeuroPAL colors and positions is 86% accurate in the head and 94% in the tail, cross-validated.

    Accuracy was 86% for the head and 94% for the tail.

    Results, Semi-automated Neuronal Identification, paragraph 2; Figures 7B and 7C; Methods S1 section 6.3, Figure 6.4

    How it will be judged

    Leave-one-out over the 10 worms (Methods S1 Figure 6.4 legend), head and tail apart. For each held-out worm, the atlas is fit on the other 9 (Algorithm 4) with 6-D features (position in um and the three landmark colors); the held-out worm is identified by Algorithm 5 and the Hungarian assignment (equation 4.18), and a neuron is correct when its assigned identity is its annotation. Open details, each run. Which neurons are identified, (a) the hand-annotated neurons, (b) the automatic detections of claim ms1-detection-accuracy matched to annotations within 3 um, unmatched annotated neurons counted wrong. Start of the alignment, Algorithm 5 initializes it at the identity on the positions as given (the Methods, main result); the CELL_ID code (AutoId.global_alignment) first aligns the worm's major axis and searches rotations about it (theta from 0 to 2 pi in steps of 0.25, both orientations, 10 alternations, color regression with ridge 1e5), shown as a variant. Colors, the dandiset's stored per-neuron colors if it holds them, and the Methods S1 Figure 6.2 readout recomputed from the images if it holds images. These are method details; the outcome is the one they give together, and differing outcomes block the claim as methods_underspecified. The reported numbers can be read as (i) the mean over held-out worms of per-worm accuracy or (ii) the accuracy over all neurons pooled, and as (1) per-neuron identity or (2) identity at class level for left/right pairs (Methods S1 footnote 1 scores midline pairs such as RMFL/R by class but does not list them; class level for every left/right pair is the upper bound of that rule). The number is matched when it falls within tolerance under any of these readings.

  • fig7b-eight-labels-above-90

    Manually annotating eight neurons, chosen by the software as the least certain, raises head identification accuracy above 90% on average.

    Adding eight manual annotations, on average, brings the head accuracy above 90%

    Results, Semi-automated Neuronal Identification, paragraph 2; Figure 7B; Methods S1 section 6.5, Figure 6.9

    How it will be judged

    In the leave-one-out runs of fig7bc-identification-accuracy (head), the neuron whose row of the Sinkhorn probability matrix is closest to uniform is annotated with its true identity, the likelihoods are updated as CELL_ID does (AutoId.update_log_likelihood sets the annotated neuron's row to the minimum except its true identity at the maximum) and the assignment recomputed (AutoId.update_id, without re-alignment); repeated eight times. "Closest to uniform" is open, read as (a) the highest entropy of the row and (b) the lowest top probability (the code's ranking, AutoId.add_to_image); a method detail, both run. The accuracy after eight annotations can be read as over all neurons with the annotated ones counted correct, or over the unannotated neurons only; matched under either. Reproduced when the mean over held-out worms exceeds 90%.

  • ms1-detection-accuracy

    The automatic neuron detection (matching pursuit) reaches 80% accuracy in the head and 88% in the tail, at 191 and 44 detections, with precision and recall both about 90% in the head and 93% in the tail.

    As shown in figures 6.3 our algorithm reaches the accuracy of 80% in head and 88% in tail. This occurs when we find K = 191 and K = 44 neurons in head and tail in which case we get similar precision and recall values that are both equal to 90% and 93% in head and tail respectively.

    Methods S1, section 6.2, Figure 6.3

    How it will be judged

    Needs the images. Detection as Methods S1 sections 2 and 3 describe and CELL_ID implements it (Preprocess.zscore_frame, Preprocess.filter_gut_lysosomes, AutoDetect.detect, with a 1.5 um exclusion radius), run for K = 191 (head) and K = 44 (tail) on each of the 10 worms. Detections are matched one to one to the hand annotations by the Hungarian algorithm on distances, with distances over 3 um set to infinity (section 6.2); accuracy is TP / (TP + FP + FN), precision TP / (TP + FP), recall TP / (TP + FN). The values are the means over the 10 worms (Figure 6.3 plots means and standard deviations). The detection bounds that the GUI takes from its preferences (minimum eigenvalue of the Gaussian shape and the size thresholds of the gut and lysosome filter) are not given in the paper; the CELL_ID defaults at d9f5df9 are used and named as a deviation. Blocked if the dandiset holds no images.

  • ms1-calibration

    The identification's confidence is calibrated; per-neuron confidence and accuracy largely agree in head and tail.

    Figure 6.8 shows that confidence and accuracy ... are largely in good agreement, as desired

    Methods S1, section 6.4, Figure 6.8

    How it will be judged

    From the leave-one-out runs of fig7bc-identification-accuracy, per atlas neuron, the mean over held-out worms of the Sinkhorn probability of the assigned identity (confidence) and the fraction of worms in which it is correct (accuracy), with left/right midline pairs scored by class as in footnote 1 of Methods S1 section 6.4. The paper gives no measure of agreement and no threshold; "largely in good agreement" is open. Two readings, each run, (a) a positive Spearman correlation between per-neuron confidence and accuracy with p < 0.05 and (b) a mean absolute difference between confidence and accuracy under 10 percentage points (a standard choice, not from the paper). Reproduced in head and tail when both readings agree that it holds; when they disagree the claim is blocked as methods_underspecified. The reliability plot is shown.

  • ms2-neuropal-color-violations

    Using the atlas of neuron positions from the 10 worms to define neighbors, NeuroPAL's designed color map leaves 3.5% of neighboring neuron pairs below the color discrimination margin.

    NeuroPAL, which comprehensively identifies all neuron types, exhibited a theoretical color violation of 3.5%.

    Methods S2, section 4.3, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Neighbors are pairs whose positional covariance ellipses, at 95% probability mass, include each other (Methods S2 section 2; Optimal_Coloring computeProbAdjacency.m, 1000 samples per neuron, seed 1), from the head and tail atlases fit on all 10 worms. Colors are the NeuroPAL colors of Optimal_Coloring data.mat (NeuroPAL_colors), normalized to sum to one per neuron as find_optimal_colors.m does; the margin is 1/3. The Methods define the violation rate as the share of neighboring pairs whose color distance is below the margin (section 4.3); the code reports the mean shortfall below the margin over neighboring pairs (find_optimal_colors.m); the Methods measure is the main result and the code's is a variant. Whether the 3.5% pools head and tail is not said for NeuroPAL itself; the solver runs of Methods S2 section 4.2 use the 235 head and tail neurons together, and Methods S2 Figure 1A shows head and tail with one value, so pooled is the main reading; head and tail apart are reported as variants, and the number is matched under any of them. The outside inputs are the NeuroPAL colors from the code repository.
