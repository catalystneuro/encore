  • Metadata errorThe DfOverF series are not ΔF/F. In the four single-colour files each ROI trace is divided by its mean (float64, mean 1); in the four two-colour files traces are centred near 0 (float32) with the first 60 s held constant. The Fluorescence series vary by about 1e-6 per frame or not at all, consistent with baselines, and carry the same description as DfOverF.
  • Description missingThe ActivityStates table is described only as classified periods of activity. It does not follow the Methods' definition (many active rows of 5 s or less), its transient rows are undefined and duplicate active rows, it holds 3 rows in the glia-only files, and it does not say which condition each row belongs to.

Closed and open loop can be read only from the gain signal. From the signals, code 33 is a 0.3 to 0.5 s speed-up of the grating in open loop, code 52 a randomised gain, and codes 51 and 53 alternate closed and open loop in six files but both have the gain at 0 in 20170228-4.
  • Metadata errorTotalStimulusVelocitySignal is described as the drift speed minus the motosensory gain times the swim power, but its values lie between -0.002 and 0.008 in every file, against baseline velocities of 0.5 to 2. The visual feedback each bout produced is not recorded.
  • OtherIn two of the four two-colour files (20161109-2 and 20170203-1) the whole-brain glial trace has an SD of 0.0016 and 0.0011, against 0.0097 and 0.0072, correlates with the neuronal trace at 0.58 and 0.59 (0.06 and 0.15 in the others), and falls after passivity onset.   • Description missingThe ROIs are in each fish's own image space, with no registration to a reference brain and no region labels, so the paper's region-level results (L-MO, NE-MO) cannot be located.
  • OtherThe two groups of files were processed differently (trace scaling, data type, censoring, chunking), and the volume rate is 1.6 to 2.9 Hz against the 3 volumes per second of the Methods.

Reproduction Futility-induced passivity and whole-brain neuronal and glial activity in zebrafish (Mu et al. 2019)

Tests the findings of Mu, Bennett, Rubinov et al. (2019) that dandiset 000350 can test. The dandiset holds 12 light-sheet sessions of paralysed larval zebrafish swimming fictively in virtual reality, with swim bouts, stimulus signals and, in 8 of them, segmented neuronal or neuronal and glial traces. The entry measures passive and active epochs, swim intervals and vigor in closed and open loop, and whole-brain neuronal and glial activity around passivity onset. Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • 12 imaged fish, where the paper's numbers come from 74.fig1h-epoch-durations
  • Pauses are measured from the end of one bout to the start of the next; the Methods say only that the fish did not swim for 5 s.fig1h-epoch-durations
  • Open loop is read from the gain signal (gain 0, grating moving); epochs cut by the edges of an open-loop period are left out, as the protocol alternates closed and open loop in 8 fish.fig1h-epoch-durations
  • The tolerance is 1.96 times the stated SEM, not the SEM.fig1h-epoch-durations
  • The paper does not say how the interval is measured or which closed-loop intervals count; four readings are shown and one matches the closed-loop value.methods-isi
  • 12 fish, against 74.methods-isi
  • The paper's Figure 1J varies the gain in 6 dedicated fish; here closed loop is all time with gain above 0 in the imaging sessions, which includes closed-loop blocks between open-loop blocks.fig1j-open-loop-passivity
  • The dandiset's gain units cannot be mapped to the paper's, so closed loop is not restricted to a naturalistic gain.fig1j-open-loop-passivity
  • The paper shows this with example traces; the measure (power ratio of bouts) and its test were fixed here before the analysis.fig1c-vigor-open-loop-onset
  • The minimum of 10 bouts per epoch and the comparison of k = 1 with k = 7 were chosen here; the paper plots the interval against the number of swims before passivity for 61 fish.fig1i-isi-before-passivity
  • The whole-brain signal is the mean of the stored DfOverF over 8 to 15% of ROIs, in units of the stored series, which is not ΔF/F as the Methods define it.fig2bc-neurons-lower-in-passivity
  • The paper shows this in one example fish; it was tested here over 8.fig2bc-neurons-lower-in-passivity
  • Whole-brain average of glia, as in the paper's Figure 2C, not the L-MO component of Figure 2G and 2I, which needs the cross-fish clustering and template registration.fig2c-glia-rise-at-passivity
  • The glia of these fish carry jRGECO1b; the paper's component analysis used GCaMP6f.fig2c-glia-rise-at-passivity
  • Figure S2A was not read; the claim is taken from the Results text.figs2a-open-loop-higher
  • Switches are those between closed and open loop; stationary-grating periods carry the label of the condition before them.figs2a-open-loop-higher
  • Closed-loop pauses include those with the grating moving, as the dandiset gives no stimulus labels beyond the velocity signal and stationary periods are 2 to 18 s long.fig2h-glia-weaker-closed-loop-pauses
