From the paper Control of the Electroporation Efficiency of Nanosecond Pulses by Swinging the Electric Field Vector Direction · claims file · checked 2026-10-23: ready

  • fig2-180-cancellation

    At a 180 degree change of field direction (near the base electrodes), paired-pulse trains give less YP uptake than single-pulse trains, 2-fold less at 1 Hz and 20-fold less at 833 kHz.

    Maximum cancellation was expectedly observed at the 180° vector change, reducing YP uptake compared to single-pulse trains 2-fold at 1 Hz and as much as 20-fold at 833 kHz

    Results, 2.1, paragraph 3; Figure 2

    How it will be judged

    From the Figure 2 legend and Results 2.1, per ROI along the bisecting line, the paired-pulse YP value as % of the single-pulse value in the same ROI, at each frequency, under normalization readings (i) and (ii), with each ROI's angle under the angle readings of the opening comment. The fold value is 100 divided by that %. "At the 180 degree vector change" is read as (1) the ROI of largest angle (nearest the base) or (2) the ROI of lowest mean value ("maximum cancellation"); these are readings of the reported numbers and a value holds when it matches under any of them with either normalization. Reproduced when both values hold, under each background reading; blocked as methods_underspecified when background readings (a) and (b) give different outcomes.

  • fig2-crossover-angle

    Cancellation weakens as the angle is reduced, and paired and single pulses become equally effective at about 160 degrees at 1 Hz and about 130 degrees at 833 kHz.

    Cancellation weakened as the angle was reduced, and at about 160° (1 Hz) or 130° (833 kHz), the effects of paired-pulse trains became the same as those of single-pulse ones

    Results, 2.1, paragraph 3; Figure 2

    How it will be judged

    Measure as in fig2-180-cancellation. The crossover is the largest angle at which the mean paired-pulse value reaches 100%, found by linear interpolation between neighboring ROIs. The paper does not say how it was located; interpolation is a standard choice that cannot move the angle by more than one ROI spacing. The weakening of cancellation is shown by the value rising from below 100% at 180 degrees to 100% at the crossover and is not tested apart. Reproduced when both angles are within tolerance under one normalization reading at least, under each background reading; blocked as methods_underspecified when background readings (a) and (b) give different outcomes.

  • fig2-facilitation-plateau

    Below the crossover, paired pulses enhance electroporation up to about three-fold over single pulses at 90 to 100 degrees for both frequencies, followed by a plateau down to 0 degrees, so pulses at 90 degrees and at smaller angles are as efficient as co-directional pulses.

    Further reduction of the angle enhanced electroporation, up to about three-fold at 90–100° for both nsEP frequencies, followed by a plateau all the way to 0°

    Results, 2.1, paragraphs 3 and 4; Figure 2; Discussion, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Measure as in fig2-180-cancellation. The level "about three-fold at 90-100 degrees, followed by a plateau" has two readings of the reported number, (1) the mean of the ROI values between 90 and 100 degrees and (2) the mean of the ROI values from 100 degrees to the smallest angle; it holds when it matches under either, with either normalization. The paper defines neither the plateau nor where it is reached and gives no test. Readings of the plateau, both run (a choice that could change the outcome) over the ROI means from 0 to 90 degrees, (P1) a linear fit of value against angle whose slope has a 95% confidence interval that includes zero, and (P2) the mean of the ROIs below 10 degrees within 10% (the claim's tolerance) of the mean of the ROIs between 90 and 100 degrees. The angle where the plateau is reached is the largest angle at which the mean value first comes within 10% of the level, a standard choice tied to the tolerance. Reproduced when, under each background reading, the levels hold for both frequencies, that angle lies within 81 to 110 degrees, and the plateau holds at both frequencies; blocked as methods_underspecified when the plateau readings or the background readings give different outcomes.

  • fig2-frequency-dependence

    Pairing pulses enhances electroporation at small angles and inhibits it at large angles in a frequency-dependent manner, with stronger cancellation at 833 kHz than at 1 Hz.

    Note that applying pulses in pairs enhanced electroporation at small angles but inhibited them at large angles in a frequency-dependent manner

    Figure 2 legend; Results, 2.1, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    The paper reports no test, and Results 2.1 reads the frequency dependence from the 180 degree values ("Stronger cancellation at sub-MHz frequencies") and the crossover angles. Measure as in fig2-180-cancellation and fig2-crossover-angle. Reproduced when, under each background reading, the mean paired-pulse value at the 180 degree reading is lower at 833 kHz than at 1 Hz and the 833 kHz crossover angle is smaller than the 1 Hz one, with values above 100% at the smallest angles and below 100% at the largest at both frequencies, under one normalization reading at least; blocked as methods_underspecified when background readings (a) and (b) give different outcomes.

  • fig2-60-fold-co-counter

    At 833 kHz, co-directional and counter-directional pulse pairs differ about 60-fold in electroporation efficiency.

    The profound, 60-fold difference in electroporation efficiency of co- and counter-directional nsEPs at 833 kHz ( Figure 2 )

    Results, 2.3, paragraph 1; Figure 2

    How it will be judged

    Measure as in fig2-180-cancellation, at 833 kHz. The paper does not say which values form the ratio. Readings of the reported number, (a) the ROI of smallest angle over the ROI of largest angle, and (b) the plateau level of fig2-facilitation-plateau over the 180 degree value of fig2-180-cancellation (3-fold over 1/20 gives the paper's 60, so (b) is the paper's arithmetic). Reproduced when one of these, under one normalization reading, is within tolerance, under each background reading; blocked as methods_underspecified when background readings (a) and (b) give different outcomes.

  • fig3-1hz-co-equals-cross

    In the square array at 1 Hz, co-directional and cross-directional (90 degree) paired pulses have the same electroporation efficiency at the array center.

    Consistently with the measurements in Figure 2 , co- and cross-directional pulses at 1 Hz had the same electroporation efficiency ( Figure 3 )

    Results, 2.2, paragraph 1; Figure 3

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. Per dish, the mean YP intensity in the single 200 x 200 um ROI at the array center (Methods 4.4), after 4 paired pulses at 10 kV/cm (Figure 3 legend), under background readings (a) and (b). The paper's test, from the Figure 3 legend, a two-tailed Student's t-test (unpaired, equal variance) of co- against cross-directional dishes at 1 Hz, n = 6 each, at p < 0.05. Reproduced when p >= 0.05, not reproduced when p < 0.05; the ratio of means and p are reported either way. The outcome is taken under background reading (b), the correction Methods 4.4 names; reading (a) and Welch's test are variants.

  • fig3-770khz-co-enhanced

  • fig4-unipolar-pattern

    With single-pulse trains on one base electrode, YP uptake follows the electric field map; with paired pulses on both base electrodes at 833 kHz, uptake is strong near the grounded apex electrode and weak or absent near the base electrodes despite the strong field there.

    In control cell monolayers exposed to a train of five single pulses applied to one of the base electrodes (833 kHz, 4 kV), the distribution of YP fluorescence ( Figure 4 A) simply followed the electric field strength map ... produced a strong effect near the apex electrode (ground) but weak or no effect near the base electrodes, despite the strong electric field there

    Results, 2.3, paragraph 1; Figure 4A to 4C

    How it will be judged

    The paper reports no test. From Results 2.3 and Methods 4.4, the mean YP intensity in 200 x 200 um ROIs adjacent to the apex electrode and to each base electrode, under each background reading. Reproduced when, in the paired-pulse sample, uptake next to the apex exceeds uptake next to each base electrode, and in the single-pulse sample uptake next to the energized base electrode exceeds uptake next to the apex. "Near" is not defined; the readings are ROIs touching the edge of the electrode footprint (1.5 mm outer diameter, Methods 4.2) and ROIs 200 um farther out, both run, and the outcome is the one all readings give, else blocked as methods_underspecified. The match to the field map is reported as the Spearman correlation of ROI uptake with field strength (field from the angle-reading models of the opening comment), with no threshold, as the paper gives none.
