  • fig4abc-tuning-correlation-single-electrode

    In the three single-electrode datasets (monkeys A, B, J) the correlation across conditions between a neuron's preparatory and peri-movement firing rates is positive on average but small (mean r 0.21, 0.19 and 0.06; mean Spearman r 0.20, 0.20 and 0.06).

    Across the four datasets the mean correlation was positive (p=0.01, p=0.01, p=0.12, p=0.03) but only very modestly so (mean r = 0.21, 0.19, 0.06 and 0.13). Non-parametric correlations were similarly modest (mean Spearman's r = 0.20, 0.20, 0.06 and 0.11)

    Results, Correlations between preparatory and peri-movement tuning, paragraph 1; Figure 4A-C

    How it will be judged

    Per neuron, the Pearson and Spearman correlations between the vector of preparatory epoch rates and the vector of peri-movement epoch rates, one entry per condition (Methods, Correlations), over neurons passing both the preparatory criterion and the 1.5 times peri-movement criterion (shared methods above). For monkey J each neuron uses its own 27-condition set. Each dataset is judged on its own; it is reproduced when its mean correlations are within tolerance. The test of the mean against zero is not named; readings are (a) a two-sided one-sample t-test on r, (b) the same on Fisher z, (c) a Wilcoxon signed-rank test; the p-values are reported for each.

    Results, Correlations between preparatory and peri-movement tuning, paragraph 1; Figure 4D

  • fig4e-correlation-time-course

    The correlation of preparatory tuning with peri-movement tuning measured at single time points starts to fall about 200 ms before movement onset and is near zero by movement onset.

    The correlation with preparatory activity began to decline ∼200 ms prior to movement onset, and reached values near zero by movement onset ( Figure 4E )

    Results, Correlations between preparatory and peri-movement tuning, paragraph 2; Figure 4E

    How it will be judged

    Per neuron (same selection as Figure 4A-D), the preparatory epoch rates correlated with the rates at each single time point of the trace smoothed with a 20 ms Gaussian (Figure 4 legend), the trace spliced from the target-, go- and movement-locked averages (Supplemental Experimental Procedures, Computing the mean firing rate versus time); the correlation averaged over neurons. The paper gives no numbers for "began to decline" or "near zero", so these thresholds are fixed here, not taken from the paper. Near zero is set from the dataset means of Figure 4 (0.06 to 0.21). Reproduced when the mean correlation at movement onset is below 0.1 in absolute value and below half of its value 250 ms before movement onset, and the mean correlation 300 ms before movement onset is at least three quarters of its value 400 ms before movement onset (no marked decline before about 200 ms).

  • results-ap-location-no-effect

    The tuning correlation of Figure 4 does not depend on anterior-posterior recording location in any dataset (r = -0.05, -0.03, -0.07 and 0.20; p = 0.82, 0.85, 0.52 and 0.27).

    Regressing tuning correlation (as in Figure 4 ) against AP location yielded little or no effect: r =-0.05, p =0.82; r =-0.03, p =0.85; r =-0.07, p =0.52; and r =0.20, p =0.27 for the four datasets

    Results, Comparison of anterior versus posterior sites, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. Per dataset, the per-neuron Pearson correlations of Figure 4 regressed on each neuron's AP location by least squares, with the p-value of the slope (that of r). The paper names no significance threshold; 0.05 is the conventional level and is an assumption. Reproduced for a dataset when p is 0.05 or above, not reproduced when it is below; r is reported either way. How AP location is assigned to array units is not stated; readings are (a) the AP coordinate of the unit's electrode on its array, (b) array membership (PMd array anterior to M1 array).

  • fig6-each-dimensionality

    At each dimensionality taken alone the peri-movement space almost always performs best; the exception is the speed task at three dimensions, where two task spaces perform very well, while endpoints + maximum speed does not perform well for the maze task.

    Even if we consider each dimensionality individually, performance was almost always higher for the peri-movement space. An exception was that, among 3-dimensional spaces, the ‘targs+instr. spd.’ and ‘endpoints + max. spd.’ performed very well for the datasets collected using the speed task. The former is undefined for the maze task, but the latter did not perform well.

    Results, PD performance in a variety of spaces, paragraph 2; Figure 6A-D

    How it will be judged

    For each space other than peri-movement and each of its dimensionalities k (each k for PCA spaces, the fixed k of a task space), compare mean performance with that of the peri-movement space at the same k. For maze data, reproduced when that holds and the peri-movement space beats endpoints + maximum speed at k = 3. The share of comparisons won is reported.

  • fig7a-shuffled-space-poor

    The PCA-based ‘shuffled-space’ performed poorly ( Figure 7A )

    Results, Controls, paragraph 1; Figure 7A and legend

    How it will be judged

    As in the Figure 7A legend, for each neuron and condition, with probability 0.5 the peri-movement response is kept, otherwise activity up to 150 ms before movement onset is kept and all later activity is reflected vertically about the rate at that time; the PCA space is then built as for the peri-movement space. Figure 7A averages over all datasets; here each dataset is judged on its own. The draw is repeated (a standard choice, 20 draws) and averaged. "Poorly" is not quantified; fixed here as the shuffled space's mean performance below the peri-movement space's at every k and its best over k below the best of the kinematic space.

  • fig7d-pd-consistency

  • fig8-oscillator-model

    In a simulated two-unit linear oscillator held at 25 initial states and then released, preparatory and peri-movement activity of a unit are nearly uncorrelated (r = 0.08), yet a PD in the two-dimensional peri-movement space captures the preparatory tuning perfectly.

    The correlation of preparatory activity with peri-movement activity (averaged over the gray interval) was 0.08. ... The PD points towards the pattern recruited by the activity of unit one (panel A ), and perfectly captures preparatory ‘tuning’.

    Results, Tuning from a dynamical systems perspective, paragraphs 1 and 2; Figure 8 and legend

    How it will be judged

    A simulation with no recorded data, from the Figure 8 legend, x(t+1) = W x(t) with W = [1 -2pi/360; 2pi/360 1], 25 random initial states held and then released. Open, the distribution of initial states ((a) uniform in a square centred on zero, (b) a standard 2-D Gaussian) and the gray interval ((a) one full cycle, 360 steps, (b) half a cycle, 180 steps). The value 0.08 comes from one random draw, so a 10% tolerance cannot be met by design; the claim's number is judged as holding when 0.08 lies within the central 95% of r over 1000 draws under one reading, with the median r reported. The PD part holds when the leave-one-out performance of a PD in the 2-D PCA space of the released activity is at least 0.99.
