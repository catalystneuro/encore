  • figs1f-angle-awake-vs-anesthetized

    Wall-induced change in whisker midpoint angle does not differ significantly between awake and anesthetized mice for the C1, C2 and Greek whiskers (paired t-tests across 4 mice, Bonferroni alpha 0.017); the mean awake minus anesthetized difference is 2.28 degrees.

    Awake mice showed similar bending angle (mean difference: 2.28°) and curvature (mean difference: 1.66 × 10 −5 mm −1 ) changes, with no significant differences from the anesthetized condition across whiskers

    Results, A wall-passing paradigm, paragraph 2; Figure S1F legend

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence, judged on the paper's test (Methods, Data Analysis: Wall-Induced Whisker Retraction; FigS1_Stats.m). Per pass, the midpoint angle (Savitzky-Golay 21 frames, order 3, mean of the upper and lower envelopes) is averaged over 3 to 11 s minus its mean over -4.5 to 0 s; per mouse, whisker and condition, averaged over distances and passes; for each whisker a two-tailed paired t-test across the 4 mice, significant at 0.05/3. Reproduced when no whisker reaches p < 0.0167; not reproduced when any does. The mean difference is reported for each whisker; the paper does not say whether 2.28 degrees is pooled over whiskers or for one, so the readings are the mean over the three whiskers and each whisker alone. The code (FigS1_Code.m) takes the midpoint as a 101-frame Savitzky-Golay smoothing of the twice 21-frame-smoothed angle, with the envelope method commented out; this is the variant, shown, and the Methods' envelope midpoint is the main.
