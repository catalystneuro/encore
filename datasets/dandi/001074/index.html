  • fig1c-cells-per-gel

    Imaging a MORSE hydrogel through the GRIN lens shows 100 to 200 green sensor cells spread through the full thickness of the gel.

    3D two-photon imaging of the resultant hydrogel through the GRIN lens (30 planes/volume, ~600×600×270 μm 3 volume) demonstrated a uniformly distributed set of 100–200 green cells spanning the full thickness of the gel

    Results, MORSE probe development and characterization, paragraph 1; Figure 1C

    How it will be judged

    Neither the Methods nor the code say how the cells were counted. Readings (a) the number of cell masks extracted per recording before curation (CNMF components); (b) the number of curated cell masks per recording. Reproduced when the per-probe counts of a reading lie in 90 to 220 for most probes (more than half) and the cells occupy all depths of the gel (cell centres found in the top, middle and bottom thirds of the gel thickness). Needs masks or imaging.

  • fig1d-collagen-reduces-warping

    Hydrogels of pure Matrigel deform substantially over tens of minutes; a mixture of Matrigel and collagen I deforms less.

    The small hydrogel volume (~40 nL) on the GRIN lens tip, when composed of pure Matrigel, exhibited substantial global and local deformations over tens of minutes. We minimized these deformations using an optimized mixture of Matrigel and collagen I

    Results, MORSE probe development and characterization, paragraph 2; Figure 1D

    How it will be judged

    Deformation is the local strain magnitude, the mean over the hydrogel volume of the magnitude of the displacement field minus its mean (Figure 1D legend, Figure S1C; morse_figs1 cells 3, 92, 97), from demons registration of Cellpose probability maps to a reference volume. With n=6 probes per recipe imaged for 9 h, reproduced when the mean deformation across probes of the 50:50 recipe stays below that of pure Matrigel over the recording (mean over the 9 h lower, and lower at the end). The paper gives no test. Needs the deformation fields or raw imaging.

  • fig1f-koh-stability

    Treating the silicone surface with KOH before applying the gel strongly reduces gel warping and detachment during dipping; with the optimized protocol (40:60 Matrigel:collagen I with 2.5% KOH, against 50:50 without KOH) probe stability rose from about 10% to about 65% in initial tests.

    This allowed us to increase probe stability from ~10% to ~65% in initial tests ( Figure 1F )

    Methods, Optimization of MORSE manufacture, paragraph 1; Figure 1F; Figure S1D-E; Results, MORSE probe development and characterization, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    A probe is early warp when the 3D translation of the hydrogel (the norm of the mean displacement over the gel) exceeds 25 um in the first hour, late warp when it exceeds 25 um in the second hour, and stable otherwise (Figure 1F legend). Stable fraction over the 9 probes of 50:50 without KOH and the 24 of 40:60 with KOH. Variant (code, morse_figs1 cell 139), late warp as more than 50 um within 2 h. The code's comments (cell 126) give "~66% success" for the KOH group. The two groups differ in both recipe and KOH (Figure 1F legend), so the comparison does not separate the two. Of 9 probes, "~10%" can only be 1 (11%), recorded as a count. Needs the deformation fields or raw imaging.
