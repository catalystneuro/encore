  • fig3-cell-type-sorting

    Units are sorted into fast spiking units (FSU) and regular spiking units (RSU) by k-means clustering of the initial phase and after-hyperpolarization phase durations of their action potentials; in one animal's recordings this gave 28 FSU and 32 RSU, 60 in total.

    K -means clustering of detected neurons from one animal's recordings, showing 28 fast spiking units (FSU) and 32 regular spiking units (RSU), for 60 total.

    Figure 3B and 3C; Methods 2.5

    How it will be judged

    The header's cell type method. The paper does not name the animal, nor whether its "recordings" are one session or all of its sessions; the readings are R1, the units of one session, and R2, the units of all of one animal's sessions in one series. Reproduced when, under one reading, some animal gives all three counts within tolerance and the two clusters separate on both features (the FSU cluster has the shorter median initial phase and after-hyperpolarization phase). Partially reproduced when the clusters separate as stated but no animal matches the counts. When the dataset carries the paper's labels (K0), the agreement of K1 and K2 with them is reported.

  • fig4-rsu-time-locked-both-states

    RSUs show time-locked responses to tFUS, an increase in firing rate during the 67 ms of stimulation, in both awake and anesthetized recordings.

    We observed time-locked responses from RSU in both awake and anesthetized recordings, with an observable increase in firing rate during tFUS stimulation

    Results 3.1, paragraph 1; Figure 4C and 4E

    How it will be judged

    Figure 4 shows single examples at 1500 Hz PRF, 30% DC and reports no group test. The paper's group test of a time-locked response is the comparison with sham (Results 3.2 and 3.3), so the claim is judged on it. RSU time-locked responses (0 to 67 ms) of the 1500 Hz, 200 us sessions against the sham sessions of the same state, two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum, p < 0.05, with the 1500 Hz mean above the sham mean and above 100%. Reproduced when this holds in both states under every reading; partially reproduced when in one state only. The units whose PSTH rises in 0 to 67 ms are counted per state.

  • fig4hi-fsu-delayed-latency

    FSU delayed responses to tFUS at 1500 Hz PRF begin later in awake rats (mean onset 0.51 s, spread 0.14 s) than under anesthesia (0.24 s, spread 0.08 s), and the onset distributions differ significantly by a Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

    In the anesthetized model, these delayed responses had an average onset latency of 0.24 s ± 0.08 s, while in the awake model they had an average onset latency of 0.51 s ± 0.14 s ... Comparing the distributions using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, we found that delayed response onset times varied significantly between awake and anesthetized models

    Results 3.1, paragraph 1; Figure 4H and 4I

    How it will be judged

    The header's latency method (readings S1/S2, D1/D2, L1/L2, Q1/Q2, T1/T2), FSU only. The paper does not say whether the spread is an SD or an SEM; it is used as the tolerance. Reproduced when, under every reading, both means are within tolerance and a two-sided rank-sum test of awake against anesthetized latencies gives p < 0.001 with the awake values later. Partially reproduced when the test holds with the awake values later but a mean is outside tolerance. Not reproduced when the test fails or the direction is reversed.

    Examining the time-locked response from RSU in the awake model using Kruskal–Wallis ANOVA, we find that PRF is a significant factor ...     How it will be judged

Part (2) includes 300 Hz because 300 Hz is one of "all higher PRFs" for 30 Hz, and Results 3.2 says the only difference under anesthesia is that 30 Hz and 300 Hz do not differ. Reproduced when all six hold under every reading; partially reproduced when (1) and at least four others hold; not reproduced otherwise.
