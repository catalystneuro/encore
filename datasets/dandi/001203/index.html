Problems that change results 2

The data are wrong in a way the files do not show, so an analysis that takes them as given comes out wrong. Each was checked against the notebook’s outputs by someone other than the attempt’s author.

  • OtherChanges resultsIn the Open Ephys files the SLEAP poses are not on the clock of the spikes and the trial table, and nothing in the files says so. The nose position at the trial table's port entries clusters (spread 7 to 15 px) at a lag of -9.8 to +6.6 s in four of the six 2018 sessions (the shock-evoked speed peak agrees within 0.6 s in three of them), less tightly in the other two (best spread 39 and 60 px), and at no lag in the six 2019 sessions (spread at best 81 to 117 px), whose poses are at 15 frames/s and span 150 to 190 s less than the recording. Fitted per quarter of the session, the lag is constant within 0.8 s in the four 2018 sessions and changes by 22 to 529 s in the other eight. In the imaging files the same check finds lags of 0.2 to 4 s (spread 2.5 to 14 px) in 32 of 34 files. An analysis that takes the pose timestamps as given places freezing and darting in the wrong trials and against the wrong spikes.
    Checked: Shown. Cell 5 shows spikes and trial events share a clock. Cell 7 shows the nose clustering at port entries at nonzero lags, with the shock-speed check agreeing within 0.6 s in 3012, 3016 and 3024, and no clustering in the 2019 sessions. Cell 8 shows the drift; cell 9 is the imaging control. Critical is right: these are signals on different clocks, and the files don't say so. The 'up to 200 s' and 'five of six' wording should be corrected.
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
  • OtherChanges resultsTwo recordings are deposited twice. sub-6197 has two Open Ephys files with identical spikes and events. sub-J933 has two imaging files with identical traces, and in the one named sub-J933-Disc4_ses-J933-Disc4 the port entries are 55 in place of 193 and sit 236 s off the video's clock.
    Checked: Shown. Cell 4: the two 6197 files have identical spike trains and CS onsets. Cell 9: the two J933 files have identical traces; the _ses- copy has 55 port entries against 193, with best lag -236.4 s and spread 27.0 px, against 1.6 s and 2.8 px for the other copy. Critical is right: one recording deposited as two, plus port entries that are silently wrong. The finding's last sentence, 'The duplication shows in the shared subject names', should be dropped. It is inaccurate, since many subjects legitimately have several files (J895, K008 and others, cell 3), and it contradicts the critical rating.
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)

Other findings 9

  • Data missingThe 13 Neuropixels files (5 paired and 4 unpaired ASt mice, 4 CeA mice) hold spikes but no trial events and no behavior; the Methods say events were recorded by TTL at 10 kHz. No cue response can be computed for them, so all unpaired ephys mice, all TS neurons and most CeA neurons of the paper cannot be analysed.
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Description missingNeuropixels units have no region or electrode column, and every electrode of a probe carries one label (ASt or CeA) although the probes span 3.8 mm. The units do carry probe_depth (micrometers from the tip) and a good or mua label, and the files hold histology images of the probe track, but nothing maps depth to region. The paper's TS units cannot be found, and the files give 1515 paired ASt units, 885 CeA and 633 unpaired against the paper's 193, 145 and 71 (574, 155 and 410 Neuropixels units labeled good, with 112 and 22 more from Open Ephys).
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Selection not documentedThe imaging files hold every CNMF-E ROI (2036 in paired and 1233 in unpaired discrimination sessions) with no column marking the manually curated neurons the paper analysed (1110 in total), so counts are 2.4 to 4.0 times the paper's and results on the files can differ from the paper's (three claims of no difference fail here).
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Data missingThe paper says raw and processed data are at this dandiset, but it holds no RNAscope data, no optogenetic activation or inhibition behavior, and no unpredicted-US imaging session. Fifteen claims cannot be tested for this reason.
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Description missingThe Open Ephys files do not say whether a mouse was in the paired or unpaired group (the Neuropixels and imaging files do); they are taken as paired from the paper's mouse counts.
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Description missingPoses are in pixels with no scale; the paper's freezing and darting thresholds are in cm. The scale was inferred from the chamber size (about 20 px/cm in discrimination sessions, 7.5 to 33 in context sessions).
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
  • OtherImaging frames are 1/15 s apart in every file, where the Methods say 15 frames/s downsampled by two, and each discrimination session has 15 imaged CS-reward trials (17 to 19 in seven sessions), where the Methods say every other reward trial of 45. Imaged clips begin about 9.6 s before the cue, so the 10 s baseline is not complete.
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
  • OtherOpen Ephys spikes respond at the trial table's CS onsets and not at onsets shifted 45 s, CS-shock tones last 20 s, and CS-reward tones end 0.4 s after the first port entry (median), as the Methods describe. Imaging mouse counts per genotype and group match the paper's.
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
  • OtherIn the imaging poses the largest frame-to-frame displacement over the 14 body points is below 0.5 px in 19.6% of the discrimination-session frames and above 1.6 px in the rest, with none in between, and the same holds in the context sessions. These frames repeat the previous pose almost exactly. The paper's freezing rule (all points below 0.08 cm per frame, about 1.6 px) counts only them, whatever the pixel scale, and the files do not say whether they are still frames or repeated video frames. Freezing so measured does not differ between the cues in paired mice; the defensive difference comes from darting.
    Found by the attempt on Mills et al. 2026 (mills-2026-01, 2026-11-20)
