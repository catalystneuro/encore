    Trial-averaged dopamine at site c8ds, aligned to the value cue, increases more for large than for small reward trials, especially between the value cue display and reward.

    Trial averaged Δ[DA] aligned to V at 0 s for large and small reward trials, showing increased dopamine release for large in comparison to small reward trials, especially between the value cue display and reward (RW) event.

    Figure 8B and its legend

    From V to reward, dopamine referenced at V averages 1.88 in large and 1.10 in small reward trials (difference 0.78 in the file's unstated unit, Cohen's d 0.19, Mann-Whitney p = 0.0035, 429 and 460 trials, 23 and 39 dropped for lack of a reference at V). Shuffling the labels within blocks of 50 consecutive trials, which keeps the drift across the session in the null, gives p = 0.0045. The difference is small in the first 2 s after V (p = 0.44) and largest from reward to 3 s after (difference 1.59, p = 0.0001, within blocks p = 0.0010); before V there is none (p = 0.50). It holds in the second half of the session (p = 0.0125) and not significantly in the first (p = 0.075). Without trial 1411, p = 0.0031.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The PCA dopamine estimate at V is subtracted, in place of subtracting the background voltammogram at V before the PCA; the two agree if the PCA model is linear, which the dandiset does not let us check.
    • The window (V to each trial's reward) and the test were chosen here; the paper states none.
    • Trial 1411, which the paper's large reward list leaves out, is included (one trial of 452); without it p = 0.0031.
    • Where dopamine is NaN at V, the reference is interpolated between the nearest valid samples if both lie within 0.2 s; none of the 62 such trials has them, so all are dropped.

    With the spike times moved to the trial clock by the offset measured in the data check (11812.11 s), all successful trials have a spike within V - 1 s to V + 3 s, and the variance across 100 ms bins of the PSTH is 0.49 against a 95th percentile of 0.06 over random alignments (p = 0.005 with 200); 0.48 with the 7 ms lag also corrected.     The spikes at site c5d have a consistent waveform (mean and standard deviation) and an interspike interval distribution (1 ms bins) consistent with a single unit.

    Average spike waveform + /− standard deviation. C. Interspike interval (ISI) histogram showing the distribution of the timing between spikes detected. Bin widths are 1 ms.

    Figure 9B and 9C and their legends

    The criterion holds, but the evidence for a single unit is weak. No ISI is under 1 ms (9 of 101732 under 2 ms), and the mean waveform's trough of -56.4 µV is 4.79 times the SD across spikes at that sample. Neither part could easily fail. The shortest ISI, 1.562 ms, just exceeds the 48-sample (1.50 ms) waveform, so the detector's dead time excludes ISIs under 1 ms, and every one of the 101733 spikes exceeds the 40 µV threshold (smallest peak 40.3 µV), which nearly forces the trough above twice the SD. Above the dead time, ISIs per ms at 1.56 to 3 ms are 0.106 times those at 10 to 30 ms (Plexon unit 0.204), which fits a refractory period in most spikes but does not rule out other units. Only 54411 of the Plexon unit's 90912 spikes lie within 0.2 ms of a spike of thresholded unit 1 (101733 spikes). The Plexon unit has 1 ISI under 1 ms, 14 (0.015%) under 1.56 ms and 39 (0.04%) under 2 ms.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper states no measure for these panels; the 1% ISI rule and the trough-to-SD ratio are common choices made here.
    • The waveforms are read as microvolts, although the file states volts.

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • File format errorThe raw ephys and the spike times are not on the clock of the trials, events, LFP and FSCV table. The raw ephys is stored with a starting time of 0 s and a rate, and the spike times count from its first sample. Locating the raw c7b minus c5c signal in the stored LFP puts raw time 0 at 11812.11 s on the trial clock (correlation 0.98 to 1.00 at eight points, drift about 4 ms per hour). Without this correction spikes cannot be aligned to the task. The dandiset description says all data are expressed relative to the start of the Neuralynx recording, which the data contradict.
  • Metadata errorThe absolute times disagree. On the trial clock the raw samples run from 12:06:44 to 17:12:20, while the Neuralynx header gives the recording as opened at 08:49:52 (the session start) and closed at 15:12:24, two hours before the raw samples end. The header span (22952 s) is also longer than the raw samples (18336 s).
  • OtherThe stored spike times of the thresholded units are about 7.1 ms after the spike troughs in raw c5d (in one 300 s segment of 1633 spikes, per-spike trough lags of -7.4 to -6.9 ms from the 10th to the 90th percentile, 98.8% within 0.5 ms of -7.1 ms).
  • Metadata errorThe series "bipolar_lfp_series" is described as beta-band LFP but is broadband with line noise removed. Its values are already in volts, while it carries the raw int16 conversion factor (3.05e-8), which would scale them about 3e7 times too small.
  • Metadata errorThe thresholded spike waveforms are stated in volts but hold microvolts (mean trough about -56), and the raw FSCV current is stated in amperes but holds nanoamperes (values around 1000).
  • Description missingThe columns of the trial-aligned FSCV table (da, ph, m, iox) state no units, and the `good` column is true for every trial.
  • Metadata errorThe subject description ("Two adult female rhesus monkeys were used for in vivo validation of the s-µIP device"), the session and experiment descriptions and the keyword "micro-invasive neural implant" describe the lab's micro-invasive probe study, not this paper's chamber.
  • File format errorThe event table stores each event once per overlapping 60 s trial window, about five copies of each (7221 stored trial-start events for 1412 distinct ones), and its event times are not sorted. The Plexon units table's spike times are not sorted either.
  • Description missingThe trial tags are all "initial cue display" and carry no trial type. Reward size and outcome can be rebuilt from the events, and they then match the paper's Zenodo trial lists except trial 1411, which the events mark as a successful large reward trial and the list leaves out.
  • Description missingThe paper says the reward size depends on whether the target is at the top or bottom of the screen. The events code left and right conditions, and both reward sizes occur on both sides, so the target position that sets the reward is not in the dandiset.
  • OtherThe raw FSCV minute files are joined without their gaps (328 min of scans against 302 min of trials, placed from a single starting time), and the trial-aligned oxidation current cannot be located in them, so the raw FSCV's clock could not be established.
  • Data missingOnly one session of monkey T is deposited. The data behind the electrode yields, cultures, drainage volumes and bone growth, and all of monkey J, are not, so eight of the thirteen claims cannot be tested.

Reproduction Dopamine, spikes and LFP through an aseptic cranial chamber in a rhesus monkey (Choi et al. 2025)

Tests the recording findings of Choi et al. (2025) on dandiset 002014, which holds one session of monkey T recorded through the paper's semi-sealed chamber. It compares dopamine and beta-band LFP power between large and small reward trials, aligns spikes to the value cue, and checks the unit's waveform and interspike intervals. The surgical, culture, drainage and yield results, and monkey J, are not in the dandiset. All files
