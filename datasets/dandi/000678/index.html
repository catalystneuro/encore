  • fig2c-dwell-times

    Epochs of the optimal state lasted longer than disengaged and sub-optimal epochs (about 37 against about 15 trials), and the median transition between discrete states took 3.4 +/- 0.3 trials.

    Additionally, epochs of the optimal state lasted longer than those of sub-optimal states ... and the median transition time between discrete states (dwell time in indeterminate states) was 3.4 ± 0.3 trials

    Results, Mice switch between several performance states, paragraph 6; Discussion, paragraph beginning "Here, we used recently implemented GLM-HMMs"; Figure 2C

    How it will be judged

    Epochs are runs of consecutive trials in one discrete state within a session (flex_hmm.get_dwell_times, called as plot_dwell_times in Figure2.py). That function records a dwell one trial longer than the run for every epoch but a session's last, and one trial shorter for the last; the dwell is read both as (x) the run length and (y) the code's count. The indeterminate dwell is the number of trials from the end of one discrete epoch (or the session start) to the start of the next, zero for a direct transition (Figure2.py, first trial minus the previous epoch's last_trial); a disengaged epoch of more than 40 trials that ends a session is left out (Figure2.py). Per mouse, the median dwell of each state type (the first length at which its cumulative distribution exceeds 0.499, as Figure2.py). The 3.4 is the mean over mice of the median indeterminate dwell. The Discussion's 37 and about 15 can be read as (a) the mean over mice of the per-mouse median dwell, (b) the mean over mice of the per-mouse mean dwell, (c) the mean over all epochs pooled; for the 15, disengaged and sub-optimal are each compared. Reproduced when optimal dwell exceeds disengaged and sub-optimal dwell by two-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank tests at p < 0.05, the 3.4 is within tolerance, and the 37 and 15 are within tolerance under one reading of (a) to (c) with (x) or (y).
