Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • OtherThe units' `brain_region` gives A 559, hippocampus 846 (AH 418, MH 16, PH 412), EC 493, PHC 293 and PIC 68 units, and 27 in FF, LG and PRC, where the paper reports A 580, H 794, EC 440, PHC 373 and PIC 99 for the same 2286 units. Neither the dandiset nor the paper maps one assignment to the other, so the paper's region analyses cannot be repeated on the paper's regions.
  • File format errorSpike times in the units table are in milliseconds (its description says so), where NWB requires seconds; the watchlogs' recording times are also in milliseconds.
  • Metadata errorThe start and stop columns of `annotations_patient_aligned` are described as seconds but hold milliseconds of the recording clock, as the table's own description says.
  • Description missingThe face labels (summer-faces, tom-faces and others) hold only 0 and 1. The Methods define face labels with frames excluded when a face is neither shown nor clearly absent; those frames cannot be identified.
  • OtherPublished version 0.260519.1133 holds 57 files, the 29 of the later version and 28 older copies (`ses-1_behavior+ecephys`) with the same units and spike times, one per patient except patient 3. Version 0.260702.0824 holds each recording once.
  • Published code disagreesThe dataset's code identifies responsive units with the ZETA test (zetapy; alpha 0.01 in its notebook), where the Methods describe a bin-wise Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Simes correction. With ZETA, 79 units respond to camera cuts at alpha 0.001 (Table S1 1025), 11 to Summer (46) and 13 to Tom (216), and 11.95% of PHC units to camera cuts (51.74%).
  • Methods not availableThe responsive-unit test as the Methods describe it (spike counts in 19 bins of 100 ms against the baseline count per 100 ms, signed-rank across pseudo-trials, Simes) counts 68% of units responsive at random event times on this data with 884 events, and 10 to 55% with as many as each label's onsets, so the procedure behind Figure 2 and Table S1 cannot be recovered from the text. The ZETA test of the dataset's code does not give the paper's counts either. The paper's LSTM code and its per-label hyperparameters are not published.
  • Paper inconsistentThe threshold for responsive units is alpha 0.01 in the Methods and 0.001 in the Results, the Figure 2 legend and Table S1; the cluster test section splits units at p 0.01.
  • Paper inconsistentThe Results give the 29 patients' ages as 22 to 63; the Methods give 19 to 62 for all 46 patients recorded. The dandiset's 29 patients are 19 to 62.
  • OtherThe dandiset has 958 camera cuts in the 83-minute film, one every 5.25 s; the supplement says one every 3 s.
  • Published code disagreesThe training code of the dataset's repository (ML_framework/nwb_loading/nwb_loading.py, get_unit_ids_for_patient_nwb) compares `brain_region` with the region group name in place of its subregions; no unit is labelled `H`, so asking for the hippocampus selects no unit.
  • Identifier missingNeither the dandiset nor the paper states that subject ids are the paper's patient numbers. Taking them as such, the patients the paper names in Figure 4 are among the best here (7 of 8 above kappa 0.2).

Reproduction Single units of the human medial temporal lobe during a full-length movie (Gerken, Darcher et al. 2025)

Tests the findings of Gerken, Darcher et al. (eLife reviewed preprint 106758, 2025) on dandiset 001616 (SUMMER), which holds 2286 units from 29 patients watching "500 Days of Summer" with 53 frame-wise annotations. It counts units responsive to the onsets of movie features, runs the cluster permutation test by region, and decodes characters, location and cuts with the paper's logistic regression, pooled, per patient, per region and on subsets of units. Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • The responsive-unit test follows the Methods as fixed in the claims file (baseline count over -1000 to 0 ms divided by 10, 19 overlapping 100 ms bins, two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank, Simes); the Methods do not say more.fig2b-phc-camera-cuts tables1-camera-cuts-regions fig2b-phc-scene-cuts tables1-few-semantic-responses tables1-no-indoor-response results-cluster-regions
  • Logistic regression in place of the LSTM.fig3e-temporal-gap fig5-region-decoding
  • Regions are read from the dandiset's `brain_region` and grouped as in the dataset's code; the paper gives no mapping.dataset-composition
  • The ZETA variant uses 100 resamplings in place of the code's 1000, for time; for PHC units and scene cuts, 1000 resamplings change the counts by 1 and 2 units.fig2b-phc-camera-cuts
  • The fraction is compared, as the dandiset's PHC holds 293 units against the paper's 373.fig2b-phc-camera-cuts
  • Cluster p is the fraction of 1000 label permutations whose largest cluster is at least the observed largest cluster; the Methods compare each cluster with the permuted histogram.fig2b-phc-scene-cuts
  • Onsets for labels with a duration also last at least 1 s (Results); the code does not require this, shown as a variant.tables1-few-semantic-responses
  • The threshold and its direction are searched over 50 quantiles of each unit's window counts on the validation set; the Methods say only that the best threshold was chosen on the validation set.fig3b-single-neuron-threshold
  • Class weights balanced in place of oversampling the minority class.fig3c-logreg-characters-location
  • C chosen on the validation kappa from 0.001, 0.003, 0.01 and 0.03; the Methods do not give the range.fig3c-logreg-characters-location
  • The fold layout is that of the dataset's code with the 1.6 s window added to the 32 s gap, which gives the paper's sample counts (30822, 7194, 7194 against 30800, 7200, 7200).fig3c-logreg-characters-location
  • The 0.28 for Summer is the pooled value of Figure 4; the paper does not state the Figure 3c logistic regression values in numbers.fig3c-logreg-characters-location
  • Scene cut labels are extended to 520 ms after the cut, as the Methods state for camera cuts.fig3c-logreg-cuts-lower
  • The expected values are derived from the stated drops (0.1, 0.07) and the LSTM values of Table S2.fig3c-logreg-cuts-lower
  • Gaps other than 32 s use the dataset's code table of repeated parts per gap.fig3e-temporal-gap
  • Assumes the dandiset's subject ids are the paper's patient numbers; neither gives a mapping, and the Figure 4 legend gives no unit counts per patient to compare. The agreement of the named patients supports this.fig4-named-patients
  • Regions as stored in the dandiset, which hold different units from the paper's (PHC 293 against 373).fig5-region-decoding
  • Logistic regression in place of the LSTM; the criterion was written after the first verification round, before these labels were decoded.fig5-faces-presence
  • Face labels keep the frames the Methods exclude (a face neither shown nor clearly absent) as negatives.fig5-faces-presence
  • Logistic regression in place of the LSTM, over all regions together.fig6-responsive-subsets
  • The four sub-splits of a test fold take each of the other four folds once as validation; the Methods say only that they alternate training and validation data.fig7b-top500-overlap
  • The Methods average the absolute coefficients 'across the five splits' within a set of four models; the mean over the four models is used.fig7b-top500-overlap
  • Logistic regression in place of the LSTM; the criterion was written after the first verification round, before any fit on top-ranked units.fig7a-500-neurons
  • Sizes of 23, 114, 150, 229, 500, 750, 1000, 1500 and 2286 units; the paper does not list its sizes besides 500 and 150.fig7a-500-neurons
