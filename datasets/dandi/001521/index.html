From the paper Sub-microsecond optical measurements of cell membrane charging and lesioning by pulsed electric fields · claims file · checked 2026-11-02: ready

  • fig6a-ca-entry-1000-1200

    A single 1-us pulse causes clear Ca2+ entry into CHO cells at 1000 and 1200 V/cm, among field strengths escalated from 0 to 1200 V/cm.

    Exposure to a single 1-μs pulse at escalating field strengths, from 0 to 1200 V/cm established a clear peak of Ca2+ entry at 1000 and 1200 V/cm

    Results, 3.6 Electroporative uptake of Ca2+, paragraph 2; Figure 6A

    How it will be judged

    Per cell, Fluo-4 dF (%) = 100 (F - F0)/F0, with F the mean over a circular region on the cell less a cell-free background region and F0 the mean before the pulse (Methods 2.4). The paper names no test for this panel (see the opening comment for the readings of the test and of the post-pulse value). Reproduced when, under at least one reading, the post-pulse dF at both 1000 and 1200 V/cm is significantly above zero (one-sample t-test) or above the 0 V/cm group (two-tailed Student's t-test) at p < 0.05, and the group means at 1000 and 1200 V/cm exceed those of every field strength of 650 V/cm or less (this comparison is a reading of "a clear peak" fixed here; the paper states no comparison). Partially reproduced when this holds at one of the two. Group means and s.e. for every field strength are reported, with the per-cell traces.

  • fig6a-ca-sustained

    After a single 1-us pulse at 1000 or 1200 V/cm the Ca2+ signal stays elevated through the 5-min observation period.

    Ca2+ signal remained elevated through the 5-min observation period

    Results, 3.6 Electroporative uptake of Ca2+, paragraph 2; Figure 6A

    How it will be judged

    dF as in fig6a-ca-entry-1000-1200. Reproduced when, at both 1000 and 1200 V/cm, the mean dF over the last 10 frames of the recording (a standard window; the paper fixes none) is above zero by a one-sample t-test at p < 0.05 (the test Section 3.6 uses for the end-of-recording signal of the trains). Partially reproduced when this holds at one of the two. The ratio of the final to the peak group mean is reported.

  • fig6a-780-not-significant

    After a single 1-us pulse at 780 V/cm the Ca2+ signal rises only subtly, at the detection limit, and not significantly.

    A subtle ΔF increase after exposure at 780 V/cm was at the detection limit and not statistically significant

    Results, 3.6 Electroporative uptake of Ca2+, paragraph 2; Figure 6A

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. The paper does not name its test; the reading the Methods and Section 3.6 best support is a one-sample t-test of each cell's dF at the end of recording (mean of the last 10 frames) against zero, at p < 0.05. Reproduced when that test does not find an increase at 780 V/cm, not reproduced when it does. The other readings (two-tailed Student's t-test against the 0 V/cm group; each cell's peak dF) are shown as variants, and the mean dF at 780 V/cm with its s.e. is reported either way.

  • fig6b-train-threshold

    Results, 3.6 Electroporative uptake of Ca2+, paragraph 3; Figure 6B

    How it will be judged

    The paper does not define a "strong" response. Readings are (a) a dF at the end of recording above the largest dF among the 0 V/cm (or lowest field) cells, (b) a dF above 3 s.d. of that group; both are choices fixed here, as the paper gives no rule. Reproduced when, under one reading, the fraction at 780 V/cm is within tolerance and at least 90% of cells respond at every field above 780 V/cm (90% is a reading of "nearly all" fixed here).
