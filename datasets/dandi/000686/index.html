  • fig3-field-simulation

    In the simulations, 1 kV applied to one electrode gives about 1.8 kV/cm in the center of the small array and 0.8 kV/cm in the center of the large array. The field near the energized electrode is about 4-fold higher (7 and 3.5 kV/cm per kV). Energizing one diagonal gives no field in the center.

    For example, applying 1 kV to one electrode produced the electric field of approximately 1.8 kV/cm and 0.8 kV/cm in center of the small and large arrays ... The electric field strength was about 4-fold higher near the energized electrode (7 kV/cm and 3.5 kV/cm per 1 kV applied, for the small and large arrays, respectively) Note that energizing either electrode diagonal produced no electric field in the center of the array

    Methods 2.3, paragraph 4; Figure 3

    How it will be judged

    The values are outputs of the paper's Sim4Life finite-element model (Methods 2.3, built as in the paper's reference 15). The paper names no public deposit of the model or its field maps. The claim is tested only if the dataset holds the simulated field maps. Then reproduced when the four values are within tolerance and the center field for diagonal energizing is zero. A field model built by the author is not the paper's and is not a test. Without the maps the claim is blocked as data_missing.

  • fig4-sham-no-uptake

    Sham-exposed cells gain no YP fluorescence over the 10 min of imaging. Cells given pulses show a gradual rise in YP signal from the first frame after exposure.

    Sham-exposed cells showed no gain in YP fluorescence during the 10 min of observation. In contrast, cells treated by electric pulses displayed gradual increase in YP signal, starting from the image frame taken immediately after nsEP exposure.

    Results 3.1, paragraph 2; Figure 4

    How it will be judged

    Per cell F minus F0, where F0 is the mean of the six frames before the pulse at 57 s (Methods 2.2). A claim of absence for sham, judged with the paper's test (Methods 2.4, a two-sided Student's t-test at p < 0.05), here one-sample against zero at the end of recording (O7, both readings). Reproduced when sham F minus F0 does not differ from zero, and every pulsed group's mean exceeds sham at the first frame after 57 s and keeps rising (end value above the first-frame value). The sham effect size is given either way.

  • fig4-bipolar-weaker

    Bipolar signals that start with the same pulse cause much weaker uptake (p < 0.001). By the end of recording the 4-phasic signal reaches 20.8 +/- 2.0 a.u., the 8-phasic 6.9 +/- 1.6 a.u., and the 8-phasic with 5-us delays 25.4 +/- 2.8 a.u.

    Bipolar signals started with exactly the same pulse (600 ns, 15 kV/cm) but caused significantly weaker effects (p<0.001). By the end of recording, YP signal reached only 20.8±2.0 a.u. for the 4-phasic signal; 6.9±1.6 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal; and 25.4±2.8 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal with 5-μs delays between phases.

    Results 3.1, paragraph 2; Figure 4

    Tolerances are the paper's s.e. Per cell F minus F0 at the end of recording (O7, both readings). Each bipolar group is compared with the unipolar group by an unpaired two-sided Student's t-test (Methods 2.4), in its equal-variance form, which is what Student's t-test names. The Welch form is shown as a variant only. Reproduced when, under one reading, all three means are within tolerance and all three tests give p < 0.001.

  • fig4-8phase-cancellation

    The 8-phasic signal with no delays causes about 20-fold weaker electroporation than a single unipolar pulse. The 4-phasic signal and the delayed 8-phasic signal cancel 4 to 5-fold.

    Note that applying the 8-phasic bipolar signal with no inter-phase delays caused about 20-fold weaker electroporation than a single unipolar pulse

    Figure 4 legend; Results 3.1, paragraph 2; Results 3.4, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    Ratio of group means at the end of recording (O7, both readings). For the "4-5-fold" of Results 3.4 the range widened by 10% of each bound is 3.6 to 5.5. The paper's own means give 18.3 for the 8-phasic signal, 6.1 for the 4-phasic and 5.0 for the delayed 8-phasic. So the 4-phasic ratio printed in Results 3.1 already falls outside the 4 to 5 range. The main test is therefore the 8-phasic ratio, plus the ordering of cancellation (8-phasic strongest, the other two weaker). Reproduced when the 8-phasic ratio is within 18 to 22 and is the largest of the three. Partially reproduced when the ordering holds but the ratio is outside tolerance. Both of the other ratios are reported against 3.6 to 5.5.
