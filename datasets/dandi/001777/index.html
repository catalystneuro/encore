  • fig1c-quinpirole-reduces-gpe-ipsc

    The D2R agonist quinpirole (2 uM) reduces the amplitude of optogenetically evoked GPe IPSCs in SNr neurons by roughly 30%.

    Quinpirole reduced the amplitude of the GPe evoked inhibitory postsynaptic currents (IPSCs) by roughly 30% (P = 0.02 and n = 5)

    Results, D2R agonists inhibited phasic GABA release from GPe terminals, paragraph 1; Figure 1C

    How it will be judged

    Whole-cell voltage clamp at -60 mV, Cs+ internal, optical stimulation of Chronos in GPe axons (Results, paragraph 1). IPSC amplitude is how the paper does not say; read as the peak of the evoked current from the pre-stimulus baseline, averaged over sweeps in each condition (a standard choice, stated as one). Reproduced when a two-sided paired t test of before against after quinpirole has P < 0.05 with amplitudes lower after, and the reduction is within tolerance under one reading of M2.

  • fig1b-gabazine-blocks-gpe-ipsc

    These optically evoked currents were blocked by bath application of the GABAA receptor (GABAAR) antagonist gabazine (10 μM).

    Results, D2R agonists inhibited phasic GABA release from GPe terminals, paragraph 1; Figure 1B

    How it will be judged

    The paper reports no test, no n and no measure of "blocked". Evoked current amplitude as in fig1c-quinpirole-reduces-gpe-ipsc, before and in gabazine, in each cell recorded in gabazine. Reproduced when the amplitude in gabazine is under 10% of its control amplitude in every such cell; the 10% is a choice made here, as the paper gives none, and the remaining amplitude of each cell is reported. Not testable when the dataset holds no voltage-clamp recording of evoked GPe currents in gabazine.

  • fig2c-quinpirole-attenuates-gpe-inhibition

    A 2-s, 30-Hz optogenetic train of GPe inputs inhibits SNr spiking, and quinpirole attenuates this inhibition.

    quinpirole attenuated the ability of GPe stimulation to slow the spiking of SNr neurons (P = 0.02 and n = 8)

    Results, D2R agonists inhibited phasic GABA release from GPe terminals, paragraph 2; Figure 2A to 2C

    How it will be judged

    Cell-attached recording, 6-s sweeps with the 2-s, 30-Hz train from 2 to 4 s (Fig. 2A and 2B). Percent inhibition is "change in discharge rate/basal discharge rate" (Fig. 2C legend), with the rate during the train from 2 to 4 s of each condition's PSTH, built as in the code (part 2) over the condition's episodes. The legend does not say which part of the sweep gives the basal rate; readings (a) 0 to 2 s, before the train; (b) 0 to 2 s and 4 to 6 s together. The paper gives no bin width; the rates are taken over the whole windows, so the bin does not matter. A per-sweep percent inhibition, its median over sweeps, is shown as a variant. Reproduced when, under one reading, a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test of control against quinpirole has P < 0.05 with less inhibition in quinpirole.
