Problems that change results 2

The data are wrong in a way the files do not show, so an analysis that takes them as given comes out wrong. Each was checked against the notebook’s outputs by someone other than the attempt’s author.

  • File format errorChanges resultsIn nine of the ten files (all written by the first conversion) the first entry of units/spike_times_index is the true end of unit 0 modulo 65536. Unit 0 is cut short (in J-array 61030 of its 257638 spikes) and its remaining spikes open unit 1, whose times then jump from the end of the session back to its start.
    Checked: reproduce_churchland_2012.ipynb cell 5 prints a stored first index below 65536 with 1 to 7 wraps in nine files and 0 wraps in the 2010 file, and cell 9 prints J-array unit 0 and unit 1 as stored (61030, 461554) against corrected (257638, 264946), where 61030 + 3 x 65536 = 257638. The backward jump in stored unit 1 is not printed directly; only the corrected train is shown as sorted, and the wrap counts for the other eight files come from the helper function.
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-01, 2026-10-16)
  • File format errorChanges resultsIn five Nitschke files (ses-20090812, 20090819, 20090910, 20090920 and 20100923) the clock restarts at recording segments after the first, as the conversion offsets only one segment. Trials overlap in time (2094 overlapping pairs in N-array), and every unit's spike times go backwards at each restart, so spikes cannot be placed in trials by time; they can be placed by their storage order, which the notebook shows decodes reach direction in every segment.
    Checked: reproduce_churchland_2012.ipynb cell 5 prints clock_resets of 1, 1, 2, 1 and 3 and overlapping trial pairs of 1064, 1064, 1415, 1187 and 2094 for the five named Nitschke files, and cell 13 prints reach direction decoding of 0.51 to 0.87 in every clock segment of the 2010 and 20090910 sessions. That every unit's spike times go backwards at each restart is stated in markdown and implied by the placement by storage order, not printed per unit.
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-01, 2026-10-16)

Other findings 12

  • File format errorThe Nitschke file (sub-Nitschke_ses-20100923) joins five recording segments whose clocks overlap, and no metadata says so. Trial start times, hand timestamps and every unit's spike times jump backwards four times (hand timestamps span 5.4 to 6075.4 s, 6074.2 to 12075.9 s, 6070.6 to 12493.5 s, 6119.8 to 13515.2 s and 6073.5 to 6656.8 s), so spike times are not sorted and a trial's spikes cannot be selected by time alone; doing so mixes spikes of up to four segments. Splitting each unit's spikes at the jumps and assigning trials to segments in file order gives condition decoding of 0.44 to 0.50 in segments 0 to 2 and 0.18 in segment 3 (all successful trials; chance 0.014; 0.010 to 0.024 with the previous trial's labels).
    Found by the attempt on Sabatini et al. 2024 (sabatini-2024-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Description missingThe units table holds 96 units per array, exactly one on each of the 192 electrodes, in both files. Its columns are electrode_group, electrodes, id, obs_intervals and spike_times, with no column for single or multi-unit, sorting quality or isolation. The paper reports 79 to 118 single and stable multi-units per array. Whether these units are threshold crossings or sorted units merged per electrode is not stated.
    Found by the attempt on Sabatini et al. 2024 (sabatini-2024-01, 2026-11-20)
  • File format errorIn the Jenkins file (sub-Jenkins_ses-20090918), unit 1's row of the units table holds two sorted spike trains one after the other. The first, 196608 spikes from 4259.1 to 15668.6 s, begins 55 ms after unit 0's train, which holds 61030 spikes from 4.9 s, stops at 4259.0 s. The second, 264946 spikes from 4.7 to 15668.6 s, spans the session as the other units do. The two share 1049 spike times; neither has intervals under 1 ms, while merged 3.5% of intervals are. Of the first run's times 37.4% are found exactly in another unit's train (unit 68), as are 27.9% of unit 0's, against at most 2.5% for the second run. Unit 1's row thus appears to hold the rest of unit 0's train after 4259 s, and unit 0's row is cut short. Nothing but the unsorted order of unit 1's spike times shows it; merging the two runs, as the main analyses do, gives unit 1 a train with another unit's spikes. The companion notebook leaves units 0 and 1 out.
    Found by the attempt on Sabatini et al. 2024 (sabatini-2024-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Metadata errorHand, cursor and eye positions are labelled "meters" with a conversion of NaN, but hold millimetres (reaches of about 130 and positions from -182 to 159).
    Found by the attempt on Sabatini et al. 2024 (sabatini-2024-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Data missingThe dataset holds no EMG, which the paper's Supplementary Figure 8 removes along with kinematic dimensions.
    Found by the attempt on Sabatini et al. 2024 (sabatini-2024-01, 2026-11-20)
  • OtherIn the Nitschke file, one M1 unit (unit 170) fires at a mean of 175.2 spikes/s in segment 3, above 100 spikes/s on 229 of its 532 trials (trials starting 6446 to 10006 s on that segment's clock), against means of 27.6 to 40.4 spikes/s and no such trial in the other segments. Its ratio of largest to median mean rate over blocks of 50 trials is 13.7, against at most 2.5 for the other units of the file. Nothing in the file marks it. It lowers condition decoding within segment 3 to 0.179, against 0.445 to 0.495 in segments 0 to 2; without it, decoding within segment 3 is 0.425. The per-trial rates summed over units have similar ranges in all segments. The companion notebook leaves this unit out.
    Found by the attempt on Sabatini et al. 2024 (sabatini-2024-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Data missingThe dandiset holds the paper's two array datasets but not the sorted isolations the paper analysed (146 for J-array, 218 for N-array). Its Units table has 192 units per file, one per electrode. The seven single-electrode datasets, the EMG, the leech and the walking-monkey data are not in it either, so six of the paper's main findings cannot be tested.
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-01, 2026-10-16)
  • Metadata errorHand position is stated in metres (with conversion NaN); its values span about -220 to +165 on the scale of the target positions (up to 145) and the median speed peaks at 600 to 730 per second during a reach, so they are in mm.
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-01, 2026-10-16)
  • Description missingThe hand position ends a median 35 mm (Jenkins) and 52 to 53 mm (Nitschke) below the reached target in y, while the cursor lands on the target; the offset between hand and screen coordinates is not described in the files.
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-01, 2026-10-16)
  • Description missingThe Units table carries only the default description ("Autogenerated by NWBFile") and does not say whether its units are sorted neurons, multi-unit activity or threshold crossings. Their number (one per electrode) and rates (8.8 to 300 spikes/s over trial time in the three sessions analysed) are consistent with channel-level activity.
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-01, 2026-10-16)
  • Description missingThe dandiset does not say which of its ten sessions are the paper's datasets. Two match the J-array and N-array dates; the other eight (27-condition and two-condition maze sessions, and one 108-condition session) are not described.
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-01, 2026-10-16)
  • OtherThe first conversion's code sets the year of every session date to 2009. The file sub-Nitschke ses-20090910 has 108 conditions with the same reached target as N-array (2010) for all of them, and is consistent with the N-9-10-2010 dataset of Supplementary Figure 5; if so, its date is a year early. The other 2009 Nitschke dates may be affected in the same way.
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-01, 2026-10-16)
