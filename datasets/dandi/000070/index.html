  • Data missingStill presentThe Units tables of all ten files hold unsorted threshold crossings, one unit per electrode (192 per session), as their description says, where the paper analysed 146 (J-array) and 218 (N-array) sorted single and multi-unit isolations. The seven single-electrode datasets, the EMG, the leech and walking-monkey recordings and the J-11-06-2009 session of Supplementary Figure 5 are not in it. Six of the paper's 22 claims cannot be tested.#
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-02, 2026-11-24)
  • Description missingNewTwo sessions, sub-Nitschke ses-20100920 and ses-20100922, have no maze_condition, correct_reach, trial_version, active_target, proto_trial, novel_maze or unhittable columns, which the other eight files have, so their trials cannot be grouped by condition or selected for consistent reaches as in the paper. The paper does not use these sessions.#
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-02, 2026-11-24)
  • Description missingNewThe trial table has two movement-onset columns, move_begins_time (described only as "movement onset time") and offline_move_onset_time ("movement onset time, calculated offline using a robust algorithm"), which differ by a median of 25 ms in J-array and 52 ms in N-array. Nothing says which one is the onset the paper aligned on, and several results change with it (the variance captured by the jPCA plane, 14.9 against 18.7% in J-array, and its frequency, 1.344 against 1.098 Hz). The hand speed starts to rise at offline_move_onset_time (median 43 and 15 mm/s); at move_begins_time it is already 137 and 160 mm/s. The choice changes the outcomes of five claims.#
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-02, 2026-11-24)
  • OtherNewThe spikes of both arrays are consistent with the clock of the trial table. Reach direction decodes at chance from each array before target onset (0.12 to 0.17 against 0.14 to 0.17 with shuffled labels) and above it after target onset (0.37 to 0.70). The lag between the arrays' movement-related changes is constant across six blocks of each session (-4 to 11 ms in J-array, 25 to 32 ms in N-array, M1 later), so there is no drift like that of the LFP's second, slower clock. After target onset, an event that does not depend on the movement onset, the arrays respond nearly together (M1 lag -4 ms in J-array and 9 ms in N-array, half-rise of the across-condition spread at 99 and 101 ms in N-array), so a fixed offset of 25 ms or more between the N-array arrays' clocks is not supported; the later M1 change around movement is consistent with a later M1 response.#
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-02, 2026-11-24)
  • OtherNewChecks that passed. No recording is deposited twice (the trial-interval and spike-count hashes of the ten files all differ). Spike times are sorted within every unit and every analysis window lies inside its trial. Hand position is in mm with a conversion to metres, its speed rises at offline_move_onset_time, and the hand ends a median 9.1 mm (J-array) and 11.4 mm (N-array) from the hit target minus the cursor offset. Electrode groups 1 and 2 are described as the PMd and M1 arrays, 96 units each.#
    Found by the attempt on Churchland et al. 2012 (churchland-2012-02, 2026-11-24)

Dataset version 0.260528.0919 an older version · tested 2026-11-20 · 6 open findings

  • File format errorThe Nitschke file (sub-Nitschke_ses-20100923) joins five recording segments whose clocks overlap, and no metadata says so. Trial start times, hand timestamps and every unit's spike times jump backwards four times (hand timestamps span 5.4 to 6075.4 s, 6074.2 to 12075.9 s, 6070.6 to 12493.5 s, 6119.8 to 13515.2 s and 6073.5 to 6656.8 s), so spike times are not sorted and a trial's spikes cannot be selected by time alone; doing so mixes spikes of up to four segments. Splitting each unit's spikes at the jumps and assigning trials to segments in file order gives condition decoding of 0.44 to 0.50 in segments 0 to 2 and 0.18 in segment 3 (all successful trials; chance 0.014; 0.010 to 0.024 with the previous trial's labels).#
    Found by the attempt on Sabatini et al. 2024 (sabatini-2024-01, 2026-11-20)
  • Description missingThe units table holds 96 units per array, exactly one on each of the 192 electrodes, in both files. Its columns are electrode_group, electrodes, id, obs_intervals and spike_times, with no column for single or multi-unit, sorting quality or isolation. The paper reports 79 to 118 single and stable multi-units per array. Whether these units are threshold crossings or sorted units merged per electrode is not stated.#
    Found by the attempt on Sabatini et al. 2024 (sabatini-2024-01, 2026-11-20)
  • File format errorIn the Jenkins file (sub-Jenkins_ses-20090918), unit 1's row of the units table holds two sorted spike trains one after the other. The first, 196608 spikes from 4259.1 to 15668.6 s, begins 55 ms after unit 0's train, which holds 61030 spikes from 4.9 s, stops at 4259.0 s. The second, 264946 spikes from 4.7 to 15668.6 s, spans the session as the other units do. The two share 1049 spike times; neither has intervals under 1 ms, while merged 3.5% of intervals are. Of the first run's times 37.4% are found exactly in another unit's train (unit 68), as are 27.9% of unit 0's, against at most 2.5% for the second run. Unit 1's row thus appears to hold the rest of unit 0's train after 4259 s, and unit 0's row is cut short. Nothing but the unsorted order of unit 1's spike times shows it; merging the two runs, as the main analyses do, gives unit 1 a train with another unit's spikes. The companion notebook leaves units 0 and 1 out.#
    Found by the attempt on Sabatini et al. 2024 (sabatini-2024-01, 2026-11-20)

  • File format errorFixedIn nine of the ten files (all written by the first conversion) the first entry of units/spike_times_index is the true end of unit 0 modulo 65536. Unit 0 is cut short (in J-array 61030 of its 257638 spikes) and its remaining spikes open unit 1, whose times then jump from the end of the session back to its start.
    In earlier_findings.ipynb the first entry of spike_times_index is above 65536 in all ten files (257638 in J-array, the corrected end of unit 0 the earlier attempt gave), and units 0 and 1 are sorted and each spans the trials of its session. Found fixed on dataset version 0.261122.2046 by churchland-2012-02, 2026-11-24.
  • File format errorFixedIn five Nitschke files (ses-20090812, 20090819, 20090910, 20090920 and 20100923) the clock restarts at recording segments after the first, as the conversion offsets only one segment. Trials overlap in time (2094 overlapping pairs in N-array), and every unit's spike times go backwards at each restart, so spikes cannot be placed in trials by time; they can be placed by their storage order, which the notebook shows decodes reach direction in every segment.
    In earlier_findings.ipynb no trial starts before the one before it and no two trials overlap in any of the ten files, and units 0, 95, 96 and 191 of each file have no backward step in their spike times, with all their spikes inside a trial.
