  • fig4-unipolar-strongest

    A single unipolar 600-ns pulse at 15 kV/cm causes by far the strongest YP uptake in individual CHO cells, 126 +/- 14 a.u. at the end of the 10-min recording (n = 25), and sham exposure causes no gain in YP fluorescence.

    Sham-exposed cells showed no gain in YP fluorescence during the 10 min of observation. ... A single 600-ns pulse at 15 kV/cm caused by far the strongest YP uptake, reaching 126±14 a.u. (n=25) by the end of recording.

    Results, 3.1, paragraph 2; Figure 4

    How it will be judged

    Per cell, F minus F0 as Section 2.2 defines it (F0 the mean of the six images before the pulses), at the last image of the 600-s series (the paper's "end of recording"). The value holds when the mean over cells is within the paper's s.e. (14 a.u.) of 126. The sham claim holds when the sham group's mean F minus F0 at the end of recording does not differ from zero by a two-sided one-sample Student's t-test at p < 0.05 (the Methods' test, Section 2.4, applied to the one group), and is reported with its size.

  • fig4-bipolar-cancellation

    Bipolar signals that start with the same pulse cause significantly weaker YP uptake than the single unipolar pulse (p < 0.001), reaching 20.8 +/- 2.0 a.u. for the 4-phase signal, 6.9 +/- 1.6 a.u. for the 8-phase signal and 25.4 +/- 2.8 a.u. for the 8-phase signal with 5-us delays; the 8-phase signal gives about 20-fold cancellation.

    Bipolar signals started with exactly the same pulse (600 ns, 15 kV/cm) but caused significantly weaker effects (p<0.001). By the end of recording, YP signal reached only 20.8±2.0 a.u. for the 4-phasic signal; 6.9±1.6 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal; and 25.4±2.8 a.u. for the 8-phasic signal with 5-μs delays between phases. The impressive 20-fold bipolar cancellation achieved with the 8-phasic signal

    Results, 3.1, paragraph 2; Figure 4 legend

    How it will be judged

    Values as in fig4-unipolar-strongest, each within the paper's s.e. The test is not named in Section 2.2 or the Fig. 4 legend; the only test the Methods name is the two-sided Student's t-test (Section 2.4), which is used here, comparing each bipolar group with the unipolar group at the last image (Welch's t-test is shown as a variant only). Reproduced when all three bipolar groups are below the unipolar group with p < 0.001 and the values are within tolerance. The ratio uses the group means. The "4-5-fold" cancellation of the other two signals (Section 3.4) is reported alongside.

(2) For "40 Packets", the center ROI above the trough, the trough being the ROI with the lowest mean between the center and e1 (the paper's "least affected area ... between the center and the electrodes", Section 3.3). (3) For "40 Packets", the ROI next to e1 above the center ROI. Reproduced when all three hold under the readings of the opening comment that apply (ROI count, center ROI, raw or LOESS); partially when two hold.

  • fig7b-targeting-independent-of-amplitude

    Normalized to the most peripheral ROI, the profiles for different first pulse amplitudes fully overlap, so the spatial distribution of electroporation, and NG-CANCAN targeting, does not depend on amplitude within the studied range.

    In contrast, changing nsEP amplitude affected the degree of electroporation but not its spatial distribution ( Fig. 7 ). ... When the YP emission was normalized to the most peripheral ROI, the data for different nsEP amplitudes fully overlapped ( Fig. 7B ).

    Results, 3.3, paragraph 2; Figure 7B

    How it will be judged

    A claim of absence. The paper names one test, the two-sided Student's t-test at p < 0.05 (Section 2.4), and reads significance from the error bar gap. Small array, 1Px4, every voltage of Fig. 7A and 7B (n = 3 dishes each). The normalized values at the center ROI and at the half-way ROI, the positions the paper reports in this paragraph, are compared between every pair of voltages, with no correction, as the paper applies none. Reproduced when no pair differs at p < 0.05; not reproduced when any does. The differences and their p-values are reported either way, and a test over all ROIs is shown as a variant. The voltages other than 2.4 and 5.6 kV are printed only in the figure, which was not read; the attempt takes them from the dataset.
