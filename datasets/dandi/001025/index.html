    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper measured the ROIs in MetaMorph with the electrode positions known; the dandiset holds no ROIs or electrode positions, so the electrodes are found by fitting four dark disks to each image and the ROIs are placed from their centre (overlays in the notebook).
    • The pixel size is not in the dataset. 0.65 um is used, from the ORCA-Flash4.0 camera and 10x objective the files name, assuming no binning. The dark footprint of each needle has a median radius of 0.650 mm (large array) and 0.718 mm (small array) at this scale, against the 0.75 mm radius of the 1.5 mm needles (Section 2.3). The footprints rule out the round-1 variant at which the large diagonal is 8.4 mm (0.591 um), which puts the needle edge inside the paper's ROI span on both arrays and is dropped, but they do not confirm the camera scale; they imply 0.679 um (small array) and 0.750 um (large array), at which the large diagonal is 10.65 mm, further from the paper's 8.4 mm than the 9.23 mm of the camera scale. The camera scale is kept for the main reading and the footprint scale is run as a variant; it does not change this claim's result.
    • At the camera scale the paper's ROI spans (Section 2.4) end 0.12 mm (small array) and 0.22 mm (large array) from the needle edge, inside the paper's exclusion zone of 5% of the diagonal (0.25 and 0.46 mm). The spans are kept as the paper states them; a variant drops the end ROIs inside the zone.
    • ROI width across the diagonal is not given (Section 2.4); square ROIs are used, a choice that changes only how many pixels are averaged.
    • The packet is not named in the session descriptions ("CanCan protocol ... 12.5% lower"); Packet 2 is assumed, as in Fig. 7 and the dandiset's title.
    • The dandiset has 4 dishes at 2.4 kV where Figure 7 states n = 3; all four are used and each leave-one-out is shown.
