  • fig1c-isolation-alcohol-sex-opposite

    Social isolation changes alcohol licking in opposite directions in the two sexes, raising it in males and lowering it in females, a significant sex by housing interaction.

    Social isolation produced opposite effects on alcohol licking in males and females ( N = 14 male and 11 female mice, two-way ANOVA, interaction: F (1, 23) = 21.04, *** P = 0.0001; Sidak’s multiple comparisons test), increasing alcohol licking in males while decreasing it in females.

    Figure 1c legend; Results, Social isolation increases alcohol drinking in males and decreases it in females, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    Per mouse, alcohol licks averaged over the last 3 baseline days and over the 11 isolation days (Methods, 2BC drinking). A two-way ANOVA of sex (between) by housing (within, repeated measures), as the error df of 23 with 25 mice implies; Sidak post hoc tests of housing within each sex. Reproduced when the interaction has p < 0.05, males rise and females fall, and the Sidak test is significant in males. Partially reproduced when the interaction holds but one sex's change is in the wrong direction.
