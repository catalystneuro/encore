From the paper DREDge: robust motion correction for high-density extracellular recordings across species · claims file · checked 2026-10-21: ready

  • edf3a-ap-dredge-slow-trend

    In the fast-motion macaque recording, DREDge's spike-based (AP) motion estimate follows the drift visible in the unregistered spike raster.

    a Spike raster before registration, with AP DREDge displacement estimate overlaid in red.

    Extended Data Fig. 3a legend; Methods, Tracking fast motion from spikes using clustering and splines

    How it will be judged

    The legend shows the estimate over the raster and states no result in words; that it follows the slow drift is read from the Methods ("First, slower timescale motion is estimated using DREDge"). Run the pipeline (opening comment, steps 1 and 2) on the whole recording. The paper shows this only as a figure, so the measure is the paper's own registration quality metric, the mean template correlation (Methods, Spike registration quality metrics) on a 1 um by 1 s raster of mean spike amplitudes, bins outside the probe after correction masked. Reproduced when, under readings (a) and (b) of step 2 and (i) and (ii) of step 1, the mean template correlation after DREDge correction exceeds that of the unregistered raster; not reproduced when it is lower under all; methods_underspecified when the readings disagree. The size of the estimated displacement (range and s.d. in um) and the plot of the trace over the raster are reported. That a small improvement may reflect little drift in an anesthetized, paralyzed animal is noted, not judged.

    Colored spikes represent high amplitude spikes that were clustered by HDBSCAN using their registered positions and amplitudes.

Reproduced when every combination does; not reproduced when none does; methods_underspecified otherwise. The number of clusters and spikes per cluster are reported for each.

  • edf3c-spline-subsecond

    A smoothing spline fit to the centered positions of the clustered spikes captures sub-second displacement left after AP DREDge registration.

    Left, centered positions of the clustered spikes (blue) with the spline fit, that captures the sub-second displacement, overlaid in red.

    Extended Data Fig. 3c legend; Methods, Tracking fast motion from spikes using clustering and splines

    How it will be judged

    Pipeline step 3 under each combination of its open readings. The paper gives no number and no test for this panel. The control is borrowed from another panel of the paper, the "randomly permuted or 'scrambled'" motion estimate of Fig. 2e (legend), which the paper uses for ISI violations, not for this figure, and whose permutation unit it does not give. For each combination, the pooled within-cluster s.d. of corrected depth of the clustered spikes is computed after correction by the spline and after correction by the same spline trace with its time bins randomly permuted. The permutation bin (0.4 s, the knot spacing), the 100 permutations with a fixed seed and the one-sided 5% level are choices of this file, not of the paper. Reproduced when, in every combination, the spline-corrected s.d. is below the 5th percentile of the scrambled s.d. values; not reproduced when in none; methods_underspecified otherwise. The spline trace's range and s.d. (um) and its power spectrum are reported, as is the noted circularity that the spline is fit to the spikes it is evaluated on.

  • edf3de-spline-improves-registration

    Correcting the AP-registered spikes with the spline estimate of sub-second displacement gives a more stable spike raster and tighter spike localizations than AP DREDge registration alone.

    d Top, AP registered spike raster. Bottom, AP registered spike raster corrected using the spline estimate of sub-second displacement. e Localizations of detected spikes after AP DREDge registration (left), and spline correction (right).

    Extended Data Fig. 3d and 3e legends; Results, DREDge rescues spike sorting and LFP features in human brain activity, paragraph 1

    How it will be judged

    The legends show the comparison and state no result in words; the improvement is read from the Results, which call the spline estimate "a faster timescale AP-based estimate". Two measures, one per panel, both from the paper. Panel d, the mean template correlation (Methods, Spike registration quality metrics) of all detected spikes, on the 1 um by 1 s raster of mean amplitudes, after AP DREDge correction and after AP DREDge plus spline correction (the spline displacement subtracted from each spike's registered depth at its time). Panel e, the pooled within-cluster s.d. of corrected depth over the HDBSCAN clusters, before and after spline correction; the paper names no measure for panel e, so this one is the file's choice, and since the spline is fit to minimize these residuals it is reported with that circularity noted. The 1 s bins of the template correlation are the Methods' and cannot show motion faster than 1 s, so the panel d measure is also reported at 0.1 s bins as a variant that does not enter the outcome. Reproduced when both measures improve (higher correlation, lower s.d.) under every combination of the open readings of steps 2 and 3; not reproduced when neither improves under any; partially reproduced when one measure improves under every combination and the other does not; methods_underspecified when the readings disagree. The size of each change is reported.
