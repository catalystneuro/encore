Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Data missingThe optogenetic sessions (Opto2, Opto4, Opto9, UG21) hold no stimulation times and no record of which fiber was stimulated; the trigger line the paper's code uses is not in the files. Both can be recovered only from the laser artefact in the DF/F, which is unambiguous in Opto2 and UG21 but spreads over many fibers in the tdTomato mice.
  • Description missingThe event table gives tone presentations without their frequency. DL18 has 44 tones, of which the paper analysed the 14 at 12 kHz; the frequency is stored only in a MATLAB object of the behaviour file on Zenodo. The tone start times are the trigger times, 11 frames before the sound according to the paper's code, which the dandiset does not say.
  • Data missingDL15's sessions from 2021-09-10 (44 to 70 rewards) were run in a ViRMEn task; the paper keeps only rewards of one task phase, but the task variables are not in the files, so unpredicted rewards cannot be separated and Figure 1D can be tested only to week 8.
  • Metadata errorThe FiberPhotometryTable column `included` is described as "whether this fiber has been successfully implanted", but it marks fibers within the striatum (the paper's in_striatum); in Opto2 two fibers labelled Caudoputamen are not included, and with them the counts match the paper's "within or on the border of the striatum" (24 locations).
  • Metadata errorUG21's indicator is given as tdTomato. Its 3 sessions, 22 included fibers and stimulated fibers 12, 18 and 24 match the paper's rDAm3.0 mouse without opsin (Figure S7A), so the indicator is consistent with being a mislabel; the files cannot show which indicator was expressed.
  • Metadata errorIn DL31 and DL32 (18 Hz, interleaved 470 and 415 nm) the fiber photometry series store a regular rate, but the frame timestamps of the behaviour drift from it by up to 0.43 and 0.54 s over the session, so times computed from the stored rate are wrong by up to half a second.
  • Data missingThe data of Figures 2, 3, 7, S1 and S5A-D are not in the dandiset (the CT scans, the freely moving recordings, the D1-cre jaw experiments, histology and the 470 and 405 nm sessions of the salient-stimulus mice); they are on Zenodo. Opto9 has 12 sessions, so one of the paper's 26 tdTomato stimulations is missing.
  • Data missingThe planned fiber targets behind Figure S3 are neither in the dandiset, whose fiber table gives only the localized tips, nor on Zenodo, where the code reads them from calibration files that were not deposited.
  • OtherThe paper gives p < 1e-6 for its test of a significant response (3 consecutive frames outside the 99% band of the null), the value for independent frames. With the same test the chance rate of a significant increase is 0.024 per fiber and stimulus, and three given consecutive frames are all outside the band at a rate of 0.0037.
  • Published code disagreesThe paper's latency code (sal_stim_lat_plots.m) divides the 1-based index of the half-maximum frame by the frame rate, so its latencies are one frame (33 ms) longer than the Methods' definition. With that count the dandiset gives the paper's means within 2 ms.
  • Published code disagreesIn the paper's cross-correlation of DF/F with locomotion (sal_stim_iti_acc_cc.m), negative lags are not shifted; they correlate the two signals at zero lag over a truncated range. The Methods describe a cross-correlation over +-500 ms.
  • Published code disagreesThe Figure 1C legend describes 23 example fibers of a 93-fiber array; the paper's code for the figure plots 33 fibers of mouse 842, whose recording has 82 fibers.
  • Description missingThe archive's summary gives the approach as "cell population imaging" and lists only NWB container types as variables measured; the files hold multi-fiber photometry, treadmill velocity and behavioural events.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • The paper shows example traces; the SD ratio and its thresholds were chosen here before the analysis.fig1c-isosbestic-control
  • Figure 1C describes a 93-fiber array; the mouse its code reads (842) has 82 fibers per channel, 61 included.fig1c-isosbestic-control
  • The code's set of fibers (goodROIs.mat) is not published; the included fibers are used.figs2d-correlation-distance
  • The threshold of 0.3 for "generally low" was chosen here.figs2d-correlation-distance
  • Surface distance is in pixels of the bundle image; the code converts it to mm, which does not change the p-value.figs2ef-no-bundle-crosstalk
  • Weeks are counted from the first session, labelled week 5, because the files give no implantation date; the paper's code puts the first session on day 35, which gives the same weeks.fig1d-chronic-reward-stability
  • Only weeks 5 to 8 can be tested.fig1d-chronic-reward-stability
  • The GLM is a re-implementation in Python; filtering and lag selection differ in detail (see the salient-stimulus claims), which explains the significance disagreements.fig4-link-published-results
  • The paper gives p < 1e-6 for its test (3 consecutive frames outside the 99% band), which is 0.01 cubed and assumes independent frames. With the same test, three given consecutive frames are all outside the band at a rate of 0.0037 in the null refits, and the per-fiber chance rate is 0.024. The protocol blocks a claim when the chance rate of a selection is far from the paper's, as a sign that the procedures differ; here the procedure is shown to be the paper's (raw averages identical, significance agreeing with the published calls for 93.6 to 94.5% of pairs, fig4-link-published-results), so the gap is in how the paper states the rate, and the outcome is kept. 240 increases against about 7 expected by chance.fig4e-light-increases-widespread
  • The low-pass filter of overall velocity is a zero-phase Butterworth filter in place of MATLAB's lowpass; locomotion lags come from a cross-correlation that shifts negative lags, where the paper's code does not (its version correlates at r = 0.997 with the light values used).fig4e-light-increases-widespread
  • DL18's tone regressors include all 44 tones (the files do not give tone frequency).fig4e-light-increases-widespread
  • The tail boundary (AP at most -1.0 mm) was fixed here; the paper gives none.fig4e-light-anterior-posterior-gradient
  • DL18 uses all 44 tones in the main analysis.fig4f-tone-tail-increase-anterior-decrease
  • The supplementary legend of Figure S4 was not available; the test per mouse was chosen here.figs4ac-patterns-consistent-across-mice
  • The supplementary legend was not available; the correlation threshold and the tone-pattern test were chosen here.figs4eh-patterns-not-movement
  • The spline basis has 9 functions at 18 Hz, as the paper's code (run_sal_stim_glm_470_415.m, knots every 3 frames) has; the Methods give 13 for the GLM at 30 Hz. The 13-function basis is shown as a variant.figs5-isosbestic-no-pattern
  • Events are placed by behaviour sample index for both channels, because the stored regular frame rate drifts from the frame timestamps by up to half a second; the clock check for these two sessions is weak (decoding 0.90 and 0.80 with true onsets against 0.40 to 0.65 with shifted ones, 20 presentations each).figs5-isosbestic-no-pattern
  • The 470 and 405 nm sessions of Figure S5A-D are not in the dandiset.figs5-isosbestic-no-pattern
  • Latency follows the Methods (from the onset frame); the code's count, one frame later, is shown as a variant and matches the paper.fig5ab-response-latencies
  • Tone onsets are shifted by 11 frames, the speaker delay in the paper's code, which the Methods do not mention.fig5ab-response-latencies
  • Relative latencies do not depend on the code's one-frame offset, which cancels in the difference.fig5g-light-latency-gradient
  • The supplementary legend was not available; the count of mice was chosen here.figs6cd-gradients-individual-mice

  • The bootstrap counts responses of either sign as significant, as the code does.fig6-inclusion-counts

  • The paper shows binned means; the comparison of fitted decay rates was chosen here.fig6g-dorsal-spread
  • In these mice the laser artefact spreads to many fibers, and the stimulated fiber is taken as the one with the largest artefact, which can be wrong; the true stimulated fiber is then among the fibers tested, which makes the test stricter.fig6-tdtomato-control-no-response
  • The paper included these sites only if they responded to reward; UG21 has no reward events in the files, so that step is not applied.figs7a-photoswitch-confined
