    The state of the population of all medial temporal lobe neurons changed abruptly in the first 300 ms after soft and hard boundaries, and only slowly in clips with no boundary, measured as the distance in principal-component space from the state at the boundary.

    During NB video clips, the neural state exhibited only slow changes as a function of time ... In contrast, the neural state changed abruptly following SBs and HBs ... Plotting MDD as a function of time revealed an abrupt change within ~300 ms after the boundary for SB and HB video clips

    Figure 5a-d; Results, Neural state shifts across boundaries reflect memory strength, paragraph 1

    After a hard boundary the distance from the state at the boundary is larger than in clips with no boundary (9.03 against 7.42, p = 0.0001) and larger than before the boundary (after minus before 0.84 against 0.04, p = 0.004); 12 of 18 subjects show it alone. After a soft boundary the distance is larger than without a boundary (8.15, p = 0.003) but equally large before the boundary (after minus before 0.00, p = 0.55). That the shift completes within 300 ms was not shown.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • 549 units from 18 sessions against the paper's 580 from 19.
    • Pseudo-trials are matched across subjects by clip name; the paper does not say how.
    • Principal components are fitted on the three condition averages from 1 s before to 1.5 s after the boundary, giving 22 components for 99% of the variance against the paper's 55. The averages are of the same trials that are then compared.
    • A trial is z-scored from 1 s before to 8 s after clip onset; the paper says "the entire trial".
    • Clips with soft boundaries are aligned to their first soft boundary, one pseudo-trial per clip. The authors' published code does not cover this analysis.
    • The comparison with the second before the boundary is this attempt's; the paper shows curves.
    • Two SB clips have their first soft boundary less than 1 s after onset. Leaving them out gives the same result (SB after minus before -0.03, p = 0.59; HB 0.84, p = 0.003).

Findings about the data

What attempts found about the dataset itself, for its owner and the archive.

  • Metadata errorIn the most recent published version (0.230530.1822) the spike times of subjects 13, 17 and 18 are shifted by 191.5, -16.8 and 9.3 s against the encoding trials, which did not move, so spikes and clips no longer line up. The version the paper cites (0.220216.0323) does not have this shift.
  • Metadata errorThe spike times of subject 19 are near -7.02e12 s while its events run from 0 to 2892 s, in both versions read. Its 16 units cannot be aligned to the task.
  • Identifier missingScene recognition and time discrimination trials with soft or hard boundaries cannot be linked to the clips they test. Their frame names are numbered differently from the clips (10 distinct numbers in recognition, 1 to 60 in time discrimination, against clips 1 to 30). The position of a tested frame within its clip is not given.
  • Identifier missingThe dandiset does not say which units the paper classified as boundary cells or event cells, so the paper's selection cannot be compared with a new one cell by cell.
  • Data missingThe dandiset has no local field potentials, no units outside the medial temporal lobe (the paper has 405), no similarity ratings and no stimuli, and it lacks the behaviour of one of the paper's 20 subjects.
  • Selection not documentedThe dandiset has 565 medial temporal lobe units where the paper analyses 580, with a different split by region (hippocampus 279 against 343, amygdala 233 against 169, parahippocampal gyrus 53 against 68). Nothing says how the two sets relate.
  • Metadata errorSeveral column descriptions do not match the values. stimCategory holds 0, 1, 2 and is described as 1, 2, 3. A missing second or third boundary is stored as the clip's start time and described as 0. The accuracy code 0 for "no response" is never used; trials without a response are coded incorrect, or, in 20 time discrimination trials, correct. resp_value is 1 for those trials and described as -1. RT is measured from the disappearance of the frames, which its description does not say, and is negative in 151 time discrimination trials.
  • Metadata errorThe events series holds 43 entries with a code 0 that its description does not list, with timestamps as low as -7.0e12 s. The spike times have no stated unit.
  • Methods not availableThe paper's Methods do not specify the permutation test, which soft boundaries are used, or how trials are matched across subjects for the pseudopopulation. The definitions of boundary and event cells, read literally, give a chance level about seventy times lower than the paper reports.
  • Methods not availableThe code the dandiset links to (github.com/rutishauserlab/cogboundary-zheng, commit 60f7ece) is four scripts that plot rasters from the NWB files. It contains none of the paper's analyses, no cell selection, chance level, statistics, state-space or reinstatement analysis, and nothing that links a tested frame to its clip.
  • Metadata errorThe authors' plotting code drops missing boundaries by testing for a stored time above 0. In version 0.220216.0323 a missing boundary is stored as the clip's start time, so the code would keep 150 soft-boundary rows where 75 exist and its fixed row ranges for the three conditions would be wrong. In version 0.230530.1822 missing boundaries are NaN and the code works, but that version has the shifted spike times.
  • OtherMethods and published code disagree on the firing-rate traces. The Methods give a 200 ms window moved in 2 ms steps; the code uses 10 ms bins smoothed with a Gaussian window of 20 bins. The code shows 0.5 s before and 1 s after a boundary. It also treats every soft boundary, including those inside hard-boundary clips, as a soft-boundary trial (75 per session), which the Methods do not state.

Reproduction Cognitive boundaries and human medial temporal lobe neurons (Zheng et al. 2022)

Tests the findings of Zheng et al. (2022) that can be tested on dandiset 000207, which holds spike times of medial temporal lobe neurons and the behaviour of 19 patients in a task with video clips that contain no boundary, a soft boundary or a hard boundary. It compares memory for temporal order and for scenes across the three kinds of clip, looks for boundary cells and event cells with the paper's definitions, counts neurons that respond to clip onsets, and measures how far the population state moves after a boundary. Eleven of the paper's twenty findings cannot be tested, because the dandiset has no local field potentials, no neurons outside the medial temporal lobe, no labels for the paper's cell classes, and no way to link test trials with soft or hard boundaries to the clips they came from.   • Trials without a response are kept as coded in the file; leaving them out moves no mean by more than 0.01.fig2a-order-accuracy
  • Paired tests over subjects are added to the paper's one-way ANOVA.fig2a-order-accuracy
  • Trials without a response have an RT of 0 in the file and are kept; leaving them out gives 2.08, 1.64 and 1.56 s.fig2b-order-reaction-time
  • Trials without a response have a confidence of 0 in the file and are kept; leaving them out gives 2.00, 2.48 and 2.55.fig2c-order-confidence
  • The paper does not say whether foils are counted. Targets and foils are; targets alone give 0.737, 0.718 and 0.688 (p = 0.59).edfig2a-recognition-similar
  • 549 units from 18 sessions against the paper's 580 from 19. Subject 19 is left out because its spike times are not on the clock of the trial tables, and the dandiset's units differ from the paper's in number and in their split by region.fig3c-boundary-cells
  • The paper's "permutation t-test" is implemented as a sign-flip test for before against after and a one-sided label-permutation test between conditions, with 1000 permutations.fig3c-boundary-cells
  • Soft-boundary trials are every soft boundary of a session, in SB clips and in HB clips (75 per session). The Methods do not say; this follows the authors' published plotting code (B01_raster_psth_encoding_BAligned_BSep_NWB.m, lines 29 to 37). The first soft boundary of each SB clip, used before that code was read, is kept in the notebook as an alternative.fig3c-boundary-cells
  • Each condition is tested on its own, five tests per class, which is the literal reading of the Methods. The authors' published code has no cell selection to check this against.fig3c-boundary-cells
  • 300 label shuffles for the chance level instead of 1000.fig3c-boundary-cells
  • The paper's one-tailed test does not state its direction; an increase is tested. A decrease is found in 3.1% of units at onset.edfig5-clip-onset-cells
  • The permutation test is a sign-flip test with 1000 permutations.edfig5-clip-onset-cells
  • Pseudo-trials are matched across subjects by clip name; the paper does not say how.fig5d-state-shift
  • Principal components are fitted on the three condition averages from 1 s before to 1.5 s after the boundary, giving 22 components for 99% of the variance against the paper's 55. The averages are of the same trials that are then compared.fig5d-state-shift
  • A trial is z-scored from 1 s before to 8 s after clip onset; the paper says "the entire trial".fig5d-state-shift
  • Clips with soft boundaries are aligned to their first soft boundary, one pseudo-trial per clip. The authors' published code does not cover this analysis.fig5d-state-shift
