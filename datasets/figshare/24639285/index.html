  • glass-specimen-set

    The deposit holds the measurements of the 22 Apollo 64455 glass subsamples that failed the paleointensity selection criteria, named as in Table 2, and replicate measurements for the twelve single crystals of Table 1.

    Plots for all the data not meeting paleointensity criteria1 (n=22) are provided26 with photomicrographs of specimens that were measured

    Background & Summary, Glass, paragraph 2; Methods, paragraph 2; Table 2; Data Records

    How it will be judged

    Specimens are matched by the names in Table 2 and Table 1 (s11 in Table 2 may be ss11; both are accepted). Holds when each named specimen has measurements with steps, moments, declinations and inclinations as the Data Records describe. Reported either way, which glass specimens have AF steps, compared with the reading of the Table 2 marks given in the opening comment (12 without, 10 with).

  • glass-success-rate

    Of the Apollo 64455 glass subsamples given thermal paleointensity experiments, 12% passed the selection criteria (3 of 25, 13 run without and 12 with AF pre-treatment).

    In the thermal studies1 of Apollo 64455 (Methods), the success rate was 12%

    Background & Summary, paragraph 3; Methods, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    The three successful specimens are in ref. 1 and its dataset, not in this deposit, so the rate is 3 over (3 plus the failed specimens found here). The split by AF pre-treatment is counted from AF steps in the deposit's failed specimens plus the Methods' count of successful ones (one without, two with AF). Holds when 22 failed specimens are found and 10 of them have AF steps.

  • table2-fit-statistics

    For the nine failed glass specimens with a line fit (ss19, ss43, ss22, ss41, ss6, ss9, ss10, ss30, ss34), the number of points, temperature range, MAD, R2 and pTRM check differences are as in Table 2, which lists the criteria each failed.

    Reasons for de-selection are provided in Table 2

    Methods, Paleointensity Selection criteria, paragraph 2; Table 2

    How it will be judged

    Per specimen, the field-off and field-on steps within the Table 2 temperature range (the range and n fix the points), NRM lost against TRM gained as in the opening comment, R2 of the least-squares line, MAD of a principal component fit to the field-off directions, and the pTRM check percentage at each listed temperature. MAD and pTRM check percentages are computed under each reading named in the opening comment; a value matches when one reading puts it within tolerance. Reproduced when n matches for all nine and at least 80% of the MAD, R2 and pTRM values match; partially reproduced for 50 to 80%. Whether each listed failure follows is reported against the stated thresholds (n of 4 or more, pTRM within 15%, R2 about 0.9), not judged, as ss6 is listed as failing R2 at 0.91 while ss43 at 0.90 is not, and no MAD threshold is given.

  • table2-no-fit

    The other thirteen failed glass specimens (g2, ss8, s11, ss18, ss24, ss27, ss29, ss36, ss38, ss39, ss45, ss46, ss48) gave no fit, most because they did not acquire TRM in a regular fashion and some because their directions trend out of the origin.

    We note that some specimens did not acquire TRM in a regular fashion, or showed directions that trended out of ... the origin of their respective orthogonal vector plots

    Methods, Paleointensity Selection criteria, paragraph 2; Table 2

    How it will be judged

    The paper gives no measure of regular TRM acquisition. Over each specimen's Table 2 range, TRM gained per step is computed as in the opening comment and the pTRM check percentages listed for these specimens are compared with Table 2 as in table2-fit-statistics. Regularity is read as (a) TRM gained increasing with each temperature step, or (b) a TRM gained against temperature curve with no step falling by more than 15% (the pTRM tolerance of the selection criteria). Reproduced when, under one reading, at least 9 of the 11 specimens marked "no regular TRM acquisition" show irregular acquisition, and ss36 and ss39 have field-off vectors whose distance from the origin grows over the last steps of the range. The counts under each reading are reported. The pTRM check comparison is reported and does not decide the outcome.

  • fig1-linear-components

    Glass subsamples ss43 and ss19, which failed the reliability criteria, show hints of linear directional components on orthogonal vector plots, from which nominal paleointensities can be calculated with the 18 uT laboratory field.

    some of these data reveal linear directional components, and nominal paleointensities can be calculated (Fig. 1)

    Background & Summary, Glass, paragraph 2; Figure 1

    How it will be judged

    Over the Table 2 ranges (ss43 250 to 400 C, ss19 175 to 450 C), MAD and R2 as in table2-fit-statistics, and the nominal paleointensity as the absolute slope times 18 uT (Figure 1 legend). The figure's values were not seen (no PDF), so none is expected. Holds when both MADs and R2 values match Table 2 under one reading; the nominal paleointensities are reported under each reading.

  • fig2-changing-field

    Glass subsamples ss41 and ss22 show field-off directions that change over a temperature interval, consistent with a changing ambient field during magnetization, and curved NRM lost against TRM gained plots.

    Other specimens show a changing vector, consistent with a changing ambient field (Fig. 2)

    Background & Summary, Glass, paragraph 2; Figure 2; Table 2

    How it will be judged

    The arrows marking the interval in Figure 2 were not seen, so the Table 2 ranges are used (ss41 500 to 580 C, ss22 250 to 500 C), with the earlier steps shown. The paper names no measure of a changing direction. Readings (a) the angle between the first and the last field-off direction of the range exceeds the MAD of a line fit over the range, (b) a great-circle fit over the range has a smaller MAD than a line fit. Reproduced when both specimens show a changing direction under one reading and R2 is within tolerance of Table 2 (0.38 each); the angles and MADs are reported.

  • glass-directions-not-random

    The field-off directions of the failed glass specimens, as seen on orthogonal vector plots, are not random, implying the presence of an ambient field.

    The directions, as viewed on orthogonal vector plots, are not random and imply the presence of an ambient field

    Background & Summary, Glass, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    The paper names no test. Per specimen, the field-off directions of its thermal steps are tested for randomness with Watson's test (n directions, resultant length R, p < 0.05), a standard choice. Reproduced when more than half of the 22 specimens reject randomness, partially reproduced for a quarter to a half. Because successive demagnetization steps of one remanence are not independent, the count is also given for the steps within each specimen's Table 2 range where one is printed.

  • glass-af-no-change

    For nearly all glass samples given AF pre-treatment, AF cleaning did not significantly change the NRM direction or magnitude.

    For nearly all samples, AF cleaning did not significantly change the NRM direction or magnitude

    Methods, paragraph 2

    How it will be judged

    Per specimen with AF steps, the angle between the NRM direction and the direction after the last AF step, and the ratio of the moments. The paper gives no threshold for "significantly". Readings (a) angle under 15 degrees and moment change under 15% (the pTRM check tolerance of the selection criteria), (b) angle under the specimen's Table 2 MAD where one is printed and moment change under 15%. Reproduced when, under one reading, all but at most two of the AF-treated specimens show no significant change.

  • table1-max-intensity

    For each of the twelve single crystals, the maximum field that cannot be excluded, given the magnetometer threshold of 8e-11 emu and the moment acquired at 590 C in a 20 uT field, is as in Table 1, between 0.20 and 0.51 uT.

    Considering a magnetometer threshold value (see Methods) the maximum field that cannot be excluded by the data can be calculated from the data for each crystal. These values are presented in Table 1

    Background & Summary, Single crystal measurements, paragraph 4; Table 1; Technical Validation, Example of threshhold detection limit

    How it will be judged

    Max intensity = 8e-11 emu / M590 x 20 uT (Technical Validation). M590 is computed under the readings named in the opening comment, (a) or (b) crossed with (i) or (ii). Reproduced when, under one reading, all 24 values are within tolerance; partially reproduced when at least 18 are. The reading that matches is reported, and whether Table 1 max intensities equal the formula applied to its own M590 column to two places.

  • crystal-14053g1-400c-stable

  • no-alteration-recording-efficiency

    Alteration during the laser heating of the single crystals was not significant; apparent recording efficiencies far above 100% were not observed and the alteration checks did not fail.

    If alteration had resulted in the formation of new magnetic minerals, apparent recording efficiencies ≫ 100% should have be observed (but these were not), and alteration checks should fail

    Background & Summary, paragraph 5

    How it will be judged

    The paper does not define recording efficiency or the failure of the alteration check for the crystals. Readings (a) the 590.3 moment does not exceed the 590.0 moment by more than the 8e-11 emu threshold, and the 590.3 directions are inconsistent as in fig3-alteration-check-inconsistent; (b) the 590.4 TRM (40 uT, vector difference from 590.3) is no more than about twice the 590.1 TRM (20 uT), as linear recording predicts; the paper gives no bound for "far above 100%", so the ratios are reported and a ratio of the 590.4 TRM to twice the 590.1 TRM above 2 is taken as failing, a choice not from the paper. Reproduced when every crystal passes under one reading; the ratios are reported. Recording efficiency against a theoretical TRM needs grain properties the paper does not give, so it is not computed.
