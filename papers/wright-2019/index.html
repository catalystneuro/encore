Wright et al. 2019 · eLife

Ventrolateral periaqueductal gray neurons prioritize threat probability over fear output

doi:10.7554/elife.45013 · paper key wright-2019

Datasets

  • CRCNS:pag-1

The paper's main findings held up in this data: ventrolateral periaqueductal gray neurons form Onset and Ramping populations of the reported sizes, both discriminate danger, uncertainty and safety, and single-trial regressions show their firing tracks threat probability over fear output. Several regression results hold only under the nose poke baselines and 0/0 rule that reproduce the paper's printed regression table on one neuron, which the paper does not state. The Figure 4 single-neuron correlations are no stronger than the Z-scoring produces. Seven claims held only in part, among them when danger and uncertainty firing diverge, the count of Onset-Ramping pairs and how many neurons' delay and post-shock betas correlate (3 of 20 against 13). No claim was blocked.

2026-11-11 · on CRCNS:pag-1

  • ✓Reproduced Over the 88 recording sessions, suppression ratio over the whole 10 s cue differs by cue, high to danger (0.80), intermediate to uncertainty (0.53) and low to safety (0.03), and each cue pair differs.
  • ✓Reproduced Suppression scales non-linearly with shock probability. Uncertainty's shock probability is closer to safety, but its mean suppression ratio is closer to danger.
  • ✓Reproduced Suppression ratios in each 1 s cue interval discriminate every cue pair in every interval, with a cue effect and a cue x interval interaction.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Baseline nose poke rates do not differ between trial types, and poking falls from baseline to cue for danger and uncertainty but not for safety.
  • ✓Reproduced Suppression ratio and cue nose poke rate are significantly correlated across sessions, particularly for danger and uncertainty.
  • ✓Reproduced K-means clustering (k = 2) of all 245 neurons on baseline firing rate, waveform half duration and amplitude ratio splits them on baseline firing rate alone into a low firing rate cluster of 199 and a high firing rate cluster of 46.
  • ✓Reproduced 29 neurons (from 5 of 6 rats, about 12% of all) increase firing in the first 1 s of danger, uncertainty or safety over the 2 s pre-cue baseline, and all of them are in the LFR cluster. These are the Onset population.
  • ✓Reproduced Of the remaining neurons, 17 increase firing over baseline in the last 1 s of at least one cue; three are in the HFR cluster, and the other 14 (from 4 of 6 rats, about 6%) are the Ramping population.
  • ✓Reproduced Onset population firing (100 ms bins, 1 s before to the end of the cue) shows main effects of cue and bin and a cue x bin interaction.
  • ✓Reproduced In the first 1 s cue interval Onset neurons fire more to danger than to uncertainty; firing to uncertainty is numerically but not significantly above safety, while an ANOVA over the first 5 s of uncertainty and safety shows a cue x bin interaction.
  • ✓Reproduced Differential firing of Onset neurons is absent in the last 1 s cue interval, for danger against uncertainty and uncertainty against safety.
  • ✓Reproduced Ramping population firing (100 ms bins over the cue) shows main effects of cue and bin and a cue x bin interaction, with ramping greatest to danger, intermediate to uncertainty and absent to safety.
  • ✓Reproduced Ramping neurons show no differential firing in the first 1 s cue interval.
  • ✓Reproduced In the last 1 s cue interval Ramping neurons fire more to danger than to uncertainty and more to uncertainty than to safety.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Ramping population firing departs from safety earlier for danger (2.8 s after cue onset at p < 0.05, 5.8 s with Bonferroni correction) than for uncertainty (5.7 s, 6.5 s).
  • ✓Reproduced The rate of firing increase from the departure window to the last 1 s window does not differ between danger and uncertainty, so the earlier departure, not a faster rise, separates them.
  • ◐Partially reproduced The population patterns hold trial by trial. Onset population firing in the first 1 s exceeds baseline on every individual trial of every cue, and Ramping population firing in the last 1 s exceeds baseline on danger and uncertainty trials but not safety trials.
  • ✓Reproduced Onset and Ramping firing is not driven by nose poke cessation. Across neurons, firing to danger (Onset first interval, Ramping last interval) is uncorrelated with firing to the first second of an ITI poke cessation and is consistently greater.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In the four sessions with an Onset and a Ramping neuron recorded together, trial by trial firing of the two is not negatively related; a significant negative beta appears for one interval of one pair only.
  • ✓Reproduced In the first 1 s interval, Onset single units' danger and uncertainty firing are correlated and biased to danger; uncertainty and safety firing are correlated but the bias to uncertainty is not significant; danger firing is biased to the first over the last interval, with no correlation between them.
  • ✓Reproduced The most common pattern of Onset neurons in the first 1 s interval is danger > uncertainty > safety (14 neurons), the only pattern with more neurons than expected by chance.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In the last 1 s interval, Ramping single units' danger and uncertainty firing are correlated and biased to danger; uncertainty and safety firing are not correlated but are biased to uncertainty; danger firing is biased to the last over the first interval, with no correlation between them.
  • ✓Reproduced The most common pattern of Ramping neurons in the last 1 s interval is danger > uncertainty > safety (11 neurons), the only pattern above chance.
  • ✓Reproduced In the first 1 s cue interval the Onset population's probability beta is positive and significant and exceeds both fear betas, neither of which differs from zero.
  • ✓Reproduced Onset single-unit betas are positively biased for threat probability but not for total or interval fear.
  • ✓Reproduced Over the ten 1 s cue intervals, Onset threat probability signaling is highest in the first interval, persists several seconds and diminishes, while total and interval fear account for firing in no interval; an ANOVA shows a main effect of regressor and a regressor x interval interaction.
  • ✓Reproduced Onset neurons prioritize probability whatever the combination of regressors; a fear beta reaches significance only when total fear is the sole regressor, and even then its relation is weaker than that of probability.
  • ✓Reproduced Varying the probability assigned to uncertainty from 0 to 1 in 0.125 steps, the Onset population's mean probability beta in the first interval peaks at the actual 0.375, with the flanking values next highest and betas falling off toward the extremes.
  • ✓Reproduced Onset firing is better captured by the actual shock probability (uncertainty 0.375) than by a binary regressor equating uncertainty with safety, in the first interval and across the cue.
  • ✓Reproduced In the last 1 s cue interval, Ramping probability and total fear betas both exceed the interval fear beta and do not differ from each other.
  • ✓Reproduced Ramping single-unit betas in the last interval are biased positive for probability and total fear, which are uncorrelated; they are not biased for interval fear, and the probability and interval fear betas are negatively correlated.
  • ✓Reproduced Over all ten cue intervals Ramping neurons prioritize probability (mean beta 0.58, against 0.12 for total fear and -0.03 for interval fear); only the probability beta differs from zero and it exceeds both fear betas, with a main effect of regressor and a regressor x interval interaction.
  • ◐Partially reproduced For Ramping neurons, the prioritization of probability over all ten intervals holds when probability is compared with total fear or interval fear alone, and when each regressor is used in isolation.
  • ✓Reproduced The Ramping population's tuning curve for the last interval is shifted right of the actual probability, peaking at 0.625, consistent with mixed signaling of probability and fear.
  • ✓Reproduced In the 5 s after cue offset (delay, shock, post-shock; the shock itself excluded) Onset neurons show no effects of trial type or bin, while Ramping neurons keep firing to danger and uncertainty through the delay, with a trial x bin interaction, and the two populations differ.
  • ✓Reproduced After cue offset Onset neurons signal neither interval fear nor probability; no effect of regressor or regressor x interval interaction, and no beta differs from zero.
  • ✓Reproduced Ramping neurons signal probability throughout the delay (the first four 500 ms intervals after cue offset), and this signal diminishes after shock delivery, with a regressor x interval interaction.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Probability betas of the delay intervals correlate with those of the post-shock intervals in 13 of 20 comparisons for Onset neurons but 4 of 20 for Ramping neurons, a significant difference (6 and 1 of 20 after Bonferroni correction, also different).
  • ✓Reproduced The three late-responsive neurons of the HFR cluster each fire most to danger in the last 1 s cue interval.
