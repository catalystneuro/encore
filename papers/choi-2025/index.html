Choi et al. 2025 · Journal of Neuroscience Methods

Aseptic, semi-sealed cranial chamber implants for chronic multi-channel neurochemical and electrophysiological neural recording in nonhuman primates

Jiwon Choi, Usamma Amjad, Raymond Murray, Ritesh Shrivastav, Tobias Teichert, Baldwin Goodell, Matthew Olson, David J. Schaeffer, Julia Oluoch, Helen N. Schwerdt

Outcomes of 13 claims

  • ✓4 reproduced
  • ◐1 partially reproduced
  • ⊘8 blocked

Summary: The recordings that demonstrate the chamber held up in this data: dopamine rose more after large than small reward cues, spiking was locked to the value cue, and the single-trial dopamine and unit waveform panels passed, the last on a weak test. The stronger beta-band suppression after large reward cues held only in direction (p = 0.112); the paper does not describe this analysis, so its band and windows here are the reproduction's choices. The paper's results on the chamber itself, recordings in two monkeys, electrode yields, asepsis cultures, drainage and bone growth, could not be tested, because the dataset holds one session of one monkey.

    In a single trial (trial 133 of the session), dopamine concentration changes at site c8ds fluctuate, especially after the value cue display.

    In trial id 133 (row 132, a large successful trial) the SD of the dopamine trace referenced at V is 4.414 over…

    Figure 8A and its legendfig8a-single-trial-dopamine
    From the paper

    Single trial measurements of dopamine concentration changes (Δ[DA]) as a function of time relative to value cue display (V) at 0 s (trial 133 in the session). ... Δ[DA] fluctuations are especially visible after the value cue display.

    Result

    In trial id 133 (row 132, a large successful trial) the SD of the dopamine trace referenced at V is 4.414 over 0 to 5 s after V and 2.180 over the 5 s before, ratio 2.025. Under the other reading (row 133, a small trial, with 17 and 22 NaN samples in the two windows) the ratio is 2.199. Over the 941 successful trials with a ratio (9 of 950 are NaN) the median ratio is 1.259 (IQR 0.804 to 1.885), 62.7% are above 1, and trial 133 lies at the 0.775 quantile. The referenced trace of trial 133 is printed from 5 s before to 5 s after V; it dips to -1.84 about 1.1 s before V and rises to 13.59 about 1.9 s after. The paper gives no values to compare with. The trial-aligned FSCV lags the raw scans (iox best matches the raw current about 1.5 s earlier, and da follows iox by a further 0.1 to 0.4 s in 13 trials), so part of the rise after V may come from changes in the raw current just before V.

    Deviations from the paper’s method
    • The stored PCA estimate (`da`, units not given) is used; the paper's color plot of the voltammograms cannot be compared, as the background-subtracted voltammograms of the trial are not in the file.

  • ✓Reproduced

    Trial-averaged dopamine at site c8ds, aligned to the value cue, increases more for large than for small reward trials, especially between the value cue display and reward.

    From V to RW the referenced dopamine averages 1.853 in 448 large and 1.095 in 498 small successful trials…

    Figure 8B and its legendquestion to the readerfig8b-dopamine-large-vs-small
    From the paper

    Trial averaged Δ[DA] aligned to V at 0 s for large and small reward trials, showing increased dopamine release for large in comparison to small reward trials, especially between the value cue display and reward (RW) event.

    Result

    From V to RW the referenced dopamine averages 1.853 in 448 large and 1.095 in 498 small successful trials, difference of means 0.758, two-sided Mann-Whitney p = 0.0028; 4 trials dropped as their window holds only NaN. Variants, RW to RW + 3 s 1.546 (p = 0.00012); 0 to 2 s after V 0.091 (p = 0.57). V to RW in the first half of the session 0.778 (p = 0.051) and in the second 0.745 (p = 0.018). By 1 s windows the large minus small difference is 0.33 or less until 2 s after V, 1.15 to 1.44 from 2 to 4 s, 1.32 from 4 to 5 s and 1.57 to 1.68 from 5 to 8 s (RW is 4.72 to 5.50 s after V). The criterion tests the V to RW window only. On the statement's "especially between the value cue display and reward", the difference arises between V and RW (from 0.33 at 1 to 2 s after V to 1.44 at 3 to 4 s) and is then held at about 1.6; referenced at RW it grows by only 0.19, 0.48 and 0.25 per 1 s window over 0 to 3 s after RW, and the RW to RW + 3 s window is referenced at V, so it carries the difference built up before RW. Only the level of the difference is as large after RW as before. A question to a reader asks whether the criterion should test this part. The trial-aligned FSCV lags the raw scans by about 1.5 s or more (data check), so the null 0 to 2 s variant may in part reflect this delay. The paper reports no test.

    Deviations from the paper’s method
    • The trace is referenced at V by subtracting the stored estimate's value there, as the criterion says, in place of the paper's background subtraction of the voltammograms at V.
    • Trials are classed from the events, leaving out the last row (id 1411), which has no next trial start and which the paper's Zenodo lists leave out; this gives the lists exactly (451 large, 499 small).

  • ✓Reproduced

    Spikes recorded at site c5d on a chamber-implanted CFE can be shown as a raster and a peristimulus time histogram aligned to the value cue display, for large and small reward trials.

    All 950 successful trials have a spike from 1 s before to 3 s after V.

    Figure 9A and its legend; Results, section 3.5, paragraph 4fig9a-spikes-task-aligned
    From the paper

    Raster plot of spikes detected on each trial (y-axis) as a function of time relative to value cue display (V) at 0 s (site c5d). ... Peristimulus time histogram (PSTH) showing average spike firing rate (y-axis) for the large (red) and small (blue) reward conditions

    Result

    All 950 successful trials have a spike from 1 s before to 3 s after V. The variance across the 40 bins of the PSTH is 0.4807 (spikes/s)^2; the 95th percentile over 200 random alignments is 0.0574 under the claim's seed and 0.0574 to 0.0607 under the seed plus 1000 to 4000, and no random alignment reached the observed value under any of the five seeds. The rate falls to 3.8 spikes/s 0.1 to 0.2 s after V and rises to 7.7 to 7.8 spikes/s 0.3 to 0.5 s after V (baseline about 5.5). V falls at every phase of the FSCV scan cycle (77 to 108 trials per 10 ms bin) and each 100 ms bin spans one whole cycle, so the spike-free gap of each scan cannot make this fall and rise. The stored times (0.4697) and the Plexon unit (0.4829) give the same result. The large and small PSTHs differ by at most 2.03 spikes/s in a bin, mean difference -0.050 spikes/s over -1 to 3 s; the paper states no difference, and none is judged.

    Deviations from the paper’s method
    • Spike times are moved by the offset measured in the data check, 0.5 ms (raw ephys clock later than the trial clock), not by the 11812.1 s the claims file's opening comment reports for an earlier version of the dandiset; in this version every stream is on one clock.

  • ✓Reproduced

    The spikes at site c5d have a consistent waveform (mean and standard deviation) and an interspike interval distribution (1 ms bins) consistent with a single unit.

    None of the 101732 interspike intervals of thresholded unit 1 is shorter than 1 ms (shortest 1.562 ms…

    Figure 9B and 9C and their legendsquestion to the readerfig9bc-unit-waveform-isi
    From the paper

    Average spike waveform + /− standard deviation. C. Interspike interval (ISI) histogram showing the distribution of the timing between spikes detected. Bin widths are 1 ms.

    Result

    None of the 101732 interspike intervals of thresholded unit 1 is shorter than 1 ms (shortest 1.562 ms, fraction under 2 ms 0.00008). The mean waveform's largest absolute value is 56.36 uV (negative) at sample 10 of 48, 4.79 times the SD across spikes at that sample (11.76 uV); 4.78 and 5.05 in the two halves of the session. The refractory test cannot fail on these spikes, as the shortest interval is about the waveform length (1.50 ms), so the detector may not report a second spike within one waveform. The Plexon unit has 2 intervals under 1 ms (0.00002). Both tests of the criterion hold, so the outcome is reproduced; that the unit is a single neuron is not shown by them, and the paper does not state it (the claim's statement does); a question to a reader asks whether the statement and criterion should change.

    Trial-averaged beta-band LFP power at c7b referenced to c5c is suppressed more in the brief window after the value cue for large than for small reward cues.

    Median log10 ratio of 13 to 30 Hz power over 0 to 0.5 s after V to the 0.5 s before is -0.156 in 451 large and…

    Figure 9D and its legendfig9d-beta-suppression-large
    From the paper

    Trial averaged beta-band LFP power showing increased suppression during the brief time window immediately following the cue (V) for large as compared to small reward cues (site c7b-c5c).

    Result

    Median log10 ratio of 13 to 30 Hz power over 0 to 0.5 s after V to the 0.5 s before is -0.156 in 451 large and -0.125 in 499 small trials, difference of medians -0.031, two-sided Mann-Whitney p = 0.112; no trial dropped. Both medians are below 0 and large is lower, with p >= 0.05, which the criterion calls partially reproduced. Variants with the N = 4 filter, 0 to 0.3 s -0.0001 (p = 0.59), 0 to 1 s -0.008 (p = 0.83), 1 s baseline -0.023 (p = 0.12), 15 to 30 Hz -0.027 (p = 0.066), 12 to 25 Hz -0.021 (p = 0.20). With N = 2 the 15 to 30 Hz band gives -0.035 (p = 0.052) and the others p 0.11 to 0.81. First half of the session -0.183 against -0.134 (p = 0.116), second half -0.126 against -0.115 (p = 0.545). The trial-averaged power ratio dips lower for large trials (to 0.532, against 0.695; mean over 0 to 0.5 s 0.841, against 0.885), which resembles the paper's figure; the criterion compares medians of the per-trial log ratios.

    Deviations from the paper’s method
    • The 4th order Butterworth band-pass of the criterion is designed with N = 4 (scipy's and MATLAB's convention, a standard choice); N = 2 is shown as a variant.
    • The stored LFP is used, as the criterion says. It equals the raw c7b minus c5c except at the FSCV scan rate (10 Hz) and its harmonics, which dominate the raw signal and are removed from the stored one (data check, spectra); this is why it carries only 0.14 to 0.17 of the raw SD in 13 to 30 Hz. The beta band thus has gaps at 20 and 30 Hz. The paper's preprocessing is not described; the Zenodo record calls it spectral interpolation.

  • Methods not availableNewThe paper describes none of the analyses of Figures 8 and 9 beyond the legends and reports no test of the large against small differences. For Figure 9D it gives no beta band, filter, power measure, baseline or window for "the brief time window immediately following the cue". Questions for the authors, which band, filter and windows were used, and was the difference in Figure 9D tested?   • openasked 2026-11-25about fig9bc-unit-waveform-isi

    The statement says the interspike interval distribution is "consistent with a single unit", but the legend of Figure 9B and 9C only describes the average waveform and the ISI histogram, and the paper nowhere calls the spikes at c5d a single unit. Should the statement drop "consistent with a single unit", and should the criterion still include a refractory test, given that the thresholded spikes have no interval shorter than the waveform window?

    Passage. B. Average spike waveform + /− standard deviation. C. Interspike interval (ISI) histogram showing the distribution of the timing between spikes detected. Bin widths are 1 ms. (Figure 9 legend)

    Why it matters. The verifier (round 1) found the refractory test cannot fail, as the shortest interval (1.562 ms) is about the 1.5 ms waveform window, and threshold-selected spikes tend to pass the waveform test. The outcome, reproduced, then says little about a claim the paper may not make. A statement limited to what the legend shows would be reproduced as well; a statement about a single unit would need a test the data can fail.

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked 2026-11-25about fig8b-dopamine-large-vs-small

    The statement keeps the legend's "especially between the value cue display and reward", but the criterion tests only that the V to RW mean is larger for large trials. Should the criterion also compare the V to RW difference with the difference after RW, so that the "especially" part is tested?

    Passage. Trial averaged Δ[DA] aligned to V at 0 s for large and small reward trials, showing increased dopamine release for large in comparison to small reward trials, especially between the value cue display and reward (RW) event. (Figure 8B legend)

    Why it matters. The criterion holds (difference 0.758, p = 0.0028). The difference arises between V and RW (1 s windows from V, 0.33 at 1 to 2 s, 1.44 at 3 to 4 s; RW is 4.72 to 5.50 s after V) and is then held (1.57 to 1.68 from 5 to 8 s after V; RW to RW + 3 s referenced at V, 1.546, p = 0.00012). Whether the "especially" part holds depends on whether it means where the difference arises or where it is largest, which the criterion does not say.

    No reader has answered it yet.
