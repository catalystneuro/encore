Summary: The paper's electrical findings held in this data, its dopamine findings only weakly or not at all, and its implant yield and longevity numbers could not be tested. LFP beta power was more suppressed after large-reward and contralateral cues, though reward and cue side change together across blocks, and a well-isolated sorted unit was found in monkey P. Dopamine was higher after large-reward cues, but weakly (p = 0.023), and the raw voltammetric current showed no dopamine redox signature. The yields and recording for over a year could not be tested without implant dates or sensor lists. The second dopamine channel is missing, and monkey P's event codes have no names, so its reward-related spiking could not be tested.

Questions about the claims 1

Where the author or a verifier believed the claims file misread the paper, they asked, citing the paper, and a reader who had not seen the data answered from the paper alone, amending the claims file where it was wrong.   • openasked about fig4b-beta-reward

    The claims file's method choices compute beta power as "the squared Hilbert envelope" of the 13 to 28 Hz signal, smoothed with a 0.25 s Hanning window. The Methods list the steps in the other order, squared and then enveloped. Should the beta power follow the paper's order (square the band-passed signal, then take its envelope, then smooth)? The same definition is used by fig3b-beta-bursts, fig4c-beta-side and zenodo-link.

    Passage. Methods, Analysis, "Signals were bandpass filtered around 13 to 28 Hz, squared, enveloped, and then smoothed with a Hanning window of 0.25 s, as done previously."

    Why it matters. The two orders give different amplitudes. Raised by the round-1 verifier, who notes that the beta power computed under the claims file ranges from 14 to 2184e-12 V^2 on the paper's example trial, against 36 to 1560e-12 in the paper's Zenodo trace. In the attempt the paper's order, with MATLAB's default envelope, matches the Zenodo trace less closely (r = 0.913 against 0.942) and gives the same outcomes for fig3b-beta-bursts, fig4b-beta-reward and fig4c-beta-side (p = 4.3e-07, 5.42e-06 and 0.00734), so the reading is unlikely to change an outcome, but the claims file states an order the paper does not.

    No reader has answered it yet.

✓Reproduced The dandiset reproduces the processed data the paper deposited on Zenodo; the trial selections and the beta power of the paper's example trial computed from the dandiset agree with the paper's own files.
Trials matched by number. The Zenodo small and left lists agree with the selections decoded here for all 602 and 594 trials; the large and right lists for 569 of 570 and 577 of 578 (99.82% and 99.83%). The one trial only here is trial 1501, whose 60 s window ends at 24978.4 s, after the end of the LFP at 24965.4 s, which likely explains its absence from the paper's lists. The beta power computed here for the 35th large-reward trial (trial 84) correlates with the Zenodo trace at r = 0.9421 at lag 0 and 0.9427 at the best lag of 6 ms, median ratio 0.943. The dataset's measures match the paper's published values (the trace) and the dataset's own trial codes give the paper's lists, so the link holds on both sides. The example file's field trial35bigrewardata holds the time axis, not the LFP, so the raw example trace could not be compared. The four Zenodo lists are in ascending trial order without repeats, so their 35th entry is the 35th trial. The trace criterion was written after r = 0.94 was known, so that part is not an independent check. With the paper's order read literally (squared, then enveloped), r = 0.9132 at lag 0 and the median ratio is 1.689; the claims file's order matches the Zenodo trace more closely, though its range (14.1 to 2184.5e-12 V^2) is wider than Zenodo's (36.0 to 1559.6e-12).
