Encoding of Multiple Reward-Related Computations in Transient and Sustained High-Frequency Activity in Human OFC

Ignacio Sáez, Jack J. Lin, Arjen Stolk, Edward F. Chang, Josef Parvizi, Gerwin Schalk, Robert Thomas Knight, Ming Hsu

  • ⊘6 blocked

The patients' choice behaviour held up in this data, as did the finding that orbitofrontal high-frequency activity carries the previous round's choices and outcomes but not earlier rounds. The electrode counts behind the encoding results did not: far more electrodes encoded current choice and outcome variables than reported (risk 84 against 50), most likely because the paper does not fully describe its selection rule. The overlap of signal types within electrodes did not match either. Past-trial signals outlasted current ones but were shorter than reported, and several results depend on how the deposited activity was baseline-corrected. The anatomical findings and the comparisons with healthy participants and broadband power could not be tested, as those data are not deposited.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Methods not availableThe encoding rule as the Methods give it (p < 0.05 in 5 or more consecutive 200-ms windows stepped by 50 ms, anywhere in either 3-s epoch) classifies 18.2% of electrodes as encoding when the labels are shuffled, more than Table S2 reports for chosen value and past risk. How do the 1,000 shuffles enter the selection? Requiring each electrode to beat its own shuffled null gives counts all below Table S2; the plain rule gives counts above it. Were the windows limited to part of the epochs, or the p-values taken from the shuffles?
  • Methods not availableFor the stepwise regression, is the longest significant stretch taken within each epoch or across both, and on which HFA (which window or average) are the nested models compared by ANOVA? With a Bonferroni correction over 192 electrodes and 10,000 shuffles, 16 of 191 electrodes keep any regressor here.
  • Methods not availableFor the nested mixed-effects models, what is the dependent variable in time (each window or an average), are the regressors entered alone or together (gamble is the sum of the win and loss indices, so all 16 cannot be estimated together), are random slopes included, and over what is the Bonferroni correction made? With fixed slopes over electrodes that encode in opposite directions, the result depends strongly on these choices and on the baseline here (3 to 14 of 16 regressors at corrected p < 10^-4 with the deposited HFA, 16 of 16 per window after subtracting each epoch's own 200-ms pre-event baseline).
  • Methods not availableThe delays and durations of choice and outcome encoding (512.5 and 287 ms; 612.15 and 712.5 ms) are not defined. Are they per regressor, from the mean %EV profile or the share of encoding electrodes, and is the buttonpress epoch locked to the button press or to the outcome reveal?
  • Methods not availableSTAR Methods say epochs were made using a 200-ms baseline but not which 200 ms or how it was removed. The deposited game epochs are already corrected over [-1000, -800] ms around game presentation, before the fixation cross. Is that the correction the published analyses used, and was the buttonpress epoch corrected with the same window? Subtracting each epoch's own 200 ms before its event changes the outcomes of five claims here (fig4-past-encoding, fig5-past-sustained, figs5d-sharp-boundary, figs6b-stepwise-similar and mixed-effects-robust).
