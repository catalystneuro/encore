Quantified Morphology of the Cervical and Subdiaphragmatic Vagus Nerves of Human, Pig, and Rat

Summary: Most of the paper's main findings held up in this data: the nerve and fascicle diameters, fascicle numbers and tissue proportions of the human, pig and rat vagus at both levels, rat nerves about ten times smaller, and perineurium thickness rising with fascicle diameter. One did not: cervical and subdiaphragmatic perineurium fits differ in humans, even with samples taken into account, where the paper reports no difference and names no test. Cervical fascicles were larger in all three species, though the pig contrast was weaker (p = 0.012 against 0.0009). Comparisons with published values held in part. Minor claims on age trends, fat in pig nerves and one peanut fascicle count did not match.
