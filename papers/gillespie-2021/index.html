Gillespie et al. 2021 · Neuron

Gillespie et al (2021) Hippocampal replay reflects specific past experiences rather than a plan for subsequent choice

doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2021.07.029 · paper key gillespie-2021

Datasets

  • DANDI:000115

Only the behavioural findings could be tested, and they held; the replay findings could not be tested. In all four rats the reward rate after a goal change started at or below chance and rose (from at most 0.133 on trial 1 to at least 0.584 by trial 15), and the session and block counts matched the paper's within 10%. The central findings that ripple replay favours the previous goal, persists across blocks and does not predict the next choice could not be tested. The dandiset holds only raw broadband, with no spikes, ripple times or decoder output, and streaming it was too slow.

  • ✓Reproduced The analysis includes 24, 32, 23 and 33 behavioural sessions and 89, 85, 105 and 166 trial blocks for the four subjects.
  • ✓Reproduced Aligned to the goal change, the outer-arm reward rate starts at chance (0.125) and rises; aligned to the first reward at the new goal, it is well above chance, so all subjects keep visiting a goal once found.
  • ⊘Blocked SWRs occur mainly during reward consumption at the home port, the center ports and the rewarded goal arm, and are rarely detected at unrewarded outer ports.
  • ⊘Blocked During movement the decoded position (posterior maximum) is usually on the same or an adjacent maze segment as the real position, and the median deviation between them is less than one 5 cm bin, on every maze segment.
  • ⊘Blocked Decoding error on the goal arm shows no systematic change over the course of a trial block, so repeated reward does not change spatial coding at the ensemble level.
  • ⊘Blocked About 95% of SWRs contain spatially continuous representations.
  • ⊘Blocked A history-independent Bayesian decoder with 20 ms bins and no movement model assigns more than 90% of SWRs to the same maze segment as the state space decoder.
  • ⊘Blocked A substantial fraction of all detected SWRs contain remote replay, 35.1%, 41.1%, 34.5% and 47.2% for the four subjects.
  • ⊘Blocked The large majority of spatially continuous replay events occur in the box area, before the arm choice, and include local and remote events.
  • ⊘Blocked In search trials where the past, future and previous goal arms are distinct, the previous goal arm is consistently and strongly over-represented in pre-choice remote replay, about 20% of these events, in all subjects.
  • ⊘Blocked In those search trials the immediately past arm is replayed far less often than chance.
  • ⊘Blocked In those search trials the upcoming (future) arm shows a small but significant enrichment in two of the four subjects only.
  • ⊘Blocked Each of the other arms is replayed at about chance per arm, yet the other arms together make up the majority of pre-choice remote replay.
  • ⊘Blocked In repeat trials where past, future and current goal are the same arm and the previous goal is distinct, all animals still strongly over-replay the previous goal, while the future (past and goal) arm shows only a small and variable effect; other arms together are again the majority.
  • ⊘Blocked In a Poisson GLM over all search trials, being the previous goal roughly doubles an arm's replay rate, the strongest effect; the past arm is consistently replayed less; the future arm has a small increase significant in three of four subjects.
  • ⊘Blocked In a Poisson GLM over all repeat trials the previous goal arm is again the strongest and most consistent driver of replay and the past arm is reliably replayed less.
  • ⊘Blocked Replay of the current goal is consistently enhanced only late in the repeat phase (five or more rewards at the goal), while the effects of the other three categories do not differ much between early and late repeat trials.
  • ⊘Blocked On correct repeat trials, the number of current goal replays correlates positively with the number of rewards already received at that goal in every subject, with low R2.
  • ⊘Blocked Replay of the future arm occurs on average less than once per trial, and more often in search than in repeat trials for three of the four subjects.
