Xue et al. 2022 · Journal of Neural Engineering

Inferring monosynaptic connections from paired dendritic spine Ca 2+ imaging and large-scale recording of extracellular spiking

doi:10.1088/1741-2552/ac8765 · paper key xue-2022

Datasets

  • DANDI:000223

Most main findings held in this data, but the acceptance count and the OUT/IN ratios did not. The recordings gave large F-scores, best-R values near Table 1 for most spines, and the negative link between spike contrast and F-score. Acceptance of connections gave 7 spines instead of 9, probably because the background regions used for the noise estimate are not in the dataset. Accepted spines still had higher best-R than rejected ones, though best-R alone did not separate them. OUT/IN matched Table 1 in only 6 of 19 recordings, probably because the paper does not say how it was computed, and its correlation with F-score was weaker than reported.

  • ✓Reproduced The paper made 19 paired spine Ca2+ imaging and HD-MEA recordings in 10 cultures, tested 20 spines (one per recording, two in one recording), and the recordings have a mean of 102 ± 43 sorted units each.
  • ◐Partially reproduced For each of the 20 tested spines, the best-R (the Pearson R of the demixed spine trace with the convolved trace of its best-matching unit, outside network bursts) has the value given in Table 1, from 0.148 to 0.765.
  • ✓Reproduced In the example recording of Figure 1(e), the best-R shows the largest increase over the next ordered R value among all units.
  • ◐Partially reproduced For each recording, simulated ground-truth connections of every unit give an F-score surface over the quantile pair (Q_test, Q_GT), whose maximum and optimal quantiles are those of Table 1 (max_F 0.485 to 0.870).
  • ◐Partially reproduced The simulated ground-truth test gives large F-scores across experiments, a mean maximum F-score of 0.692 ± 0.136 over 19 recordings with 1'000 surrogates per unit.
  • ◐Partially reproduced The best-matching unit passed the acceptance test, and a monosynaptic connection was accepted, for 9 of the 20 spines (Table 1 rows 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 10, 12 and 16).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Accepted connections have larger best-R values than rejected ones (0.584 ± 0.149 against 0.331 ± 0.124; Mann-Whitney U = 12.0, n1 = 9, n2 = 11, P < 0.05).
  • ◐Partially reproduced The best-R alone does not predict the outcome of the acceptance test; connections with R as high as about 0.6 were rejected, while an R of about 0.2 was enough for acceptance in another case.
  • ✓Reproduced Connections were rejected in three scenarios, best-R below the test threshold only, below the GT threshold only, or below both, which shows the advantage of having two thresholds.
  • ✓Reproduced For some recordings the test threshold is larger than the GT threshold, and for others the reverse.
  • ?Not reproduced Each recording's spike contrast (0.17 to 0.69) and ratio of spikes outside to inside network bursts (0.060 to 2.003) have the values given in Table 1.
  • ✓Reproduced The network's spike contrast is negatively correlated with the maximum F-score of the recording (Spearman R = -0.737, P = 0.0002), so more synchronous networks give poorer classification.
  • ◐Partially reproduced The ratio of spikes outside to inside network bursts is positively correlated with the maximum F-score of the recording (Spearman R = 0.672, P = 0.0012).
