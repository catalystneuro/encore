Hart et al. 2023 · Cell

Sparse and stereotyped encoding implicates a core glomerulus for ant alarm behavior

doi:10.1016/j.cell.2023.05.025 · paper key hart-2023

Datasets

  • DANDI:000467

Of the 17 findings tested here, 3 held up: 3-hexanone gives the broadest response, and the alarm pheromones activate few glomeruli, more of them at the higher concentration. Most others did not reproduce, including that the vehicle gives no response, a single glomerulus shared by all three panic pheromones, and the stereotyped positions of PGb, 6G and PGa, which were identified in only 5, 2 and 0 of 13 ants. The paper drew its regions of interest (ROIs) by eye, and the readings that would stand in for that disagree, so the counts depend on the reading adopted. The behaviour, transgenesis and confocal findings could not be tested because those data are not in the dataset. The single-glomerulus timing results rest on a second identification that found the third glomerulus (PGa) in only 4 ants.

  • ⊘Blocked After the vehicle control the initial nest pile persists far longer than after any native alarm pheromone.
