Sit et al. 2023 · Nature Communications

Dataset for "Coregistration of heading to visual cues in retrosplenial cortex"

doi:10.1038/s41467-023-37704-5 · paper key sit-2023

Datasets

  • DANDI:000483

Some of the paper's main findings hold in this data, but its three cell classes do not. The heading-selective fraction under controlled rotation (15.3%, paper 14.2%) and unimodal ADN versus bimodal visual axon tuning in light both reproduced, though the selection test is liberal and the shuffled control was not met. Coherent remapping and shared head and chamber rotation preferences held only partly, because matched cells did no better than random pairs. The three functional classes did not reproduce, and decoding from all tuned cells gave 0.71 against 0.82, with the decoder code missing from the linked repository. The free-chamber heading fraction could not be tested because those recordings are not in the dataset.

  • ⊘Blocked In head-fixed mice moving a free floating chamber with a single cue, 14.0% of RSC neurons (373 of 2665) are heading selective, with no head movement.
  • ✓Reproduced With the chamber rotated by a motor (single cue), 14.2% of RSC neurons (452 of 3179, 16 recordings over 5 mice) are heading selective by the paper's reliability and Gaussian-fit test, a percentage similar to the free-chamber experiments.
  • ?Not reproduced Run on pre-shuffled data, the heading-selection test passes no cells, so the count of heading-selective cells is not due to chance.
  • ◐Partially reproduced The preferred headings of heading-selective cells tile the whole circumference of the chamber, including headings with the cue outside the visual field, with a moderate overrepresentation near 0 degrees, where the cue is centered in front of the mouse.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Matched ROIs have on average the same preferred heading relative to the wall pattern whether the head is rotated in a fixed chamber or the chamber is rotated around a fixed head (V-test against 0 degrees, V = 14.1, p = 2.5e-4), though not all cells keep their preference.
  • ◐Partially reproduced When all the wall cues are replaced, heading cells remap coherently, each shifting its preferred heading by about the same angle as the rest of the population in its recording (V-test of the Delta phase offsets against 0 degrees, V = 25.6, p = 4.9e-6).
  • ✓Reproduced In the light-on condition with two symmetric cues, ADN axons in RSC show unimodal heading tuning curves, while visual cortical (AM/PM) axons show bimodal tuning curves with peaks about 180 degrees apart.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In the light-off condition ADN axons keep their heading tuning for several trials, while visual cortical axons lose their tuning.
  • ✓Reproduced The diversity of RSC responses (a second peak in light, as in visual axons, and a single peak in the dark, as in ADN axons) persists regardless of the direction of chamber rotation (bootstrapped KS test, p = 0.0094, 4 recordings over 2 mice).
  • ?Not reproduced The diversity of RSC responses persists whether or not the mouse is in control of the arena (bootstrapped KS test, p = 0.031, 7 recordings over 3 mice).
  • ?Not reproduced Clustering the Gaussian-fit coefficients of RSC somatic tuning curves in light-on and light-off conditions gives an optimum of three functional classes (heading, landmark and alignment cells), though the responses span a continuum.
  • ?Not reproduced 12.1% of RSC neurons are tuned in the dual-cue experiment; of these, landmark cells are the plurality (33%), followed by alignment cells (28%) and heading cells (22%).
  • ◐Partially reproduced In alignment cells the single light-off peak is always at the location of one of the two light-on peaks, so landmark and heading responses are coregistered.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Heading cells show no significant difference in flip score between light-on and light-off conditions.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Landmark and alignment cells have significantly lower flip scores in light-off than in light-on (landmark F(1,1042) = 1062.5, p = 3.1e-161; alignment F(1,816) = 209.5, p = 2.0e-42).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Projected into the soma clusters' discriminant space, ADN axons fall mostly within the heading cluster and visual axons mostly within the landmark cluster, with some overlap with the alignment cluster.
  • ⊘Blocked When only the visual cues rotate (no proprioceptive or somatosensory input), the visual contributions to tuning are retained, but heading-cell responses are strongly degraded.
  • ?Not reproduced Decoding heading from all tuned RSC cells within single 100 ms frames is accurate in the light-on condition despite the symmetric cues (mean decoder accuracy 0.82, 95% CI 0.78 to 0.86).
  • ◐Partially reproduced In the light-off condition the decoder's accuracy from all tuned cells drops (mean 0.44, 95% CI 0.34 to 0.54; p = 7.6e-3 against light-on) but stays above chance.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Heading cells alone decode heading accurately in both light-on (0.61, 95% CI 0.56 to 0.66) and light-off (0.44, 95% CI 0.36 to 0.52) conditions, with no significant difference between them (p = 0.11).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Landmark cells decode heading very well in light-on (0.80, 95% CI 0.75 to 0.84) but fall to chance in light-off (0.20, 95% CI 0.14 to 0.27; p = 8.0e-4).
  • ?Not reproduced Alignment cells decode between heading and landmark cells, better in light-on (0.71, 95% CI 0.66 to 0.76) and fairly accurately in light-off (0.51, 95% CI 0.43 to 0.59), with no significant difference (p = 0.54).
  • ✓Reproduced Decoding from landmark cells in light-on gives an overrepresentation of 180 degree errors from the symmetric cues, while heading cells show no preponderance of 180 degree errors.
  • ✓Reproduced In light-on, heading cells alone decode worse than the whole tuned population, so alone they may not give an optimal representation of instantaneous heading.
