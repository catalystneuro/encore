  • Transferring learned inter-electrode matrices from other subjects, through correlation-based electrode mapping and per-electrode donor selection, reconstructs a target subject's trial-averaged iEEG with a mean DistCorr of 0.389 ± 0.064, without fitting weights on the target.

    Results, III B, paragraph 3; Table II, Average row, Cross-Subject columncross-ieeg-mean
    From the paper

    In this setting, the model achieved an ... DistCorr of 0.389±0.064

    Criterion

    Method from Methods II D 2 and Algorithm 2, with the code where they are silent. Donor matrices M_learn are the intra-subject weight matrices at m = 1.0 (code, donor directory intesity_100). For each target and donor, C(i, j) is the Pearson correlation of target electrode i and donor electrode j averaged over paired trials 1 to min(R_s, R_s') with NaN trials left out (Eq. 3); the code instead correlates the trial-concatenated series truncated to a common length (similarity.cross_subject_pearson), to be shown as a variant. A one-to-one assignment maximising summed correlation (Hungarian, scipy linear_sum_assignment); unmatched target electrodes get no row from that donor. M_est = P M_learn P^T. The target's trials are split 50/50 into validation and test (random_state 42). Which of the target's trials enter C is not stated (Algorithm 2 splits before computing C but does not say which part is used); readings (a) all of the target's trials, as the code does; (b) the validation trials only. For each electrode, the donor whose row gives the highest DistCorr on the z-scored trial-averaged validation signals is chosen, and DistCorr on the z-scored trial-averaged test signals is its score (Methods II D 2). Electrodes with no matched donor are left out of the mean (the code's nanmean). Reproduced when the average is within tolerance under one reading.

  • Adding lagged predictors (0, 20, 30, 50 and 60 ms) barely changes iEEG reconstruction (0.554 ± 0.067 against 0.553 ± 0.068).

    Results, III C, paragraph 2; Table II, Lagged columnlagged-ieeg-no-gain
    From the paper

    For iEEG, the corresponding values were 0.554±0.067 and 0.553±0.068, respectively. ... adding delayed predictors did not materially improve reconstruction

    Criterion

    The lagged model of Eq. 6 with lags {0, 20, 30, 50, 60} ms, each lag its own weight vector, offsets ceil(tau fs / 1000) samples (10, 15, 25 and 30 at 500 Hz, where the code's floor gives the same), predictors shifted within each trial with zeros before the trial start (model.LaggedReconModelConfigurable), all other settings as the instantaneous run at m = 1.0 (Methods II E; main.py run_lagged_too). A claim of no material gain; the paper reports no test and no threshold for "materially" or "minimal changes". The 10% bound below is the protocol's default tolerance applied to the instantaneous average, an assumption and not the paper's. Reproduced when the lagged average is within tolerance and differs from the instantaneous average by less than 10% of the instantaneous average, in either direction; not reproduced otherwise. The paired per-subject differences are reported; a Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the subjects may be shown as context but does not decide the claim.

  • On iEEG surrogates, intra-subject DistCorr falls from 0.553 to 0.181 (phase shuffle), 0.232 (IAAFT) and 0.201 (block shuffle).

    Results, III F, paragraph 3; Table II, Phase Shuffle, IAAFT and Block Shuffle columnssurrogate-ieeg
    From the paper

    For iEEG (Table II), the mean DistCorr decreased from 0.553 ± 0.068 for the origi ... nal data to 0.181 ± 0.029, 0.232 ± 0.060, and 0.201 ± 0.168 for phase-shuffled, IAAFT, and block-shuffled surrogates

    Criterion

    Surrogates made per trial and per channel independently from the preprocessed trials (Algorithm 4; Load_preprocess_data.load_per_subject), then put through the common pipeline at m = 1.0 (notebooks/visualization.ipynb). Phase shuffle, the positive-frequency phases of the FFT permuted with Hermitian symmetry and the DC kept (Load_preprocess_data.shuffle_phase). IAAFT, 5 iterations from a random permutation (iaaft_1d). Block shuffle, blocks of 0.5 s permuted with leftover samples appended (block_shuffle_1d; the paper does not give B, the code's default settles it). The Methods say end-matched segmentation was used for the phase surrogates and the code has none; readings (a) as the code, no end matching; (b) each trial trimmed to the sub-segment whose first and last samples and slopes match best before the shuffle. Random draws are a standard choice. Reproduced when all three averages are within tolerance and each is below the original average under one reading. Per-subject values are reported; in Table II subject 3's block-shuffle value (0.698) is above its original (0.535).
