Shukla et al. 2026 · The Journal of Neuroscience

A Novel Cooperative Behavior Assay in Rats Reveals Distinct Social Coordination Strategies of Increasing Complexity and Social Reciprocity Deficits in a Model of Fragile X Syndrome

doi:10.1523/jneurosci.0143-26.2026 · paper key shukla-2026

Datasets

  • DANDI:001845

Few of the main findings held up cleanly in this data. Higher match rate and performance in WT pairs appeared under one reading of the repeated-measures ANOVA and not the other, which the paper does not specify. Lower WT choice entropy held only at 50% reward, while higher exploration efficiency, shorter arrival and departure lags and greater Fmr1 reliance on follower gaze did not reproduce. Equal leadership and rising follower gaze over a trial held. Mixed pairs, sensory deprivation and the cue and virtual-agent tasks are missing from the dataset.

  • ⊘Blocked WT pairs reach significantly higher match (coordination) rates than Fmr1 pairs under both the 100% and the 50% reward contingency.
  • ⊘Blocked Fmr1 rats make fewer task-related arm transitions than WT rats (they are slower in the task), under both contingencies.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In the 50% condition, match rate rises linearly with transition rate in both genotypes, with a steeper slope and a better fit in WT (beta 0.79, R2 0.74) than in Fmr1 (beta 0.64, R2 0.44).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Both WT and Fmr1 pairs coordinate no better than chance early in training, and above chance late in training, by a permutation test that circularly shifts one rat's sequence of well visits.
  • ⊘Blocked Cooperative performance (matches relative to transitions, Eq. 1) is significantly lower in Fmr1 pairs than in WT pairs under both contingencies, so their impairment is not only due to slower transitions.
  • ?Not reproduced A multinomial logistic regression predicts a rat's next choice from its partner's current location above chance in both genotypes, and more accurately in WT than in Fmr1 rats, under both contingencies.
  • ⊘Blocked Mixed WT-Fmr1 pairs also reach lower coordination rates and performance, make fewer transitions and have lower partner-based prediction accuracy than WT pairs.
  • ⊘Blocked Replacing the transparent divider with an opaque one (with white noise) lowers the performance of WT and Fmr1 pairs compared with standard 50% sessions, with no genotype difference in this effect.
  • ⊘Blocked In mixed pairs the opaque barrier also lowers performance (condition effect with WT pairs), though the paired test within mixed pairs is not significant.
  • ⊘Blocked Both WT and Fmr1 rats learn the visual cue-reward association task; performance rises over sessions at 100% reward, and there is no significant genotype effect or genotype x session interaction at 100% or 50%. The Results also say Fmr1 rats learned more slowly, which the reported interaction tests do not show.
  • ⊘Blocked In the visual-cue task WT rats respond to the cue with shorter latency than Fmr1 rats over pooled responses (100%, 22.0 against 42.2 s; 50%, 14.0 against 28.4 s); in the session-wise mixed-effects models with rat as random intercept the genotype effect is not significant (p = 0.058 at 100%, 0.064 at 50%).
  • ?Not reproduced Under 100% reward rats mostly alternate between two arms, while under 50% they explore all three arms through the optimal 1-2-3 or 1-3-2 triplets, especially WT rats.

  • ?Not reproduced WT rats have higher exploration efficiency (fraction of optimal choice triplets) than Fmr1 rats, strongly in the 50% condition and modestly in the 100% condition.
  • ⊘Blocked When foraging with a virtual agent under a 50% contingency, WT and Fmr1 rats perform alike.
