  • ◐Partially reproduced The data are 2286 single and multi-units from 29 patients (17 female), 30 to 137 units per patient, in the amygdala (580), hippocampus (794), entorhinal cortex (440), parahippocampal cortex (373) and piriform cortex (99).
  • ⊘Blocked Over half of the parahippocampal neurons responded to camera cuts (193 of 373, 51.74%).
  • ⊘Blocked Camera cuts evoked responses in a large share of neurons in every region (amygdala 46.90%, entorhinal cortex 45.68%, hippocampus 38.92%).
  • ⊘Blocked Few parahippocampal neurons responded to scene cuts (25 of 373, 6.70%), but the modulation was consistent across the responsive neurons (cluster permutation test, p = 0.018).
  • ⊘Blocked Only a few neurons responded to the onset of semantic features (Summer 46, Tom 216, Persons 6 and Outdoor 4 of 2286 neurons), in contrast to camera cuts (1025).
  • ⊘Blocked No neuron responded to the onset of indoor scenes.
  • ⊘Blocked By the cluster permutation test, parahippocampal neurons responded to the largest set of features (Summer, Persons, Camera Cuts, Scene Cuts, Outdoor), compared with the hippocampus (Tom, Camera Cuts, Outdoor), the amygdala (Camera Cuts) and the entorhinal cortex (none).
  • ✓Reproduced Thresholding the activity of single neurons does not reliably predict whether Summer is on screen; most neurons perform near chance, below the population decoder.
  • ✓Reproduced A logistic regression on spike counts decodes characters and location about as well as the LSTM; pooled over patients it reaches kappa 0.28 for Summer and 0.31 for Indoor/Outdoor.
  • ✓Reproduced For scene and camera cuts the logistic regression performs below the LSTM, by 0.1 and 0.07, that is about kappa 0.10 and 0.11.
  • ✓Reproduced Without temporal gaps between training, validation and test samples, decoding of Summer is almost perfect (kappa 0.99), and it falls to 0.31 as the gap grows to 32 s.
  • ✓Reproduced Decoding from a single patient's neurons is lower than from the pooled population; no patient matches the pooled performance, for Summer, Scene Cuts or Indoor/Outdoor.
  • ✓Reproduced For Summer, patients 7, 10, 15 and 22 exceed kappa 0.2; for Indoor/Outdoor, patients 2, 13, 20 and 25 exceed 0.2.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Decoding from single regions depends on the feature; the amygdala is best and the hippocampus worst for Summer, and the parahippocampal cortex is best and the amygdala worst for Indoor/Outdoor.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Face labels decode slightly better than character labels (Summer 0.33, Tom 0.27), and Summer Presence (0.27) is decoded best from the amygdala (0.26).
  • ⊘Blocked Responsive neurons drive decoding of camera cuts but not of Summer; without them, Summer is decoded about as well as from all neurons while camera cuts drop the most, and responsive neurons alone decode camera cuts about as well as all neurons but Summer less well.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Across the five rankings of neurons by logistic regression weights for Summer, the top 500 neurons share 78 neurons, against about 1 expected by chance.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Decoding Summer from the 500 top-ranked neurons gives the peak performance (kappa 0.38), above that of the full population.
  • ⊘Blocked An LSTM decodes every label above chance from the pooled population, with kappa 0.36 for Persons, 0.31 for Indoor/Outdoor, 0.31 for Summer, 0.32 for McKenzie, 0.23 for Tom, 0.20 for Scene Cuts and 0.18 for Camera Cuts.
  • ⊘Blocked Shuffling the order of time bins in the test data lowers the LSTM's performance, most for visual transitions.
