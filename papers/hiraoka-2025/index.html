Hierarchical organization of bursty trains in event sequences

Takayuki Hiraoka, Hang-Hyun Jo

Summary: In the paper's example hippocampal neuron, burst sizes and merging numbers are heavy-tailed across timescales, and the scaling relation between their exponents holds. The burst-size exponent stays near the paper's 2.35 only above about 130 ms, and the rescaled merging-number distributions narrowly miss collapsing, on a fit setting the paper does not give. Other neurons share the pattern only under a fit poorly suited to integer counts. The Wikipedia and earthquake findings could not be tested, as the edit histories were never deposited and the catalog has no fixed version. In the model, heavy tails held for some timescale series only, and the two algorithms differ because the paper's memory function does not match its merging distribution.

  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. Which grid of timescales was used, and which units were meant by "other sequences in the datasets"? The Figure 2 labels fit a log-spaced grid of 40 steps of 0.0514 over the relevant range; this attempt used its 40 points below the upper end (55.2 to 5581.5 ms), since the upper end has fewer than 100 bursts. Whether all units or the pyramidal ones were analysed is not said. Which merging-number distribution did the decremental algorithm draw from, given that Eq. 16 is not the memory function of the zeta distribution?#
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. Which release of powerlaw was used, and was the exponent bounded? Release 2.0.0 caps the power-law exponent at 3 by default and lets the cap move the KS choice of xmin; the method of Clauset et al. that the paper cites has no cap. On the representative unit the cap decides the collapse of the merging-number exponents (largest deviation from the median 0.490 capped, 0.538 unbounded, against a band of 0.5) and narrows the spread of beta at the smallest timescales (2.907 capped, 3.349 unbounded at 55 ms). This attempt assumed an unbounded exponent.#

Questions about the claims 2

Where the author or a verifier believed the claims file misread the paper, they asked, citing the paper, and a reader who had not seen the data answered from the paper alone, amending the claims file where it was wrong. The author then ran the claim as answered. Attempts before protocol pilot-11 also asked here for method details the claims file did not give.

  • openasked about fig2-other-neurons-similar

    The criterion reads the set of other neurons as the pyramidal units, of session Achilles-10252013 or of every session. The paper's own representative unit (unit 9 of Achilles-10252013, found by its spike count and IETs as row 9 of the units table) is labelled inhibitory in the version the paper cites and interneuron in the latest. Since the paper's "other sequences in the datasets" are compared with a unit the files call inhibitory, should the set be all units of the recordings, or include the inhibitory units, and not the pyramidal units alone?

    Passage. Section III, paragraphs 2 and 5. "The first dataset consists of neuronal firing obtained from electrophysiological recordings of pyramidal cell activity in rat hippocampus" and "we show the results for a representative event sequence for each dataset ... unit 9 in the Achilles-10252013 session ... Other sequences in the datasets show similar patterns."

    Why it matters. The paper calls all its neuronal data pyramidal, but the unit it shows is not labelled so, which suggests it analysed every unit without the cell types. The fraction of units showing the pattern differs by class. With the exponent unbounded, under the KS fits it is 0.68 to 0.74 for the 128 inhibitory units against 0.23 to 0.45 for the 562 pyramidal ones, and 0.32 to 0.51 for all 690 units. The claim is reproduced under every set by the continuous fit from xmin = 1 (0.78 to 0.91), so the outcome does not change, but the set decides whether any KS reading of the fit passes.

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked about model-decremental-equivalent

    The criterion reads "statistically equivalent" as two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests of the burst sizes at each relevant timescale, none differing at p < 0.05 after Bonferroni correction. On the model's sequences this test rejects two independent runs of the same algorithm (Algorithm 2 against itself, 4 and 11 of the relevant timescales on the two grids under the claim's seed), as the bursts of one sequence at nearby timescales are not independent samples and n = 10^6 gives the test great power. Should equivalence be read with a test whose level is checked on two runs of one algorithm, such as one that takes independent runs of each algorithm as the units?

    Passage. Section IV, paragraph 9. "We omit the results for event sequences generated by the decremental algorithm since it produces statistically equivalent sequences as the incremental one."

    Why it matters. A criterion that rejects identical algorithms cannot show that two algorithms are equivalent, so it could only give not reproduced. The outcome here does not turn on it, as the literal set-up of Section IV also differs under a test calibrated across five runs (p = 0.0079 in each series, where two runs of one algorithm give p 0.373 to 0.913), but under the set-up with both algorithms on the zeta distribution the calibrated test finds them equivalent and the criterion does not.

    No reader has answered it yet.
