Excitation and polarization of isolated neurons by high-frequency sine waves for temporal interference stimulation

DANDI:001048: Most of this paper's main findings could not be fully tested in this data. The dandiset itself holds none of the paper's data, so the checks ran on the nine dandisets the paper lists, using an assumed soma region. Reproduced: pure sine waves fire more action potentials than modulated ones, and firing locks to the beats of modulated waves. Partly reproduced: AP peak height held but duration did not, and modulated thresholds came out higher with only one of three tests significant. Not reproduced: the excitation block at the highest fields. Blocked: the 20 kHz versus 2 kHz threshold ratio, which needs voltage calibration that is not deposited, and the low-conductivity thresholds, whose sessions are missing. The Figure 4 to 6 membrane-charging findings were also blocked, because the strobe files hold no ROIs or cell identities.

DANDI:001271: Two main findings held up, several did not, and the tetrodotoxin results could not be tested. Pure sine waves evoked more spikes than 20-Hz modulated waves, and modulated firing locked to the beats, both on an assumed reading of the criterion. Firing rates stayed well below the reported 60 to 90 Hz, and 20-kHz thresholds were not about twice the 2-kHz ones. Sustained depolarization was not detected, though the measure may miss plateaus this small. The tetrodotoxin effects stayed untested because the paper does not say how the soma was outlined on the deposited crops, the low-conductivity recordings are absent, and the pulse-kinetics findings were not attempted.

DANDI:001272: Some of the paper's main findings held in this data, others did not, and two could not be tested. Unmodulated waves evoked more spikes than 20-Hz modulated waves, firing mostly locked to the beats, and tetrodotoxin abolished spikes. Excitation of every neuron with delayed spikes, the doubled 20-kHz threshold, higher thresholds for modulated waves and the membrane charging times did not reproduce; the amplitude calibration and the debris ROI are not deposited. The low-conductivity finding has no deposited data and the sine-versus-pulse finding was not attempted. The control holds 3 neurons, against 8 in the paper.

DANDI:001273: Of the paper's main findings on membrane charging by 2-us pulses, two held here, the pooled time constants did not, and the shape and alignment effects could not be tested without shape labels in the dataset. Polarization at the electrode-facing poles held (13 of 14 experiments), as did equal charging at both poles, though that test has little power. Pooled discharging times came out longer than reported, and the paper's ROIs and fit settings are not given. Sustained firing partly held and the Di-8-ANEPPS control did not, under a 5% level set for another dye. The sine wave excitation and threshold findings are in other dandisets and were not tested for lack of time.

DANDI:001274: This is the paragraph for that file: Most of the paper's main findings on sine wave excitation of neurons did not hold up in this data, and the polarization findings could not be tested. Unmodulated waves evoked more action potentials than modulated ones, and modulated thresholds were higher, though only in part. The delayed onset of firing, the firing rate ceiling, the excitation block and the twofold higher 20-kHz thresholds missed, and the 2-kHz threshold was 75.6 against 57.7 V/cm. The generator calibration behind the field values is not deposited, which may explain the threshold misses. The polarization and sodium gluconate findings could not be tested because the dataset has no ROIs, cell identifiers or gluconate sessions.

  • ◐Partially reproduced Neurons were selected for APs peaking above 5% dF and lasting about 10 ms; typical APs were 11.3% dF high and 8 ms long, excluding the afterhyperpolarization (mean of 22).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Unmodulated 250-ms sine waves at 2 and 20 kHz excited every neuron tested; near threshold isolated APs came 100 to 200 ms after onset, and stronger fields shortened the delay and evoked sustained firing whose maximum rate rose linearly to a ceiling of 60 to 90 Hz.
  • ?Not reproduced At the highest fields, unmodulated sine waves merged APs into low-amplitude oscillations on a continuous depolarization, which could flatten and leave the membrane depolarized for the rest of the stimulus (an excitation block), and this depolarization reversed immediately when the stimulus stopped.
  • ⊘Blocked All excitation thresholds at 20 kHz were about 2-fold higher than at 2 kHz.
  • ✓Reproduced All thresholds to elicit 5 APs were higher than those for 1 AP.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Thresholds for sine waves modulated at 20 Hz were significantly higher than for pure sine waves at the same carrier frequency for 5 APs at 2 kHz and for 1 and 5 APs at 20 kHz, so modulation did not lower excitation thresholds; the 2-kHz unmodulated AP threshold was 57.7 ± 3.6 V/cm.
  • ✓Reproduced With 20-Hz modulation, firing above threshold locked to the beats, one AP near the peak of each of the five beats and at most two per beat (ten in all), and modulated sine waves triggered no sustained firing or sustained depolarization.
  • ✓Reproduced In the same neuron, the threshold was typically lower for unmodulated sine waves, and at the same field strength pure sine waves triggered more APs than modulated ones.
  • ⊘Blocked Replacing NaCl with sodium gluconate, which lowered the solution conductivity from 16.4 to 8.4 mS/cm, reversibly lowered excitation thresholds about 2-fold at every sine wave frequency from 2 to 20 kHz.
  • ⊘Blocked Extrapolated to a 2 mS/cm solution, excitation by 2-kHz sine waves would be expected at about 7 V/cm.
  • ⊘Blocked Excitation by pure sine waves was not caused by heating; the adiabatic temperature rise was 2.5 C after 250 ms at 63 V/cm in NaCl, at most 1.24 C for the same stimulus in the low-conductivity solution, where it evoked sustained firing, and 4.1 C at 81 V/cm in NaCl.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In the low-conductivity solution, a 2-s unmodulated 2-kHz sine wave ramped up over 250 ms evoked firing for its whole duration; the firing rate rose with field until replaced by a firing block with sustained depolarization, which developed before the end of the ramp.
  • ◐Partially reproduced With Di-8-ANEPPS in place of FluoVolt, in eight neurons, 2-kHz sine waves near threshold elicited one or a few delayed APs, and stronger fields shortened the delay and induced sustained firing ending in sustained depolarization, as with FluoVolt.
  • ◐Partially reproduced 1 uM tetrodotoxin abolished both the APs and the sustained depolarization evoked by intense unmodulated 2-kHz sine waves, while the vehicle did not.
  • ⊘Blocked Membrane charging by 2-us pulses is fast; pooled over 19 experiments, charging and discharging time constants averaged 0.7 and 0.91 us at the cathodic pole and 0.65 and 0.8 us at the anodic pole, so the membrane charges fully within each sine wave cycle up to 100 to 200 kHz.
  • ⊘Blocked Aligning elongated cells with the field doubled the time constants compared with the same cells perpendicular to it or with randomly shaped cells; dFmax and the time constants did not differ between the cathode- and anode-facing poles; and discharging was slower than charging except in elongated cells aligned with the field.
  • ⊘Blocked Sine waves polarized the two poles of a cell more asymmetrically than 2-us pulses; in the Figure 5 cell the depolarized ROI 2 was 30% smaller than the hyperpolarized ROI 1 with the pulse and up to 90% smaller with sine waves, and in the Figure S3 cell the hyperpolarized ROI 1 was 20% smaller than the depolarized ROI 2 with the pulse and 40% smaller with sine waves, the oscillations not migrating to other regions.
  • ⊘Blocked During a single beat of a modulated 2-kHz sine wave, the optical TMP in ROIs 1 and 2 oscillated with the carrier in opposite phases, with amplitudes proportional to the sine wave amplitude, on top of a gradual depolarization of the whole cell that developed as the amplitude rose.
  • ⊘Blocked Across 9 cells FluoVolt sensitivity to 2-us pulses was 15% to 47% per 1 kV/cm (mean 28%); sensitivity to 2-kHz sine waves, linearly correlated with the field, was 1.5% to 28.4% (mean 11.8%), smaller in every ROI, and 42% of the pulse value.
  • ⊘Blocked The 2-kHz AP threshold of 57.7 V/cm times the sine wave sensitivity gives a 0.68% emission change, about 6.8 mV with FluoVolt's calibration, so the threshold depolarization is 5 to 15 mV, as with intracellular current injection.
