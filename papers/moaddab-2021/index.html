Moaddab et al. 2021 · Current Biology

Retrorubral field is a hub for diverse threat and aversive outcome signals

doi:10.1016/j.cub.2021.02.055 · paper key moaddab-2021

Datasets

  • CRCNS:rrf-1

Most of the paper's main findings held up in this data: the eight cue-firing clusters at the stated sizes, their danger, uncertainty and safety patterns, and stronger signaling of exaggerated threat probability than of fear output. This rests on the paper's own code, whose normalization differs from its Methods text. The k1 shock response did not reproduce in the 0 to 8 s window drawn in Figure 6, though it did in 0 to 4 s, which fits the supplementary table. Some outcome-period results and the per-cluster regression table held only in part, the latter because the paper leaves out two unnamed units.

  • ✓Reproduced The paper recorded 743 single units from 11 rats over 228 fear discrimination sessions.
  • ✓Reproduced 423 units, from 190 sessions, were cue responsive, with no fewer than 11 per rat.
  • ✓Reproduced In the sessions that yielded cue-responsive units, rats fully discriminated the cues by suppression ratio (danger > uncertainty > safety), with a main effect of cue and 95% bootstrap CIs of every differential suppression ratio excluding zero.
  • ✓Reproduced In k-means clustering of the 423 cue-responsive units, the mean squared Euclidean distance to the centroid falls as the cluster number rises from 1 to 15, and the decrease diminishes after eight clusters.
  • ✓Reproduced Eight k-means clusters were kept, of sizes k1 33, k2 59, k3 18, k4 63, k5 58, k6 61, k7 64 and k8 67.
  • ✓Reproduced Clusters differed in baseline firing rate, coefficient of variance and coefficient of skewness, but not in anterior-posterior location, waveform half-duration or waveform amplitude ratio.
  • ✓Reproduced Units of clusters k1 and k3 had lower baseline firing rates than all other clusters.
  • ✓Reproduced Every cluster showed a significant trial type x interval interaction for cue firing.
  • ✓Reproduced Clusters k1, k3, k5 and k7 show excitatory firing to danger and uncertainty; k1 fires most to danger at cue onset with lower, similar firing to uncertainty and safety; k3 (whole cue and delay), k5 (late cue and delay) and k7 (delay) fully differentiate danger > uncertainty > safety.
  • ✓Reproduced Clusters k2, k4, k6 and k8 show sustained inhibition through the cue and delay; k2 is inhibited to uncertainty only, k4 equally to danger and uncertainty, k6 most to danger, less to uncertainty, with excitation to safety, and k8 to danger only.
  • ✓Reproduced From cue onset to delay, k1 firing decreases for all three cues (phasic onset firing), k5 firing increases for danger and uncertainty (ramping), and k2 firing increases for danger.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Except for k2, each cluster's uncertainty firing has the direction and time course of its danger firing at lower magnitude, and safety firing tends to oppose danger firing; k4 and k6 are inhibited to danger but excited to safety, k3 and k5 the reverse.

  • ✓Reproduced Danger and safety firing are positively related at cue onset (R2 = 0.14) but negatively related in the late cue (R2 = 0.03) and delay (R2 = 0.02).
  • ✓Reproduced The danger-uncertainty and danger-safety correlations differ from one another in each period (Z = 7.69 onset, 8.94 late cue, 8.20 delay).
  • ✓Reproduced Threat tuning curves of k1, k3 and k5 peak at an uncertainty assignment of 0.375 and k7 at 0.500, above the actual shock probability of 0.250 (exaggerated threat probability signaling).
