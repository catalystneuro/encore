  • fig3-chain-model-complexity

    In the 12-neuron chain model, random data (c = 0) give zero integration, ordered data (c = 1) give high integration, and only the complex data (c = 0.8) give non-zero complexity.

    Note that random data shows no integration, while ordered data shows high integration ... Only the complex data shows non-zero complexity.

    Methods 2.9, paragraph 6; Figure 3B and 3C

    How it will be judged

    A simulation, not a test on recorded data. The chain model of Methods 2.4 (12 neurons, first neuron alternating active and inactive between runs, p(a_i+1 = a_i) = 0.5(1 + c)), with complexity from Eq. 10 on the exact joint distribution (Methods 2.9, last paragraph says the model's distributions were defined exactly). Reproduced when C_N is zero within 1e-6 bits for c = 0 and c = 1 and above that for c = 0.8, and the whole-system integration is near zero for c = 0 and the largest for c = 1.

  • fig5d-bethe-small-avalanches

    In the critical Bethe lattice model (p_trans = 0.5), avalanche sizes are power-law distributed and show shape collapse, and the small avalanches (stopped by the 8th layer) are power-law distributed in size and have non-zero complexity.

    In the original small avalanches, the sizes of the avalanches were power-law distributed and a non-zero complexity was observed

    Results 3.1, paragraph 1; Figure 5B to 5D

    How it will be judged

    A simulation, not a test on recorded data. Bethe lattice of Methods 2.4 (two outgoing connections per neuron, p_trans = 0.5, activity stopped at layer 10^4, one neuron active in the first layer per run), as in the toolbox's bethelattice.m. The number of runs is not stated; a standard choice of 10^4 runs is used and cannot change the direction of the result. Small avalanches are those that stop on or before layer 8; each run is one time series of network states over the neurons of layers 1 to 8. Reproduced when the size distribution of the small avalanches is accepted as a truncated power law by plparams (p at least 0.2) and their complexity (toolbox complexity, sub-sampling correction on) is above zero.

  • fig5e-shuffle-removes-complexity

    In the Bethe lattice small avalanches, neuron shuffling preserves the avalanche size distribution but removes the complexity.

    First, we randomized the neuron identities in the avalanches (neuron shuffling). This operation preserved the avalanches and their power-law size distributions, but removed the complexity

    Results 3.1, paragraph 1; Figure 5E

    How it will be judged

    A simulation, not a test on recorded data. Neuron shuffling as in Methods 2.5 (at each time bin the active count kept and the identities drawn uniformly over all neurons). Reproduced when the shuffled size distribution equals the original and the complexity falls to below 10% of the original value ("removed" read as near zero; 10% is a choice the paper does not make).

  • fig5f-network-state-poisson-keeps-complexity

    In the Bethe lattice small avalanches, randomizing the order of whole network states preserves the complexity but changes the avalanche size distribution dramatically.

    when we randomized the order of the network states, but preserved neuron spiking states at each individual time bin (Poisson randomization for the whole network state), the complexity was preserved, but the distribution of avalanche size changed dramatically

    Results 3.1, paragraph 1; Figure 5F

    How it will be judged

    A simulation, not a test on recorded data. Whole network states (one per time bin) placed at random times, as Methods 2.5, paragraph 4, last sentence. Complexity depends only on the distribution of states (Methods 2.9), so it is preserved exactly when the same states are kept; reproduced when the complexity differs from the original by less than 5% (the residue of the random subset draws; 5% is a choice the paper does not make) and the size distribution of the permuted data is no longer accepted as the original power law (plparams p below 0.2 or exponent outside the original's error).

  • fig6-cb-distribution-curvature

    In the cortical branching model, low transmission probabilities give size and duration distributions that curve downwards, high ones give distributions that curve upwards, and p_trans near 0.25 gives the straightest distributions.

    note that the low transmission probability models produced distributions that curved downwards, whereas the high transmission probability models produced distributions that curved upwards. The model with a transmission probability near the infinite system critical transmission probability ( p trans = 0.25) produced the straightest size and duration distributions

    Results 3.2, paragraph 1; Figure 6A and 6B

    How it will be judged

    A simulation, not a test on recorded data. Cortical branching model of Methods 2.4 (100 neurons on a 10 by 10 torus, four nearest neighbours, p_spont = 10^-4, 3 x 10^5 steps), as cbmodel.m. Curvature read as the sign of the quadratic term of a quadratic fit of log10 probability on log10 size (and duration) over the values kept by the cuts of Methods 2.7; a measure the paper does not define. Reproduced when, averaged over 30 models each, the quadratic term is negative at p_trans 0.2, positive at 0.3, and smallest in magnitude at 0.25.

  • fig7ab-cb-complexity-susceptibility-peak

    When we plotted the complexity and susceptibility vs. the transmission probability, we found both peaked near p trans ≈ 0.265

Open, the p_trans grid. Methods 2.4 prints "increments of 0.05" on 0.2 to 0.3, which gives three values and cannot place a peak at 0.265, while Figure S1 shows p_trans = 0.26 and Figure S2 a curve over the interval; readings (a) steps of 0.005 (21 values), (b) steps of 0.01 (11 values). Open, the sub-sampling layout (Methods 2.10, "30 times at evenly spaced systems sizes"); readings (a) 30 sub-samples whose sizes are evenly spaced from 40 to 100 neurons, (b) 30 random sub-samples at each of the integer sizes from 40 to 100. Reproduced when the mean over models of each extrapolated quantity has its interior maximum within tolerance of 0.265 (the rise of complexity at the upper boundary, fig7a-complexity-boundary-rise, is not the peak).
