Summary: The split of V1 units into fast-spiking (FS) and regular-spiking (RS) clusters on their waveforms held up in this data, and RS units formed the paper's sub-clusters, but FS units did not. Clustering the paper's own published features recovers its sub-clusters, so the gap lies in the features recomputed from the files, which hold only 13 of the paper's 25 sessions. Most sub-cluster differences in visual responses and LFP phase-locking did not reproduce, several weaker effects losing significance with fewer units. Opto-tagged Pvalb units fell in FS and were more symmetric than Sst units, though mainly from a single animal. The mapping onto biophysical models and in vitro cells largely held, tested on the paper's published values.

  • OtherThe model parameters (conductances) of the 33 models are not public beyond the few columns of the Source Data; the model repository holds one example model. The in vitro features of the Source Data equal those of the Allen Cell Types Database API.#
  • OtherThe code's classifiers (ecp_func.classifier after upsampling) upsample the minority classes before the 75/25 split, so copies of a unit fall in training and test. For the random forests of Fig 5c and 5d this inflates the validation accuracy: with permuted RS1 to RS3 labels the procedure scores 61.7% where chance is about 33%, and splitting first gives 97.5% against 98.6% (in vivo) and 85.3% against 93.5% (SP models).
