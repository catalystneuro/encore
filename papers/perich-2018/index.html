Perich et al. 2018 · Neuron

A Neural Population Mechanism for Rapid Learning

doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2018.09.030 · paper key perich-2018

Datasets

  • DANDI:000688

The dataset holds only the Baseline epoch, so the paper's central claims about curl field and rotation adaptation, including output-null activity failing to generalize in the curl field only, could not be tested. Two Baseline findings were partly supported. PMd was higher-dimensional than M1 in every session, but the medians were below the paper's, and this rests on an assumed noise scale. Output-null activity led output-potent, significantly only under one reading of the kernel width, with a smaller lag than the paper's. Minor checks on dimensionality across sample sizes and the null/potent index held, but pooled counts of units above 5 Hz fell short. I did not create `summaries/perich-2018-01.md`.

Attempt perich-2018-01

2026-10-31 · on DANDI:000688

  • ◐Partially reproduced Per session, 137 to 256 PMd and 55 to 93 M1 neurons were isolated in Monkey C and 66 to 121 PMd and 26 to 51 M1 neurons in Monkey M; neurons above 5 Hz, pooled over all CF and VR sessions, number 918 in M1 and 2221 in PMd.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In every session the PMd manifold had more dimensions than the M1 manifold (median 14 against 8.5).
  • ✓Reproduced With random subsamples of equal, fixed numbers of neurons, estimated dimensionality fell for small samples but PMd stayed higher-dimensional than M1 for every neuron count tested.
  • ✓Reproduced Single neurons contributed to both output-potent and output-null subspaces; the null/potent index was centered on zero with virtually no neurons near -1 or 1.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In Baseline, output-null activity preceded output-potent activity by 35 ms on average across the 16 sessions, a lag significantly below zero (t-test, p = 0.02), and output-null also led at the end of Force.
  • ⊘Blocked On exposure to the curl field the monkeys made large reach direction errors that shrank gradually until behavior stabilized, and mirror-image after-effects appeared at the start of Washout.
  • ⊘Blocked In VR sessions behavioral errors were similar in magnitude and time course to those of CF sessions, with large initial errors in Rotation and strong after-effects in Washout.
  • ⊘Blocked In a representative CF session, the pairwise covariance of neurons in the Force and Washout blocks correlated with Baseline values at r of at least 0.85 in M1 and at least 0.93 in PMd.
  • ⊘Blocked Across all CF sessions the similarity of the covariance between Baseline and the Force and Washout blocks resembled that between random subsamples of Baseline, so the covariance structure did not change with adaptation.
  • ⊘Blocked In VR sessions as well, the changes in neural activity preserved the population covariance structure.
  • ⊘Blocked In the CF sessions pooled, the number of M1 or PMd neurons with significant GLM fits was 214 for M1-M1, 338 for PMd-PMd, 170 for PC-M1, 144 for Pot-M1 and 97 for Null-M1; the Basic model was significant for 522 of 918 M1 and 612 of 2221 PMd neurons.
  • ⊘Blocked Pot-M1 and Null-M1 had similar cross-validated accuracy within the end-of-Force training trials.
  • ⊘Blocked The rpR2 distributions of the within-area models M1-M1 and PMd-PMd did not differ between Early and Late Force trials (Kolmogorov-Smirnov p = 0.72 and p = 0.19).
  • ⊘Blocked Model error of M1-M1 and PMd-PMd did not differ between Early and Late Force; the local models generalized throughout CF adaptation.
  • ⊘Blocked Null-M1 generalized significantly worse in Early than in Late Force, while Pot-M1 generalized well in both blocks.
  • ⊘Blocked The Null-M1 model error changed over Force trials with a time course similar to that of behavioral adaptation, while Pot-M1 stayed near zero.
  • ⊘Blocked PC-M1, with all PMd latent activity as input, also failed to generalize during CF adaptation, its accuracy changing with a time course like that of behavior.
  • ⊘Blocked In each monkey separately, only Null-M1 and PC-M1 had significantly larger model error in Early than in Late Force; M1-M1, PMd-PMd and Pot-M1 did not.
  • ⊘Blocked Of the unnormalized rpR2 distributions of the five models in Early against Late CF trials, only Null-M1 (p = 0.04) and PC-M1 (p = 0.01) changed; Pot-M1 (p = 0.40), M1-M1 (p = 0.72) and PMd-PMd (p = 0.19) did not.
  • ⊘Blocked Without normalization by cross-validated performance, the change in rpR2 between Early and Late CF was significant only for Null-M1 (p = 0.01) and PC-M1 (p = 0.00); in VR no model changed (p from 0.28 to 0.80).
  • ⊘Blocked The Null-M1 failure and Pot-M1 generalization held for M1 dimensionalities from 7 to 20 (PMd twice that); Null-M1 was significant at all tested values except 9, Pot-M1 at none.
  • ⊘Blocked The main result held with Factor Analysis in place of PCA; in CF only Null-M1 and PC-M1 had larger Early error, and in VR no model did.
  • ⊘Blocked With single-neuron spiking as inputs, M1sn-M1 and PMdsn-PMd generalized well through CF adaptation while PMdsn-M1 did not, in each monkey; in Washout, PMdsn-M1 alone showed de-adaptation in CF (p = 0.00), and no model changed in VR.
  • ⊘Blocked Models trained on Baseline predicted the first eight Washout trials worse than later Washout trials for Null-M1 only; Pot-M1 predicted both blocks well.
  • ⊘Blocked Changes in preparatory (planning) activity from Baseline were largely confined to the output-null subspace and grew significantly from Early to Late Force; in the movement window, M1 activity changed significantly more in Late Force.
  • ⊘Blocked During VR adaptation all four GLM models, including Null-M1, generalized to Early and Late Rotation trials, with no significant change in model error.
  • ⊘Blocked In VR Washout neither Pot-M1 nor Null-M1 showed a change in performance during de-adaptation, despite behavioral after-effects.
  • ⊘Blocked The Null-M1 failure to generalize is specific to CF adaptation; it is absent in VR adaptation of similar behavioral magnitude, so it is not a necessary consequence of changing behavior.
