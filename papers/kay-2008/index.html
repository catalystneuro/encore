  • fig4a-single-trial

    Identification from single-trial voxel activity patterns was 51% (834 of 1,620) for S1 and 32% (516 of 1,620) for S2, against chance of 0.8%.

    Single-trial performance was 51% (834/1620) and 32% (516/1620) for subjects S1 and S2, respectively (Fig. 4a); once again, chance performance is just 0.8% (13.5/1620)

    Main text, paragraph 7; Figure 4a

    How it will be judged

    Single-trial responses are BRS amplitudes per trial with the HRF from the repeated-trial analysis, all trials of the identification runs used, standardized per session (Supplementary Methods 1, 3 and 4), giving 1,620 patterns per subject; each is identified among the 120 images by P5. The models and voxel selection follow O6, readings (a) and (b); the value must hold under one reading, with O1 and O5 as in fig3-s1-repeated-120.

  • fig4a-noise-ceiling

    The estimated noise ceilings suggest that the lower performance of S2 is due to intrinsically noisier data; the ceilings are near 99% for both subjects with repeated trials, and about 80% (S1) and 64% (S2) with single trials.

    The noise ceiling estimates suggest that the difference in performance across subjects is due to intrinsic differences in the level of noise

    Figure 4a and its legend; Supplementary Methods 7, Estimation of the noise ceiling

    How it will be judged

    Noise ceiling as Supplementary Methods 7 gives it. For each of the 120 images, 25 simulations; in each, the measured pattern is the mean of a random sample of the correct image's single-trial patterns, each candidate's predicted pattern the mean of a random sample of its own single-trial patterns, and the most correlated candidate is chosen; the ceiling is the percentage of successes. The values are read from the figure, no sentence gives them. Open are the voxels and the sample size. Voxels, the 500 selected for identification (P5). Sample size, readings (a) a bootstrap sample with replacement of the size of the available trials (13, or 14 for an image among the first 6 trials of a run, which the single-trial analysis keeps) for the repeated-trial ceiling and of size 1 for the single-trial ceiling, (b) without replacement, same sizes. Reproduced when, under one reading, the four values are within tolerance and S2's single-trial ceiling is below S1's, as S2's performance is (the paper's argument, read from the figure; no test is reported).

  • fig4b-extrapolation

    Extrapolated, performance falls to 10% at set sizes of 10^11.3 (S1, repeated trial), 10^7.3 (S2, repeated), 10^5.5 (S1, single trial) and 10^3.5 (S2, single).

    Extrapolation of these measurements (see Supplementary Methods) suggests that performance for this subject would remain above 10% even up to a set size of 1011.3 images

    Figure 4b; main text, paragraph 8; Supplementary Methods 7, extrapolation

    How it will be judged

    The extrapolation of P6 over the 999 library images, the set size at which the mean of f(m,s) over patterns reaches 0.1. Tolerances are 10% of the printed exponent, since the paper reports and compares these set sizes as powers of ten (10% of the set size itself would be far below the precision of one printed decimal of the exponent). Needs the library of fig4b-set-size-1000.

  • fig4c-retinotopy-only-extrapolation

    A retinotopy-only model, capturing only the location and size of each voxel's receptive field, falls to 10% correct at a set size of 10^5.1 images, against 10^9.5 for the Gabor wavelet pyramid model (repeated trials, averaged across subjects); it is worse for each subject, with single trials too.

    Performance for this retinotopy-only model declined to 10% correct at a set size of just 105.1 images, whereas performance for the Gabor wavelet pyramid model did not decline to 10% correct until 109.5 images were included in the set

    Main text, paragraph 9; Figure 4c; Supplementary Figure 5a to 5d

    How it will be judged

    Retinotopy-only model as Supplementary Methods 8 gives it, two channels, the absolute spatially-weighted luminance and the spatially-weighted contrast with weights from the Gaussian fitted to the best initial model's envelope, at the original resolution, fitted by the gradient descent of P2; voxels selected by its own predictive power (Supplementary Methods 7). How the subjects are averaged is open. Readings are (a) the mean of the two subjects' performance curves, then the set size at 10%, (b) the mean of the two exponents; Supplementary Figure 5 gives 11.3 and 7.3 for the GWP model and 7.0 and 3.8 for the retinotopy-only model, whose means (9.3, 5.4) are not the printed 9.5 and 5.1, so (a) is the better supported. Reproduced when, under one reading, both values are within tolerance and the retinotopy-only exponent is below the GWP exponent for each subject and for repeated and single trials (Supplementary Figure 5 gives 3.7 against 5.5, S1, and 2.5 against 3.5, S2, single trial). Needs the library of fig4b-set-size-1000.

  • suppm8-retinotopy-only-120

  • suppfig8-constrained-models

    In constrained versions of the GWP model, both orientation and spatial frequency tuning add to identification (C > A, G > A), spatial frequency more (G > C), and voxel-to-voxel variation in both adds too (F > E, H > E); at 1,000 images the nine models give A 17%, B 22%, C 28%, D 30%, E 51%, F 60%, G 64%, H 62% and I 63%.

    Both orientation and spatial frequency tuning contribute to identification performance (C > A and G > A), but spatial frequency tuning is relatively more important (G > C). Voxel-to-voxel variation in orientation and spatial frequency tuning also contributes to identification performance (F > E and H > E)

    Supplementary Figure 8a and 8b; main text, paragraph 10; Supplementary Methods 9

    How it will be judged

    The nine models of Supplementary Methods 9 at 64 px, the spatial envelope fixed by the Gaussian of the RF location, channels summed over position, transformed by the 640-grating least-squares map and summed over phase, then flat, ROI-averaged (mean tuning across the voxels of the same area) or individual tuning on each dimension, fitted by P2 over one fixed voxel set selected by the predictive power of "the model that imposed no constraints on orientation and spatial frequency tuning" (Supplementary Methods 7). Which model that is the paper leaves open; readings are (a) model I, which fixes the spatial envelope, (b) the final GWP model of P3. Performance at 1,000 images by P6, averaged across subjects. The figure's p-values (C over A 8e-6, G over A 2e-22, G over C 2e-16, F over E 4e-4, H over E 4e-5) are read from the low-resolution table of panel b; the legend states only that all five are below 0.05. Which unit the sign test pairs (each measured pattern's f(m,1000), pooled over subjects) is a reading, the one the legend's "paired" allows. Reproduced when the five orderings hold with one-tailed sign-test p < 0.05 and at least four of the six values are within tolerance. Needs the library of fig4b-set-size-1000; the orderings at set size 120 can be shown as a variant, which does not decide the outcome.

  • errors-similar-images

    Identification errors tended to occur when the selected image was visually similar to the correct image.

    Inspection revealed that identification errors tended to occur when the selected image was visually similar to the correct image

    Main text, paragraph 12

    How it will be judged

    The paper judged this by inspection and gives no measure. The two image similarity measures the paper itself uses (Supplementary Discussion 1) are the readings, (a) correlation of pixel luminance, (b) correlation of local RMS contrast in 0.6 degree blocks. For each error of fig3-s1-repeated-120 and fig4a-s2-repeated-120, the percentile of the selected image's similarity to the correct image among the correct image's similarities to the other 119 images. The test is fixed here, not by the paper. Reproduced when, under one reading, the errors pooled over subjects have a median percentile above 50 by a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test against 50 at p < 0.05.

  • supptable1-snr

    SNR differs greatly between subjects but is similar across V1, V2 and V3 within a subject; the fraction of voxels with SNR above 1.5 is 32%, 30% and 22% in S1 and 18%, 19% and 13% in S2.

    Although SNR varied greatly across subjects, SNR was fairly consistent for areas V1, V2, and V3 within each subject

    Supplementary Table 1 and legend, column High SNR

    How it will be judged

    A voxel's SNR is the median, over its model estimation responses aggregated across sessions, of the absolute response divided by its standard error, from the BRS fits (Supplementary Methods 3). The paper does not say how the standard error of an amplitude is obtained; readings are (a) the ordinary least-squares standard error from the final iteration of the BRS fit after the autoregressive transform, (b) a bootstrap over runs. Reproduced when, under one reading, at least four of the six values are within tolerance and S1's fraction is higher than S2's in every area.

  • suppfig11-rf-size-eccentricity

    Receptive-field size increases with eccentricity, and from V1 to V2 to V3.

    RF size increases with eccentricity and across visual areas, consistent with previous fMRI studies

    Supplementary Figure 11 and legend

    How it will be judged

  • suppdisc1-classification-extension

    Extending classification to novel images (matching the measured pattern to the most similar of the 1,750 training patterns, then picking the identification image most similar to that training image) works poorly, 1.7% and 0.8% correct with pixel luminance similarity (not above chance) and 5% and 5.8% with local contrast (above chance), for S1 and S2.

    Identification performance using the pixel luminance metric was 1.7% (2/120) and 0.8% (1/120) for subjects S1 and S2, respectively (repeated trial). These values were not significantly above chance (p ≥ 0.05, one-tailed binomial test). Identification performance using the local contrast metric was 5% (6/120) and 5.8% (7/120) for subjects S1 and S2, respectively (repeated trial). These values were above chance (p < 0.0001, one-tailed binomial test)

    Supplementary Discussion 1, An extension of classification-based decoding methods yields poor identification performance, paragraphs 2 and 3

    How it will be judged

    As Supplementary Discussion 1 gives it. Pattern similarity by Pearson r; image similarity by correlation of pixel luminance, or of local RMS contrast in 0.6 degree blocks (n = 0.6, the best of the values the paper tried). Open are the voxels and the contrast measure. Voxels, readings (a) the 500 voxels of P5, (b) all V1 to V3 voxels of O1. RMS contrast, readings (a) the SD of luminance in the block, (b) that SD over the block mean. With 120 images a tolerance of 10% is less than one image, so the counts are judged instead. Reproduced when, under one reading, each count is within one image of the paper's (2, 1, 6, 7), the luminance results are not above chance (one-tailed binomial p at or above 0.05) and the contrast results are (p < 0.0001); partially when the significance pattern holds but a count is further off. The paper wrote p ≥ 0.05, recorded here as p > 0.05.
