Claims 13

From the paper Relative importance of intensity and spectrum of artificial light at night in disrupting behavior of a nocturnal rodent · claims file · checked 2026-11-25: ready

  • Light of 0.5 lx and brighter reduced mouse activity during the 1-h exposure at every CCT, except 0.5 lx at 1750 K.

    Results, paragraph 1; Table 1table1-suppression-0p5-and-above
    From the paper

    Light at 0.5 lx and brighter, except for the 0.5 lx/1750 K treatment, reduced activity of mice ( Table 1 )

    Criterion

    Per treatment, the mean over the 16 mice of percent suppression (method in the opening comment). Reproduced when the mean is above zero for all eight treatments at 0.5, 5 and 50 lx other than 0.5 lx/1750 K; partially reproduced when it is above zero for at least six of them. The Table 1 values are not in the text read, so the twelve means are reported for comparison once the table is supplied.

  • All three 0.01 lx treatments, and the 0.5 lx/1750 K treatment, increased mouse activity during the exposure.

    Results, paragraph 1; Table 1table1-increase-0p01
    From the paper

    All 0.01 lx treatments, and the 0.5 lx/1750 K treatment, increased activity during the treatment

    Criterion

    Per treatment, the mean over the 16 mice of percent suppression. Reproduced when it is below zero for all four treatments named; partially reproduced for three of four.

  • In Tukey-Kramer comparisons within color temperatures, the effect of illuminance on activity was significantly greater only for 50 lx at 1750 K.

    Results, paragraph 1; Methods, Photic suppression of nocturnal activity, paragraph 3tukey-illuminance-within-cct
    From the paper

    Within color temperatures, the effect of illuminance on mouse activity was only significantly greater for 50 lx at 1750 K

    Criterion

    Tukey-Kramer HSD on per-mouse percent suppression, alpha 0.05 (the paper gives no alpha for the activity tests; 0.05 is a standard choice and the alpha of its power analysis) (Methods, "testing for significant differences for each while holding the other constant (Tukey-Kramer HSD for multiple comparisons)"). The sentence can be read two ways. (a) Within each CCT, the four illuminances are compared; at 1750 K the 50 lx treatment differs significantly from the lower illuminances, and at 1950 K and 3000 K no illuminance pair differs. (b) Within each illuminance, the three CCTs are compared; only at 50 lx do CCTs differ, with 1750 K differing from the others. Reproduced when the significant pairs match the sentence under one reading; every significant pair is reported under both. Treatments are compared as independent groups, as Tukey-Kramer implies; the repeated measures of each mouse are not modelled in this test.

  • At 0.01 lx, CCT had no significant effect on activity, although 1750 K light increased activity more than 3000 K light.

    Results, paragraph 1cct-0p01-not-significant
    From the paper

    at 0.01 lx, CCT was not statistically significant, although 1750 K CCT increased activity more than 3000 K CCT

    Criterion

    A claim of absence, judged on the paper's test, Tukey-Kramer HSD over the three CCTs at 0.01 lx on per-mouse percent suppression, alpha 0.05 (a standard choice; the paper gives no alpha for the activity tests). Reproduced when no CCT pair differs significantly and the mean increase in activity at 1750 K is larger than at 3000 K; not reproduced when a pair differs. The mean differences are reported either way.

  • In a generalized linear model of suppression, illuminance, CCT and their interaction each contributed significantly, and the whole model was significant.

    Results, paragraph 2; Methods, Photic suppression of nocturnal activity, paragraph 3glm-illuminance-cct-interaction
    From the paper

    Mouse activity was associated with light conditions in a generalized linear model ( P <0.001), with significant contributions of illuminance ( P <0.0001), spectrum ( P <0.0001) and the interaction term ( P <0.002)

    Criterion

    The GLM of the opening comment on all mouse-by-treatment suppressions. Reproduced when the whole model has p < 0.001 and the illuminance, CCT and interaction terms each have p below their reported bounds (0.0001, 0.0001, 0.002) under one reading of the open choices (illuminance in lux or log10 lux; likelihood-ratio or Wald tests; log or identity link); partially reproduced when all three terms have p < 0.05 under one reading. Every reading is reported.

  • Over the tested ranges, the effect of illuminance (0.01 to 50 lx) on activity was several times greater than that of CCT (1750 to 3000 K).

    Results, paragraph 2; Methods, Photic suppression of nocturnal activity, paragraph 3effect-size-illuminance-over-cct
    From the paper

    The influence of illuminance on mouse activity across the 0.01-50 lx range was several times greater than the effect of CCT across the 1750-3000 K range

    Criterion

    Effect sizes from the GLM with each factor centered at its mean and scaled by half its range (Methods; JMP's scaled estimates). The paper does not say how the categorical CCT term is put on that scale. Readings are (a) the largest absolute CCT parameter under JMP's effect coding, against the absolute scaled illuminance estimate; (b) a refit with CCT as a continuous term in kelvin, centered and scaled by half of 1250 K. Illuminance in lux or log10 lux as in the opening comment. "Several times" is read as a ratio of at least 2. Reproduced when the ratio of the illuminance effect to the CCT effect is at least 2 under one reading; every ratio is reported.

  • The influence of CCT on activity was smallest at the lowest illuminance and grew as illuminance increased, as shown by the significant CCT x illuminance interaction.

    Results, paragraph 2; Discussion, paragraph 5cct-effect-grows-with-intensity
    From the paper

    with the influence of CCT decreasing at the lowest light strength

    Criterion

    The spread of mean suppression across the three CCTs (largest minus smallest of the per-treatment means over mice) at each illuminance. The paper does not say how the influence of CCT at one illuminance is measured; this spread is a standard choice, and the CCT effect at each illuminance predicted by the GLM is reported as a variant. Reproduced when the spread at 0.01 lx is the smallest of the four illuminances and the spread at 50 lx is larger than at 0.01 lx, and the interaction term of the GLM is significant (claim glm-illuminance-cct-interaction); the four spreads are reported.

  • At equal illuminance in lux, lower CCT light suppressed mouse activity less than higher CCT light.

    Discussion, paragraph 1; Table 1; Figure 2Alower-cct-less-suppression
    From the paper

    a lower CCT reduced the effect of equal intensity light (as perceived by humans) on an organism more sensitive to shorter wavelengths of light than humans when the intensity is measured in lux

    Criterion

    Per-treatment mean suppression over mice. Reproduced when the 1750 K mean is below the 3000 K mean at each of 0.5, 5 and 50 lx, the illuminances at which the paper reports suppression; partially reproduced when it holds at 50 lx and one other of them. The 1950 K means are reported alongside. Table 1 values are missing from the text read.

  • Adding sex, or mouse identity, to the GLM improved it; the model with mouse identity had the lowest AICc (1608.1), then the model with sex (1624.6), then the model with neither (1640.3).

    Results, paragraph 3; Methods, Photic suppression of nocturnal activity, paragraph 3aicc-individual-sex
    From the paper

    Both sex and mouse individual added explanatory power to the model, with the model including individuals having the lowest AICc (1608.1), compared with sex (1624.6) or without either variable (1640.3)

    Criterion

    The base GLM, the same with sex added as a main effect, and the same with mouse identity added as a categorical main effect ("Sex and mouse ID were included in separate models as additional explanatory factors"). The paper does not say whether they enter with interactions; the reading run is main effects only, as the sentence implies. Reproduced when the AICc order is identity < sex < neither and the two differences are within tolerance under one reading of the open choices; partially reproduced when the order holds. The absolute values carry the default 10% tolerance, which is too wide to test anything, so the order and the differences (10% of each) decide the claim.

  • In the model including sex, males' activity was suppressed significantly less by light at night than females'.

    Results, paragraph 3; Figure 2Csex-males-less-suppressed
    From the paper

    From the model including sex, activity of males was suppressed significantly less than females by light at night ( P <0.0001; Fig. 2C )

    Criterion

    The sex term of the GLM with sex (claim aicc-individual-sex). Reproduced when it has p < 0.0001 with lower suppression in males under one reading of the open choices; partially reproduced when p < 0.05 with males lower.

  • From the model with sex, at 0.01 lx and 1750 K male activity increased by 9.3% while female activity was suppressed by 3.4%; at 50 lx and 3000 K male activity was suppressed by 85.7% and female activity by 98.3%.

    Results, paragraph 3; Figure 2Csex-predicted-suppression
    From the paper

    For example, at the lowest intensity (0.01 lx) and CCT, male activity was increased by 9.3% (95% CI=3.8-14.7% increase), whereas female activity was suppressed by 3.4% (95% CI=2.1% increase to 8.9% decrease). At the maximum intensity and CCT, male activity was suppressed by 85.7% (95% CI=75.6-95.7%), whereas female activity was suppressed by 98.3% (95% CI=88.3-108.3%)

    Criterion

    Predicted suppression from the GLM with sex, for each sex at the two settings ("From the model including sex"). Tolerances are the half-widths of the paper's 95% CIs. Readings are the open choices of the opening comment (illuminance in lux or log10 lux; log or identity link). The paper's numbers may also be read as raw means by sex; that reading is reported as a variant. Reproduced when all four predictions are within tolerance under one model reading; partially reproduced when three are.

  • Mice varied greatly in their response, individual effects ranging from up to 27.9 percentage points less suppression to 21.5 percentage points more suppression than average.

    Results, paragraph 3; Figure 2Bindividual-variation
    From the paper

    The model that included the animal identification number ( Fig. 2B ) revealed a great variability in activity among the mice, with individual mice ranging from being much more active regardless of light treatment (up to 27.9 percentage points less suppression; 95% CI=18.3-37.4) to being substantially more affected (21.5 percentage points increased suppression; 95% CI=8.1-34.8)

    Criterion

    From the GLM with mouse identity. The paper does not say how a mouse's effect is expressed in percentage points under a log link. Readings are (a) the mouse parameters under JMP's effect coding (deviations from the mean of mice) in the identity-link reading of the opening comment; (b) for each mouse, its mean predicted suppression over the 12 treatments minus the mean over all mice. Tolerances are the half-widths of the paper's 95% CIs. Reproduced when both extremes are within tolerance under one reading.

  • The number of cFos-positive cells in the SCN after 1 h of 50 lx light did not differ between 1950 K (123.7 +/- 17.89 s.d.) and 3000 K (113.4 +/- 8.22 s.d.) light.

    Results, paragraph 4; Figure 3Bcfos-no-cct-difference
    From the paper

    No differences were observed in the number of cFos positive cells in the SCN of WT mice exposed to either 1950 K (123.7±17.89 s.d.) or 3000 K (113.4±8.22 s.d.) light (unpaired t -test ... t 10 =0.9409, P <0.3689)

    Criterion

    A claim of absence, judged on the paper's test, a two-tailed unpaired t-test with equal variances (10 degrees of freedom) on one value per animal, alpha 0.05 (Methods, cFos positive cell counting; Fig. 3 legend). Per animal, the left and right SCN counts of each section are averaged, then the 3 to 5 sections. The paper does not say how the three observers' counts are combined; the mean over observers is the reading run, and each observer alone is a variant. Reproduced when p > 0.05; not reproduced when p < 0.05; the difference of means is reported either way. The paper's means and s.d. give t near 1.28 for this test, not 0.9409, so its t and p are not used as expected values. Tolerances on the means are the paper's s.d. (its stated spread).
