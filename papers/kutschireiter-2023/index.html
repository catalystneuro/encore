The circular Kalman filter's behavior, the Bayesian ring attractor's tracking and its edge over a conventional attractor held. The noise-driven upward drift of bump amplitude did not, as amplitude fell, and independence of network size failed at one value (0.041 off).   • ✓Reproduced In the circular Kalman filter, an HD observation consistent with the current estimate increases certainty, while one that strongly conflicts with it (opposite direction) decreases certainty.
  • ✓Reproduced In a simulated Bayesian ring attractor of 80 neurons, the bump position tracks HD about as accurately as the circular Kalman filter, the bump amplitude tracks the filter's certainty, and when the visual cue is removed the amplitude decays toward its baseline.
  • ✓Reproduced The Bayesian ring attractor tracks HD with the same accuracy as the circular Kalman filter when HD observations are reliable and with slightly lower accuracy when they are unreliable; in the unreliable regime its certainty stays near its baseline and overestimates the filter's certainty.
  • ✓Reproduced A conventional ring attractor (fast decay to a fixed bump amplitude, with that amplitude optimized) is less accurate than the Bayesian ring attractor at every HD observation reliability, and reaches its accuracy only when observations are highly unreliable.
  • ✓Reproduced The weight given to a new HD observation by the Bayesian ring attractor depends on both the observation's reliability and the current certainty and is close to that of the circular Kalman filter, deviating only for very uninformative observations; a conventional attractor weights observations by their reliability alone.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Across ring attractors with different bump decay speeds and baseline amplitudes, the best network has a slow decay (low beta) and performs about as well as the Bayesian ring attractor; performance drops as decay speed increases, which a larger baseline amplitude partly offsets.
  • ✓Reproduced In a network of 64 neurons with additive neural noise, inference accuracy drops below 70% of its noise-free value once the noise is large enough.
  • ✓Reproduced Retuning the bump decay speed and baseline amplitude for each noise level reduces the drop in inference accuracy by about 35% compared with the network tuned for noise-free performance, and raises the signal-to-noise ratio.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Neural noise makes the bump position diffuse without bias and makes the bump amplitude drift upward and diffuse, which leads to overconfidence; for additive noise, a four-fold larger network halves the noise's effective SD.
  • ◐Partially reproduced The connectivity among EPG, Delta7 and PEN1 neurons in the hemibrain connectome resembles the connectivity of a network that implements the approximate circular Kalman filter, with shifted PEN1 to EPG projections and broad EPG to Delta7 to EPG inhibition.
  • ◐Partially reproduced A Drosophila-like multipopulation network, constrained by the connectome, tracks HD with an accuracy indistinguishable from the idealized Bayesian ring attractor across observation reliabilities.
  • ?Not reproduced The Bayesian ring attractor's inference accuracy depends little on its number of neurons.
  • ⊘Blocked The paper predicts that the bump amplitude in the HD system is higher in the presence of reliable external HD cues, such as salient visual landmarks. The paper does not test this.
