Bouchard et al. 2013 · Nature

Functional organization of human sensorimotor cortex for speech articulation

doi:10.1038/nature11911 · paper key bouchard-2013

Datasets

  • DANDI:000019

No central finding was cleanly confirmed in this data. The dorsal-to-ventral layout of the articulators could not be tested, because the dataset lacks electrode positions relative to the central sulcus and Sylvian fissure. The staggered timing of articulator activity and the early peak of consonant separability did not reproduce. The state-space clustering results and the count of about 30 active electrodes stayed undecided, since the paper does not fix the baseline, z-scoring or syllable set. Mixed single-electrode tuning held, but under a test that barely discriminates. It needs to be written there.

Attempt bouchard-2013-01

2026-10-21 · on DANDI:000019

  • ◐Partially reproduced Three subjects with high-density ECoG (two left, one right hemisphere) read aloud CV syllables made of 18 or 19 consonants followed by /a/, /i/ or /u/, each CV produced 15 to 100 times, with the microphone recorded with the ECoG at 3052 Hz.
  • ⊘Blocked About 30 active vSMC electrodes were found per subject during syllable articulation, an electrode being active when its high-gamma z-score changed by more than 2 for any syllable.
  • ⊘Blocked In one subject, dorsal electrodes (e124, e108) were active for /b/ (lips), mid vSMC electrodes (e129, e133, e105) for /d/ (front tongue), and a more ventral electrode (e104) was most active for /g/ (back tongue).
  • ?Not reproduced Fricatives with different tongue-tip constriction locations (/th/, /s/, /sh/) engaged different electrodes; front-tongue consonants of different manner (/l/, /n/, /d/) engaged overlapping electrodes with different magnitudes; for /ja/, /ji/, /ju/ the activity was similar before the C-V transition, while a dorsal electrode was active during /u/ only.
  • ⊘Blocked Each articulator is represented across vSMC; the lip representation lies in dorsal vSMC and the tongue representation is spread over its ventral part.
  • ⊘Blocked Across subjects the articulator representations are laid out from dorsal to ventral as larynx, lips, jaw, tongue and larynx, with a laryngeal representation at the dorsal end of vSMC as well as a ventral one.
  • ⊘Blocked Electrodes with similar articulator representations lie closer together than chance; the mean spatial autocorrelation within 3 mm is 0.45 against 0.26 for permuted electrode positions.
  • ⊘Blocked Each articulator's representation lies in a more consistent place across subjects than chance (mean cross-subject correlation within 3 mm 0.195 against 0.031).
  • ⊘Blocked Within each subject, adjacent articulator representations (larynx-tongue, tongue-jaw, jaw-lips) are spatially sequenced along the dorsal-ventral axis, with positive cross-correlation on one side of the diagonal (0.099) and negative on the other (-0.102), both beyond chance.
  • ⊘Blocked The observed map is neither a perfect linear somatotopy nor random; the mean distance between electrodes preferring the same articulator (11.06 mm) and between those preferring different ones (15.11 mm) lie between the two null models.
  • ✓Reproduced Single electrodes show a clear preferred articulator and also carry weights for other articulators.
  • ?Not reproduced Articulator correlations follow a staggered sequence consistent across subjects; lip and tongue correlations begin well before sound onset, jaw and larynx align to the C-V transition, and the high and back tongue vowel features peak near the vowel midpoint (~250 ms).
  • ⊘Blocked Onset latencies of the articulator model fit do not differ between electrodes just anterior and just posterior to the central sulcus (within 10 mm), nor across the guenon.
  • ⊘Blocked The cortical state-space has nine spatial PCs in every subject, the eigenvalues falling fast up to the ninth and slowly after, together explaining about 60% of the variance.
  • ⊘Blocked At the consonant time (-25 ms) k-means clustering of the cortical state-space groups syllables by major oral articulator (labial, coronal, dorsal), and three clusters are more parsimonious than two or four (mean silhouette 0.47 against 0.45 and 0.43).
  • ⊘Blocked Clusters of the cortical state-space match the major articulators at the consonant time (median co-clustering 0.50 against 0.32 by chance) and the vowels at the vowel time (0.65 against 0.37), across subjects.
  • ⊘Blocked Hierarchical clustering of the state-space puts the major oral articulator (dorsal, labial, coronal) at the top tier for consonants, superordinate to constriction location, with constriction degree only at the lowest level; for vowels the first split is lip rounding (/u/ against /a/ and /i/).
  • ⊘Blocked Over early times (-375 to 120 ms) constriction location correlates with the state-space structure more than constriction degree, which correlates more than the upcoming vowel; over later times (125 to 620 ms) vowel configuration correlates most.
  • ?Not reproduced Cluster separability, averaged over subjects, rises from about -300 ms and peaks about 200 ms before the C-V transition for consonants, and rises from about 50 ms and peaks at +250 ms for vowels.
  • ⊘Blocked Cluster separability and the correlation of the state-space with phonetic features are tightly linked over time for consonants and vowels in each subject (R2 0.42 to 0.98, all P < 1e-10).
  • ⊘Blocked Consonant and vowel representations occupy distinct regions of the state-space; consonant-vowel distances are larger than consonant-consonant and vowel-vowel distances, and consonant-consonant distances are larger than vowel-vowel ones.
  • ?Not reproduced The first three PCs are weighted toward tongue over lips, and toward lips over jaw and larynx, while jaw and larynx do not differ.
