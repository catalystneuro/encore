  • illusion-fig1c-isomap-geometry

    In an example session of the Illusion data, three stimulus manifolds of V1 activity appear continuous with little overlap in the as-is data, discontinuous at slope 0 and heavily overlapping at slope 2.

    As the slope increases from 0 to 2, the stimulus manifolds become increasingly overlapping. At slope 0, the stimulus manifolds appear discontinuous with one another, hindering smooth transitions between stimuli. At slope 2, the stimulus manifolds are hard to discriminate due to the large overlap.

    Figure 1C bottom; Results, Neural variability structure is highly consistent across visual stimuli and mice, paragraph 6

    How it will be judged

    Session sub_630506 (O4), V1 units (O3), spike counts in the 400 ms from onset, the 12 stimuli with 400 repeats. Slope manipulation M2 at target slopes 0, 1 and 2 with M1 fits on these stimuli (fit_slope_openscope.ipynb). The Isomap pictures (n_neighbors 5, 2 components, fitted on all trials of the 12 stimuli, visualize_manifold.ipynb cell 8) are drawn for Ic1, Lc1 and Tre1 and shown, but the claim is judged on the measures the paper uses to formalize this picture (Results, Neural variability structure enables maximal discriminability, paragraph 1), computed in the full N-neuron space. Among Ic1, Lc1 and Tre1, with the M3 overlap percentages and a directed edge wherever one is above zero, (i) at slope 0 the three do not form one strongly connected component, (ii) in the as-is data they do, (iii) the mean of the six directed overlap percentages is larger at slope 2 than as-is and larger as-is than at slope 0. The finding is stated of the 2D Isomap picture, so the same three tests on the 2D Isomap coordinates of the three stimuli are a second reading; both are run. Reproduced when all three hold, partially when two hold. The same measures over all 12 stimuli (the size of the largest strongly connected component, the mean overlap) and the other sessions are shown as variants.

  • illusion-fig3a-orthogonal-variance-example

    In the example session of the Illusion data, the Lc1 manifold has larger orthogonal variance toward the faraway Ic1 manifold than toward the nearby Tre2 manifold.

    Purple and green arrows indicate the orthogonal variances of the orange manifold relative to the purple and green manifolds, respectively.

    Figure 3A (legend; the labels "Faraway stimulus, larger variance" on Ic1 and "Nearby stimulus, smaller variance" on Tre2 are from ortho_variance.ipynb cell 9 at commit ac3300b)

    How it will be judged

    The arrows of Figure 3A are drawn in the 2D Isomap with lengths set by hand in the code (60 toward Ic1, 22 toward Tre2), so the claim is tested with the paper's measures in the N-neuron space. As-is data of sub_630506 (O4), V1 units (O3), the 12 stimuli with 400 repeats. Geodesic distance between centroids by M5. Orthogonal variance as the Methods define it, the variance (ddof 1, code) across Lc1 trials of the norm of the component of each trial's deviation from the Lc1 centroid along the line joining the two centroids, divided by the summed per-neuron variance of Lc1. Reproduced when Ic1 is geodesically farther from Lc1 than Tre2 is and Lc1's orthogonal variance toward Ic1 is larger than toward Tre2; partially reproduced when only the variance order holds. The Spearman correlation between geodesic distance and orthogonal variance over all 132 ordered pairs of the 12 stimuli is shown as a variant.

  • illusion-geometry-similar-across-datasets

    The changes in manifold geometry that come with changing the slope are qualitatively the same in the Illusion data as in the natural scenes data.

    The geometric changes accompanying log-mean vs. log-variance slope changes are qualitatively similar across datasets, as well as across Isomap and UMAP.

    Methods, Visualization of stimulus manifold geometry, paragraph 3

    How it will be judged

    The paper does not say which geometric changes it compared. Read as the two changes it reports for the main data, effective dimensionality falling with slope (Figure 1D) and overlap rising with slope (Figure 2A). Every session of dandiset 000248 with an ICwcfg1 block, V1 units (O3), 400 ms counts, the stimuli with 400 repeats, M2 at slopes 0 to 2. Effective dimensionality per stimulus as the Methods define it, the participation ratio of the covariance eigenvalues divided by the number of neurons, averaged over stimuli; overlap by M3, averaged over ordered stimulus pairs. Reproduced when, across sessions, the mean dimensionality decreases and the mean overlap increases by the O2 reading (with the M4 test across these sessions); partially reproduced when the direction holds for both but the tests do not. The example session alone is shown as a variant.

  • fig1d-dimensionality-falls-with-slope

    The effective dimensionality of individual stimulus manifolds falls as the slope rises from 0 to 2.

    When the slope is 0, the stimulus manifold has the same variance across all axes, resulting in a spherical shape and a higher effective dimension. Conversely, when the slope increases to 2, variance becomes even larger in the axes with larger means, resulting in a more elliptical shape and a lower effective dimension.

    Figure 1D; Results, first section, paragraph 5

    How it will be judged

    Effective dimensionality per stimulus as the Methods define it, (sum of eigenvalues) squared over the sum of squared eigenvalues of the covariance, divided by N; averaged over stimuli per session; M2 slopes 0 to 2. Judged by O2 (decreasing) with M4.
