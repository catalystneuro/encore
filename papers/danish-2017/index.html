Rhythmic syllable-related activity in a songbird motor thalamic nucleus necessary for learned vocalizations

Husain H. Danish, Dmitriy Aronov, Michale S. Fee

In this data the paper's main findings on zebra finch Uva activity during song largely hold up: multiunit activity rises during singing, dips before syllable offsets and leads song amplitude, though each bird's Uva channel had to be guessed. After Uva lesions, song lost structure as reported in all 7 birds, mostly with values that differ from the paper's, and the drop in maturity index appears only under a spectral measure the agent assumed. Lesioned birds sang less per hour, where the paper reports similar rates, but only under one way of counting song. The loss of directed song and the control lesions could not be tested, as the dataset lacks directed-song labels and control birds.

Reproduction Uva lesions and multiunit activity in Uva during singing (Danish, Aronov and Fee 2017)

Tests the findings of Danish, Aronov and Fee (2017) on CRCNS aa-3, which holds song from 7 zebra finches before and after bilateral Uva lesions, with the authors' segmentations, and multiunit recordings in Uva during singing from 3 of the paper's 5 recorded birds. It tests the lesion effects on song structure and rhythm, and the timing, consistency and rhythm of Uva activity relative to syllables, song amplitude and spectral song features. Control lesions, marked directed song, sorted single units and GTE times are not in the data set. All files

Environment: Python 3.13, 15 packages pinned with their hashes in requirements.lock.txt; CI runs each notebook from a new environment built from it.

  • Rests on the channel chosen for each bird (see fig4a-elevated-during-song).fig4c-call-premotor fig6-peak-before-onset fig6-dip-before-offset fig7b-10hz-power-peaks bout-offset-suppression no-motif-bout-signal

  • Song is counted from the segments the authors selected in their analysis files, assumed to be the song syllables they analysed; all segments are shown as a variant and do not give a significant decrease.lesion-undirected-rate-similar
  • Assumed that the power spectra are log-scaled and that each frequency's mean (over all slices of the bird's condition) is removed, and divided by its SD, before correlating. The Methods say only "correlation of power spectra" and cite Aronov et al. 2008, which this run could not read. The literal reading cannot give values near the paper's 0.08, so it is unlikely to be the paper's computation; all four forms are shown.   • The scale of Gamma is not defined in the paper; ref 41 could not be read in this run, so the claims file's two readings were used.fig2f-closer-to-exponential
  • Bouts shorter than 1 s, and longer than 4.1 s, are left out (the shortest is not given; a standard choice for a 4 Hz taper resolution). R is the maximum between 3 and 30 Hz (the upper bound is not given; 3 to 50 Hz is shown).fig2e-rhythmicity-drop

  • The local-maximum and prominence tests are added variants; the claims file's test takes the ratio's maximum in the band.fig2e-residual-rhythm-peak
  • The label "k" is read as distance calls from its duration (about 210 ms) and from the spreadsheets, which mark the files where it occurs as distance calls; the data set does not name its labels.fig4c-call-premotor
  • Rests on the channel chosen for each bird.fig4b-introductory-note-premotor
  • Assumed that the neural channel of each bird is the one of its three with the largest range of the syllable-onset-aligned MUA (b871 channel 6, Orange150 channel 7, Uva4 channel 3).
