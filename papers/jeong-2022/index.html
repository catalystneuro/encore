Jeong et al. 2022 · Science

Mesolimbic dopamine release conveys causal associations

doi:10.1126/science.abq6740 · paper key jeong-2022

Datasets

  • DANDI:000351

Most findings on recorded data held up: reward responses grow with experience and track the preceding interval, cue responses persist after extinction licking stops, and learning survives dopamine inhibition. Two did not reproduce: faster decay of the cue response with background rewards (p = 0.053, and one animal's first background session is missing from the dataset) and a lower cue response under inhibition (p = 0.117, hinging on one short session). The seven model simulations were not run because their code is Matlab. The test that dopamine is unchanged after the cue delay change cannot detect a change under the code's fitting bounds.

  • ◐Partially reproduced The subjects and "Day" numbers that the paper's analysis code names for each experiment are sessions of the dandiset, so that the code's selection of sessions can be applied to it.
  • ✓Reproduced Animals were trained for 7 +/- 2.1 sessions of random rewards, 13.3 +/- 2.7 sessions of initial conditioning, 4.3 +/- 0.8 sessions with the 8 s cue, 8.1 +/- 2.5 sessions with background rewards and 2.6 +/- 0.8 sessions of extinction; in sequential conditioning, 5.6 +/- 2.4 (wild type) and 7.1 +/- 0.7 (DAT-Cre) sessions (mean +/- SD).
  • ✓Reproduced In Experiment 1, 22.6 +/- 2.4% of rewards came less than 3 s after the previous reward and 5.7 +/- 0.1% had no lick before the next reward; both were excluded.
  • ✓Reproduced In experimentally naive mice given unpredicted sucrose, the dopamine response to reward increases with the number of rewards experienced, in every animal (Test 1).
  • ✓Reproduced The dopamine response to an unpredicted reward is positively correlated with the interval since the previous reward (Test 2).
  • ✓Reproduced The positive correlation between reward response and previous IRI holds across sessions and does not change over the first 7 sessions.
  • ✓Reproduced Total, consummatory and non-consummatory lick rates do not change across the first 7 random reward sessions.
  • ✓Reproduced The consummatory lick rate after a reward is not correlated with the dopamine response to that reward.
  • ⊘Blocked Dopamine rises at the onset of consummatory lick bouts but falls slightly at the onset of non-consummatory (dry) lick bouts, so the rising reward response is not a general response to licking.
  • ✓Reproduced Spontaneous baseline dopamine events, away from rewards and licks, do not change in size across random reward sessions.
  • ✓Reproduced Across animals, the change of baseline dopamine activity over sessions is not correlated with the change of the reward response over sessions.
  • ✓Reproduced In a separate group of 6 mice, the non-consummatory lick rate depends on the session's mean IRI (6, 12 or 30 s) while the consummatory lick rate does not, so animals learn the average reward rate within at most two sessions.
  • ✓Reproduced During initial cue-reward learning, the dopamine response to CS+ develops before anticipatory licking, and behavioral learning is much more abrupt than dopaminergic learning (Test 3).
  • ✓Reproduced By the session at which behavior was acquired, the dopamine cue response had reached 93% of its value on the final session (about 100% relative to the mean of the last two sessions).
  • ✓Reproduced When the cue is lengthened from 2 s to 8 s (cue-reward delay 3 s to 9 s), animals change the timing of anticipatory licking abruptly, and this change is better fit by a changing model than by a constant one.
  • ✓Reproduced After the cue-reward delay is lengthened, the dopamine cue response shows no significant change (Test 4).
  • ✓Reproduced After the delay change, there is no suppression of dopamine at the old reward time (3 s) in any animal, whereas omission of the reward in extinction does suppress dopamine at the reward time (Test 5).
  • ✓Reproduced Behavioral learning of extinction is abrupt, much more so than the change of the dopamine cue response.
  • ✓Reproduced The dopamine cue response remains significantly positive well after animals stop licking to the cue in extinction (Test 6).

  • ✓Reproduced In the trial-less task, the dopamine response to an intermediate cue (one arriving before the previous cue's reward) is positive but weaker than the response to the previous cue (Test 8).
  • ✓Reproduced The dopamine response to the reward paired with an intermediate cue is larger than the response to the reward of the previous cue (the ratio is above 1).
  • ✓Reproduced With the baseline taken immediately before the intermediate cue and the response measured as a peak, the intermediate cue response is still positive.
  • ✓Reproduced During initial cue-reward learning, dopamine activity does not shift backward from just before reward to the cue; the late (pre-reward) response does not differ from the early (cue) response in the first 100 trials (Test 9).
