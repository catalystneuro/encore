DANDI:001515: Almost none of the paper's findings could be tested here, and none was shown to fail. The dandiset holds FluoVolt time courses of single 1-us pulses, not the paper's Di-8-ANEPPS and FluoVolt escalation sets, so the polarization pattern, the loss of pole charging at electroporating fields, and the Gd3+, calcium and two-pulse resealing results could not be tested. The one claim needing no recordings, the effect of membrane conductivity on charging, partly held: the 100-fold value matches, but a 10-fold rise gives 1.3% where the paper says about 2%.

DANDI:001516: Almost none of the paper's findings could be tested in this data, and the one that could be tested did not match the paper's text. The dandiset holds a FluoVolt two-pulse experiment the paper does not report, not the Di-8-ANEPPS recordings behind its loss of pole charging at high fields, the Gd3+ extension of anodic charging, the calcium entry or the membrane recovery after a conditioning pulse. Those findings could not be tested for lack of data. The only claim needing no recordings, that a modest rise in membrane conductivity cuts charging by about 2%, gave 1.33% from the paper's own equation and constants. The larger-rise value agreed.

DANDI:001517: The pole dimming at high fields, the Di-8-ANEPPS rise up to 570 V/cm and the within-set decline from 1000 V/cm held, but the dye saturation limits and the equator following the cosine law did not. FluoVolt peaked at 49% cathodic dF against the paper's 66%. The likeliest reason is the background region, which the paper does not place and which shifts pole dF up to threefold. Gd3+, calcium, 0.3-us and two-pulse findings could not be tested because those recordings are not in the dataset. Charging below linear scaling held only with generator volts matched in order to the paper's V/cm.

DANDI:001518: The paragraph is below, ready to save. Most of the paper's main findings did not hold in this data or could not be tested. The Gd3+ effects, calcium responses, two-pulse recovery and 0.3-us pulses could not be tested because the dandiset lacks those experiments. The dye response ceilings (FluoVolt peaked at 36% against 71%), the dye calibration, the pole dimming and the cosine behaviour near the equator did not reproduce. These tests assume a volts to V/cm conversion that the files and paper do not give, though the ceilings fail at every conversion tried. Only the within-set decline of Di-8-ANEPPS response reproduced, mostly as a drift present at every field strength.

DANDI:001521: Most of the paper's main findings, the optical measurements of membrane charging and pole leakage with voltage-sensitive dyes, could not be tested, because this dandiset holds only Fluo-4 calcium imaging. Of the calcium findings, clear Ca2+ entry after a single pulse at 1000 and 1200 V/cm held, though the effect is small. The signal staying elevated through 5 minutes did not hold, because unpulsed cells lose about 12% of their signal, which the paper's figure does not show. The strong response in 28% of cells at 780 V/cm with pulse trains did not reproduce either. The records give no way to match the paper's cell counts, since the cells were recorded repeatedly. A minor conductivity calculation matched only in part.

DANDI:001525: The paper's membrane recovery findings mostly hold in this data, which covers only its two-pulse experiment, so most other findings could not be tested. Test-pulse charging stays cosine-shaped and unchanged after mild conditioning pulses and falls after the strongest ones, as reported. The fall differs in size from the paper's at the cathodic pole, and at the anodic pole it is not significant after the weaker of the two strongest pulses. Full resealing after mild pulses and residual leak after strong ones hold, but the test pulse also finds a pole difference after the strongest pulse. These results depend on an automatic membrane trace, and the dye calibration, escalation, Gd3+ and calcium findings are absent from the dataset.

DANDI:001526: Few of the paper's findings could be tested, and none held up cleanly. The dandiset holds only the Di-8-ANEPPS alternating-field and short-pulse experiments, so the FluoVolt, Gd3+, calcium and two-pulse recovery findings could not be tested. Stable responses before electroporation did not reproduce, and the lasting drop afterwards was larger than reported, both depending on an undescribed background subtraction and on which cells the paper used. The fall in polarization within a set at the electroporating field held only under one background choice made after seeing results. The maximum polarization with short pulses reached 34.9% against the reported 53%.

  • ⊘Blocked Below and near the electroporation threshold (43 to 570 V/cm, 1-us pulses), the membrane depolarizes at the cathode-facing pole and hyperpolarizes at the anode-facing pole, polarization decreases from the poles towards the equator, and it scales proportionally with field strength, as the Schwan equation describes.
  • ⊘Blocked With Di-8-ANEPPS and 1-us pulses, polarization grows with field strength up to 570 V/cm; at 780 V/cm the cathodic pole stops increasing and dims, and at 1000 to 1400 V/cm the poles show reduced or absent polarization while regions nearer the equator stay polarized and may polarize more than the poles.
  • ⊘Blocked With FluoVolt, pole dF rose with field strength until it saturated at 66% (255 mV) at 360 V/cm at the cathodic pole and -83% (-320 mV) at 570 V/cm at the anodic pole; stronger fields did not increase the anodic response and decreased the cathodic one.
  • ⊘Blocked FluoVolt measurements were stable without pulses (no significant bleaching or photosensitization), and the response within each set stayed stable through the 60 pulses, even at 1000 V/cm and above.
  • ⊘Blocked Di-8-ANEPPS dF kept increasing with field strength up to 570 V/cm at both poles.
  • ⊘Blocked Di-8-ANEPPS responses were somewhat affected by bleaching, most visibly at 43 V/cm, where bleaching added about 5% to dF at the anodic pole and took about 5% from it at the cathodic pole.
  • ⊘Blocked With Di-8-ANEPPS, responses to pulses of 780 V/cm and above decreased gradually within each set of 60 images.
  • ⊘Blocked Repeated alternating sets at 150 and 230 V/cm (480 pulses and 800 flashes) gave dF responses constant within and between sets.
  • ⊘Blocked At 1000 V/cm the cathodic pole dF fell from 40 +- 10% at the start of the set to 26.6 +- 8% at its end, a trend that was not statistically significant.
  • ⊘Blocked Set-averaged dF stayed nearly constant before the 1000 V/cm exposure but was about 20% lower in the sets after it.
  • ⊘Blocked Fitted linearly through the origin over -150 to +150 mV IMPC, FluoVolt changes by 26% and Di-8-ANEPPS by 9.1% dF per 100 mV, with correlation coefficients 0.96 and 0.78.
  • ⊘Blocked FluoVolt departed from linearity from about +-200 mV IMPC, while Di-8-ANEPPS stayed nearly linear from -500 to +500 mV; so FluoVolt's early departure reflects its dynamic range, not electroporation.
  • ⊘Blocked FluoVolt measured and calculated IMP matched only at 43 and 150 V/cm; with stronger 1-us pulses the pole dF peaked at 71 +- 10% (cathodic) and -83.5 +- 5% (anodic), about +260 and -330 mV.
  • ⊘Blocked FluoVolt cathodic polarization fell from 71% at 360 V/cm to 45 to 50% at 1000 to 1400 V/cm.
  • ⊘Blocked With Di-8-ANEPPS and 1-us pulses, measured IMP fell below the calculated value at 570 V/cm, where dF peaked at 57 +- 14% (cathodic) and -55 +- 4% (anodic), over 600 mV.
  • ⊘Blocked Averaged over only the first 10 of the 60 pulses, Di-8-ANEPPS cathodic polarization reached 66 +- 15% (710 +- 200 mV), so the 600-mV maximum is not the dye's limit.
  • ⊘Blocked Above 780 V/cm the pole IMP fell progressively with field strength, more at the cathodic pole; at 1400 V/cm the cathodic pole no longer charged and the anodic pole fell to about -30% dF (-330 mV).
  • ⊘Blocked At the apparent electroporation threshold of 570 V/cm and at 780 V/cm, permeabilization judged by the shortfall from the calculated IMP was about the same at the two poles.
  • ⊘Blocked Membrane regions nearer the equator were unaffected by pole electroporation and followed the cosine dependence of the Schwan equation up to 1000 V/cm.
  • ⊘Blocked With 0.3-us pulses at higher fields (357 to 3320 V/cm), imaged at 275 ns, the maximum dF was 53 +- 4% (about 600 mV) at both poles, as with 1-us pulses, and the response changed with field strength in the same way.
  • ⊘Blocked With 1 mM Gd3+ the anodic pole charged to -864 +- 44 mV, against -542 +- 57 mV in parallel controls (p < 0.001).
  • ⊘Blocked With Gd3+, the measured IMP stayed nearly identical to IMPC down to -780 mV, so the Di-8-ANEPPS calibration holds at high IMPs.
  • ⊘Blocked At the cathodic pole Gd3+ cells reached 647 +- 44 mV, not significantly above controls, but Gd3+ prevented the decline of IMP with stronger fields.
  • ⊘Blocked Gd3+ and control data differ significantly for datapoints with IMPC below -700 mV and above 1200 mV.
  • ◐Partially reproduced By Eq. (2), the effective membrane conductivity barely affects charging while it stays far below cytosol and medium conductivities; a 10-fold rise reduces IMP by about 2% and a 100-fold rise by 12%.
  • ⊘Blocked For most datapoints, Gd3+ reduced the electroporation-induced effective membrane conductivity 2 to 3-fold.
  • ⊘Blocked A single 1-us pulse caused clear Ca2+ entry at 1000 and 1200 V/cm, with the signal elevated through 5 min; the small rise at 780 V/cm was not significant.
  • ⊘Blocked After a train of sixty 1-us pulses at 6 Hz, 28% of cells responded strongly at 780 V/cm and nearly all at higher field strengths.
  • ⊘Blocked At 650 V/cm only 2 cells (5.9%) responded strongly, yet the mean Ca2+ signal at the end of recording differed from zero (p < 0.001) and was marginally higher than at 230 V/cm (p < 0.05).
  • ⊘Blocked The azimuthal profile of a 150 V/cm test pulse given 5 us after a conditioning pulse kept the cosine shape of an undamaged membrane after conditioning pulses up to 1000 V/cm, while after 1200 and 1400 V/cm charging at the poles was markedly reduced.
  • ⊘Blocked Normalized to 150 V/cm, pole polarization during the conditioning pulse fell 1.5 times below linear scaling at 560 V/cm and 2-fold at 760 V/cm, and further at stronger fields (p < 0.01 for every point from 560 V/cm).
  • ⊘Blocked A 150 V/cm test pulse 5 us after the conditioning pulse charged the poles as in undamaged cells after conditioning up to 760 V/cm, to about 80% (not significant) after 1000 V/cm, and to about 70% and 50% after 1200 and 1400 V/cm, a persistent permeabilization.
  • ⊘Blocked Within 6 us of the electroporating pulse, effective membrane conductivity at the poles fell by a factor of about 10 (range 7 to 20) for most datapoints; the cathodic pole, more conductive during the pulse, no longer differed from the anodic pole at the test pulse.
  • ⊘Blocked After mildly electroporating 570 and 760 V/cm pulses the membrane resealed fully within 6 us; after stronger pulses a residual conductivity increase persisted.
