Spectral Imprints of Working Memory for Everyday Associations in the Frontoparietal Network

Elizabeth L. Johnson, David King‐Stephens, Peter B. Weber, Kenneth D. Laxer, Jack J. Lin, Robert Thomas Knight

In this intracranial dataset the paper's behavioural results held up: identity trials were slower than spatial and temporal ones, and accuracy did not differ by condition. Its per-subject power differences after the test cue held in part, with similar subject counts but smaller shares of electrodes, and sat at low frequencies. The frontoparietal phase-slope findings did not reproduce: with the null the Methods describe, 78 of 1135 pairs were directional in delta-theta, about what trial-shuffled data give, and the paper's counts appear only with the deposited code's null, which flags shuffled data as well. The absence of ERP differences failed in one subject, under a neighbour rule the paper does not state.

    Results, ERPs

    What the paper reports

    • cluster-based permutation test, per subject, p > 0.1, cluster-based-corrected (n = 7 subjects, identity against spatial and against temporal)

    Under the Methods' reading (segments zero padded, 30-Hz low-pass), neighbours adjacent by contact number and clusters judged at 0.05 per tail, S2 has a significant cluster for identity against temporal (p = 0.041); no other subject-contrast is below 0.05, and 2 of 14 have p <= 0.10 (paper, all p > 0.10). At 0.025 per tail the adjacent reading finds none, which would be reproduced; the distance reading (S5, p = 0.014) and no spatial clustering (S2, p = 0.024) find one at either threshold. Variants with adjacent neighbours at 0.05 per tail give, with the 1-30 Hz band-pass of the Results, no cluster (smallest p 0.085); per-trial baseline, S5 p = 0.030; the whole epoch filtered (erp_compute.m), S2 p = 0.031 (0.053 in round 1 with another random seed); 900-ms-delay trials only, S5 p = 0.023. Under the adopted reading the deciding p (0.041) is close to 0.05, where the Monte Carlo standard error of 1000 shuffles is about 0.006; the distance reading's p = 0.014 is about 6 standard errors below 0.05. The neighbour rule and alpha per tail are put to the paper's authors (findings).

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • Assumption. The paper says only that clusters were formed over "neighboring electrodes", and the deposited scripts hold no cluster test. The readings give different outcomes, so neighbours adjacent by contact number on the same lead or strip are adopted. This uses only the channel labels and follows the bipolar montage, which re-references each electrode "to the next adjacent electrode" (Methods, Data Acquisition and Preprocessing); the distance reading rests on placing numbered contacts on the grids of the MNI files, whose numbering the files do not document.
    • Clusters are judged at 0.05 per tail (the Methods' "fewer than 5% of randomizations yielded a larger effect", and FieldTrip's behaviour when cfg.alpha is 0.05 and the tails are not corrected); 0.025 is shown as a variant.
    • The 200-ms baseline and 900-ms processing segments are cut, zero padded to 7.5 s and low-passed (Methods, Event-Related Potentials, paragraph 1, "The correct-trial 200-ms pretrial baseline and 900-ms post-cue processing data segments were zero-padded to 7,500 ms ... and passed through a 30-Hz low-pass finite impulse response filter"). erp_compute.m filters the whole epoch and cuts the windows afterwards; where the Methods and the code differ the Methods come first (protocol, Reader step 2), and the code's order is a variant. The baseline is the pretrial temporal mean averaged over trials, one value per channel, as erp_compute.m computes it; it cannot change a t-test, so a per-trial baseline is run as a variant. The Results' "1-30 Hz bandpass" is a variant. The FIR order 3*fix(fs/30) (3*fix(fs/1) for the band-pass), forwards and backwards, is FieldTrip's default, implemented in scipy (a standard choice). FieldTrip itself was not run.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Published code disagreesThe deposited psianalysis.m computes the cross-spectrum as the product of the two channels' Fourier coefficients without the conjugate, unlike the cross-spectral density of the Methods and of Nolte et al. (2008). The resulting normalised spectrum depends essentially on one channel's phase (it equals plus or minus exp(2i x the phase of the second channel)), and the PSI z it gives is not antisymmetric; on these data it is +58.67 (delta-theta maintenance, mean over subjects) with the PFC channel first and -55.47 (sign reversed) with the parietal channel first. Its null is also not the statistic it z-scores. It averages per-trial PSI and compares the average with the slope of the trial-mean normalised spectrum, frequency-shuffled with both factors permuted independently, whose spread shrinks with the number of trials. The Methods say the frequencies of one signal were shuffled and PSI recomputed. Even with the conjugate added, this null gives |z| > 1.96 in 90% (delta-theta) and 92% (alpha-beta) of pairs when each subject's PFC and parietal trials are shuffled against each other, where a calibrated z gives about 5%.
  • Published code disagreesThe deposited psi_compute.m does not run as written. It uses an undefined `freq` (the signature takes subid and elecs only), saves to an undefined `savDir`, and tests the electrode argument with `nargin > 2`. Its subfunction subtracterp.m selects temporal trials with `trialinfo(:,5)==2`, the delay column, which selects no trial.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. Which electrodes were neighbours in the per-subject cluster tests (Methods, Event-Related Potentials, "neighboring electrodes"), and were clusters of each sign judged at 0.05 or 0.025? The deposited scripts hold no cluster test. The outcome of the ERP test depends on it here (adjacent contacts, S2 p = 0.041, significant at 0.05 per tail and not at 0.025; positions within 1.5 contact spacings, S5 p = 0.014). This attempt assumed contacts adjacent on the same lead and 0.05 per tail.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the paper's authors. Was task-induced and time-resolved PSI computed from cross-spectra averaged over trials (Nolte et al. 2008) or per trial and averaged, as psianalysis.m does, and with which channel first, given that the deposited code omits the conjugate? And was the null the frequency shuffle of one signal with PSI recomputed, as the Methods say, or the slope of the trial-mean spectrum, as psianalysis.m computes it? With the Methods' null, 1 (per trial) or 78 (trial-averaged) of 1135 pairs are directional in delta-theta, and 74 or 104 in alpha-beta, near the counts of trial-shuffled data; the paper reports 1008 and 984, which only the code's null reproduces (1014 and 1041), and that null gives about the same counts on trial-shuffled data. This attempt used the Methods' null and adopted the trial-averaged reading, whose z is calibrated on trial-shuffled data, where the readings give different outcomes; per-trial PSI with the Methods' null gives z offset from zero on data without coupling.

Reproduction Frontoparietal working memory iEEG (Johnson et al. 2019) from CRCNS fcx-3

Tests the findings of Johnson, King-Stephens, Weber, Laxer, Lin and Knight (2019) on CRCNS data set fcx-3, which holds intracranial recordings from prefrontal and parietal cortex of 7 patients doing a visuospatial working memory task. It checks the channel and trial counts, the behaviour, ERPs and power between identity, spatial and temporal trials with per-subject cluster tests, and the phase slope index between prefrontal and parietal channels over the delay and in time.
