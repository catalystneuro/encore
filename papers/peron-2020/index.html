  • model-amplification-increases

    In the L2/3 network model, the sensory input needed to match in vivo responses declines as subnetwork connectivity rises, so amplification (network output over input, normalized to Pconn 0.2) increases with subnetwork connectivity.

    The strength of sensory input required to match in vivo responses declined with increasing subnetwork connectivity. Amplification ... therefore increased with subnetwork connectivity

    Results, paragraph 3; Figure 1c

    How it will be judged

    The network of the Methods (2,000 leaky integrate-and-fire neurons; 200 input-recipient excitatory, 1,500 other excitatory, 300 inhibitory; connection probabilities 0.2 among excitatory and 0.6 for all with inhibitory; PSP 1 mV, and 1.6 mV within the subnetwork at Pconn 0.4, linearly interpolated or extrapolated in between; beta(3,5) input peaking at 10 ms; Poisson background 5,000 Hz excitatory and 2,000 Hz inhibitory; dt 0.1 ms; 20 s per run). The input strengths for Pconn 0.2 and 0.4 are set under each reading named in the opening comment and interpolated linearly for other Pconn. Amplification is the inverse of input strength normalized to Pconn 0.2 (Methods). As the strengths between 0.2 and 0.4 are interpolated from the two matched ones, amplification over that range follows from them alone. Reproduced when the matched input strength at Pconn 0.4 is below that at 0.2, so that normalized amplification at 0.4 is above 1, under each reading; the readings' strengths and amplification are reported.

  • model-equal-connectivity-ablation-increases

    In networks with equal subnetwork connectivity (Pconn 0.2), ablating the strongest encoders raises the encoding scores of spared neurons, from 0.237 to 0.274, owing to reduced feedback inhibition.

    Encoding scores for spared neurons increased after simulated ablation of 10% of the subnetwork14, owing to reduced feedback inhibition13,20 (encoding score, from 0.237 ± 0.027 to 0.274 ± 0.032

    Results, paragraph 4; Figure 1d-f

    How it will be judged

    Model as in model-amplification-increases at Pconn 0.2, 30 random networks. Encoding score per neuron as the Methods define it (spikes convolved with a 20 ms s.d. Gaussian, rate and stimulus downsampled to 0.5 ms, peak normalized cross-correlation within lags of 10 ms). The 20 excitatory neurons with the highest score are ablated by setting their outgoing synapses to 0 (Methods, which fix 20; the Results say 10% of the subnetwork, also 20) and the run is repeated. Neurons with score above 0.1 before or after, ablated neurons left out; per network the median score before and after; grand median across networks; Wilcoxon signed-rank test over networks. Reproduced when both grand medians are within tolerance and the test gives an increase at p < 0.001.

  • model-all-or-none-robust

    Subnetwork connectivity above 0.4 gives all-or-none responses, a transient input driving the subnetwork into persistent activity, and such networks keep their all-or-none response after ablation of 10% of the subnetwork.

    Networks in the all-or-none regime19 were robust to ablation, and maintained their all-or-none response

    Results, paragraphs 3 and 5; Extended Data Fig. 1 (Pconn 0.44)

    How it will be judged

    Model at Pconn 0.44 (Extended Data Fig. 1), with the input strength twice the extrapolated value and a strong excitatory pulse to the inhibitory population 300 ms after each stimulus onset (Methods). The pulse strength is not given; the reading used is the smallest pulse that returns the subnetwork rate to its pre-stimulus level before the next stimulus. All-or-none is read as a bimodal response, each stimulus either driving the subnetwork to a sustained rate until the reset pulse or leaving it near baseline. The paper gives no measure of robustness beyond "maintained their all-or-none response". Reproduced when the response is all-or-none before and after ablating the 20 strongest encoders (10% of the subnetwork, Extended Data Fig. 1 legend); the fraction of stimuli that trigger the persistent state and the change in encoding score are reported.

  • model-more-ablation-larger-decline

Whether the 0-ablation networks enter is open; both readings are run. Reproduced when R is within tolerance with p < 0.01 under one reading, and the median change is more negative for each larger ablation size.

  • model-response-similarity

    In networks with increased subnetwork connectivity, neurons whose activity is more similar to the mean activity of the ablated neurons lose the most encoding score after ablation; with equal connectivity the relationship is absent.

    In networks with increased subnetwork connectivity, neurons with high response similarity showed the largest decline in encoding score after ablation (Fig. 3b). In networks without increased subnetwork connectivity, the relationship disappeared

    Results, paragraph 10; Figure 3a, b

    How it will be judged

    Response similarity is the Pearson correlation of a neuron's Gaussian-convolved rate (20 ms s.d.) with the mean convolved rate of the ablated neurons (Methods, Response similarity), neurons with encoding score above 0.25 (Methods). Per network, a line is fitted to the change in encoding score against response similarity of the neurons (Methods, Statistical analyses, "we first fit a line to individual networks or mice (for example, Fig. 3e; the linear fit is distinct from the cross-cell mean that is shown)"). A fit to the means in 0.1-wide similarity bins, as the analysis code does for the mice (ablation_figure_correlation.m), is run as a variant. Sign test of the 30 slopes against 0. Reproduced when at Pconn 0.4 the slopes are negative with p < 0.05 and at Pconn 0.2 the sign test gives p >= 0.05.
