Eyherabide et al. 2008 · Other journals

Burst firing is a neural code in an insect auditory system

doi:10.3389/neuro.10.003.2008 · paper key eyherabide-2008

Datasets

  • CRCNS:ia-1

Most of the paper's tested findings on burst coding held up in this data, but its session counts and the share of information carried by bursts did not. Its threshold model could not be tested because Data1 holds no thresholds. Held: bursts are less correlated than single spikes, burst probability follows stimulus statistics, burst-triggered stimulus averages differ by burst length, and their latency falls with length. Table 1's counts did not reproduce, probably because Data1 gives 115 sessions against the paper's 86 and does not mark which were kept. Information per burst rising with length held only partly, since estimator bias is of the order of the rise.

  • ◐Partially reproduced Thirty-seven receptor cells were recorded from 23 animals, giving 132 sessions, of which 86 met the firing-rate and stationarity conditions; each stimulus was repeated 98 to 503 times, 172 on average.
  • ?Not reproduced The 86 kept sessions divide among the seven stimulus protocols as 43 (6 dB, 200 Hz), 6 (3 dB, 200 Hz), 7 (12 dB, 200 Hz), 7 (6 dB, 25 Hz), 8 (6 dB, 100 Hz), 7 (6 dB, 400 Hz) and 8 (6 dB, 800 Hz).
  • ✓Reproduced The kept sessions had a mean firing rate of 113 Hz (SD 16 Hz) that did not differ significantly across stimulus conditions (one-way ANOVA, p = 0.58).
  • ⊘Blocked The mean threshold of the cells at their best frequency was 58 dB (SD 14 dB).
  • ?Not reproduced The number of sessions with bursts of n > 1 depends on the stimulus, from none for 3 dB and for 800 Hz to all 7 for 12 dB and 7 of 8 for 100 Hz; 40 of 43 reference sessions, 6 of 7 at 25 Hz and 3 of 7 at 400 Hz.
  • ?Not reproduced The percentage of isolated spikes among all bursts of the bursting sessions depends on the stimulus (74, 100, 62, 60, 55, 96 and 100% for protocols 1 to 7), and in every protocol isolated spikes are the most probable burst.
  • ?Not reproduced The highest n and the average n depend on the stimulus; the longest bursts (up to 15 spikes, average 2.10 with SD 3.67) occur at 25 Hz, and no bursts occur at 3 dB or at 800 Hz.
  • ✓Reproduced The probability of generating isolated spikes is lowest, and high-n bursts most frequent, for large amplitude fluctuations (12 dB) and cutoff frequencies around 100 Hz.
  • ✓Reproduced For a cell tested with all stimuli, larger stimulus SD gives correlation functions with sharper peaks, and faster stimuli lead from multi-modal (25 Hz) to single-peaked (100, 200 Hz) to shallower and broader correlation functions (400, 800 Hz).
  • ✓Reproduced The temporal average of the estimated error of the correlation function was on average 3.4% (SD 1.5%) of the span of C_s.
  • ⊘Blocked A threshold-linear Poisson model with refractoriness and no free parameters classifies sessions as bursting or not in agreement with the real data in 86% of sessions, and where both are bursting their limiting ISIs differ by less than 1 ms in 81% of cases.
  • ✓Reproduced Representing the spike train as n-bursts reduces correlations between time bins; the mean squared Pearson coefficient over t from 200 to 990 ms and tau from 0 to 10 ms is 2.89 times larger (SD 1.49) for spikes than for bursts, on average over the bursting sessions (2.94 for the example cell).
  • ◐Partially reproduced The information carried by each n-burst increases with n; every bursting session has a significantly positive slope of I_n(1) against n, with an average slope of 1.5 bits (SD 0.7 bits) over 59 bursting cells.
  • ◐Partially reproduced High-n bursts are rare; in every session the frequency of occurrence decreases significantly with n, with an average slope of -2.6 (SD 0.7).
  • ✓Reproduced The jitter of the first spike of a burst is roughly independent of n in 80% of the bursting sessions, and the mean slope of jitter against n is -0.03 ms (SD 0.2 ms).
  • ?Not reproduced The information rate of n-bursts decreases with n (average slope -11 bits/s, SD 16 bits/s), yet bursts with n > 1 together carry on average 47% of the total information over the bursting cells; in the example cell the total is 220 bits/s.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Information rates from the burst code are highly correlated with those of the direct method (R = 0.95), averaging 191 against 222 bits/s, the direct method being higher in all sessions but one by 31 bits/s on average, so the burst code keeps about 85% of the information.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In every bursting cell the burst-triggered averages for n = 1 to 4 differ significantly (in the example cell their central peaks grow higher and wider with n); 50 of the 58 bursting sessions show significantly different central peaks in the time domain, the other eight having too few bursts.
  • ✓Reproduced The latency between the stimulus upstroke and burst onset decreases with n, so that high or wide deflections produce prompt responses with high-n bursts.
  • ?Not reproduced The standard deviation of the stimuli preceding an n-burst has a minimum at a position nearly the same for all n (about 7 ms before burst onset in the example cell, with an SD of 0.33 ms across n); over the bursting cells the SD of the minimum's position across n = 1 to 4 averages 0.4 ms (SD 0.7 ms), roughly 0.05 times the inverse cutoff frequency.
  • ✓Reproduced In 98% of bursting cells, for all n, the SD of the stimuli preceding an n-burst is smaller than the SD of the stimuli preceding all spikes, by a ratio of minima of 1.62 on average (SD 0.56).
  • ✓Reproduced The nBTA differs from n 1BTAs shifted by the burst's own ISIs and summed; for a large fraction of cells, real and simulated, the difference is significant, the fraction diminishing as n grows; results are similar with shifted STAs.
  • ✓Reproduced In the seven cells driven at 100 Hz cutoff and 6 dB, the population-average nBTAs show that bursts with more spikes follow higher or wider stimulus deflections, with large spread across cells.
  • ?Not reproduced The probability of a burst with n spikes after a stimulus maximum depends on its height, low excursions giving no response or an isolated spike and high ones doublets, triplets or 4-bursts; all bursting cells show this.
  • ✓Reproduced The firing rate has practically no effect on the limiting ISI; a 50 Hz increase in rate shifts it by less than 0.4 ms and raises the average n by less than 25%.
  • ⊘Blocked The threshold-linear model with refractoriness reproduces the coding trends of the data, at single-cell and population level, though it is less precise in time than the real cells.
