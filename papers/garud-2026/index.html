Continuous Temporal Difference Learning as a Unifying Theory of Dopamine Function

Sankalp Garud, Laurel S. Morris

Summary: In this dandiset's mouse photometry the behavioural findings held: anticipatory licking was higher in short-ITI sessions and rose with training. The tonic dopamine dip late in long inter-trial intervals held only against a baseline of zero ΔF/F, which the session-wide 405 nm correction forces, and there was no dip against the pre-cue level. The short-ITI reward response did not persist to the next cue as reported. The dip deepened with training, but no reading of methods the paper leaves open matched its r and p together. On the rat maze data held elsewhere, the dopamine ramps and the Gaussian continuous-time TD model fit reproduced, while the recovered value function held only in part.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • Two files that hold the same recording as another day's file (JF-dLight-ramp2-F2 Day4, JF-dLight-ramp2-F4 Day6) are counted once, at the first day.floeder-subjects-sessions
  • The paper reports no test for this panel; the one-sample one-tailed t-test across mice is the criterion's, taken from the paper's test of the stationary dip.fig4b-long-iti-dip-below-baseline
  • The session's tonic value is the mean over trials of each trial's mean over its kept bins in the window, a standard choice the Methods do not state.fig4b-long-iti-dip-below-baseline
  • Photometry samples are placed on the event clock by a fit of TTL2 rising edges to cue times; the files state no link between the two clocks.fig4b-long-iti-dip-below-baseline
  • The files hold no depositor ΔF/F (processing/ holds only the task parameters), so ΔF/F is formed from the raw 470 and 405 nm channels under the criterion's two readings.fig4b-long-iti-dip-below-baseline
  • The mixed model is fitted with statsmodels MixedLM (REML), random intercepts and slopes per mouse, in place of the Julia package the paper used. It is fitted with L-BFGS, Powell and Nelder-Mead and the converged fit with the highest REML likelihood is kept, as L-BFGS alone stopped at a lower optimum in some runs (fit, cue, samples, all sessions gave p = 0.014 in the round 2 notebook, against 0.00064 at the higher optimum).fig4d-dip-deepens-with-training
  • The Methods do not name the columns; the mapping above is this attempt's, as the criterion asks.krausz-included-observations
  • The criterion's "position-mean DA" is read two ways, the mean DA at each distance to the goal (the axis the paper plots; a hex position has many distances, as the goal changes by trial), and the mean DA at each hex position placed at its mean distance. Both are shown.fig2a-ramp-toward-goal
  • With 5 to 12 sessions per rat, a quartile is round(n/4) sessions, at least one (1 to 3); the Methods say only "first quartile" and "last quartile".fig2c-ramp-similar-early-late
  • The 20 distances of readings (a) and (b) are 1 to 20, the 20 nearest the goal; distance 21 has more rows than 20 (1097 against 908), and the 20 with the most rows are shown as well. The paper says only df = 18.fig2f-coupling-grows-near-goal
  • Assumed that "approach and non-approach" observations are all rows off the ports. The Figure 2G-H legend says both were used; the port rows are the rat at the port itself, before or after reward, which the Methods' selection also sets apart. All three row sets are shown.fig2gh-stationary-dip
  • The gradient is computed per hex position and distance to the goal, averaged per distance, and integrated toward the goal with scipy's RK45 (Dormand-Prince) on the linear interpolant, in place of Julia's Tsit5; both are explicit 5(4) Runge-Kutta solvers.fig3-recovered-value-increases
  • Profile forms as the criterion reads them (A exp(-λd) + c, A / (1 + kd) + c, A (d + 1)^(-k) + c, A exp(-d²/2σ²) + c); BIC = n ln(SSE/n) + k ln n. A and c are solved by least squares for each value of the shape parameter, which is searched on a grid and refined. The shape parameter is kept positive (a profile decaying with distance), as the paper reads A > 0 as a net increase toward the goal, which holds only for a decaying profile; it is searched from 1e-6 to 20 per hex (hyperbolic to 1000) and σ from 0.1 to 10,000 hexes.fig3-recovered-value-increases
  • In the (b1) row set the start hex of each trial, which has no distance in the table, is placed one hex beyond the trial's next row; the approach rows (a) are the reading recorded.fig3-recovered-value-increases
  • Assumed no minimum observation count, the literal reading of the Methods, which name none; the minimum of 5 is shown as the other reading.fig3-recovered-value-increases
  • Fits and readings as in fig3-recovered-value-increases.fig3cd-gaussian-shape-wins
  • The Methods fit all parameters by multi-start Nelder-Mead (4 initial vectors and 10 perturbations each). Here the linear parameters are solved by least squares for each value of the spatial scale, which is searched on a grid (λ from -5 to 5 per hex, 301 values; σ from 0.3 to 10,000 hexes, 200 values) and refined, then checked by Nelder-Mead from the optimum. This finds the least-squares optimum without random starts.fig5bc-gaussian-ct-td-best
  • Fits as in fig5bc-gaussian-ct-td-best.fig5d-tonic-term-and-gaussian-contribute
