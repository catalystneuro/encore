Claims 18

From the paper Continuous Temporal Difference Learning as a Unifying Theory of Dopamine Function · claims file · checked 2026-11-25: ready

  • The Floeder dataset holds 18 mice, each with 16 to 24 sessions, alternating between short-ITI and long-ITI conditions across days.

    Methods, Datasets, paragraph 2; Methods, Floeder (2025) dataset, paragraph 1floeder-subjects-sessions
    From the paper

    Mice (n = 18) completed 16–24 sessions each, alternating between short-ITI (~15 s median inter-trial interval) and long-ITI (~45 s) conditions across days

    Criterion

    Mice with photometry and event data are counted, so the tolerance on the count is zero; the per-mouse session counts must fall within 16 to 24 widened by 10% of each bound (14.4 to 26.4). Sessions are classed as the Methods say, by the median inter-cue interval of the session, below 25 s short and 25 s or more long. Reproduced when the count is 18, every mouse's session count is in range, and both classes occur in every mouse. The session median inter-cue intervals of each class are reported against the two sets of values the text gives (about 8 and 55 s in the Results, about 15 and 45 s in the Methods).

  • Mice show higher anticipatory lick rates in short-ITI sessions than in long-ITI sessions.

    Results, paragraph on tonic dopamine (Fig. 4A); Figure 4A legendfig4a-lick-short-above-long
    From the paper

    Mice in short-ITI sessions showed higher anticipatory lick rates (Fig. 4A)

    Criterion

    Methods, Floeder (2025) dataset, paragraph 2. Anticipatory lick rate per trial is the number of lick onsets 5 to 8 s after cue onset divided by 3 s; sessions are short or long by median inter-cue interval (25 s). Short against long rates are compared with a paired t-test across subjects, at p < 0.05 (Methods, Analysis). Open, the paper does not say how a subject's rate in each condition is formed; the readings are (a) the mean over that subject's sessions of the session mean over trials, (b) the mean over all its trials of that condition pooled. It does not say whether the test is one- or two-tailed; the two-tailed test is used, as the paper names one-tailed where it uses it (Figure 2G-H). Lick onsets are read as the lick event timestamps the NWB files hold. Reproduced when, under a reading, short is higher with p < 0.05.

  • During the late inter-trial interval of long-ITI sessions, dopamine settles below baseline, while in short-ITI sessions a slowly decaying reward response does not resolve before the next trial.

    Results, paragraph on tonic dopamine (Fig. 4B); Figure 4B legend; Discussion, paragraph 7fig4b-long-iti-dip-below-baseline
    From the paper

    long-ITI dopamine settled reliably below baseline ... while the short-ITI trace showed a slowly decaying reward response that did not resolve before the next trial (Fig. 4B)

    Criterion

    Methods, Floeder (2025) dataset. The 470 nm signal is corrected by regressing out the 405 nm signal and computing ΔF/F; it is binned in 1 s windows locked to reward delivery, each trial's trace cut 1 s before the next cue onset, and bins with fewer than 20 observations dropped. The tonic measure is the mean ΔF/F from the midpoint of the ITI to 1 s before the next cue, in long-ITI trials with ITI of 30 s or more, per subject and session. Open, and named as readings, (1) the correction, (a) a least-squares fit of the 405 nm signal to the 470 nm signal per session, ΔF/F = (470 - fit) / fit, or (b) ΔF/F of each channel against its own session mean, then the fitted 405 ΔF/F subtracted; no filtering is named, so none is applied. (2) ITI duration and midpoint, (a) from reward delivery to the next cue (the trace is reward locked), or (b) from cue to next cue. (3) An observation in the 20-observation rule, (a) a trial contributing to the bin, or (b) a sample. Baseline is read as ΔF/F of zero; the paper names no pre-cue baseline window. The paper reports no test for this panel; the test used is the one it uses for the matching stationary dip (Methods, Krausz dataset), a one-sample t-test across subjects of each subject's mean tonic measure against zero, one-tailed as there. For the short-ITI part, "did not resolve" is read as the trace not having returned to baseline, so the across-subject mean ΔF/F of the last kept bin before the next cue in short-ITI trials must be above zero; the paper reports no test for it, so the value is reported with its SEM. Reproduced when the long-ITI measure is below zero at p < 0.05 under every reading and the short-ITI part holds; partially reproduced when only the long-ITI part holds. If the readings differ, the one the paper best supports is adopted as an assumption.

  • In long-ITI sessions the tonic dopamine dip deepens across training sessions (r = -0.29, p < 0.001).

    Results, paragraph on tonic dopamine (Fig. 4D); Figure 4D-E legendfig4d-dip-deepens-with-training
    From the paper

    we found that the dip deepened across training sessions as V̂ was learned (Fig. 4D, r=-0.29, p<1e-3)

    Criterion

    The per-session tonic measure of fig4b-long-iti-dip-below-baseline (with its readings) is regressed on session number in a linear mixed-effects model with random intercepts and slopes per subject (Methods, Floeder (2025) dataset, paragraph 2). Open, (1) session number, (a) the session's place among all of the mouse's sessions in date order, or (b) its place among the mouse's long-ITI sessions; (2) the r the paper prints beside the model, (a) Pearson r over all subject-session values pooled, or (b) Pearson r after removing each subject's mean. Reproduced when the fixed slope on session number is negative with p < 0.001 and r is within tolerance under one reading; partially reproduced when the slope is negative with p < 0.05 but r is out of tolerance or p is not below 0.001.

  • Anticipatory lick rate increases across training sessions, in parallel with the deepening tonic dip (r = 0.65).

    Results, paragraph on tonic dopamine (Fig. 4C); Figure 4C legendfig4c-lick-rate-rises-with-training
    From the paper

    while anticipatory vigour increased in parallel (Fig. 4C, r = 0.65, p=so this 1e-4)

    Criterion

    Per-session anticipatory lick rate (lick onsets 5 to 8 s after cue onset over 3 s, mean over trials) regressed on session number in a linear mixed-effects model with random intercepts and slopes per subject (Methods, Floeder (2025) dataset, paragraph 2). The p-value is printed garbled ("p=so this 1e-4") and is read as 1e-4. Open, (1) the sessions, (a) long-ITI sessions only, as the Results focus on the long-ITI condition and the legend says "Lick rate increases with long ITI sessions", or (b) all sessions; (2) session number and (3) r, with the same readings as fig4d-dip-deepens-with-training. Reproduced when the fixed slope is positive with p < 0.05 and r is within tolerance under one reading; partially reproduced when the slope is positive with p < 0.05 and r is out of tolerance.

  • The approach-epoch selection of the Krausz hex-level data keeps 264,042 of 298,918 observations across 10 rats.

    Methods, Krausz (2023) dataset, paragraph 1; Methods, Datasets, paragraph 1krausz-included-observations
    From the paper

    This yielded 264,042 included observations across 10 rats

    Criterion

    Observations in an approach epoch are kept; those not in transit, at the reward port, after reward was received, or with missing values are dropped (Methods). The columns that mark each condition are not named; the author maps them from the table and states the mapping.

  • In freely moving rats, NAc dopamine ramps up as the rats approach a reward port.

    Results, paragraph after Figure 1 legend; Figure 2Afig2a-ramp-toward-goal
    From the paper

    DA ramped as animals approached their goals (Fig. 2A)

    Criterion

    Included approach observations; DA averaged within each rat at each hex position, then across rats (Methods), plotted against distance to the chosen port. The paper names no test for the ramp. The reading used is built from the tests it names, per rat the Pearson r between distance to goal and position-mean DA, tested against zero across rats with a one-sample t-test at p < 0.05. Reproduced when the mean r is negative (DA higher nearer the goal) and significant.

  • The dopamine ramp is steeper on approaches to high reward (80 µL or more) than to low reward (20 µL or less).

    Results, paragraph after Figure 1 legend; Figure 2B legendfig2b-ramp-scales-with-reward
    From the paper

    The ramp is steeper on approaches ending at high reward (>80 µL juice) versus low reward (< 20 µL)

    Criterion

    Within each rat, approaches split by destination reward of 80 µL or more against 20 µL or less (Methods; the legend prints > and <, the Methods 80 and 20 inclusive, and the Methods are followed). No test is named. Readings for "steeper", (a) per-rat slope of DA on distance to goal, or (b) per-rat mean DA over the approach, each compared high against low with a paired t-test across rats (the paper's paired test). Reproduced when high is steeper (more negative slope) or larger at p < 0.05 under a reading.

  • Ramps in early and late sessions look similar, with at most a modest reduction in ramp amplitude from early to late sessions.

    Results, paragraph after Figure 1 legend; Figure 2C legendfig2c-ramp-similar-early-late
    From the paper

    the ramps in early and late sessions look similar (i.e. the ramps do not flatten across time in the absence of learning)

    Criterion

    Sessions split within each rat into first and last quartile (Methods). The legend says "a modest reduction in ramp amplitude" and the Results that the ramps "look similar"; no test is named. Judged as a claim of absence with the paper's paired t-test across rats of per-rat ramp amplitude, read as the slope of DA on distance to goal. Reproduced when p is 0.05 or more; the size and sign of the change are reported either way.

  • On fast approaches, dopamine is larger at the same positions than on slow ones.

    Results, paragraph after Figure 1 legend; Figure 2D legendfig2d-ramp-scales-with-velocity
    From the paper

    fast trials show larger DA at the same positions, consistent with velocity scaling the ramp

    Criterion

    Within each rat, observations split into top and bottom velocity quartiles (Methods). No test is named; the reading used is the paper's paired t-test across rats of per-rat mean DA over positions present in both splits, fast against slow. Reproduced when fast is larger at p < 0.05.

  • Velocity co-varies with distance to goal, and average dopamine and velocity covary across distance.

    Results, paragraph after Figure 1 legend; Figure 2E legendfig2e-velocity-covaries-with-distance
    From the paper

    We found that the velocity co-varied with the distance to goal (Fig. 2E)

    Criterion

    Mean velocity and mean DA per distance to goal, averaged within rat then across rats (Methods). No test is named; per rat the Pearson r between distance and mean velocity, and per rat the Pearson r between mean DA and mean velocity across distance values, each tested across rats with a one-sample t-test against zero. Reproduced when both are significant at p < 0.05; partially reproduced when only the velocity and distance part is. The signs are reported, as the paper does not give them.

  • The within-position coupling of velocity and dopamine increases with proximity to the goal (r = -0.494, df = 18, p = 0.027).

    Results, paragraph after Figure 1 legend; Figure 2Ffig2f-coupling-grows-near-goal
    From the paper

    the velocity-dopamine coupling increased with proximity to goal, consistent with the idea that velocity scales with the gradient (Fig 2F, r=-0.494, df=18, p = 0.027)

    Criterion

    Within-position DA-velocity correlations as Pearson's r at each hex position (Methods), then correlated with distance to goal. df = 18 means 20 points, which neither the 49 positions nor 10 rats give. Readings, (a) one point per distance-to-goal value (20 values), the within-distance r over observations pooled across rats; (b) the same with r computed per rat and averaged; (c) the 20 hex positions with enough observations. Reproduced when r is within tolerance with p < 0.05 under a reading.

  • When rats are stationary (velocity below 5 cm/s), dopamine dips below zero (t = -4.27, df = 9, p = 0.001, one-tailed).

    Results, paragraph on the stationary prediction; Figure 2G-H legendfig2gh-stationary-dip
    From the paper

    found that indeed the dopamine dipped below baseline (df =9, t = -4.27, p = 0.001, one-tailed) in instances where the rats’ velocity was near zero (Fig 2G-H)

    Criterion

    Stationary observations are velocity below 5 cm/s (Results and Methods); per-rat mean stationary DA tested against zero with a one-sample t-test across rats, one-tailed (Methods). Open, which observations, (a) approach observations only, as the Methods selection says, or (b) approach and non-approach observations, as the Figure 2G-H legend says. Reproduced when t is within tolerance and p < 0.05 under a reading. The paired t-test of moving (15 cm/s or more) against stationary per-rat DA (Methods) is reported as well.

  • The value function recovered from moving minus stationary dopamine shows a net increase toward the goal in 8 of 10 rats.

    Results, paragraph on internal consistency (Fig. 3A-B); Figure 3 legendfig3-recovered-value-increases
    From the paper

    The recovered value function showed a net increase toward the goal in 8 out of 10 rats (A>0 in BIC winning model)

    Criterion

    Methods, Krausz (2023) dataset, paragraph 3. Per hex position, gradient = (mean moving DA - mean stationary DA) / mean moving velocity, moving 15 cm/s or more and stationary below 5 cm/s; integrated along the approach axis with Tsit5 to give V(x) up to a constant; four three-parameter profiles fitted per rat and the best chosen by BIC. A count of rats has no 10% tolerance short of the exact value. Open, (1) the approach axis, read as distance to the chosen port, with gradients averaged per distance; (2) the profile forms, not given, read by analogy with the Figure 5 forms as A exp(-λd) + c, A / (1 + kd) + c, A (d + 1)^(-k) + c and A exp(-d²/2σ²) + c; (3) BIC from the sum of squared errors, n ln(SSE/n) + k ln n, the standard form for least squares. Reproduced when 8 rats have A > 0; partially reproduced for 7 or 9.

  • All four candidate shapes fit each rat's recovered value function well (mean R² above 0.8), and the best shape varies across rats, Gaussian in 8, exponential in 1 and power-law in 1.

    Results, paragraph on internal consistency (Fig. 3C-D); Figure 3 legendfig3cd-gaussian-shape-wins
    From the paper

    All four provided good fits (mean R² > 0.8), with the best-fitting shape varying across rats ... a Gaussian profile won in 8, exponential in 1, and power-law in 1 (Fig 3C-D)

    Criterion

    Fits and readings as in fig3-recovered-value-increases. Reproduced when the mean R² over rats exceeds 0.8 for each of the four shapes and the BIC winners are 8 Gaussian, 1 exponential and 1 power-law; partially reproduced when the Gaussian wins in most rats but the counts differ.

  • Of six models of approach dopamine, the Gaussian continuous-time TD model fits best summed across rats and wins in 8 of 10 rats by BIC.

    Results, paragraph on model comparison (Fig. 5B-C); Figure 5B-C legendfig5bc-gaussian-ct-td-best
    From the paper

    The Gaussian CT TD model provided the best fit across rats (Fig. 5B, Krausz 2023 dataset), and was also winning in 8 of 10 individual rats by BIC (Fig. 5C)

    Criterion

    Methods, Model Comparison. Six models of DA on distance d and velocity v (CT TD exponential and Gaussian with 4 parameters; dV/dt exponential and Gaussian and Value with 3; Velocity with 2), fitted to each rat's approach observations by Nelder-Mead from 4 initial vectors plus 10 random perturbations each, 200,000 iterations per start, keeping the lowest sum of squared errors; compared by BIC, ΔBIC relative to the best model summed over rats. Open, (1) the BIC formula, read as n ln(SSE/n) + k ln n; (2) the initial vectors and perturbation size, not given, which affect only whether the optimum is found (a standard spread and a fixed seed are used and stated). Reproduced when the Gaussian CT TD model has the lowest summed BIC and wins in 8 rats; partially reproduced when it is best summed but wins in a different number of rats.

  • In a factorial decomposition, the tonic term improves fit by ΔBIC = 1156 and the Gaussian spatial profile by ΔBIC = 706.

    Results, paragraph on model comparison (Fig. 5D); Figure 5D legendfig5d-tonic-term-and-gaussian-contribute

    The tonic term −ρV̂ improved fit by ΔBIC =1156, while the Gaussian ... profile improved fit by ΔBIC = 706

Open, how a factor's ΔBIC is formed; readings, (a) the main effect, the mean over the two levels of the other factor of the summed BIC difference; (b) the difference at the Gaussian level (tonic) and at the CT TD level (profile); (c) the difference at the exponential level (tonic) and at the dV/dt level (profile). Reproduced when both values are within tolerance under one reading.
