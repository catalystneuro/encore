Internally organized mechanisms of the head direction sense

Adrien Peyrache, Marie Masako Lacroix, Peter C Petersen, György Buzsáki

Outcomes of 21 claims

Summary: The findings that could be tested mostly held up, but the comparisons between thalamus and post-subiculum could not be tested. Head-direction cells kept their rates and pairwise correlations across waking, REM and slow-wave sleep, and the 60 degree offset, noise correlations beyond independent firing and decoding also reproduced, with regions pooled. The tenfold faster internal signal in slow-wave sleep was only partly reproduced, and the estimator's bias leaves the size of the speed-up undetermined. The fraction of cells classed as head-direction did not match, and the matching count depends on one session whose position and spikes are misaligned. The region comparisons are blocked because the units carry no region labels.

  • ◐Partially reproducedResults, paragraph 3; Figure 2e,f; Methods, Estimation of angular velocityfig2f-sws-tenfold-fasterlink to this claim

    The angular velocity of the internal HD signal, estimated from the temporal profile of pairwise cross-correlograms, is about tenfold faster during SWS than during waking.

    Over 1509 pairs (64 of 1573 dropped for a fit at the edge of the speed range), the median ratio of SWS to…

    The angular velocity of the internal HD signal, either estimated by a Bayesian decoder or from the temporal profile of pairwise cross-correlograms ... was approximately tenfold faster during SWS than during waking ( Fig. 2e,f )

    Results, paragraph 3; Figure 2e,f; Methods, Estimation of angular velocity

    Over 1509 pairs (64 of 1573 dropped for a fit at the edge of the speed range), the median ratio of SWS to waking angular velocity is 8.33 (quartiles 5.91 to 11.31), below 9 to 11; SWS is faster in 1420 pairs (sign test p = 4.1e-309). Median speeds are 36.9 deg/s in waking, 33.5 in REM and 324.4 in SWS. By mouse the ratio is 7.89 to 13.52. The Bayesian decoder variant gives a median of 11.53 over 28 sessions. Under reading (b) the median is 8.02 over 2129 pairs; with pairs whose fits reach R2 of 0.25 or 0.5 in every state, 8.46 (1063 pairs) and 8.56 (748 pairs). Median R2 is 0.84 in waking, 0.55 in REM and 0.78 in SWS. The waking estimates are 0.60 times the measured mean angular speed of the head (median over sessions) and correlate with it at r = 0.20 across 33 sessions. On Poisson surrogates driven by the measured head direction and the waking tuning curves, the estimator returns 0.37 of the true mean speed (median 37.0 deg/s, as on the data, against a true 99.9), and for the same trajectory played ten times faster it gives a median ratio of 4.99 (quartiles 2.76 to 7.02), not 10. Scaled by that compression the data's 8.33 becomes 16.69, so the data are consistent with a speed-up below, at or above tenfold. The estimator shows that SWS is faster but does not determine the fold; how far its bias in SWS matches that of the surrogates is not known.

    How the test differed from the paper’s method

    • The paper does not say how the expected and observed cross-correlograms are put on one scale; a free scale and offset are fitted here.
    • The lag range (4 s) and speed grid are chosen here.
    • The expectation over the chi-squared speed distribution is computed exactly from its characteristic function.
    • Pairs within and across structures are pooled; the paper shows ADn-ADn and PoS-PoS pairs.

  • openasked about fig4d-ole-near-bayesian

    The criterion takes the signal OLE's Q "from the tuning curves read at the head direction". The paper contrasts "tuning curve covariances (i.e., signal correlation only)" with "spike train covariances (i.e., including noise correlation)", which excludes the noise correlations between cells, not each cell's own variance. Should the criterion say that the signal Q keeps the observed variances on its diagonal (independent neurons, the usual signal-only OLE), with the tuning curves giving only the off-diagonal terms? The same estimator decides fig4e-ole-covariance.

    Passage. Results, paragraph 5, "Optimal linear estimates (OLE, see Methods ) of the head-direction were enhanced when using spike train covariances (i.e., including noise correlation) instead of the tuning curve covariances (i.e., signal correlation only)."

    Why it matters. Read as the criterion words it (Q from the tuning curves throughout), the signal decoder errs by up to 103 degrees in some ensembles, beyond the 90 degrees of chance, so the covariance OLE wins by default (fig4e median ratio 0.25 in ADn). With the observed variances on the diagonal the two OLEs barely differ (ratio 0.99), and the first part of fig4d holds only with the ensembles of hd-b cells. The round-2 verifier reported the wording as inviting the reading that does not work; the attempt now adopts the observed-variance reading and shows the other as a variant.

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked about recording-sample

    The criterion defines only "reproduced" (all four values within tolerance under one reading, or the total matching the table's 50 sessions). It gives no rule for "partially reproduced" or "not reproduced". Should it say which applies when the mice and session counts match and the counts of sessions and mice with both structures do not?

    Passage. Results, paragraph 1, "in 7 mice foraging for food in an open environment (42 sessions) and in their home cages during sleep ... In 3 mice (21 sessions) ADn and PoS recordings were performed simultaneously."

    Why it matters. The files label units of four mice ADn and PoS, so 24 sessions in 4 mice hold both structures, against 21 in 3; 7 mice and 41 sessions are within tolerance. The attempt records partially reproduced by its own judgement, which the round-2 verifier found reasonable but noted is not set by the criterion.

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked about fig4d-ole-near-bayesian

    The first part of the criterion needs only that the covariance OLE beats the signal OLE with p < 0.05 in each structure, so any small but significant gain satisfies it. Should it also require a size of effect, such as the "approximately 50%" the paper gives in ADn, or is that left to fig4e-ole-covariance?

    Passage. Results, paragraph 5, "Optimal linear estimates (OLE, see Methods ) of the head-direction were enhanced when using spike train covariances (i.e., including noise correlation) instead of the tuning curve covariances (i.e., signal correlation only)."

    Why it matters. With the ensembles of hd-b cells the covariance OLE is better with p = 0.019 (ADn) and 0.042 (PoS), but by about 1% of the error in ADn. The round-3 verifier reported that the criterion lets so small a gain decide the first part, while the paper's size of effect is tested only in fig4e.

  • Regions pooled, as the units carry no region.hd-cell-fraction
  • The von Mises fit (maximum likelihood fit to the tuning curve as a distribution of angles) and the Rayleigh test (resultant length of the tuning curve with the number of waking spikes) are choices made here; the Methods do not specify them.hd-cell-fraction
  • The open-field epoch is found from position, as the files do not mark it.hd-cell-fraction
  • Mouse17-130201 and Mouse32-140820 have no processed position and are left out.hd-cell-fraction
  • Mouse12-120808 is counted as deposited, with position and spikes on different clocks; the realigned session is a variant.hd-cell-fraction
  • ADn and PoS cells pooled; the paper plots them separately.fig2a-rates-preserved
  • Pairs within and across structures are pooled, and pairs on the same shank cannot be removed.fig2b-pair-correlations-preserved
  • Bin sizes are those of Figure 3a; Figure 2b does not state them.fig2b-pair-correlations-preserved
  • ADn-ADn, ADn-PoS and PoS-PoS pairs are pooled.fig3a-packet-60-degrees

  • The expectation over the chi-squared speed distribution is computed exactly from its characteristic function.fig2f-sws-tenfold-faster
  • Pairs within and across structures are pooled; the paper shows ADn-ADn and PoS-PoS pairs.fig2f-sws-tenfold-faster

  • Regions pooled.fig4b-noise-correlations
