The central finding held in this data: awake activity patterns were reactivated more strongly in slow-wave sleep after the task than before it, in all three encoding-strength groups. Two related findings did not hold. The link to encoding strength was positive but weaker than reported (r squared 0.38 against 0.61), and the heavier-tailed post-task distributions appeared in only one group. The ripple, delta-wave, spindle and learning findings could not be tested, because the data set has no LFP or event times. The paper's session and cell counts and its rule-learning counts were not matched, partly because the data set holds fewer sessions of one rat and does not say which sessions were analysed. Several controls held only in part.

Reproduction Replay of rule-learning related patterns in rat prefrontal cortex during sleep (Peyrache et al. 2009)

Tests the findings of Peyrache, Khamassi, Benchenane, Wiener and Battaglia (2009) on CRCNS pfc-6, which holds the prefrontal spike trains, behaviour, position and the awake and SWS intervals of 86 sessions from four rats. It counts sessions, rules and signal components, and tests reactivation of awake patterns in post against pre SWS, its relation to encoding strength, its tail, the shuffle control, cross-correlations between patterns, reactivation around DOWN to UP transitions, and behaviour around learning. The LFP, the SPWR, delta and spindle times and the tetrode of each cell are not in the data set. All files

Environment: Python 3.13, 38 packages pinned in requirements.lock.txt; CI runs each notebook from a new environment built from it.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • SWS time per rest epoch is the summed length of the data set's SWS intervals; the paper's own SWS detection, from the LFP, cannot be repeated.sessions-and-cells
  • Sessions are ordered by the month and day in their ids, with rat 20's December sessions put before its January to March ones; the data set gives no year.rules-learned
  • The learning point is computed over all trials of the session, as the definition says, even where the rule changed within the session.learning-sessions
  • All 86 sessions are searched, not only those passing the inclusion rule; the table shows which of the listed sessions are included.learning-sessions
  • The criterion takes the session selection from the matching reading of sessions-and-cells; neither matches, so the paper's stated inclusion rule, reading (b), is used.fig1b-signal-components
  • The awake epoch is the data set's single awake interval, binned whole in 100 ms bins.fig1b-signal-components
  • The outcome follows the criterion, which tests post against pre; two of the three group sizes listed as expected values fall outside tolerance, because the data set holds fewer sessions that pass the inclusion rule.fig2b-post-greater-than-pre
  • The data set does not give the bounds of the rest epochs. Assumed, as the claim's criterion directs, from the data set's epoch times, pre from the start of the recording to the start of the awake interval and post from its end to the end of the position record, or to the end of the last post SWS interval if that is later; this makes pre 23 to 58 min and post 22 to 59 min in the included sessions, where the Methods give 20 to 30 min. A 30 min variant is shown.supp5-sws-only
  • Non-SWS bins are z-transformed with the whole rest epoch.supp5-sws-only
  • The ANOVA is a repeated-measures ANOVA with the signal component as subject, since the four means of a component are not independent; the legend names only an ANOVA. The ordinary two-way ANOVA is shown.supp5-sws-only
  • SWS episodes are the data set's SWS intervals; their merging rule (gaps under 1 min) is the paper's, applied before deposit.supp6-pre-post-sws-alike
  • The bootstrap p-value uses a normal approximation from the bootstrap standard error, since 2000 resamples cannot resolve p below 5e-4; no resample gave a slope difference of zero or less.fig2b-pre-weaker
  • Cross-correlations are computed within each SWS interval and pooled, so that no pair of bins spans a gap between intervals; lags of -1 to 1 s (a standard choice).supp10-no-coactivation
  • The mean over shuffles is computed in closed form, as the mean over all permutations of the cells within each bin, instead of 1000 random shuffles per bin; on one session it agrees with 1000 shuffles (-0.0082 against -0.0086).supp11-shuffle-control
  • Assumed that "the pre average is lower" holds only if pre is lower both by the rise above baseline in the window where post is significant (-0.5 to -0.3 s) and by the largest value of the whole average, or if the per-component difference is significant. Pre is lower in the window (0.063 against 0.107) but its largest value is higher (0.145 against 0.132), and the per-component difference is not significant (p = 0.15), so this part is not established. Comparing by the window alone would make the claim reproduced. Both comparisons are shown.fig6b-down-up
  • Event-triggered averages use R over all bins of the rest epoch with Q z-transformed over the whole epoch, since the -4 to 4 s windows reach outside SWS; transitions are taken inside SWS.fig6b-down-up
  • The learning sessions and trials are those listed in the data set's description, since the paper's definition does not recover them (learning-sessions).supp12-behavior-unchanged
  • Speed is shown in ten bins of the fraction of the trial's path length, as the data set holds no maze geometry for the paper's linearization; it is reported, and does not decide the outcome.supp12-behavior-unchanged
