Summary: Most findings on lateral septum (LS) high-frequency oscillations held up on the lab's deposited detections: they are shorter and slower than CA1 ripples, occur mostly in NREM sleep and follow CA1 ripples with the reported lag. LS neurons lock to the oscillation trough, but fewer than reported (60.6% against 92.5%), and ripple features predict LS firing above chance, not at the paper's rates. Confinement to LS and stronger ripple-triggered LS firing in NREM sleep did not reproduce; the first rests on site labels that alternate along the septal shank. Travelling waves, depth within LS and optogenetic induction could not be tested, as electrode positions, depth estimates and the optogenetic rats are not in the dataset.
