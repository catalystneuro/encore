Mannion et al. 2015 · PeerJ

Sensitivity to the visual field origin of natural image patches in human low-level visual cortex

Damien John Mannion

Outcomes of 9 claims

Counted separately: 7 claims that also use data from outside the dataset.

  • ⊘7 blocked

None of the paper's main findings could be tested in this data. These are the interaction between where a natural image patch is shown and where it was taken from, the larger lower-field responses to patches from below fixation, and the links between patch statistics such as mean luminance and the field preference. All rest on responses in V1, V2 and V3, and the dataset holds the raw functional runs without the anatomy or area definitions. The image statistics also need the monitor's colour calibration, which is not public. Of the minor claims, equal task performance across conditions held up, while the residual and radial-bias checks could not be decided.

    Results and Discussion, paragraph 6

    The test needs no responses, only the 120 stimulus screenshots, which are in the data set, and the DKL transform, which needs the CIE coordinates of the monitor's primaries (Methods, Image patch analysis). These are given neither in the paper, nor in its code (a local PsychoPy monitor profile and the unlinked package monitors.conversions), nor in the data set, so the screenshots cannot be put in DKL space as the paper did. 118 of the 120 patches have R, G and B not all equal in more than half their pixels inside the mask, so the chromatic channels depend on the calibration. The claims file lists fig5bc-spatial-frequency under depends_on for the filter method only; that claim is blocked for the V1 to V3 responses, which this test does not use, and the filter method itself was run here. As a variant on which no outcome rests, the test was run as imstats.py runs it under two assumed calibrations (sRGB and Adobe RGB primaries) at 78.7 pixels per degree, and under sRGB at 78.2. None of the 12 paired t-tests has p < 0.05 under any of the three (smallest p 0.063, 0.065 and 0.064, each the luminance channel for patches from below on the right, t(29) = -1.93 to -1.92), and none of the 4 luminance tests does; the mean 45 minus 135 degree difference is at most 7% of the mean output. The patch geometry was checked on the screenshots.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Published code disagreesThe Figure S2 legend describes task performance for target onsets near each condition, but task.py skips targets and button presses at or before 32 s, bins the others in absolute run time and assigns each target to the event starting within 2 s after the bin time plus 32 s (lines 53, 99 and 141 to 145, printed in the notebook), so the condition labels come from events about 32 s after the target. It also puts every press and target after 300 s into one last bin. With the assignment by absolute time the conclusion of no difference is unchanged.

Reproduction Visual field origin of natural image patches in human V1 to V3 (Mannion 2015)

Tests the findings of Mannion (2015) on CRCNS vim-3, which holds the raw functional runs, event sequences and central-task records of the seven participants and the stimulus screenshots. The V1, V2 and V3 definitions, cortical surfaces and anatomical images are not in the data set, so the response claims are blocked; the task performance claim is tested, the data check fits the paper's GLM in the volume to check timing, run order and retinotopic layout, and the radial-bias test is run on the stimulus screenshots under assumed monitor calibrations, for information.
