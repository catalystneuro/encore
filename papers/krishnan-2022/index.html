Krishnan et al. 2022 · Nature Communications

Reward expectation extinction restructures and degrades CA1 spatial maps through loss of a dopaminergic reward proximity signal

doi:10.1038/s41467-022-34465-5 · paper key krishnan-2022

Datasets

Here is the paragraph to save in that file: The decoder findings held in this data: a position decoder trained on rewarded laps failed once licking stopped after reward removal, and its error rose along the whole track. The place-field findings held only in part: reliability fell without reward, but field counts, widths, out-of-field firing and field turnover did not match, probably because the paper does not describe how it computed dF/F and fields from raw fluorescence. The dopamine inhibition results held in direction but not in the saline control. The dopamine axon imaging and time controls are missing from the dataset, so those findings could not be tested. Figures 2 and 3 rest on only 2 mice, so the place-field results are weak evidence either way.

  • ✓Reproduced Mice ran on average 34 laps in R, 28 in UR and 27 in RR (mean +/- 95% CI 34 +/- 2, 28 +/- 4 and 27 +/- 3), and 31 +/- 5 laps in the novel rewarded environment.

  • ✓Reproduced Averaged over 12 mice, lap by lap decoder R2 in UR falls as an S-shaped curve around the lap where pre-licking reaches zero; a reverse Boltzmann sigmoid fits the mean R2 with r = 0.94, and its point of maximum change is at lap 10.

  • ✓Reproduced The drop in decoder R2 from RE high to RE low holds whether the lick stop is the first no-lick lap or after 2 or 4 consecutive no-lick laps, or when laps are grouped by presence of licks; with 6 consecutive no-lick laps the result differs.
