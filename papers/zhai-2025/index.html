State-dependent modulation of spiny projection neurons controls levodopa-induced dyskinesia in a mouse model of Parkinson’s disease

Shenyu Zhai, Qiaoling Cui, David L. Wokosin, Linqing Sun, Tatiana Tkatch, Jill R. Crittenden, Ann M. Graybiel, Dalton James Surmeier

Outcomes of 40 claims

  • ✓33 reproduced
  • ⊘7 blocked

Summary: The paper's main findings on striatal neurons held up in this data: on-state dSPNs were more excitable and iSPNs less, at the soma and in dendrites, with M1 receptors and CDGI behind the iSPN dendritic change. Elevated off-state acetylcholine release, the cortical synaptic changes and reduced dyskinesia after iSPN M1 receptor deletion also reproduced. The SCH 23390 reversal in dSPN dendrites held only with one incompletely deposited cell left out (P = 0.027, 0.066 with it). Spine density could not be tested, because the counts used manual steps the paper does not describe. Dyskinesia in CDGI knockouts could not be tested, as the deposited scores differ from the paper's.
