Neural population dynamics during reaching

Summary: In the dataset's two array sessions, the paper's central finding of rotational population dynamics during reaching holds up: the neural state rotates the same way for nearly all conditions, and a rotational dynamics matrix fits almost as well as an unconstrained one. The rotation plane captures less variance than the paper's 28% average, perhaps because the dataset holds unsorted threshold crossings, not sorted units. Simulated kinematic populations fit poorly, as claimed, but their angle distributions match the paper only in part, under simulation details the paper does not give. The leech, walking, single-electrode, muscle and speed-task findings could not be tested, as those data are not in the dataset. Supplementary shuffle and per-array controls held only in part.

    In the first jPCA plane the neural state rotates in the same direction for all conditions of the J-array and N-array datasets, including reaches in opposite directions.

    Under the main reading 103 of 108 J-array conditions (95.4%) and 107 of 108 N-array conditions (99.1%) rotate…

    Results, Quasi-rhythmic responses during reaching, paragraph 4; Figure 3e and 3ffig3ef-same-direction
    From the paper

    Second, the neural state rotates in the same direction across conditions (different traces) ... Yet for each dataset the neural state rotates in the same direction across conditions

    Result

    Under the main reading 103 of 108 J-array conditions (95.4%) and 107 of 108 N-array conditions (99.1%) rotate counter-clockwise from -50 to +150 ms; the median net rotation is 97 and 113 degrees. Under the four readings the shares are 94.4 to 96.3% (J-array) and 99.1 to 100% (N-array), and 96.3% and 99.1% with 24 ms smoothing. After shuffles 2 and 3 the median share stays at 93 to 98%, and after shuffle 1 it is 58 to 61% (see suppfig3-shuffle-controls). The units are unsorted threshold crossings (192 per session).

    Deviations from the paper’s method
    • Units are the dandiset's 192 unsorted threshold crossings per session, where the paper used 146 and 218 sorted isolations, as the claims file says.
    • The paper's custom smoothing step that discards features not shared across conditions is not applied; the Methods say it had essentially no effect on the population analyses (Methods, "Computing average firing rate as a function of time").
    • Rates are aligned on movement onset only; the paper also aligned on target onset and the go cue, for presentation.
    • The trial table has two movement-onset columns, offline_move_onset_time ("calculated offline using a robust algorithm") and move_begins_time, 25 ms (J-array) and 52 ms (N-array) apart at the median, and the paper does not say which onset it used ("data are precisely aligned on movement onset", Results). The window over which a unit's range is taken for the soft normalisation is not stated either; the codepack takes it over all the times it is given. Both details are run under both readings (four in all); here all four give the same outcome.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • Trials flagged discard_trial are also left out, as the column's description says to do; no trial of the two sessions is flagged.array-dataset-composition
  • Units are the dandiset's 192 unsorted threshold crossings per session, where the paper used 146 and 218 sorted isolations, as the claims file says.fig3ef-same-direction
  • The paper's custom smoothing step that discards features not shared across conditions is not applied; the Methods say it had essentially no effect on the population analyses (Methods, "Computing average firing rate as a function of time").fig3ef-same-direction
  • Rates are aligned on movement onset only; the paper also aligned on target onset and the go cue, for presentation.fig3ef-same-direction
  • The trial table has two movement-onset columns, offline_move_onset_time ("calculated offline using a robust algorithm") and move_begins_time, 25 ms (J-array) and 52 ms (N-array) apart at the median, and the paper does not say which onset it used ("data are precisely aligned on movement onset", Results). The window over which a unit's range is taken for the soft normalisation is not stated either; the codepack takes it over all the times it is given. Both details are run under both readings (four in all); here all four give the same outcome.fig3ef-same-direction
  • The heading is undefined while the hand is at rest, at onset and when it stops. The net change of heading is taken between the mean path's direction over 5 to 20% of its length and over 80 to 95% of it, from movement onset to +500 ms (path smoothed with a 10 ms Gaussian). A variant takes the unwrapped heading of the velocity where speed exceeds 10% of its peak. Both are choices made here, and they agree on the outcome.fig3-independent-of-curvature
  • Assumed, as the main reading, that the paper's movement onset is the trial table's offline_move_onset_time, which the file describes as calculated offline with a robust algorithm and at which the hand speed starts to rise (median 43 and 15 mm/s at it, under 6 mm/s 50 ms before). The paper says only that the data were precisely aligned on movement onset. Under move_begins_time, with the hand paths aligned on it too, the outcome would be reproduced. The file describes move_begins_time only as "movement onset time"; at it the median hand speed is already 137 mm/s (J-array) and 160 mm/s (N-array).fig3-independent-of-curvature
  • The shuffle-3 runs are this claim's own, drawn from its seed, with the dividing point of suppfig3-shuffle-controls (median go cue relative to movement onset plus 50 ms, -277 ms in J-array and -268 ms in N-array); each shuffle's R2 is computed in its own jPCA plane.fig3-phase-from-prep-state
  • Assumed the offline_move_onset_time column as the movement onset (see fig3-independent-of-curvature); under move_begins_time the outcome would be reproduced.var-captured-jpc-plane
  • Assumed the offline_move_onset_time column as the movement onset (see fig3-independent-of-curvature).pc23-variance
  • Planes are ordered by the magnitude of their M_skew eigenvalues (Methods, "the plane defined by the eigenvectors associated with the largest two (complex-conjugate) imaginary eigenvalues"), and each is oriented as the first.suppfig4-j-array-planes

  • Shuffle 1 inverts exactly half of the conditions (54 of 108) per unit, drawn at random (Supplementary Figure 2 legend).
