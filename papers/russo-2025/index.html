Thalamic feedback shapes brain responses evoked by cortical stimulation in mice and humans.

Summary: The paper's central finding held in this data: late EEG, LFP and CSD responses to motor cortex stimulation in mice are larger at rest than during running, as are the human responses in the paper's Source Data. During running, thalamic baseline firing roughly doubled as reported, but evoked thalamic firing rose only when trials were pooled across sessions. The thalamic rebound did not lead the cortical one, and relay units showed no split into high- and low-firing groups, so most claims built on that split failed. Optogenetic thalamic inhibition delayed the rebound but failed one of six tests, and the model could not be run within the time limit. The dataset holds 10 of the paper's 26 mouse sessions.
