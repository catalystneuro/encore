Valero et al. 2022 · Science

Probing subthreshold dynamics of hippocampal neurons by pulsed optogenetics

doi:10.1126/science.abm1891 · paper key valero-2022

Datasets

  • DANDI:000568

Only part of the main findings held up in this data. The drop in light-induced spiking during sharp-wave ripples, the larger gain inside place fields and the share of light-responsive cells reproduced, but the theta-trough gain and the place fields unmasked in non-place cells did not. The light-intensity, intracellular, decoding, assembly and replay results could not be tested, because data were missing or the Methods were unavailable. Light pulse times exist for only 2 of 28 sessions, so every light test rests on one mouse, and most pulses are missing from those files.

  • ◐Partially reproduced CA1 pyramidal neurons were recorded and probed in four CaMKIIa-Cre::Ai32 mice, 822 pyramidal neurons in all, 43.3 plus or minus 8.37 per session.
  • ◐Partially reproduced uLEDs were driven with 20 ms pulses at random sites with randomly variable 20 to 40 ms offsets, so that the stimulation of each site recurred at about 0.3 to 0.6 s intervals.
  • ✓Reproduced Of 822 pyramidal neurons, 611 responded to light from the uLEDs.
  • ✓Reproduced Light-induced spike responses of individual pyramidal cells decreased during sharp-wave ripples (n = 485 neurons, P < 1e-55, Wilcoxon paired signed-rank test).
  • ◐Partially reproduced The firing-rate change of pyramidal neurons during SPW-Rs is negatively correlated with their baseline firing rate (rho = -0.19, P < 1e-7).
  • ?Not reproduced During track running, the light-induced rate gain is largest at the trough of the theta cycle (P < 1e-6, Wilcoxon test).
  • ◐Partially reproduced More than half of the pyramidal neurons were place cells, 73% of light-responsive and 71% of non-responsive neurons, with no difference between them (P = 0.90, chi-squared test).
  • ✓Reproduced The light-induced rate increase is higher within than outside the place field (n = 553 place fields, P < 1e-115, Wilcoxon test).
  • ?Not reproduced The in-field gain is positively correlated with the out-of-field firing rate (rho = 0.17, P < 1e-5) and with the home-cage firing rate of the neuron (rho = 0.25, P < 1e-7).
  • ?Not reproduced Light responses in the home cage before the track were stronger in place cells than in non-place cells (P < 1e-4, Kruskal-Wallis test).
  • ?Not reproduced Optogenetic depolarization revealed place fields in the majority of non-place cells, 289 of 417 (69.3%).
  • ◐Partially reproduced The spatial location of light-induced spikes matches that of spikes without stimulation, both for place fields of place cells (P < 1e-40) and for unmasked place fields of non-place cells, whose sparse control spikes form ghost fields (P < 1e-10).
  • ?Not reproduced Place fields during light stimulation were more stable between the first and second halves of the session than during control epochs, for both true place fields and unmasked place fields (P < 1e-9 and P < 1e-19, two-way ANOVA).
  • ◐Partially reproduced Spatial information of control and light-stimulated epochs is correlated, and the light-boosted increase is stronger for unmasked place fields of non-place cells than for place cells (P < 1e-15, Kruskal-Wallis test).
  • ✓Reproduced Cofiring of place cell pairs during SPW-Rs in the home cage is positively correlated with their spatial overlap on the track (P < 1e-27), while pairs of a place cell and a non-place cell show no such relationship (P = 0.11).
  • ?Not reproduced With light-induced spikes, the relationship between SPW-R cofiring and spatial overlap appears for pairs that include non-place cells (P < 1e-5, Spearman correlation).
