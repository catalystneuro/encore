Summary: The paper's links between striatal dopamine and syllable use held up in this data, but its central optogenetic learning result did not. Dopamine differed across syllables, syllables with more dopamine were used more, and larger transients predicted more use over the following minutes. Pairing a syllable with optogenetic dopamine release did not raise its use significantly over controls when the baseline was the day before stimulation; a later check with the Monday baseline of the paper's code gave the paper's result. The effects of stimulation on transition entropy and later predictability did not hold either. The food-reward and calibration sessions are missing from the dataset, and the decoding and reinforcement-learning models were not run for time.
