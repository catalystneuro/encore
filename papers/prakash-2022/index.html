Selective control of synaptically-connected circuit elements by all-optical synapses

Mansi Prakash, Jeremy W. Murphy, Robyn St. Laurent, Nina G. Friedman, Emmanuel L. Crespo, Andreas Björefeldt, Akash Pal, Yuvraj Bhagat, Julie A. Kauer, Nathan C. Shaner, Diane Lipscombe, Christopher I. Moore, Ute Hochgeschwender

Summary: The paper's culture findings held on its published per-electrode counts, as the dandiset holds only the mouse work: bioluminescence from CTZ activated or silenced opsin-expressing neurons across synapses, and cutting the connections or botulinum toxin stopped it. In mice, CTZ raised bioluminescence and gamma power rose more with the opsin than without, but gamma events were far fewer than reported (about 30 against 214 per contact), likely through detection steps the Methods leave open. The larger multi-unit rise in opsin mice did not reproduce, and under the described rule nearly every contact in both groups exceeded baseline. Whether gamma rose in superficial layers could not be tested, as the files do not say which probe end is superficial.
