Kauvar et al. 2025 · Science

Conserved brain-wide emergence of emotional response from sensory experience in humans and mice

doi:10.1126/science.adt3971 · paper key kauvar-2025

Datasets

  • DANDI:001326
  • DANDI:001347

DANDI:001326: Most main findings held: ketamine spared fast activity but removed the persistent, accumulating brain-wide response, and it shortened regional decay times while rise times stayed unchanged. The twelve response clusters did not reproduce (11 found, 6 of 12 sizes in range), nor did the stronger ketamine effect on slow clusters, and the clustering shifted with the random seed and BLAS thread count. Partly held: the late emotion-like drop and weaker coupling under ketamine, and the eye-closure reduction, which depends on the code's normalization. Human iEEG persistence, timescale and phase locking were not tested for lack of time. Participant reports, dissociation scores and licking data are absent from the archive.

DANDI:001347: Most of the paper's main findings held up in this data. Ketamine cut late but not early eye closure in humans and mice, lowered persistent activity along the coding dimension in humans and mice, and shortened the human intrinsic timescale. The brain-wide spectral factors and the network and region changes also reproduced. Human beta phase locking fell under ketamine, but by more than the paper reports. In mice, the late drop along the emotion-like dimension failed with the Methods window, though the code's different window gives it, and the claimed lack of change in fast activity failed. Verbal reports and licking could not be tested because the dataset lacks them.
