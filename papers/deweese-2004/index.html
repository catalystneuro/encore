Deweese et al. 2004 · Journal of Neurophysiology

Shared and Private Variability in the Auditory Cortex

doi:10.1152/jn.00197.2004 · paper key deweese-2004

Datasets

  • CRCNS:ac-2

Most of the paper's main findings held in this data: the split of variability into a shared part and a small private part (best private estimate near 1.5 mV), the total variability across neurons, the drop after LFP-based culling, and the negative link between spontaneous activity and the evoked response. The random-culling controls did not reproduce (3.82 mV against 4.5), most likely because the paper's handling of its 10-trial rule is unclear. PSP-based culling alone and the largest LFP-based reduction held only in part. The miniature EPSP size could not be pinned down, since the paper gives no detection method. Many outcomes depend on a spike-removal rule the paper does not describe, which the agent assumed.

2026-11-04 · on CRCNS:ac-2

  • ◐Partially reproduced Tones evoke brief, short-latency PSPs in all neurons. In the example neuron the mean responses to the 32 tones peak 27 to 42 ms after onset with heights of 1.2 to 18 mV, and are similar in shape once normalized and aligned.
  • ✓Reproduced For the most reliable neuron, the responses to 20 presentations of a 32-kHz tone cluster tightly about the mean (q = 0.23 mV), and its variability averaged over the 26 tones with a mean PSP above 3 mV is 0.88 mV.
  • ✓Reproduced Across the 33 neurons the tone-averaged variability index ranges over more than an order of magnitude, from below 1 to above 10 mV; about a fifth (7/33) have low variability (1 to 2 mV) and about half (18/33) have high variability (4 to 12 mV).
  • ✓Reproduced The mean total variability index across neurons is 4.6 mV.
  • ✓Reproduced LFP-based culling reduces the mean variability index by 35%, from 4.8 to 3.1 mV, across the 29 neurons with an LFP.
  • ✓Reproduced After LFP-based culling, PSP height still correlates with LFP height across trials for many tones, as in the 60 unculled responses to one tone of the Figure 5 neuron.
  • ◐Partially reproduced LFP-based culling and estimation together reduce the variability index by 40% on average, to 2.9 mV across 29 neurons, reduce it in 26 of 29 neurons, by as much as 71%, and have little effect when total variability is already small.
  • ◐Partially reproduced PSP-based culling alone reduces the mean variability index to 2.2 mV across 33 neurons, and leaves it at most about 3 mV in 32 of 33 neurons.
  • ◐Partially reproduced PSP-based culling reduces the tone-averaged variability for all but one neuron, but increases the variability for at least one tone in half (18/33) of the neurons; in the Figure 4 neuron, it raises q for a 9.2-kHz tone from 0.47 to 0.57 mV.
  • ✓Reproduced PSP-based culling followed by LFP-based culling and estimation reduces variability by as much as 85%, to a best estimate of private variability of 1.6 mV across 28 neurons, comparable and low for all neurons.
  • ?Not reproduced Random culling of as many traces as LFP-based culling removes leaves the variability index unchanged (4.5 against 4.6 mV), while matching the PSP-based or both cullings lowers it (3.67 and 2.98 mV) because tones with fewer than 10 non-aberrant trials drop out; keeping only those tones without culling gives 2.9 mV. PSP-based culling removes 29% of trials and LFP-based culling 38%.
  • ✓Reproduced For tones with mean PSPs of at least 15 mV, total variability is lower (2.9 mV, 19 neurons) than for all tones, but the private variability is the same as for all tones (best estimate 1.5 mV, LFP-based bound 1.9 mV, 16 neurons).
  • ?Not reproduced For tones of at least 15 mV, random culling and keeping only tones that survive both cullings give 3.2 mV, so the private estimate of 1.5 mV is not an undersampling effect.
  • ◐Partially reproduced In coefficient of variation, both the total and the private variability of the large (at least 15 mV) responses are lower than for the full data set (total 0.37 against 0.73; private 0.27 against 0.41, significantly lower), unlike the variability index.
  • ✓Reproduced The LFP recorded with the more variable neurons is not more variable than that recorded with the less variable neurons (CV 0.66 against 0.74, P = 0.5), so differences in total variability are not explained by gross state differences seen in the LFP.
  • ✓Reproduced Total variability does not differ significantly between the three anesthesia protocols (pentobarbital 3.7, pentobarbital with diazepam 6.1, urethane 5.0 mV), and private variability differs slightly but significantly between pentobarbital alone (1.1 mV) and urethane (2.2 mV).
  • ✓Reproduced The size of spontaneous activity before the stimulus is anticorrelated with the evoked PSP, in the LFP (mean correlation -0.14, 29 neurons) and in the whole-cell record (-0.22, 33 neurons), the whole-cell correlation being negative in 30 of 33 neurons.
  • ✓Reproduced For the Figure 5 neuron, the shuffle control attributes 0.02 mV to overfitting of the linear estimator, and the corrected value after both cullings and estimation is 0.57 mV.
  • ◐Partially reproduced The private component of the variability is small, about 1 to 3 mV, and relatively homogeneous across neurons, while the shared component is often much larger and more heterogeneous, ranging from about 0 to 10 mV.
  • ✓Reproduced In 14 of 33 neurons at least one tone elicits responses whose variability index is within a factor of 2 of the 0.38 mV expected from stochastic release alone.
  • ◐Partially reproduced Miniature EPSPs recorded in vivo after TTX have a mean size of 0.39 mV with a CV of 0.36 across 6 neurons, consistent with in vitro estimates.
  • ✓Reproduced Each tone was presented 18 times on average (10 to 63) per neuron; rest potential ranged from -58 to -92 mV (median -70) after junction potential correction, and input resistance from 19 to 141 MOhm (median 64).
  • ✓Reproduced Response variability is not correlated with access resistance (4.2 against 4.9 mV on either side of the 60 MOhm median) and does not vary systematically with recording depth.
