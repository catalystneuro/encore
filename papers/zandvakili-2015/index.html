  • ⊘4 blocked

The paper's central finding, that V1 population coordination is stronger just before spikes of middle-layer V2 neurons, held in this data, though the increase is about twofold against the paper's threefold and some elevation also appears after V2 spikes. Narrow V1-V2 cross-correlation peaks were far more common than the paper reports, which may come from the five deposited sessions or from this attempt's method. In these pairs V2 spikes lag V1 spikes by less than the paper's delay, a measure limited by the files' 1 ms spike times. The lack of coordination before spikes outside the middle layers could not be tested, as the data hold no recording depths, and the network simulations were not attempted.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. Which test gave the p-values for the median CCG peak offsets against zero (p < 0.001 for V1-V2; p = 0.45 and 0.42 for V1-V1 and V2-V2)? On this data set the Wilcoxon signed-rank test finds the V1-V1 offsets different from zero (p = 0.016, mean 0.10 ms) and the sign test does not (p = 0.076); the attempt assumed the signed-rank test.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. How were the bootstrap p-values comparing divergence in spike and control epochs computed, with the same resampled neurons for both (paired) or from the two bootstrap distributions taken as independent? The paired reading finds after-spike epochs and epochs far from the spike above control at many offsets (Figure 7D) where the independent one finds them at the 80 ms window (and 40 ms in one reading). Comparing two independent control sets on this data set, the paired reading runs somewhat above its nominal rate (13 of 150 epochs at p < 0.05, about 7.5 expected; none at the Bonferroni threshold) and the independent one is conservative (0 of 150). The outcomes here are the same under both.
  • Methods not availableQuestion for the authors. The CCG jitter window used for the significance of narrow peaks is given only for Figure 4C (10 ms); was it the same for all pairs and sites? With 25 ms, 22 of 31 sites and 185 pairs pass on this data set, against 16 and 171 with 10 ms.

Reproduction V1 coordination before V2 spiking in anesthetized macaques (Zandvakili and Kohn 2015)

Tests the findings of Zandvakili and Kohn (2015) on CRCNS data set v1v2-1, which holds the spike times of V1 Utah array units and V2 tetrode units in 5 sessions from 3 anesthetized macaques viewing drifting gratings, with receptive field fits per channel. It computes the jitter-corrected V1-V2, V1-V1 and V2-V2 cross-correlograms, finds the V2 sites with a narrow V1-V2 peak, and measures the Jensen-Shannon divergence of V1 population states around V2 spikes against control epochs and shuffled data, under every reading of the open method details. The V2 depths, unit SNR and the pixel-to-degree scale that the layer, single-unit and receptive field analyses need are not in the data set. All files

Environment: Python 3.13, 27 packages pinned with their hashes in requirements.lock.txt; CI runs each notebook from a new environment built from it.

Method differences

How the tests differed from the paper’s method or data, and the claims each applies to.

  • A site is a V2 tetrode in one session; the files hold no depth, so "all laminar locations" is tested as sites with and without a narrow peak, as the criterion says.fig4c-broad-ccg-peaks
  • Spike times are in 1 ms bins in the files; the paper used 0.1 ms.fig4c-broad-ccg-peaks
  • CCGs at 1 ms resolution (the files' resolution) against the paper's 0.1 ms. Equation 1 is combined over the eight stimuli by summing coincidences and the normaliser, which weights each stimulus by its V1 spike count (a standard choice).fig4c-narrow-peak-sites
  • The jitter correction subtracts the expected CCG under the jitter the claims file describes for the divergence analysis (each spike redrawn from the neuron's spikes in the same window on trials of the same stimulus), computed analytically for both neurons, with windows tiling the trial from 0 ms (Pairwise V1-V2 Analysis cites Smith and Kohn 2008 for the jitter). The 25 ms window is this attempt's choice for reading j-b, which the claims file leaves unnamed.fig4c-narrow-peak-sites
  • The SNR >= 2 unit criterion cannot be applied (no waveforms); units below 0.5 sp/s are removed.fig4c-narrow-peak-sites
  • 1 ms bins against the paper's 0.1 ms; jitter as in fig4c-narrow-peak-sites.fig5-narrow-pairs-rare
  • The files hold spike times in 1 ms bins, so offsets are whole milliseconds where the paper's were at 0.1 ms resolution; this limits both the median and the width. Smoothing a single 1 ms bin with the Gaussian of SD 1.5 ms alone gives a width at half height of 3.54 ms, so reading (a) has little room below that, and the unsmoothed width of reading (b) is limited by the bin.fig5b-v1v2-latency
  • Assumed that the paper's test of a median offset against zero is the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (MATLAB signrank, documented as a test of zero median, the routine choice in a MATLAB-based analysis such as the data set's own scripts). The paper names no test; the sign test gives the opposite outcome for V1-V1 pairs.fig5b-intra-areal-zero-lag
  • The lag sign within an area follows the order of the unit lists, as the criterion says; 1 ms bins.fig5b-intra-areal-zero-lag
  • The integral within 2.5 ms of the peak is the sum of the five 1 ms bins centred on the peak lag (-2.5 to +2.5 ms at the files' resolution).figs3-efficacy
  • Reading e-b, one 1 ms bin centred 2.5 ms before the spike, cannot be formed from 1 ms data; it is run as the bins centred 2 ms and 3 ms before, which bracket it.fig6a-coordination-before-v2
  • The V2 spikes and the V1 states are both in 1 ms bins, so the epoch of the bins 2 and 3 ms before the spike covers V1 activity from about 1 to 4 ms before it, wider than the paper's 1.5 to 3.5 ms; this may dilute the spike-epoch divergence and lower the spike to control ratio (fig7a-threefold). Forced by the data.fig6a-coordination-before-v2
  • The jitter pool includes the spike's own trial (the claims file says other trials), a standard choice; one trial in 400 changes the pool little. Epochs falling before 50 ms are dropped.fig6a-coordination-before-v2
  • Middle-layer sites are those of fig4c-narrow-peak-sites, 16 of 31, a larger share than the paper's.fig6a-coordination-before-v2
  • Reading e-b approximated by single bins 2 and 3 ms before the spike, as in fig6a-coordination-before-v2.fig7a-threefold
  • The 1 ms binning widens the spike epoch to about 1 to 4 ms before the spike (see fig6a-coordination-before-v2), which may lower the ratio.fig7a-threefold
  • The shuffled value is one realization per reading, compared as a point value with the bootstrap bounds, as the criterion says; four more realizations show its spread. The shuffled data are jittered with the same rule as the data.fig7a-above-shuffle
  • 2000 bootstrap draws per offset, a standard choice: the smallest p they give, 1/2000, is below the Bonferroni threshold 0.05/31, and 5000 draws for the 1240 comparisons of each reading would not fit the notebook's time. Offsets beyond 15 ms are not run; epochs falling before 50 ms are dropped.fig7d-time-course
  • An epoch counts as after the spike when it lies wholly after it (centre -1 ms or less); the epochs that hold the bin simultaneous with the spike (centre -0.5 ms for 2 ms epochs, 0 for 1 ms bins) are counted apart. Before the first verification round the 2 ms epoch at -0.5 ms counted as after; the outcome does not change.fig7d-time-course
