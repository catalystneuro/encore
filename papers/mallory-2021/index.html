Mouse entorhinal cortex encodes a diverse repertoire of self-motion signals

Summary: In head-fixed mice, the finding that a quarter of entorhinal cells encode eye position or velocity held (718 of 2861 against 711), as did conjunctive coding and the stronger influence of body position and speed, though the counts depend on a fold assignment the paper does not describe. Two eye findings did not hold: vertical eye velocity tuning was not less stable than speed tuning, and some eye-tuned cells clustered by their other variables. The test without visual landmarks could not be run, as no file marks those sessions. Head pitch and roll are not in the dandiset; on the paper's figshare deposit fewer pitch and roll cells were found, and head velocity models were too slow to run.
