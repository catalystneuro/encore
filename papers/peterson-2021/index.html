Summary: The paper's main neural findings held up in this data: at movement onset low-frequency power fell and high-frequency power rose, most in sensorimotor regions, power differed across subjects, and reach angle and recording day were the most informative features of single-event power. Day-to-day changes in power matched only in part, likely because this attempt read the neural data of three recordings about 8 h late. Per-subject event counts did not reproduce, as the files hold more events than the paper analysed and mark none as removed. Whether low-frequency decreases are more widespread could not be tested, as the paper gives no measure. Several minor details, such as per-subject band power, matched only in part.

    Result

    Deviations from the paper’s method
    • Spectral power is computed from the dataset's continuous ECoG, not from the paper's curated Figshare epochs, with the Methods' and the code's parameters (helper docstring).
    • Assumed, as the Methods do not say how power is pooled for the segment rejection; Welch power of the 10 s segment averaged over the subject's good electrodes, 10*log10 of its mean over 1-200 Hz (below 0 dB drops) and over 115-125 Hz (above the subject's mean + 3 SD drops). The weights are renormalised to sum 1 per region (code). Three variants are shown for every region claim, on the same events; the flags of each event's own file (the assumption of round 1), the electrodes good in at least one file (only those bad in every file removed), and the main set with the electrodes holding a 50 ms run of zeros left out of each event.     • An electrode whose wavelet power is not finite in an event gets weight 0 in that event and the weights are renormalised (standard choice); no kept event needed it.
    • The bootstrap mask's Benjamini-Hochberg correction runs over the bins computed here (-1.5 to +1.0 s in 0.1 s steps, 25 frequencies); the paper's time range is not stated.

    The criterion gives the code's rule for matching the rows of the projection matrices to electrodes as "drop all-zero rows". The code also drops the first of the rows that are not all zero. Should the criterion give the code's full rule?

    No reader has answered it yet.
  • openasked about fig2-reach-shape

    The criterion evaluates a Gaussian kernel density with Scott's bandwidth on the folded angle and asks for its two highest maxima within 15 degrees of -90 and +90. The folded angle ends at +-90, and a kernel density without boundary correction moves a maximum that sits at the boundary inward by about one bandwidth. Should the window allow for this, or should the criterion use a boundary-corrected density or a histogram?

    Passage. Results, Behavior during naturalistic movements, paragraph 3, "For reach angle, the distributions tend to be bimodal, with peaks at +-90"; Figure 2 (seaborn kdeplot).

    Why it matters. The angle part decides the outcome (partially reproduced). In every subject the density at +75 degrees is above that at +90, and the pooled maxima are at -71.5 and +75 degrees, just outside the window. A boundary-corrected reading could pass the +90 side. The -90 side may still fail, as only 6.4% of the pooled events lie within 15 degrees of -90 and the density at -75 degrees is below that at 0. The verifier of round 1 reported this.
