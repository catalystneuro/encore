Brainstem networks construct threat probability and prediction error from neuronal building blocks

Jasmin A. Strickland, Michael A. McDannald

Most of the paper's main findings held up in this data, using the paper's own cluster membership from its Source Data. Cue clusters ordered by threat probability, the cue subnetwork carrying threat probability and behaviour signals, positive prediction error coding, and the shock and prediction error signals of the outcome networks reproduced. The link between faster danger responses and larger contribution to the threat signal did not (R2 = 0.067), as the paper's latencies do not follow its stated method and no script computes them. The cue hubs, the subnetwork lesion and the separation of the outcome networks held only in part; the networks separate with squared correlations but not with Pearson r. Every claim could be tested.

Notes on the paper

What the attempts found about the paper itself: a method or rule it does not give, a place where it disagrees with itself, published code that does something else. What they found about the data is on each dataset’s page.

  • Paper inconsistentSupplementary Table 1 lists 75 recording sessions (8 for subject 5 and 10 for subject 10); the Results and the data set have 73 (7 and 9).
  • Methods not availableThe Source Data danger latencies (Fig. 2d) do not follow the Supplementary Methods' search within the 10-s cue (k1 mean 182 ms there, 4046 ms with the stated method), and 1498 of 1812 units have one, by a rule not stated. A search from 2 s before cue onset does not reproduce them either, and none of the scripts in the data set computes the latency.   • Methods not availableThe Methods call the cluster correlations "Pearson's correlation coefficient (R2)" and the scripts plot r squared.
