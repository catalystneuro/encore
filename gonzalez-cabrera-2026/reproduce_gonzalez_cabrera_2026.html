Reproducing Gonzalez-Cabrera et al. (2026) from dandiset 001955¶

Paper. Gonzalez-Cabrera C. et al. (2026). A VTA-pontine GABA pathway biases locomotor direction via local and distal inhibition. bioRxiv preprint, doi:10.64898/2026.02.17.706301. The claims file (claims/gonzalez-cabrera-2026.yaml) was written from the preprint's version 1 full text (main text, Figure 1 to 4 legends, Methods, Supplementary Figure 1 to 4 legends), without the figure panels, and the paper's code at github.com/CGonzC/VTAPnO_Locomotion_Analysis, tag v1.0_manuscript_submission (commit 1ff5196).

Dandiset. DANDI:001955, version 0.261103.0758. The paper, a preprint of February 2026, cites no dandiset version (the dandiset was created in August 2026). There are two published versions, 0.260828.0749 and 0.261103.0758. They list the same 66 files, with the same asset identifiers, paths and sizes; the later one differs only in metadata (a longer description that names the paper and the three experiment groups, the contact's affiliation and ORCID, the funding statement). The most recent published version is pinned, as the protocol says when the paper cites none; no file content differs.

What the dandiset holds. Raw broadband silicon-probe recordings of the acute (7 mice) and chronic (4 subjects) experiments, with the Open Ephys ADC channels, and fiber photometry of VTA dopamine neurons in 5 opsin and 3 control mice at 5, 10 and 20 Hz and continuous stimulation. The ecephys files hold no spike-sorted units and no table of events; the juxtacellular, slice, tracing and open-field experiments are not in the dandiset.

In each of these recordings one ADC channel carries 210 pulses of about 1 ms at 0.2-s intervals, which matches the tagging protocol (Methods, Acute silicon-probe recordings: "1 ms light pulses at 5Hz ... 210 pulses in total"); it is ADC1 in some recordings and ADC2 in others. Other channels carry analog signals or irregular crossings whose meaning the files do not give.

Every ADC channel of every recording¶

Every ADC channel of every recording in the 34 ecephys files is read whole and searched for TTL-like pulses (helper vtapno.adc_scan): 210-pulse tagging trains, trains of 1-s pulses (the 1-s light protocol of the acute experiment) and pulses of 1.5 to 2.5 s, the 2-s rotarod bouts of the chronic experiment (Methods, Chronic silicon-probe recordings: "driven in 2-s bouts ... inter-bout intervals were pseudo-random 4-35 s").

Tagging trains of 210 pulses of 1 ms are found in 33 of the 34 files (none in the chronic file of 2024-04-09), 133 in all: 69 on ADC1, 45 on ADC2 and 19 on ADC3, all with the same 4.59-V amplitude. Most files have trains on two channels, in a fixed order within a file (in the acute-VI and acute-VII files two trains on ADC1, then two on ADC2), which fits one channel per laser, but the pairing differs between subjects (ADC3 and ADC2 in the acute-IV files, ADC1 and ADC2 elsewhere) and nothing in the files says which channel or which train is the 470-nm and which the 635-nm light. The light colour of each train, which separates DA from VTA-PnO units, is therefore not recoverable from the files.

Pulses of 1.5 to 2.5 s occur on ADC1 of the chronic files, in trains of 7 to 22 pulses with median intervals of 5.3 to 14.5 s, in recordings after the tagging trains: these match the 2-s rotarod bouts, so rotation onsets are in the files, though unlabelled. All of them are on the same channel with the same width, and nothing distinguishes a reverse block from a forward block (Methods: "Forward sessions (same timing and speed) were run in separate blocks"); the direction that Figure 4d-f compare is not recorded. The acute files have only isolated long pulses among thousands of irregular crossings on ADC2 or ADC3. Some chronic and acute recordings also carry trains of 3-s or 4-s pulses, a protocol the Methods do not describe.

fig3b-photometry-continuous-only¶

In opsin mice, VTA-PnO stimulation raises the DA population calcium signal above its pre-stimulus level with continuous light, but not with 5, 10 or 20 Hz trains.

Criterion (claims file): per mouse and condition, the trial-averaged z-scored trace; Pre = -1.0 to -0.1 s, Stim = 0-3 s; a two-way ANOVA epoch by frequency (repeated measures on both factors, or not); post hoc paired Pre against Stim per frequency, Benjamini-Hochberg over the four. Reproduced when under one reading continuous gives p < 0.0001 with Stim higher and 5, 10 and 20 Hz each give p > 0.05. The readings the criterion names are all run: z baseline (Methods -1.0 to -0.1 s, code -3.0 to -0.1 s), z form (mean/SD, median/MAD), epoch value (mean, area) and ANOVA (repeated, not). Two details the claims file does not fix are also run: the sampling rate after the "downsampled" step of the Methods (100 Hz, a standard choice, or the native 12 kHz, as the code), and whether the two stray intervals of photometry-exp-5 at 5 Hz are used (all intervals, as the code).

The post hoc test is not fixed by the claims file beyond "paired Pre against Stim". The legend says "two-way ANOVA with FDR-corrected post hoc comparisons" and the Methods (Quantification and statistical analysis) name GraphPad Prism v10. GraphPad documents how Prism computes these comparisons ("How Prism 8.0 computes multiple comparisons tests following ANOVA", cdn.graphpad.com/faq/1688/file/MulitpleComparisonAlgorithmsPrism8.pdf): after an ordinary two-way ANOVA every comparison uses the residual mean square of the model; after a repeated-measures ANOVA with sphericity assumed, a comparison within one level of the other factor, with both factors repeated, uses the epoch x frequency x subject mean square; with sphericity not assumed, each comparison uses only its own two groups, which for matched groups "is identical to a paired t test". Five forms are run (helper within_opsin_tests):

rm prism epoch x frequency x subject mean square (Prism, sphericity assumed), df 12

With 32 readings of the data this gives 160 rows. A second table gives, for the main reading, the difference, SE, df and uncorrected p of each form per frequency.

FORMS_3B = [("rm", "prism"), ("rm", "separate"), ("rm", "pooled"), ("plain", "prism"), ("plain", "separate")]

def run_3b(measures):
    out = []
    for key, d in measures.groupby(reading_keys):
        for anova, posthoc in FORMS_3B:
            res = vtapno.within_opsin_tests(d, anova, posthoc)
            row = dict(zip(reading_keys, key), anova=anova, posthoc=posthoc, interaction_p=res["interaction_p"])
            for c in vtapno.FREQUENCIES:
                row[f"diff_{c}"] = res["mean_diff"][c]
                row[f"p_{c}"] = res["p_bh"][c]
                row[f"praw_{c}"] = res["p_raw"][c]
            row["holds"] = bool(row["p_Cont"] < 1e-4 and row["diff_Cont"] > 0
                                and all(row[f"p_{c}"] > 0.05 for c in ("5hz", "10hz", "20hz")))
            row["cont_p05"] = bool(row["p_Cont"] < 0.05 and row["diff_Cont"] > 0)
            row["trains_ns"] = int(sum(row[f"p_{c}"] > 0.05 for c in ("5hz", "10hz", "20hz")))
            out.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

def run_3c(measures):
    out = []
    for key, d in measures.groupby(reading_keys):
        for anova, posthoc in FORMS_3C:
            res = vtapno.opsin_vs_control_tests(d, anova, posthoc)
            row = dict(zip(reading_keys, key), anova=anova, posthoc=posthoc, interaction_p=res["interaction_p"])
            for c in vtapno.FREQUENCIES:
                row[f"diff_{c}"] = res["mean_diff"][c]
                row[f"p_{c}"] = res["p_bh"][c]
            row["holds"] = bool(row["p_Cont"] < 0.05 and row["diff_Cont"] > 0

Blocked claims¶

The other 39 claims are not tested.

  • The acute (9 claims) and chronic (6 claims) silicon-probe claims rest on spike-sorted, curated and opto-tagged units. How the analysis differs from the paper's. The photometry preprocessing is the paper's code; the downsampling rate is a standard choice (100 Hz; native rate as a variant, with the same outcomes). The legends name a two-way ANOVA with FDR-corrected post hoc comparisons, and the Methods name Prism, without saying whether the ANOVA had repeated measures or how the comparisons were made. The fig3b outcome rests on the ordinary two-way ANOVA with Prism's comparisons on its residual mean square, which are not paired as the criterion asks. Of the paired forms, the pooled-difference form also gives reproduced in 20 of 32 readings, the repeated-measures error that Prism uses when sphericity is assumed gives continuous p 2.0e-04 to 5.3e-04, which the criterion counts as partially reproduced, and separate paired t-tests give p 0.072 to 0.11. The fig3c outcome rests on Prism's within-cell error, which is the same under the ordinary and the mixed design. With separate t-tests per frequency neither photometry claim holds, because the continuous effect, positive in all five opsin mice, varies in size from 1.9 to 15.0 z, and with five mice its separate test is weak. Removing the 405-nm correction changes no outcome.

What was found about the dataset:

  • The ecephys files (25 acute, 9 chronic; 161 GB) hold only raw voltage and unnamed ADC channels: no spike-sorted units, no tagging results, no event tables. The tagging trains are on ADC1, ADC2 or ADC3 without a label for the light colour, and the 2-s rotarod bouts are on ADC1 of the chronic files without a label for the direction. None of the paper's 15 silicon-probe findings can be tested from them without redoing the spike sorting and its manual curation.
  • The chronic files are from 4 subjects; the paper has 3 mice.
  • Electrode locations are "unknown" in every ecephys file.
  • In the photometry files, session_start_time is often earlier than the end of the previous file of the same mouse, so it is not the recording start.
  • One photometry file (photometry-exp-5, 5 Hz) has two extra 3.0-s intervals before the regular train; the outcomes are the same with and without them.
  • The isosbestic signal explains little of the GCaMP signal in 12 of 32 files (R² under 0.30, the warning level of the paper's own code); the outcomes do not depend on it.
  • The juxtacellular, tracing, slice and open-field data are not in the dandiset; the Zenodo record it cites holds figure-level tables, Figure 1a images and open-field videos without tracking (listed above).
