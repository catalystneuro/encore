Reproducing Costabile et al. (2023) from dandisets 000235, 000236, 000237 and 000238¶

Paper. Costabile JD, Balakrishnan KA, Schwinn S, Haesemeyer M (2023). Model discovery to link neural activity to behavioral tasks. eLife 12:e83289. doi:10.7554/eLife.83289

Datasets. DANDI:000235 "Thermoregulatory Responses Forebrain" version 0.230316.1600, DANDI:000236 "Midbrain" version 0.230316.2031, DANDI:000237 "Hindbrain" version 0.230316.1655 and DANDI:000238 "Thermoregulatory Responses Reticulospinal system" version 0.230316.1519. The paper cites no version (its data list names the dandisets only). Each dandiset has a single published version, all made on 2023-03-16, and the draft of each has the same asset count and size; the published versions are pinned because they are the only fixed ones and the most recent.

Claims. claims/costabile-2023.yaml, written from the version of record (eLife, 6 June 2023) and checked by a second reader. The paper's code is github.com/haesemeyer/mine_pub at commit 368d756; the tail-processing code it names is bitbucket.org/jcostabile/2p, read here at commit 5ffaa2d.

What the dandisets hold. Per fish, one NWB file with, for each of 30 planes, the raw two-channel movie, CaImAn's segmentation and inferred calcium, the temperature stimulus and the 250 Hz tail tracking. They hold no fitted models, no Taylor metrics, no swim-bout calls, no registration to the Z-Brain atlas, and no annotation of the backfilled reticulospinal neurons.

Scope. Most claims rest on the MINE CNN fit to every one of 432,882 neurons (and again to rotated control data), with Taylor bootstraps on the fit ones. Section 5 fits the paper's linear comparison model to every bright component and times the CNN on this machine; the full set of CNN fits is far beyond the run limit, so those claims are blocked too_large. A second notebook, mine_fits_subsample.ipynb, fits the CNN to a random subsample of 600 components and estimates MINE's side of fig6d-mine-identifies-more-than-lm and figs6-mine-more-at-every-cutoff; the estimates decide no outcome. The simulation claims (Figures 1 to 4) and the mouse claims (Figure 5) use data that are not in these dandisets (data: elsewhere); they were not attempted in this run (rough costs in section 5).

One pass over every plane¶

Each plane is read once and reduced as it loads (mine_data.plane_pass): counts and shapes, the temperature trace on the 5 Hz base, the tail metrics and bouts under each bout reading, the brightness of every component (the mean of the GCaMP movie over imaging time, over the component's pixels), and the lagged correlations of the calcium traces with temperature and with swim starts for the clock check. The movie is read for the 25 whole-brain fish only; the backfill files are summarised without it. Four processes run in parallel.

Checks of the description against the values:

  • 25 whole-brain fish (8 + 9 + 8 files), and 6 backfill fish in 000238, as the paper states. Every whole-brain file has 30 planes, the paper's "30 planes ... in each fish"; one backfill file (RSpinal-Random-Wave-01) has 25 (section 2). A few planes have 1181 frames, not 1182.
  • The imaging rate is 2.384 Hz in every file, the reciprocal of the frame duration of 0.4194304 s in the experiment description. The Methods say 2.5 Hz. The plane length, 1182 frames of 0.419 s (495.8 s), matches the paper's 495 s of stimulus per plane, so 2.384 Hz is the acquisition rate and the paper's 2.5 Hz is rounded (a finding, minor).
  • Pixel size 0.78125 um (the paper's 0.78 um) and the 512 x 512 frame.
  • Temperature is stored in degrees C at 20 Hz and starts at 22 C, the baseline the code adds to the temperature model's output, so it is the converted trace. Its maximum exceeds the paper's 30 C in some planes, and each trace is up to about 11 s longer than the plane's imaging (section 2).
  • The tail is sampled every 4.0 ms (250 Hz). In most planes the tail time runs from a few seconds before the first imaging frame to its end, so tail time 0 is the start of imaging (as the code treats it); the planes that differ are listed in section 2.
  • The cumulative tail angle is exactly the sum of the segment angles, so the segment angles are relative angles between tail segments.
  • The pixel masks' x is the column and y the row: a trace averaged over the mask pixels of the movie follows the CaImAn trace only in that orientation (next cell).

Do the calcium, stimulus and tail data share a clock?¶

For each plane of the 25 whole-brain fish, the components that pass the brightness cut are put on the 5 Hz base, z-scored and smoothed as the code does. Two checks:

  • Temperature, by decoding with the labels shifted in time: a Ridge model of each component on the temperature over the preceding 10 s (as the paper's linear comparison model, temperature only) is fit on the first two-thirds and scored on the last third, with the temperature trace displaced by -30 to +30 s. With a shared clock the held-out correlation peaks at no displacement. The temperature of the next plane of the same fish, with the same statistics, is the mismatch control. Raw lagged correlations are printed too; for a slow stimulus they are high by chance, so they do not separate a response from an offset.
  • Swim starts (bout reading thr5_min10): the lagged correlation of calcium with the bout-start count; motor-related activity should rise in the seconds after a bout and not before.

Each curve is the mean over planes of the mean of the top 5% of components.

With that fixed, the curve is smooth: it is highest for the temperature delayed by 0 to +5 s (0.649 at 0 s, 0.663 at +3 s), falls slowly for advances (0.604 at -5 s, 0.497 at -10 s) and for delays beyond +5 s. The per-plane best displacement has a median of +3.4 s (25th to 75th percentile +1.8 to +4.8 s), as expected when calcium lags a slow stimulus. Because the 10 s history covers lags of several seconds whatever the displacement, this check cannot locate the clocks to better than a few seconds.

All series store a starting time of 0 s (calcium at 2.384 Hz, temperature at 20 Hz), and the tail timestamps start a median 5.4 s before the first frame. The temperature trace runs about 10.5 s longer than imaging. Read as starting with imaging, it predicts the calcium better than read as ending with it (a displacement of -10.4 s), in 98.3% of planes (median 0.656 against 0.493), and only 0.3% of planes have their best displacement within 2 s of -10.4 s. So the extra 10.5 s lies at the end of the trace, as the code assumes; this is shown to within a few seconds, not exactly. The correlation of calcium with swim starts is already above its level at -5 s (0.086) a second before the bout (0.108 at -1 s), rises at the bout (0.144 at 0 s) and peaks 1.8 s after it (0.212 at 2 s). Both checks are consistent with the calcium, stimulus and tail sharing one clock, to within a few seconds.

5. The fits: the linear comparison model on every component, and how long MINE takes here¶

The remaining zebrafish claims need, for every one of the 432,882 bright components, the MINE CNN of the code (get_standard_model: 80 linear filters over 50 timepoints, two 64-unit swish layers, dropout 0.5, L1 1e-3, Adam 1e-3, batch 256, 100 epochs on the first two-thirds of the plane), the same again on rotated control data (figs6-shuffle-enrichment), and Taylor bootstraps on the fit neurons. The linear comparison model is closed-form, so it is fit here to every bright component in the plane pass (mine_data.lm_test_r, following rwave_data_fit.py and create_time_shifted_predictor_matrix; checked against sklearn's Ridge to 1e-12 while preparing). That gives the linear model's side of fig6d-mine-identifies-more-than-lm and figs6-mine-more-at-every-cutoff; the MINE side is estimated from a random subsample in mine_fits_subsample.ipynb. The second cell fits the paper's architecture to 20 bright components of one plane, on one thread, to time it.

How this analysis differs from the paper: brightness is computed on the movie the files hold (whether CaImAn's projection came from a motion-corrected movie is not stated); bouts are re-derived from the tail with thresholds above the tracking noise, since the files hold no bout calls and the paper gives no threshold.

What was found about the dataset: the counts of fish, planes and components match the paper exactly, and the brightness cut gives the paper's count within 0.4%. No plane is deposited twice. The calcium follows its own plane's temperature far better than the next plane's, and the swim starts by about 2 s, which is consistent with shared clocks; the temperature trace's extra 10.5 s lies at its end. In 10 planes the tail timestamps jump once by 128.004 s, so part of the tail recording of each is misplaced by 128 s relative to the imaging; the time ranges show which part, and moving it by the jump gives the range of every other plane. The files hold no swim bouts or behavior predictors, no registration to the Z-Brain atlas, and no annotation of the backfilled neurons, so most of the paper's analyses need these re-derived or taken from elsewhere. The imaging rate is 2.384 Hz, where the Methods say 2.5 Hz. Section 2 lists the rest.
