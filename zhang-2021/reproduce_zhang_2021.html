Reproducing Zhang, Gao, Cukur & Gallant (2021) from CRCNS vim-4¶

Paper: Zhang T, Gao JS, Cukur T, Gallant JL (2021). Voxel-Based State Space Modeling Recovers Task-Related Cognitive States in Naturalistic fMRI Experiments. Frontiers in Neuroscience 14:565976. doi:10.3389/fnins.2020.565976.

Claims: claims/zhang-2021.yaml, written from the version of record (PMC8145286) and its supplement (Data_Sheet_1.DOCX), with the paper's code, gallantlab/state-space at commit 6ffb3c3.

Dataset: CRCNS vim-4, "Whole-brain BOLD activity recorded by fMRI during a visual attention task and a video game task", doi:10.6080/K0668BDF, streamed from the public bucket s3://crcnsarchive/vim-4/live/. Version: unversioned. CRCNS data sets have no versions; the files read are those under vim-4/live/ as of 2026-11-14 (data description version 0.8 of 2026-04-06), which is the only published copy of the data files (vim-4/old/ holds only older versions of the description). The paper cites the data set by its DOI, with no version. The MD5 sums of the files read, from the data set's checksums.md5, are:

Checks of the description against the data, from the outputs above:

  • The deconvolved attention files hold 900 TRs per condition for S1, S2, S3, S5 and S6 (30 min each at a 2 s TR, three 10-min runs as the paper says); S4 is absent, as the description says. The passive deconvolved data are already subsampled to the 900 TRs of the attentive movies. The selection indices cover 1800 s, and applying the code's subsampling to the passive semantic labels reproduces the attentive labels (correlation printed above), which is consistent with the passive data and labels sharing the attentive clock.
  • states.hdf has the same presence labels in all three conditions; the third column codes the condition as -1 (attend humans), 0 (passive) and +1 (attend vehicles). The description does not give this coding. The vehicle column follows the semantic category vehicle.n.01 exactly, and the human column follows person.n.01 closely.
  • The attentive conditions are z-scored per voxel within each condition (voxel means of zero, so the norm of each attentive condition's mean pattern is zero), while the passive TRs, subsampled after z-scoring, are not. With the attention variable coded -1, 0, +1 and an intercept, its OLS weight is the difference of the two attentive means, which is zero: the printed attention weight is at the level of rounding (1e-13), so the third axis of the state space is set by rounding noise. This is analysed in each attention claim below.
  • The PC-space rewrite gives the code's human and vehicle axes exactly (|r| = 1.0000); its third axis differs, being noise in both. 24 PCs explain 68 to 90% of the variance here, against about 70% in the paper. The code's own OLS coefficients in voxel space give the same picture: the norm of the attention row is about 1e-13, against about 1e2 for the human and vehicle rows, so the code's third axis is set by rounding too. The R3 values below can therefore move slightly between runs on different machines (S2 attend-humans 0.740 in the first run, 0.739 in this one); two runs of this revision on the same machine gave identical values.
  • Labels and BOLD share a clock as far as shifted labels show: the attention CSI peaks at lag 0, as deconvolved data should. The game CSI peaks at lag +1 TR in both subjects (S1 0.729 against 0.691 at lag 0; S7 0.566 against 0.550), as a hemodynamic delay in data that are not deconvolved would give. The paper does not say whether the game labels were shifted; lag 0 is used, and the game CSI and classification are also shown at lag +1. This is consistent with, not proof of, aligned labels.
  • Video game: S7 has 1320 TRs (44 min, the paper's 45 min). S1 has 1950 TRs, 65 min, where the paper says one subject played 90 min in six 15-min runs. The correlation of consecutive TRs drops to -0.44 to -0.48 exactly at TRs 325, 650, 975, 1300 and 1625, so S1's data are six blocks of 325 TRs (10.9 min each), as six runs of the 325-volume length the paper gives for its acquisitions would be. Attention task: CSI per subject and condition¶

Shared by fig1-csi-s2, att-csi-group and supp-csi-per-subject. The CSI of the four presence clusters within each condition, in the state space fitted once on the three conditions stacked. Because the third axis is undefined in these data, three readings are computed:

  • R3, "code": the paper's code run as written on the full voxel data (its third axis is whatever rounding gives on this machine);
  • R2, "random third axis": the median over 100 random third axes, orthogonal to the human and vehicle axes in the space of the 24 PCs (the distribution of what an arbitrary third axis gives);
  • R1, "2D": the human-vehicle plane alone.

CSI values are means over 10 Monte Carlo seeds. The null refits the state space with permuted presence labels, 1000 times per reading of the claims file: (a) the (human, vehicle) rows shuffled over all TRs, and (b) circularly shifted by a random lag within each condition. Each null draw uses a random third axis (for R3 and R2) or the plane (for R1) and one Monte Carlo seed.

att-weights-cortex¶

The human-presence dimension of the attention-task state space has its weights in face and body areas (FFA, OFA, EBA), MT+, LO, the frontal operculum and the inferior frontal sulcus; the vehicle-presence dimension in RSC, PPA, OPA, IPS, FEF and around the inferior frontal sulcus.

The paper drew its ROIs per subject from functional localizer and retinotopic mapping data (Methods, Preprocessing and Visualization, paragraph 2), which are not public. The claims file lets the author use an outside source, naming an fsaverage atlas through the data set's mappers, and that is done here (an assumption: group atlas regions in place of each subject's localizer ROIs). The category areas come from visfAtlas (Rosenke et al. 2021), whose authors relate its fusiform face areas (mFus, pFus) to the FFA, its lateral body areas (LOS, ITG, MTG) to the EBA and CoS-places to the PPA; IOG-faces is taken as the OFA and TOS-places (right hemisphere only) as the OPA. RSC, LO, FO, IFS, IPS, FEF and SEF come from HCP-MMP1.0 (Glasser et al. 2016) on fsaverage. Other definitions of the body, face and place areas are shown as variants.

Weights, the readings of the claims file: (a) the OLS weights B, (b) the denoised weights B_L, (c) the state directions Q_S, each oriented so that its own state's TRs have a positive mean position. They are computed exactly from the region-averaged responses and the PCA of all voxels (helper, region_weights). Thresholds: (i) none; (ii) voxels whose prediction by the WordNet FIR model (delays 1 to 4, ridge, 10 contiguous folds per condition, on the non-deconvolved BOLD of the three conditions) correlates with the response at one-sided p < 0.05 uncorrected (t test of r), computed for the voxels the regions reach. Per subject and region, the mean weight over the region's vertices. A subject counts when the human-presence weight is positive and above the vehicle-presence weight in FFA, OFA and EBA, and the reverse in RSC, PPA and OPA; 4 of 5 subjects are needed under a reading.

States with no TRs are dropped (flash in S7; Supplementary Figure 4 shows no flash weights for that subject), so S7's state space has 8 dimensions. game-classification¶

Held-out TRs of the video game task are classified into the nine behavioural states from their position in the learned state space with balanced accuracy 32.8 +/- 3.8% in S1 and 30.4 +/- 4.2% in S7, above the chance of 11%.

Folds and distance as in att-classification. Labels: (a) TRs with exactly one state only; (b) every labelled TR, a prediction correct for each of its states it names, recall per state over the TRs that have it. The state space is fitted on all training TRs with the full state matrix. Classes with no TRs are left out; for the density rule, a class needs more training TRs than dimensions, so classes with fewer than 2 x (dimensions + 1) TRs under the label reading are left out of that rule (listed below). Under labels (a) a class with too few single-state TRs in a training fold is left out of that fold, and the fold's balanced accuracy is taken over the classes it could predict (in the first run such a class was scored with a recall of 0). Null: training state rows permuted, 1000 times. As a variant, the labels moved one TR later (200 permutations).

def game_classification(s, X, names, rng, n_perm):
    gc_rows, gc_null = [], {}
    n, d = X.shape
    nst = X.sum(1)
    for scheme in ["contig", "random"]:
        fs = ss.fold_scores(game[s]["G"], ss.folds(n, scheme, rng))
        for lab in "ab":
            use = (nst == 1) if lab == "a" else (nst >= 1)
            single = np.where(nst == 1, X.argmax(1), -1)
            for rule in ["euclid", "density"]:
                counts = X[use].sum(0)
                minc = 1 if rule == "euclid" else 2 * (d + 1)
                classes = [c for c in range(d) if counts[c] >= minc]
                left = [names[c] for c in range(d) if counts[c] < minc]
                # held-out TRs scored under this label reading, and which of the classes each has
                scored = [(use[te], X[te][use[te]][:, classes] > 0) for tr, te, Ztr, Zte in fs]
                def run(perm_rng=None):
                    accs = []
                    for (tr, te, Ztr, Zte), (m, H) in zip(fs, scored):
                        Xtr = X[tr] if perm_rng is None else X[tr][perm_rng.permutation(len(tr))]
                        ptr, pte = ss.project_fold(Ztr, Zte, Xtr)
                        if lab == "a":
                            ntr = Xtr.sum(1)
                            lab_tr = np.where(ntr == 1, Xtr.argmax(1), -1)
                            cl = [c for c in classes if (lab_tr == c).sum() > (d if rule == "density" else 0)]
                            pred = ss.nearest_cluster(ptr, lab_tr, pte[m], cl, rule)
                            # score only the classes this fold could predict (a class too small to fit
                            # in this fold would otherwise count with a recall of 0)
                            accs.append(ss.balanced_accuracy(H[:, [classes.index(c) for c in cl]], pred, cl))
                            continue
                        else:
                            # one cluster per state from every training TR that has it
                            if rule == "euclid":
                                means = np.array([ptr[Xtr[:, c] > 0].mean(0) for c in classes])
                                pred = np.asarray(classes)[((pte[m][:, None] - means[None]) ** 2).sum(-1).argmin(1)]
                            else:
                                ll = np.column_stack([ss._logpdf(pte[m], *ss._fit_normal(ptr[Xtr[:, c] > 0])) for c in classes])
                                pred = np.asarray(classes)[ll.argmax(1)]
                        accs.append(ss.balanced_accuracy(H, pred, classes))
                    return np.array(accs)
                accs = run()
                null = np.array([run(rng).mean() for _ in range(n_perm)])
                gc_null[s, scheme, lab, rule] = null
                gc_rows.append(dict(subject=s, folds=scheme, labels=lab, rule=rule, n_classes=len(classes), left_out=left,
                                    bal_acc=100 * accs.mean(), fold_sd=100 * accs.std(), chance=100 / len(classes),
                                    null_mean=100 * null.mean(), null_max=100 * null.max(),
                                    p=(1 + (null >= accs.mean()).sum()) / (n_perm + 1)))
    return gc_rows, gc_null

game-weights-two-groups¶

The video game state dimensions fall into two groups, dead, round start, safe explore, unsafe explore and flash in TPJ, precuneus and prefrontal cortex (default-mode areas), and kill, engage close, engage far and run away in motor, premotor and supplementary motor areas, IPS, FEF and SEF; the weights are largely consistent across the two subjects.

Thresholds: (i) none; (ii) voxels whose prediction by the nine-state OLS model (10 contiguous folds) correlates with the response at one-sided p < 0.05 Bonferroni-corrected over all of the subject's voxels.

How the analysis differs from the paper's:

  • The attention claims over subjects use five of the paper's six subjects: S4 is not released.
  • The state space is the paper's code, computed exactly in the space of the 24 PCs; the code itself is run on the full voxels for the reported attention CSIs (R3).
  • The attention variable has no weight in the deposited data, so the third axis of the attention state space is set by rounding; three readings are shown and the outcomes rest on the code's.
  • Permutations: 1000 (20,000 for the game projections), so p-values reach 1e-3 (5e-5), not the paper's 1e-5. How labels are permuted, how folds are formed, the cluster distance, and how multi-state TRs enter are the claims file's readings, each run.
  • S7's game state space has 8 dimensions (no flash TRs).
  • The paper's functional ROIs are not public; the two cortical claims use group-atlas regions on fsaverage (visfAtlas and HCP-MMP1.0) through the data set's mappers, an assumption.
  • The game labels are used at lag 0; the CSI peaks at lag +1, shown as a variant.
  • The video game BOLD is z-scored per voxel over the session, as the paper's Methods say; S7's deposited data are not, and S7 as stored is shown as a variant.

What was found about the data set:

  • The attentive conditions of the deconvolved data are z-scored per voxel within each condition, so the attention variable (coded -1, 0, +1 in states.hdf, a coding the description does not give) carries no mean difference between them.
  • S1's video game data hold 1950 TRs (65 min) in six blocks of 325 TRs, where the paper and the description say one subject played 90 min in six 15-min runs; S7's hold 44 min, as stated.
  • S1's video game data have 73,594 voxels, a voxel set no mapper in the data set matches, current or superseded.
  • No functional ROI, localizer or retinotopy data are deposited.
