Reproducing Natraj et al. (2025) from dandiset 001535¶

Paper. Natraj N, Seko S, Abiri R, Miao R, Yan H, Graham Y, Tu-Chan A, Chang EF, Ganguly K (2025). Sampling representational plasticity of simple imagined movements across days enables long-term neuroprosthetic control. Cell 188, 1208-1225. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2025.02.001. The claims file was written from the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC11932800), with the supplementary legends; it is not the version of record.

Dandiset. DANDI:001535, version 0.260608.0617. The paper cites no version of the dandiset (its data statement points to OSF for code and to the lead contact for data). There are two published versions, 0.250714.1218 and 0.260608.0617. Both list the same single asset (sub-BRAVO1/sub-BRAVO1.nwb, asset fa402651-4564-4d67-bb11-941843453978, the same blob, 4773567412 bytes); they differ only in metadata (a second contributor and the citation), none of which the analysis uses. The most recent published version is pinned, as the protocol asks when the paper cites none.

What the dandiset holds. One NWB file for participant B1: 9737 trials of 256 features at 100 Hz (high-gamma amplitude and low-frequency signals on the 128 channels, the input of the paper's biLSTM decoder), with a target id and two context flags per trial. It holds no data from participant B3, no Figure 1 imagined-movement task, no delta, beta and high-gamma band-power features of the MLP decoders, no session, day, open-loop or closed-loop labels, no online decoder outputs and no robot kinematics or task outcomes.

Every trial has 256 features sampled every 0.01 s, as described (100 Hz), and lasts 1.8 to 11.1 s. The first 128 columns have no negative value in 9239 of the 9737 trials (the smallest value is -0.015) and the last 128 take both signs in every trial, which fits high-gamma amplitude followed by the low-frequency signal; the description names both but not the order, so this order is consistent with the data, not shown. Each block of 128 columns has an L2 norm of 1 over the whole trial, which is what the Methods (Spatiotemporal feature extraction for RNNs) describe for the biLSTM input: "each feature data matrix was transformed to have an L 2 norm of 1 and concatenated together". The values are therefore not in physical units and are scaled per trial; the unit "arbitrary" says so. The duplicate checks above count trials deposited more than once. They find exact copies, and copies whose time-averaged features agree to 7 decimals; a trial deposited twice with different trimming would have a different length and L2 scaling, and these checks would not catch it.

Do the trial labels belong to the trial data?¶

There are no event times inside a trial and no session clock, so the check is that each trial's target id belongs to the series of the same id. Each trial is reduced to its 256 time-averaged features and the target is decoded by cross-validated logistic regression (5 stratified folds, a standard choice). The contrast is the same decoder with the labels shifted by one trial, and with the labels shuffled.

rnn-training-data-amount¶

The biLSTM decoder was trained on about 14 hours and 48 minutes of open-loop and closed-loop data from B1's virtual and real-world hDoF experiments, before augmentation and segmentation.

Expected 888 min, tolerance 88.8 min. The criterion: the total duration of the trials used to train the RNN, before segmentation; reading (a) the whole stored trial, reading (b) the active portion only; shown with and without the real-robot trials when the dataset labels contexts.

A trial's duration is its number of samples divided by 100 Hz. The dandiset's description says its trials are the ones "uploaded for the development and testing of novel BCI decoding architectures" and points to the paper's biLSTM; it does not say that they are exactly the paper's training set. Reading (b) cannot be run, because the file has no task states, but the active portion of a trial is part of the stored trial, so reading (b) is at most reading (a). Below, the durations are broken down by context and target, the order of contexts in trial-id order is shown, and the high-gamma time course at the start and end of each trial is shown by context, to see whether the stored trials hold a pre-cue baseline or are already the active epochs that the OSF code (get_lstm_features.m, task states 2 and 3 plus padding) extracts.

Which robot trials the paper counts is open. The 14 h 48 min paragraph counts data "in the virtual 3D environment and with practice with the Kinova Jaco in the real-world using the MLP"; the same paragraph says "We split the collected data from the virtual environment into 85% training and 15% validation when training the RNN", and the RNN was then fine-tuned on "multiple blocks of open-loop and closed-loop center-out and stop data with the physical robot". If the 1886 robot trials are that fine-tuning data, they are not in the 14 h 48 min.

figs3-spatial-pooling (blocked; a variant under assumed layouts)¶

In B1, 2x2 spatial pooling of the grid makes single-trial high-gamma activity more consistent and improves online decoding in pilot blocks.

The criterion: variance over time and trials of binned (200 ms) high gamma during right thumb trials, per channel, for the 128 raw channels against the 32 channels after a 1 s running average and 2x2 pooling with stride 2; Mann Whitney U; reproduced when p < 0.05 with pooled lower.

The claim is blocked as metadata insufficient: the file has no electrodes table, so the place of each channel on the 8x16 grid, which the 2x2 pooling needs, is not given. Two other differences stand: the stored high gamma is the biLSTM's amplitude at 100 Hz, L2 normalized per trial, not the MLP pipeline's 200 ms band power, and there are no session labels, so all right thumb trials are used where the paper used one session. Target 1 is the right thumb under both the Methods' map (X+) and the OSF code's names (RealRobotBatch/RunTrial.m), as the claims file's header gives them.

The variant below bins the stored high gamma into 200 ms bins, applies a running average over 5 bins within each trial, and pools 2x2 under two assumed layouts (channels in row-major and in column-major order on the 8x16 grid) and under 200 random layouts. Averaging lowers variance whatever the layout, so the random layouts show how much of the result is mechanical. The accuracy part needs online pilot blocks, which the file does not hold.

def mw(a, b):
    """Mann Whitney U of a against b, with the normal-approximation z."""
    u, p = mannwhitneyu(a, b, alternative="two-sided")
    n1, n2 = len(a), len(b)
    return (u - n1 * n2 / 2) / np.sqrt(n1 * n2 * (n1 + n2 + 1) / 12), p

How the analysis differs from the paper's: the one tested claim is a count of stored data, and the dandiset does not say whether its trials are the whole of the paper's training data or how much of each trial was kept.

What was found about the dataset:

  • It holds only participant B1's biLSTM input features (high gamma and low frequency, 100 Hz, L2 normalized per trial), with a target id and a virtual or robot flag per trial. Participant B3, the Figure 1 task, the band-power features of the MLP decoders, online decoder outputs and robot kinematics are not in it.
  • No trial has a day, session, block or open-loop or closed-loop label; trial start and stop times are placeholders.
  • The target ids carry no names, and there are 9 of them where the description says 7 targets.
  • There is no electrodes table, so the channels cannot be placed on the grid.
  • The robot trials (1886, 6.0 to 11.1 s each, all after the virtual trials in id order) are not said to be either the MLP practice the paper counts in its 14 h 48 min or the later fine-tuning of the RNN. Without them the stored total is 540.0 min, outside the claim's tolerance.
  • Nothing says whether a stored trial is the whole trial or only its active portion.
  • No trial is deposited twice as an exact copy (no two trials have identical data or identical time-averaged features); a copy with different trimming would not be found by these checks.
  • The acquisition group holds only the 9737 trial series, one per trial id.
