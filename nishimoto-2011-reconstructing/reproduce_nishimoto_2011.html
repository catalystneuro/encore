Reproducing Nishimoto et al. (2011) from CRCNS vim-2¶

Paper. Nishimoto, Vu, Naselaris, Benjamini, Yu & Gallant (2011), "Reconstructing Visual Experiences from Brain Activity Evoked by Natural Movies", Current Biology 21, 1641-1646, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2011.08.031.

Claims file. claims/nishimoto-2011-reconstructing.yaml, written from the version of record (main text, legends, Experimental Procedures) and its Supplemental Information.

Dataset. CRCNS vim-2 (Gallant Lab Natural Movie 4T fMRI Data), doi:10.6080/K00Z715X, read from the public S3 bucket crcnsarchive under vim-2/. CRCNS data sets are not versioned, so the version is recorded as unversioned (docs/archives.md); there is only one published version to pin, and it is the one the data descriptor (version 0.61, 2015-02-17) describes as holding the data of this paper. The MD5 sums of the files read, from the data set's checksums.md5, are printed in the data check below so that a later change can be detected: Stimuli.tar.gz d06069177dd2ccad303009b7003c67b9, VoxelResponses_subject1.tar.gz 0e389bfa0b2235d1763d8a0d3bebf0af, VoxelResponses_subject2.tar.gz 92f3e621677ac1c8c226c87d77977c88, VoxelResponses_subject3.tar.gz 3ea1084138f70583228ad5649c935921. The raw per-session data (raw_data/) are not used: the paper's analysis starts from the preprocessed responses these files hold.

Each file is a tar.gz holding one MATLAB 7.3 (HDF5) file. It is streamed once from the bucket into memory and opened there (vim2.py); nothing is written to disk.

Main deviation. The paper fits each voxel with L1-regularized least squares by coordinate descent (Model Fitting, refs. 15 and 16). Every model here is fit by ridge regression, solved through the kernel of the training features, with the penalty chosen per voxel on held-out training data, under both readings of the claims file: (a) a held-out 10% in 50 s blocks, (b) 5-fold cross-validation over such blocks. This is an assumption: the paper gives neither its L1 penalty nor its stopping rule, and ridge with a penalty per voxel is the fit the same laboratory used for later encoding models of the same kind. An approximate L1 fit (accelerated proximal gradient, stopped after a fixed number of iterations) is run as a check on 120 voxels, 40 per subject, after fig2de-prediction-accuracy: on those voxels it predicts better than ridge, by 0.025 (static and directional) against ridge with penalty reading (a) and by 0.005 (static) and 0.011 (directional) against reading (b).

Stimuli and motion-energy features¶

Frames are downsampled to 96 x 96, converted to CIE LAB*, and the luminance passes through the 6,555-filter motion-energy bank (Supplemental, Motion-Energy Encoding Model; Gabor Wavelet Basis Set), one 600 s run (or 60 s test block) at a time. The bank is pymoten's pyramid; its default envelopes give the paper's count of 6,555 filters (see vim2.py). Four training frames are shown to check the orientation of the stored frames (rows and columns).

Features, kernels and splits¶

Each model's features are summed per column, z-scored with the training statistics, clipped at 3 and delayed by 3 to 6 s within runs. The ridge fits go through the kernel X X' of the training samples, which depends only on the features, so it is computed once per model and serves the three subjects. The held-out 10% (reading a of the penalty and the voxel selection of identification) and the five folds (reading b) are drawn once and used for every subject (standard choice).

Responses, per subject¶

For each subject the V1, V2, V3, V3A and V3B voxels are taken from the data set's ROI masks. Voxels with any missing value are dropped and counted. The test responses are the ten repeats (rva); following Data Preprocessing ("signals were then normalized to mean 0.0 and standard deviation 1.0" for each run), each repeat is z-scored per voxel, and the observed test signal of reading (a) is their mean. The stored repeat mean rv is also scored, as a check.

Fits¶

For each model and each split the training block of the kernel is decomposed once; the penalty of each voxel is the one whose fit best predicts the held-out samples (reading a: the held-out 10%, over the 19 penalties of the grid; reading b: the mean correlation over the five folds, over the whole decades of the grid, for time), and the model is refit on all training samples with it. The clock check fits the directional model at a single delay of 0, 4 or 8 s: if responses and stimuli share a clock, the 4 s delay (the hemodynamic lag the paper assumes) predicts best and 0 s worst.

idx_all = np.arange(int(ktr.sum()))
for m in MODELS + ["delay0", "delay4", "delay8"]:
    t = time.time()
    K, Kte = KER[m]
    tr, ev = idx_all[~held], idx_all[held]
    w, U = vim2.eig_split(K, tr)
    Kev = K[np.ix_(ev, tr)]
    for s in SUBJECTS:
        Y = DATA[s]["Y"]
        rh = vim2.ridge_path_corr(w, U, Kev, Y[tr], Y[ev], ALPHAS)
        FIT[s][m] = dict(ai_a=rh.argmax(0), held_acc=rh.max(0))
        if m in ("directional", "delay4") and s == 1:
            C90 = vim2.ridge_dual(w, U, Y[tr], FIT[s][m]["ai_a"], ALPHAS)
            FIT[s][m]["Pheld"] = Kev @ C90
            FIT[s][m]["R90"] = Y[tr] - K[np.ix_(tr, tr)] @ C90
            del C90
    if m in MODELS:
        cv = {s: 0 for s in SUBJECTS}
        for k in range(5):
            tr_k, ev_k = idx_all[fold != k], idx_all[fold == k]
            wk, Uk = vim2.eig_split(K, tr_k)
            Kk = K[np.ix_(ev_k, tr_k)]
            for s in SUBJECTS:
                Y = DATA[s]["Y"]
                cv[s] = cv[s] + vim2.ridge_path_corr(wk, Uk, Kk, Y[tr_k], Y[ev_k], ALPHAS[::2]) / 5
            del wk, Uk, Kk
        for s in SUBJECTS:
            FIT[s][m]["ai_b"] = 2 * cv[s].argmax(0)  # whole decades of the same grid, for time
        if m == "directional":  # S1 fit on 90% with the reading (b) penalties, for identification
            Y = DATA[1]["Y"]
            C90 = vim2.ridge_dual(w, U, Y[tr], FIT[1][m]["ai_b"], ALPHAS)
            FIT[1][m]["Pheld_b"] = Kev @ C90

Sensitivity to the fit: L1 on a voxel subsample¶

The paper fits by L1-regularized least squares (Model Fitting, refs. 15 and 16), which is too slow here for all 13,007 voxels. As a check of how far the ridge means of fig2de may move, the static and directional models are fit by L1 on 40 voxels drawn at random per subject (120 voxels). The solver is accelerated proximal gradient (FISTA, vim2.lasso_path), along a path of seven penalties from 0.5 to 0.01 times the smallest penalty that zeroes every weight, each warm-started from the last and run for 60 iterations; the paper gives no stopping rule, so this is an approximate L1 fit, stopped early, and the relative change of the objective over the last iterations is printed. The penalty of each voxel is chosen on the held-out 10% (penalty reading a) and the model refit on all training samples along the same path. The L1 accuracies are compared with the ridge accuracies of the same voxels (both penalty readings, signal reading a).

fig2fgh-retinotopic-receptive-fields¶

Voxel receptive fields estimated from the motion-energy model are spatially localized and retinotopically organized; their angle maps are similar to those from conventional multifocal mapping, and their eccentricity maps are similar except in the far periphery, where the multifocal mapping stimulus was coarse.

Selectivity Estimation: each voxel's directional model (penalty reading a) is driven by dynamic Gaussian white noise in each cell of a 17 x 17 grid on uniform gray, and its predicted response minus the response to uniform gray gives a spatial map, to which a 2D Gaussian is fit (vim2.gaussian_fit). The multifocal maps are not in the data set, so the claims file's proxy test is used: in each subject, more than half of the retained voxels have a fit centered inside the 20 degree display; left-hemisphere V1 centers lie over 75% in the right visual field and the reverse; and eccentricity rises along V1 from the occipital pole forward (positive correlation, p < 0.05). Voxels are retained when the prediction accuracy is positive with p <= 0.01 (two-sided t test of the correlation) on (a) the 486 test samples or (b) the 728 held-out training samples. The posterior-anterior axis is the slice axis of the 64 x 64 x 18 volume (18 coronal slices, Experimental Procedures); its direction is taken from the data, as the direction from the V1 masks to the MT masks, which lie anterior to V1.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. Ridge regression replaces the L1-regularized regression, with the penalty chosen per voxel on held-out training data under both readings of the claims file; an approximate L1 fit on 120 voxels predicts better than ridge (by 0.005 to 0.025), which would raise the means, not lower them. The test repeats are z-scored per voxel before averaging, since the data set's test responses are not in the units of the training responses. The filter bank is pymoten's, whose default envelopes give the paper's 6,555 filters; its 855 zero-spatial-frequency filters have no direction and are kept as single features of the nondirectional model (4,275 features). Receptive fields and tuning are estimated with simulated probes whose contrast and duration the paper does not give (standard choices), and judged on the claims file's proxy test, since the multifocal maps are not public.

What was found about the dataset. The files match the descriptor in shape (7,200 and 540 samples, 73,728 voxels, 108,000 and 8,100 frames) and rv is the mean of rva. The descriptor does not say that the responses include the first 6 s of each block that the paper discards (the mean training response there is 0.28 to 0.42, against 0.01 elsewhere), that the test responses are on a different scale from the z-scored training responses (s.d. 0.10 to 0.11 for rv, 0.20 to 0.23 for rva, against 0.99 for rt), or that 29 to 179 V1-V3B voxels per subject hold missing values. The "peak BOLD responses" it mentions are not delay-shifted: the clock check gives 0.30 to 0.33 at a 4 s delay against 0.06 to 0.09 at 0 s. The descriptor names one ROI mask by example; the files hold many more, by hemisphere, and the set differs between subjects (printed in the data check: 30 masks in S1, among them V4, MT, MTp, IP, OBJ, VO and latocc; 30 in S2, among them EBA, OFA and V7; 44 in S3, among them IPS1 to IPS4, MST, TOS and cortALL); one subject 3 voxel lies in two V1-V3B masks.
