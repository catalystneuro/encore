Dataset. DANDI:001752, "Electrophysiological Dataset of Neuronal Post-Synaptic Currents in the Primate Anterior Insular Cortex", version 0.260825.0222 (doi:10.48324/dandi.001752/0.260825.0222). The paper cites the dandiset without a version, and this is its only published version (the draft has the same 84 assets and size), so it is pinned.

Outside inputs (with_labels). The NWB files carry no cell type. The paper's code, github.com/RFLiu2021/AIC_proj at commit 44cd8a6dfbb1f1ce363d73742e4f3652086b0f9d, holds a table of the deposited files with their cell types (ephysio/data_ephy/rawdata_ephysio/metadata_PSC.csv) and the paper's per-cell frequencies and amplitudes (ephysio/data_ephy/sPSCs_Fig7ab.xlsx, ephysio/data_ephy/miniEPSCs_Fig7ab.xlsx). The table supplies the labels; the sheets are used only to check the link (claim psc-file-labels-link) and as the variant labelling of the mEPSC cells that the claims file asks for. All measurements are made here from the NWB traces.

Data check¶

What each file holds: one VoltageClampSeries (current in amperes, one sweep, starting at 0 s) and a VoltageClampStimulusSeries of three zeros (no stimulus). There are no trial tables, intervals or intracellular_recordings tables, and no cell type anywhere in the file. The holding potential is given only in the session description. The checks below are of the experiment, durations, sampling rate, holding current, padding, duplicates and noise.

# Near-duplicates: correlation of the first 60 s, referred to the running median (so that the slow drift of
# the holding current, common to many files, does not dominate), between every pair of files of an experiment.
near = []
for exp, g in info.groupby("experiment"):
    fs = list(g.file)
    for i in range(len(fs)):
        for j in range(i + 1, len(fs)):
            a, b = traces[fs[i]][:60000], traces[fs[j]][:60000]
            a, b = a - psc.baseline(a, 1000.0), b - psc.baseline(b, 1000.0)
            n = min(len(a), len(b))

Events, under every reading¶

For each file, events are detected (helper psc.detect) in the direction set by the holding potential: outward for the sIPSC files, held at +20 mV by their description, and inward for the others. Each file is run with the two threshold readings of the claims file, (a) 5 pA and (b) the recording's RMS noise, over the whole recording and over its first 30 s (the code's window). Detector checks¶

Two checks of the detector, asked for by the verifier. (1) A null from the data: peaks of the opposite polarity. Noise is symmetric about the baseline and synaptic events are not, so the rate of peaks detected in the wrong direction estimates how many false events each threshold admits (it overestimates a little, as the decay of a real event can make an opposite peak against the baseline before it). (2) A positive control: synthetic events of known times and amplitudes (psc.inject_events, 2 Hz, log-normal amplitudes of median 10 pA, the experiment's decay) are added to every real trace, and the share each reading recovers within 3 ms is counted. Neither decides an outcome.

Group comparisons: shared code¶

Per cell, frequency (Hz) and mean amplitude (pA) under each reading. The cell groups are the table's rows (the dataset's labels, as the claims file asks). The readings the claims file and the data require:

  • threshold (a) 5 pA, (b) the recording's RMS noise, over the whole recording (both decide); the first 30 s (the code's window) is a variant;
  • sEPSC: the PC group as (a) the paper's 11 entries, which count file 15e3e16 (cell 20201126s1c2) twice, and (b) the 10 distinct cells;
  • sIPSC: the table gives PC 20201126s5c11a and VEN 20201126s5c11b the same file (1s3ysck) and names no row for fwphyw, the other file of cell Cell_AS011 in that session. Readings: (A) the table as it is, one file in both groups; (B) 1s3ysck is 11a (PC) and fwphyw 11b (VEN); (C) the reverse;
  • mEPSC: the table's labels decide; the sheet's labelling (the other way round) is a variant.

The test is the two-tailed Wilcoxon rank-sum test as MATLAB's ranksum computes it by default, the function Fig5ab_ana_sPSC.m and Fig5ab_ana_mPSC.m call (helper psc.ranksum): exact when the smaller group has fewer than 10 cells and both together fewer than 20 (sEPSC, sIPSC, mEPSC), otherwise the normal approximation (mIPSC). The column test says which.

Each claim also shows robust(...): variants at 5 pA over the whole recording that bear on the recordings' quality and decide nothing (files with noise below 2 pA; files of at least 5 min and with at least 200 events, the Methods' minimums; and the frequency minus that of opposite-polarity peaks, the noise null of the detector check).

def groups(exp, reading_rows="table", s11=None, sheet_labels=False):
    """The rows (file, cell type) of one experiment under a reading."""
    g = lab[lab.Experiment == exp][["filename_original", "rawFileName", "cellType", "key"]].copy()
    if reading_rows == "distinct":
        g = g.drop_duplicates("rawFileName")
    if exp == "sIPSC" and s11 in ("B", "C"):
        other = [f for f in files - set(lab.rawFileName) if "fwphyw" in f][0]
        tgt = "20201126s5c11b+20mV" if s11 == "B" else "20201126s5c11a+20mV"
        g.loc[g.filename_original == tgt, "rawFileName"] = other
    if sheet_labels:
        st = tab[tab.Experiment == exp].set_index("key").sheet_type
        g["cellType"] = g.key.map(st)
        g = g.dropna(subset=["cellType"])
    return g.merge(info, left_on="rawFileName", right_on="file")

def compare(g, col):
    v, p = g[g.cellType == "VEN"][col].dropna(), g[g.cellType == "PC"][col].dropna()
    if len(v) < 2 or len(p) < 2:
        return dict(n_VEN=len(v), n_PC=len(p))
    u, pval, method = psc.ranksum(v, p)
    return dict(n_VEN=len(v), n_PC=len(p), VEN_median=v.median(), PC_median=p.median(),
                VEN_range=f"{v.min():.2f}-{v.max():.2f}", PC_range=f"{p.min():.2f}-{p.max():.2f}",
                diff=v.median() - p.median(), U=u, p=pval, test=method)

def table(exp, measure, extra=()):
    rows = []
    for rd in ("5pA_whole", "rms_whole", "5pA_30s", "rms_30s", "3rms_whole"):
        for opt in extra or [dict()]:
            g = groups(exp, **opt)
            rows.append(dict(reading=rd, **{k: str(v) for k, v in opt.items()}, **compare(g, f"{measure}_{rd}")))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def robust(exp, measure, **opt):
    """Variants at 5 pA over the whole recording that bear on the recordings' quality (they decide nothing):
    all files; files with noise below 2 pA; files of at least 5 min; files with at least 200 events; and,
    for frequency, the frequency minus that of opposite-polarity peaks (the noise null of the detector check)."""
    g = groups(exp, **opt)
    col = f"{measure}_5pA_whole"
    sets = [("all files", g, col), ("noise < 2 pA", g[g.noise_pA < 2], col),
            ("at least 5 min", g[g.duration_s >= 300], col), ("at least 200 events", g[g.n_events_5pA_whole >= 200], col)]
    if measure == "freq":
        g = g.assign(freq_net=g.freq_5pA_whole - g.opp_freq_5pA)
        sets.append(("minus opposite-polarity rate", g, "freq_net"))
    return pd.DataFrame([dict(variant=name, **compare(s, c)) for name, s, c in sets])

def by_subject(exp, col, **opt):
    g = groups(exp, **opt)
    return g.groupby(["subject", "cellType"])[col].agg(["size", "median", "min", "max"]).round(2)

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper detected events in miniAnalysis and measured them in Clampfit, with settings it does not give beyond a 5 pA threshold; here a detector of the same scheme with standard parameters is used (helper docstring). The detector check shows where it can be trusted: in files with noise below 2 pA (all mEPSC, 34 of 36 mIPSC, 10 of 18 sEPSC files) it recovers 91 to 94% of injected events and finds few opposite-polarity peaks; in the sEPSC files with noise of 2 to 4 pA the opposite-polarity peaks are about half as frequent as the events (0.76 to 1.00 in the three sEPSC files above 4 pA), and in 13 of the 15 sIPSC files 0.87 to 1.54 times as frequent (about half in the other two), so a large part of the 5 pA frequencies there is noise, and they are several times the code's sheets. For this reason the two sIPSC claims are blocked (methods_underspecified). The threshold at the recording's RMS noise, reading (b) of the claims file, finds as many opposite-polarity peaks as events in every experiment; a question to a reader asks whether the claims file misreads the Methods there. The analysed stretch is the whole deposited recording (the code's mIPSC sheet used 30 s, shown as a variant). Cell types come from the code's table, not from the dataset. The paper's own sheet values, tested as the figure tests them, give VEN lower in all four frequencies (p = 0.0068 to 0.028) with the sheet's labels; with the dataset's labels the mEPSC order reverses.

What was found about the dataset.

  • The NWB files carry no cell type; the type of each file is only in the paper's code (metadata_PSC.csv).
  • That table names one sIPSC file twice (as a PC and as a VEN) and does not name fwphyw; and for every mEPSC cell its label is the opposite of the paper's analysis sheet. The dandiset holds 15 mEPSC files where the paper reports 16 cells.
  • The spontaneous IPSC files are described as held at +20 mV and their holding currents are positive (107 to 979 pA), where the Methods state IPSCs were held at -60 mV.
  • 22 of 36 mIPSC files end in runs of exact zeros (6,795 to 30,167 samples); 44 of 84 files are shorter than the 5 min the Methods give as the minimum (sEPSC 14 of 18, sIPSC 8 of 15, mIPSC 22 of 36 after the padding is cut).
  • The noise of 11 of 15 sIPSC files and 2 sEPSC files exceeds the 5 pA the Methods give as the largest RMS noise; no sIPSC file has noise below 2 pA.
  • All files have rate 1000 Hz; the decay times of the average events (sEPSC median 7 ms, sIPSC 14 ms, mEPSC 9 ms, mIPSC 16 ms) are consistent with it. There are no trial tables or stimuli, so no other clock check applies.
  • No two files are identical, and no pair correlates above 0.15 after the drift is removed.
  • cell_id is not unique within a session (three pairs), so it does not identify a cell.
