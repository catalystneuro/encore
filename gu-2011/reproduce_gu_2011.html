Reproducing Gu et al. (2011) from CRCNS stc-1¶

Paper. Gu Y, Liu S, Fetsch CR, Yang Y, Fok S, Sunkara A, DeAngelis GC, Angelaki DE (2011). Perceptual learning reduces interneuronal correlations in macaque visual cortex. Neuron 71:750-761. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2011.06.015. The claims file was written from the version of record (journal PDF text, with the Experimental Procedures and the legends of Figures 1 to 8) and the Supplemental Information (legends of Figures S1 to S8).

Data set. CRCNS stc-1 (doi:10.6080/K07P8WKF), data descriptor version 0.6 (5 September 2018), read from the public bucket crcnsarchive under stc-1/live/. CRCNS data sets are not versioned, and the paper cites no version, so the version is recorded as unversioned (docs/archives.md, CRCNS). The bucket holds one copy of the data, stc-1/live/ (orig/ holds only an older description document), so live/ is the version pinned. The file read, MSTd.mat, is checked on loading against its MD5 sum in the data set's checksums.md5, cd4c13b360aa05689015f1bd2b88a05a, so a later change makes the notebook fail (VIP.mat, read only to check for overlap, 8d7924e0a1ae86c56b78cd1e2f530e2d).

What the data set holds. Derived values only. For the paper, experiment 3 of MSTd.mat holds the 127 MSTd pairs separated by less than 1 mm, with per condition (vestibular, visual) the signal correlation, the noise correlation and the two units' preferred headings, and the recording's file_id, which names the monkey. It holds no spike times or trial responses, no training-group label, no distances, no tuning curves of the pair units, none of the 52 pairs over 1 mm, none of the single-electrode database of Figure 4, Figure S4 and Figure 8A, no training histories and no eye records. Experiments 1 and 2 hold the tuning curves, choice probabilities and neuronal thresholds of 129 single units of two trained monkeys from Gu et al. (2008); they are used for fig4dh-lateral-more-sensitive and fig8ab-choice-probability-lateral as a subset of the paper's database.

Outside inputs. None beyond the paper itself: the training group of each monkey comes from the paper's per-animal pair counts (Figure S2B legend), checked by the link claim training-group-link.

Random numbers. Only the bootstrap intervals of fig5-intercept-lower-trained and fig5-regression-coefficients draw random numbers, each from its own generator with a fixed seed.

fig2b-trained-lower-noise-correlation¶

Mean noise correlation among MSTd pairs (separated by less than 1 mm) is significantly smaller in trained animals (0.023 +- 0.017 SEM, n = 89) than in naive animals (0.116 +- 0.031, n = 38).

The pooled rnoise is the mean of the vestibular and visual values (assumption, see stc1.py). The criterion's readings are a two-sided Student and a Welch t test; whether the stored values include the renormalization in blocks of 20 trials cannot be told from the data set, which holds one value per condition and no trial responses, so that reading cannot be run. The per-condition values are shown as variants.

def ancova(cond, measure):
    s = f"rsig_{cond}"
    full = smf.ols(f"{measure} ~ {s} * C(group)", pairs).fit()
    add = smf.ols(f"{measure} ~ {s} + C(group)", pairs).fit()
    a_full, a_add = sm.stats.anova_lm(full, typ=2), sm.stats.anova_lm(add, typ=2)
    return {"condition": cond, "rnoise": measure,
            "slope_all": add.params[s],
            "p_rsignal_full": a_full.loc[s, "PR(>F)"], "p_rsignal_additive": a_add.loc[s, "PR(>F)"],

            "p_interaction": a_full.loc[f"{s}:C(group)", "PR(>F)"],
            "group_effect_additive": add.params["C(group)[T.trained]"]}

fig4dh-lateral-more-sensitive¶

Neurons preferring lateral headings are more sensitive to heading variation around straight ahead than neurons preferring fore-aft headings (p << 0.001, factorial ANOVA).

The paper's single-electrode database (992 visual and 556 vestibular neurons, naive and trained) is not in the data set. The experiment 1 tuning curves of 129 units of two trained monkeys are, so the preference-group effect can be tested within trained animals only, without the training factor of the paper's ANOVA. # Selection proxy: one-way ANOVA F from the mean curve, with n repetitions and variance = Fano x mean
# (counts over 1 s). n = 5, Fano 1 is the paper's typical protocol with equation 4's variance; n = 3,
# Fano 2 a stricter selection (the paper's minimum repetitions and a variance twice the mean).
def tuning_p(resp, n, fano):
    r = np.asarray(resp, float)
    k = len(r)
    F = n * ((r - r.mean()) ** 2).sum() / (k - 1) / (fano * r.mean())
    return stats.f.sf(F, k - 1, k * (n - 1))

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The data set holds per-condition noise correlations, not the pooled one, so the pooled value is taken as the mean of the two. Under that reading the group means of the main result (0.0241 and 0.1190) lie within the SEMs of the paper's 0.023 and 0.116, and the t test gives p = 0.0056 against the paper's 0.006. Seven of the 17 tested outcomes rest on this assumption. Training groups come from the paper's per-animal pair counts. Only the 127 pairs under 1 mm are available, where Figure 1F uses 179. The simulations use a tuning amplitude and baseline that the paper does not give, and the threshold ratios depend on them; four outcomes rest on this assumption. Single-unit thresholds and choice probabilities come from 129 units of two trained monkeys, not the paper's database of naive and trained animals (992 and 556 neurons, 311 choice probabilities), so the preference-group test of Figure 4 runs without the training factor and without the paper's selection of significantly tuned units, which needs trial responses.
