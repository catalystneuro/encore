Reproducing Geiller, Vancura et al. (2020) from dandiset 000625¶

Paper: Geiller T, Vancura B, Terada S, et al. (2020). Large-Scale 3D Two-Photon Imaging of Molecularly Identified CA1 Interneuron Dynamics in Behaving Mice. Neuron 108(5):968-983. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2020.09.013. The full text was used: the NIH author manuscript on PubMed Central (PMC7736348), with its STAR Methods and figure legends. The supplementary figures were not available, so claims about them rest on the Results text.

Data: DANDI:000625, version 0.231114.0019, streamed from the archive. The paper cites no version of the dandiset (it says the data "will be made available soon"), so the most recent published version is pinned. The only other published version, 0.231114.0017, has the same three assets; the two differ only in the description text (checked below).

What can and cannot be tested¶

The paper imaged 3626 interneurons in 16 mice across random foraging, novel contexts, goal-oriented learning, sharp-wave ripple recordings and two further staining strategies. The dandiset holds one VGAT-Cre mouse (tg19) in three random-foraging sessions: dF/F of 231 ROIs, the subtype of each ROI, belt position, and reward and water times. It holds no cell depth, LFP, licking, novel-context or goal-oriented sessions, and none of the VIP-Cre or strategy 3 mice.

Checks of the description against the values:

  • Subject and sessions. One mouse, tg19, which the paper lists under staining strategy 1. Three sessions, two on 2019-06-13 about 2.5 h apart and one two days earlier. All three are described as "session 1 of 4", and the experiment description mentions four random-foraging sessions; one is not in the dandiset.
  • ROIs and subtypes. 231 ROIs, of which 215, 216 and 224 have traces in the three sessions; the others are entirely NaN. The subtype table has 232 rows; the row with id 231 has no ROI and is left out, and the others are matched to ROIs by id. 74 ROIs carry one of the paper's six subtypes (24 PVBC, 10 SOM, 3 BISTR, 9 AAC counting "AAC (RIGHT CELL)", 15 CCK, 13 NGFF). The labels "UNIDENTIFIED" and "NOTHING" are not explained. The column's description reads "Recording location in Middle Earth".
  • dF/F. The values run from about -0.15 to 8.8 with a median near 0.12, so they are fractions, not percent. The stated unit, lumens, is wrong. The DfOverF and Fluorescence series are the same HDF5 object, so the raw fluorescence the description lists is not there.
  • Position. Stated in metres, it runs from 0 to 2.11 m, so the belt is 2.11 m long here, where the paper says 2 m. Position is stored only when it changes (gaps of up to 66 s), so the value at a frame is the last stored sample.
  • Imaging rate. Every file states 5.7981 Hz. For session 21333 that gives 899 s of imaging against 897 s of behaviour. For 21324 and 21322 it gives 680 s against 900 s and 879 s. This is checked next.
  • Raw images. One cropped series per file (33 x 46 or 25 x 46 pixels), where the description says "individual cropped ROIs". The emission wavelength is given as 920 nm, the same as the excitation. The subject's age is P60D in all three sessions, where the paper gives 3 to 5 months.
  • Depth and coordinates. The plane segmentation has only the columns id, pixel_mask and pixel_mask_index, and the pixel masks have the fields x, y and weight, with no z. The masks run over x from 0 to 10625 and y from 0 to 24 or 32, which fits 231 crops of 46 pixels side by side, so they give no position in the field of view. The imaging plane has origin_coords (0, 0) and grid_spacing (50, 50) um, which are placeholders, and there are no reference images. Nothing in the files gives a cell's depth or layer.
  • Other series. Acquisition holds the position and the one cropped image series; the behaviour module holds only the reward and water intervals. There is no lick record.
  • Session order. The session ids run 21322, 21324, 21333, but the start times put 21333 first, two days before the other two. The raw image series all start at 0 s, and the files were all created on 2023-11-13, so nothing else in the files dates the sessions. This notebook orders the sessions by their stated start times; if those are wrong, the stand-ins for 1 h and 24 h in s6-later-lower-above-chance change.

Do imaging and behaviour share a clock?¶

The files give no frame timestamps, only a rate. For each session, fit_frame_rate places the frames at i / rate for rates from 3.5 to 6.5 Hz and scores each rate by the best correlation (over lags within 5 s) between the mean z-scored dF/F and running speed. Then, as a contrast, running (speed above 5 cm/s) is decoded from the population with the labels shifted in time, on the stated and on the fitted clock. With the right clock, decoding peaks near zero shift and falls to chance far from it.

Repeated deposits and cell identity across sessions¶

The three files are checked for duplicated recordings (identical traces or behaviour), and for whether the ROI with the same index is the same cell in every session, as the dandiset's description implies ("231 ROIs were observed over 3 separate imaging sessions") and as the paper's averaging over sessions requires. The tests compare, for the same ROI in two sessions, mask area, velocity correlation and tuning curve, against the same quantity with ROIs shuffled.

Spatially selective interneurons (fig4-fraction-spatial)¶

17.5 ± 6.4% of interneurons, blind of subtype, passed the test for significant spatial selectivity.

The paper's rule, on frames with speed above 5 cm/s: tuning curves of 100 bins smoothed with a Gaussian of 3 bins; 1000 shuffles rotating position against dF/F; a field is 10 consecutive bins above the shuffles' 95th percentile, with a peak above 100% dF/F. A failure to reproduce here may be a failure of this attempt, not of the paper: see the reasoning and the differences from the paper's method.

How this differs from the paper's analysis:

  • One mouse with 74 identified interneurons, where the paper pools 6 or 7 mice per analysis.
  • The frame rates are fitted from the data for two sessions, as the stated rate does not fit.
  • The ROI with the same index is taken as the same cell in every session; the data are consistent with this but do not show it.
  • The 1 hour and 24 hour comparisons use sessions 2.5 h and about two days apart.
  • Choices the methods leave open: velocity smoothed over 0.5 s, run events at least 5 s apart, place fields allowed to wrap around the belt, a one-vs-one linear SVM, shuffled position made by rotating dF/F against position.

What was found about the dandiset:

  • The stated imaging rate, 5.7981 Hz, is wrong for sessions 21324 and 21322, whose frames fit about 4.39 Hz; on the stated clock their activity does not follow running.
  • The dF/F unit is given as lumens; the Fluorescence series is a link to the dF/F series, so raw fluorescence is missing; the subtype column's description is a placeholder ("Recording location in Middle Earth"), and the table has one row more than there are ROIs.
  • The labels "NOTHING" and "UNIDENTIFIED" are not explained, nor are the reward and water intervals; there is no cell depth, no mapping of ROIs across sessions, and no lick record.
  • All three sessions are called "session 1 of 4", and the fourth session is not included. The session ids and the start times give different orders.
  • The pixel masks are stored on a strip of the 231 cropped frames side by side, and the imaging plane's origin and grid spacing are placeholders, so ROIs have no position in the field of view.
  • The dandiset holds one of the paper's 16 mice and only its random-foraging sessions, without saying which part of the paper it covers.
  • With the paper's place-field rule and a peak above 100% dF/F, almost no ROI qualifies, as only 1.1% of tuning curves reach 1.0 dF/F; the dF/F here may be scaled differently from the paper's.
