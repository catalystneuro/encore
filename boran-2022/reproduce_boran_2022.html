Reproducing Boran et al. (2022) from dandiset 000575¶

Paper. Boran E, Hilfiker P, Stieglitz L, Sarnthein J, Klaver P (2022). Persistent neuronal firing in the medial temporal lobe supports performance and workload of visual working memory in humans. NeuroImage 254, 119123. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119123. The claims file was written from the version of record (Methods, Results, figure legends and Table 1; the paper has no supplement) and is claims/000575/10.1016-j.neuroimage.2022.119123.yaml.

Dandiset. DANDI:000575, version 0.231010.1811 (doi:10.48324/dandi.000575/0.231010.1811). The paper cites no version of the dandiset (its data statement names the GIN repository USZ_NCH/Human_MTL_units_visual_WM, of which the dandiset is a converted form). 0.231010.1811 is the only published version; the draft lists the same 18 assets with the same total size. The files are streamed; nothing is downloaded.

What it holds. 18 NWB files for 16 sessions of 13 participants (two pairs of files of participant 12 are copies): trial tables with set size and the correct answer, the answer per trial, task epochs, 809 sorted units with spike times inside the trial windows and their mean waveforms, and, in 12 files, LFP (not used).

Outside inputs. None for the analysis. The conversion code named in the dandiset's metadata (github.com/janhohenheim/usz-neuro-conversion, commit c31dad9) was read to interpret the response timestamps (data check); no values are taken from it.

The electrodes table has one row per macro contact (AHL1 to AHL8 and so on, MNI coordinates about 5 mm apart, as the contacts of the depth electrodes are), and each unit points to the row whose number is its microwire's number. The microwires of a bundle all protrude from the tip of the electrode (Methods 2.3), so the location of a unit is the atlas label of a macro contact, not of its wire: units on wires 5 to 8 get lateral temporal labels (MTG, STG, ITG). The location column does not give the units' regions.

The claims file names two readings of the region (neurons-recorded). Reading (a), the anatomical location of the microwire tips as Figure 1d shows them, is taken here as the atlas label of the tip contact of each bundle: Hipp is hippocampus, PhG (parahippocampal gyrus, which holds the entorhinal cortex) entorhinal cortex and Amyg amygdala. The files hold no location for the wires themselves, so this is the nearest the dataset comes to the tip location. 133 units are on bundles whose tip contact has another label (unspecific or FuG, all on hippocampal electrodes); Figure 1d colours every site as one of the three regions, so these keep the region of their electrode name, and the count without them is printed as well.

Where those 133 units go is not fixed by the paper, and it changes the region counts, so both choices are run. (a1) gives them the region of their electrode name. (a2) gives them the label of the contact nearest the tip that has an MTL label, and the electrode name only where no contact of the bundle has one. The crosstab above shows the difference: the 28 units on PHR of participant 8 (contacts 2 and 3 in PhG) go to entorhinal cortex under (a2), the 45 units on AHL of participant 8 (contact 2 in Hipp) stay in hippocampus, and the remaining 60 units (P05 PHL, P06 PHR, P07 AHR, P13 AHR) have no MTL contact and keep their electrode name under both. (a2) gives 467, 196 and 146. It is adopted for reading (a) because it uses the atlas labels of the bundle's own contacts before the electrode name. The grounding is weak: it moves only the 28 PHR units, and it places them by contacts 2 and 3, which lie further from the protruding wire tips than contact 1. It was examined after the verifier's first report pointed at these units, and it is not the paper's assignment (which the files do not hold); the outcomes that depend on it say so. Reading (b) is the electrode name (AH and PH hippocampus, EC entorhinal cortex, A amygdala, Table 1 legend); 5 units are on bundles it does not cover (PCL in participant 8, HL and HR in participant 11, whose subject description lists only AHL, AHR, AL and AR). Reading (a2) gives 467, 196 and 146 units, (a1) 495, 168 and 146, reading (b) 361, 217 and 226, against the paper's 454, 209 and 146. Every per-region result below is given under all three.

Within trials the timing is consistent with the epochs: the population rate rises after the memory array (peak 0.175 s after onset, printed above), and many neurons differ between periods (fig2c and fig3b below), so the spikes share the clock of the epochs within each trial. The pairing of spikes with the trial labels does not hold as stored. Workload information in the spikes peaks when the spikes stored in trial window i are paired with the label of trial i + 3, not of trial i (pooled value 1.16 at k = 3 against 0.91 to 1.05 at other shifts), and decoding workload from all neurons gives 0.46 at k = 0, below the scrambled runs (0.50), against 0.60 at k = 3 (printed above).

    n, k = Y.shape

def workload_counts(maint, seed, sessions=None, n_rep=N_REP, region_col="region"):

    workload, correct trials, within the maintenance neurons; null from n_rep shuffles of the
    set-size labels across correct trials within each session."""

    rows, reps = [], [[] for _ in range(n_rep)]
    for s in (sessions or S):
        sel = maint[maint.key == s["key"]].sel.values

def cluster_counts(maint, seed, n_perm=1000, n_rep=N_REP):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    rows, reps = [], [[] for _ in range(n_rep)]
    for s in S:
        sel = maint[maint.key == s["key"]].sel.values
        if not sel.any():
            continue
        t_ = s["trials"]; ok = t_.correct.values
        wc = m.window_counts(s, t_off, WIN)[sel][:, ok, :].astype(float) / WIN   # units, trials, time
        hi = t_.set_size.values[ok] >= 4
        n1, n2 = hi.sum(), (~hi).sum()
        thr = stats.t.ppf(0.975, n1 + n2 - 2)
        def tcurve(h, u):
            a, b = wc[u][h], wc[u][~h]
            with np.errstate(divide="ignore", invalid="ignore"):
                return np.nan_to_num((a.mean(0) - b.mean(0)) / np.sqrt(a.var(0, ddof=1) / len(a) + b.var(0, ddof=1) / len(b)))
        perms = [rng.permutation(hi) for _ in range(n_perm)]
        reg = s["units"].region.values[sel]
        reg_tip = s["units"].region_tip.values[sel]
        reg_tip2 = s["units"].region_tip2.values[sel]
        for u in range(wc.shape[0]):
            null_max = np.array([max([abs(c[2]) for c in m.cluster_masses(tcurve(h, u), thr)] or [0]) for h in perms])
            crit = np.percentile(null_max, 95)
            def classify(h):
                sig = [c for c in m.cluster_masses(tcurve(h, u), thr) if abs(c[2]) > crit and in_maint[c[0]:c[1]].any()]
                if not sig:
                    return 0
                return int(np.sign(max(sig, key=lambda c: abs(c[2]))[2]))
            rows.append({"region": reg[u], "region_tip": reg_tip[u], "region_tip2": reg_tip2[u], "dir": classify(hi)})
            for k in range(n_rep):
                reps[k].append((reg[u], reg_tip[u], reg_tip2[u], classify(rng.permutation(hi))))
    res = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    out = []
    for (col, rname), j in zip(REGION_READINGS, [2, 1, 0]):

def kernel(sd):
    half = int(round(0.5 / DBIN)); x = np.arange(-half, half + 1) * DBIN    # 1000 ms window
    k = np.exp(-0.5 * (x / sd) ** 2); return k / k.sum()

def smooth(r, k):
    """Gaussian smoothing along time; at the trial edges the kernel is renormalised to the samples inside."""
    return convolve1d(r, k, axis=-1, mode="constant") / convolve1d(np.ones(r.shape[-1]), k, mode="constant")

def cond_trials(s, shuffle_rng=None):
    t = s["trials"]; ok = np.where(t.correct.values)[0]
    ss = t.set_size.values[ok]
    if shuffle_rng is not None:
        ss = shuffle_rng.permutation(ss)
    return [ok[ss == c] for c in SS]

def population(k, sessions=S, shuffle_rng=None, trial_sets=None):
    """Trial-averaged rates (units, set sizes, time), smoothed, minus each unit's mean fixation rate."""
    X = []
    for i, s in enumerate(sessions):
        idx = trial_sets[i] if trial_sets is not None else cond_trials(s, shuffle_rng)
        X.append(np.stack([s["bins"][:, ix, :].mean(1) / DBIN for ix in idx], 1))
    X = smooth(np.concatenate(X), k)
    return X - X[:, :, :int(0.9 / DBIN)].mean((1, 2))[:, None, None]

def fit_dpca(X, reg=None, n=15, seed=0):
    np.random.seed(seed)                     # the package's randomized SVD draws from numpy's global generator
    d = dPCA.dPCA(labels="st", join={"st": ["s", "st"]}, n_components=n, regularizer=reg)
    d.protect = ["t"]
    d.fit(X)
    Xr = X.reshape(X.shape[0], -1); Xr = Xr - Xr.mean(1, keepdims=True); tot = (Xr ** 2).sum()
    comps = []
    for key in d.D:
        for i in range(d.D[key].shape[1]):
            z = d.D[key][:, i] @ Xr
            comps.append((1 - ((Xr - np.outer(d.P[key][:, i], z)) ** 2).sum() / tot, key, i))
    comps.sort(key=lambda c: -c[0])           # explained variance as in the dPCA toolbox (Kobak et al. 2016)
    F = np.stack([d.P[k_][:, i] for _, k_, i in comps[:n]], 1); W = np.stack([d.D[k_][:, i] for _, k_, i in comps[:n]], 1)
    cum = 1 - ((Xr - F @ (W.T @ Xr)) ** 2).sum() / tot
    Z = (W.T @ Xr).reshape(n, *X.shape[1:])
    return {"comps": comps, "cum15": cum, "d234": sum(c[0] for c in comps[1:4]),
            "setsize": sum(c[0] for c in comps[:n] if c[1] == "st"), "Z": Z, "W": W}

def maint_distance(Z):
    sl = slice(int(1.7 / DBIN), int(2.6 / DBIN))
    return np.mean([np.linalg.norm(Z[:, a, sl] - Z[:, b, sl], axis=0).mean() for a in range(4) for b in range(a + 1, 4)])

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper's analysis code is not public, so every method comes from the Methods as the claims file reads them, with the readings it names run side by side. Every per-region result is given under three region readings. Two follow reading (a) of the claims file, the location of the microwire tips, taken as the atlas label of each bundle's tip contact; for the 133 units whose tip contact has no MTL label, (a2) takes the label of the contact nearest the tip with an MTL label (and the electrode name where none has one), and (a1) the electrode name. The third is reading (b), the electrode name. The outcomes rest on (a2), which was chosen after seeing the counts it gives and differs from (a1) only for 28 units of participant 8, placed by contacts that lie further from the wire tips than the tip contact. The files hold no location for the microwires themselves. The number of label-scrambled repetitions is 499 for the neuron counts as in the claims file, but 99 for the dPCA null, the cluster-based reading and reading (b) of the test neurons, and the decoders use 100 resampling runs and 200 scrambled runs (50 and 100 for the other readings and for performance), where the paper used 500 and 1000. The per-neuron tests use 1000 permutations, and 5000 for the fig2c counts, the claims file's check. Spike counts for dPCA are taken in 10 ms bins before smoothing, in place of 1 ms. The speed and distance of Figure 4b and 4c use the claims file's averaging, which the paper does not define.

What was found about the dataset.

  • Behavior matches the paper exactly where it can be compared: 2678 correct trials, F = 89.78 for accuracy (paper 89.79), all Table 1 capacities, and F = 48.47 for response time when the per-session value is the median RT.
  • Participant 12's two sessions are each deposited twice (four files), and both carry the same start time.
  • Sessions hold 174 to 192 trials where the description states 192; the missing trials are not documented.
  • Spikes are stored only inside trial windows (fixation onset to test offset).
  • The location of each unit is the atlas label of the macro contact whose number is the unit's microwire number, not the location of the microwire. The electrode name does not give the paper's three region counts; the atlas labels of each bundle's contacts, read from the tip, give 467, 196 and 146 against 454, 209 and 146. The files do not hold the paper's assignment of units to regions. Five units are on bundles (PCL, HL, HR) that the subject descriptions do not list.
  • The response timestamps put 1385 of 3038 responses before the test array. Read as the stored response time from test onset plus a 1.7 s offset the conversion adds, they are plausible.
  • The workload information in the spikes peaks when the spikes stored in trial window i are paired with the label of trial i + 3. No session has set-size information at k = 0 beyond shuffled labels; pooled over the 7 sessions with 192 trials the information peaks at k = 3 (corrected p = 0.005), and over the 9 sessions with fewer trials at no shift. Single-neuron and population measures of workload are at chance as deposited (decoding about 45%, below the scrambled 50%), and decoding rises to about 59% with every session moved by three trials. Session by session the shift by three trials is supported only in P03S1 and P06S1 among the complete sessions (and weakly in P04S1 and P09S1); the pooled peak is weighted toward those two sessions. The spikes and the trial table may be misaligned, but a single offset is not established. Moving every session by three trials recovers little: fig2d counts of 1, 1 and 1, dPCA set-size variance at the null median, and decoding at 0.585 (p = .04) against the paper's 87.5%. A misalignment could explain part of why the claims that use trial labels (workload, set size, correct against incorrect) fail while those that do not (period contrasts, rates, behavior) hold, but the variants do not show that it explains the failures.
  • The paper's 118 ms/item equals the slope of the median response times on the condition step (1 to 4), not on the set size (68.8 ms/item); asked as a question for a reader.
