Dana et al. (2016), red calcium indicators: Figure 4 on CRCNS cai-2¶

Paper: Dana, Mohar, Sun et al. (2016), "Sensitive red protein calcium indicators for imaging neural activity", eLife 5:e12727, doi:10.7554/elife.12727. The claims file (claims/dana-2016.yaml) was written from the version of record (PMC4846379) with its Methods, figure legends and Supplementary files 1 and 2.

Dataset: CRCNS cai-2 (doi:10.6080/K0W37T87), read anonymously from the public S3 bucket crcnsarchive under cai-2/live/. CRCNS data sets are not versioned, so the version is recorded as unversioned (docs/archives.md). cai-2/live/ is the only published copy of the data (the bucket also has cai-2/orig/, which holds only an older description document), so it is the one pinned. The data set's checksums.md5 gives e0bdb7e619b82a99fbe594ef62f647cf for data/jRGECO1a_data.tar.gz and d29a21d0d4a40ebc774ca9edb1079228 for data/jRCAMP1a_data.tar.gz; the data check below computes the MD5 of each archive as it streams it and compares the two, so a later change to the files can be seen. The data set's description is version 0.8 (April 19, 2016).

Outside inputs: Supplementary files 1 and 2 of the paper (doi:10.7554/eLife.12727.030 and .031), streamed from the eLife CDN, for the three data: elsewhere claims on the culture screen; and Supplementary Tables 1 and 3 of Chen et al. (2013b), Nature 499:295 (doi:10.1038/nature12354), read from the Supplementary Information PDF of the author manuscript in the PMC open-access bucket (pmc-oa-opendata, PMC3777791.1), for the GCaMP6 values of fig4g-kinetics-like-gcamp6.

The data set holds only the simultaneous imaging and cell-attached recordings of Figure 4. Claims on the paper's other experiments are blocked, as the record says.

Recordings deposited twice¶

Files are compared by the MD5 of their soma fluorescence and of their raw ephys trace. A pair with both identical is the same recording deposited twice, and only the first file of the pair is kept. Files split into _part1 and _part2 are parts of one recording; their time ranges are checked for overlap.

Shared clock of spikes and fluorescence¶

The spikes (10 kHz ephys clock) and the fluorescence frames (frame times) are on one time base by the description ("the times are relative session start"). This is checked per recording: the correlation of the spike count in each frame with the frame-to-frame rise of the corrected fluorescence, at lags of -15 to +15 frames. A shared clock puts the peak at lag 0 or +1. As a null, the same correlation with the spike train circularly shifted by a random amount (at least 300 frames; 20 shifts, seed 0) gives the size expected without a relation.

function [event,last_spike,freq]=find_nspike_event(peak,n,bin,interval_pre,interval_post)
fs=10000;
%Freq measure the mean frequency of a burst - last minus first spikes divided by time

ind=find(peak);
event=zeros(size(peak));
freq=zeros(size(peak));
last_spike=zeros(size(peak));
for i=2:(length(ind)-n)
    if (ind(i+n-1)-ind(i))<bin   % if first spike and last spike occur within bin
        if (ind(i)-ind(i-1))>interval_pre    % if the previous spike occur > 1000ms earlier than first spike
            if (ind(i+n)-ind(i+n-1))>interval_post  % if the next train start >1000ms after the last spike
                event(ind(i))=1;
                last_spike(ind(i+n-1))=1;
                if n>=2
                    freq(ind(i))=1/((ind(i+n-1)-ind(i))/n/fs);
                end
            end
        end
    end
end

The Figure 4 analysis rests on 199, 70, 29, 34, 35, 22, 14 and 21 jRGECO1a events and 135, 65, 71, 52, 33, 20, 14 and 11 jRCaMP1a events of 1 to 8 APs in their time bins, and on 320 (jRGECO1a, 4 s) and 274 (jRCaMP1a, 5 s) no-AP noise events.

Isolation readings of the claim: (a) more than 1 s before and after, (b) before only. The data set's script isolates by 1 s before and 0.8 s after (interval_post=800*fs/1000), which disagrees with the Methods ("isolated from other APs by >1 s") and with the script's own comment (">1000ms after the last spike"); the claims file makes the Methods the main reading where they and the script disagree, so the script's isolation is run as a variant, not a reading of the claim, and reading (a) is the main one. Noise readings (i), (ii) and the script's (find_no_event.m, one trace per spike-free gap longer than the bin, at its midpoint), which settles a detail the Methods leave open and is the main noise reading. A combined reading pairs one isolation reading with one noise reading; the claim counts how many of the 18 counts are within tolerance. The duplicate jRCaMP1a recording is counted once.

Detection: traces, templates and projections (claims fig4e-* and fig4df)¶

The traces of every event set are cut once per segment reading. Segment readings (a), (b), (c) are those of the claim; "script" is the data set's script (10 frames before to 20 after the frame nearest the event, interpolated ten-fold, F0 from the 10 frames before, noise traces with |dF/F0| >= 0.3 dropped). Templates (i) all events and (ii) leave-one-out. The detection rate is the fraction of event projections above the 95th percentile of the noise projections (5% false positives, Methods and criterion); the script's 99th percentile is shown as a variant. The main reading is isolation (a), the script's noise traces and segment, and the template from all events.

Some combinations of the named readings cut noise traces that hold a spike's transient: noise reading (i) starts its first bin at a spike, and the script's noise traces (centred in gaps of more than 4 or 5 s) run past the next spike under the 4 or 5 s segments (a) and (b). Tallies are given over all combinations and over the others ("clean" combinations).

The script is written for jRGECO1a at 30 Hz. Its frame window applied to jRCaMP1a at 15 Hz covers 0.67 s before to 1.33 s after the event, twice the time; that it was the jRCaMP1a analysis is an assumption.

The script segment places each event at its ephys time stamp, which the clock check above supports (peak at lag 0 in 41 of 46 recordings). The script itself places it at the sample index divided by the sampling rate plus the first frame time (ind_isolate(n)/fs+...time(1)), about 16 ms later at 30 Hz, 33 ms later at 15 Hz and 0.286 s later in data_20150804_cell5_002.mat. def script_rates(ind, iso, n, align="ephys", drop=()):
    # script segment and noise traces, template from all events, for one isolation reading; align "script"
    # places events at the script's times (cai2.script_time); recordings in `drop` are left out
    keep = [j for j, r in enumerate(recs[ind]) if r["file"] not in drop]
    N = [cai2.segment_traces(recs[ind][j], noise[(ind, "script")][j], "script", ind, noise=True, align=align)[0] for j in keep]
    S = [cai2.segment_traces(recs[ind][j], events[(ind, iso, n)][j], "script", ind, align=align)[0] for j in keep]
    N = np.concatenate([x for x in N if len(x)]); S = np.concatenate([x for x in S if len(x)])

def pick(df, **kw):

How the analysis differs from the paper's: events are isolated by more than 1 s on each side, as the Methods say, where the data set's script uses 0.8 s after the event; the detection claims use the frame window and the noise traces of the script, which the Methods do not give, with the Methods' 5% false-positive threshold in place of the script's 1%; the script is written for jRGECO1a and its frame window is assumed for jRCaMP1a.
