Farrell et al. (2020) on CRCNS ear-1: voltage sensing in the lateral membrane of outer hair cells¶

Paper. Farrell B, Skidmore BS, Rajasekharan V, Brownell WE (2020). A novel theoretical framework reveals more than one voltage-sensing pathway in the lateral membrane of outer hair cells. J Gen Physiol 152(7), doi:10.1085/jgp.201912447. The claims file (claims/crcns-ear-1/10.1085-jgp.201912447.yaml) was written from the version of record (PMC7335013) as flattened text: its displayed equations and Tables S1 to S4 are missing, and the forms of the equations used here are the claims file's reconstructions.

Data set. CRCNS ear-1, doi:10.6080/K0571975, "Data collection curated with an application ontology describes the methods and results upon performing an ex-vivo voltage-clamp assay on outer hair cells of the mammalian cochlea" (Farrell and Bengtson), data description version 0.62 (May 11, 2020). Files are read from ear-1/live/data_and_scripts.zip in the public S3 bucket crcnsarchive, streamed in place.

Version. CRCNS data sets are not versioned, so the version is recorded as unversioned (docs/archives.md). The bucket holds two copies of the zip: ear-1/live/ (MD5 9955733f5e7314a1f0983fbc18aab714 in the data set's checksums.md5, the file the data set's file list names) and ear-1/old/ver1/. The paper cites the data set by its DOI only, with no version. The live copy is the current published one; its description (v0.62) is the one that cites this paper, so it is pinned here. The MD5 is read from checksums.md5 below and printed, so that a later change is detectable. The old copy was not read.

What the data set holds. 89 outer hair cells, one HDF5 file each (HDF5_data/), and the four original MATLAB files they were translated from (Original_Data/, one per sex and maturity group), with the per-cell values the authors derived: CL, QTm, V0.5 (raw and corrected), alphaB, Rm, Rs. It does not hold CLW, CLn, the stereocilia capacitance CSB, the sequencing, the protein model, or the values from other rodents.

CLW. The claims file's shared method was amended after a question to a reader (questions/crcns-ear-1.yaml): CLW = CL - CNLW with CNLW = CSB + root determined per cell (Eqs. 8a and 8c as rebuilt), judged under reading L-a (stored CLW, CNLW or CSB) where it exists, otherwise L-b. The data set stores none of them, so L-b is used. The paper takes the stereocilia bundle capacitance from Corbitt et al. (2012), doi:10.1016/j.bpj.2012.04.054, and that paper's rule is used here: bundle area 0.07 um2 per um2 of membrane area AM = 2 pi r (L + r) up to 2000 um2, about 140 um2 above, at 0.0075 pF/um2 (Corbitt et al. 2012, Methods). AM is the stored cell surface area; the 6 cells without one take the median CSB of their turn. The root is that of QTm against ln(CL - CSB) over all 89 cells. The rule is an assumption (the paper does not give it), and it passes the claims file's admissibility test: it gives the Fig. S1 cell CLW 18.30 pF, within 1.856 pF of the legend's 18.56 pF. Every outcome that uses CLW, dVmin or C_Leq rests on it. Reading L-c, CLW = CL - 4.82 pF for every cell, is shown for comparison and decides nothing.

Groups outside the range of Eq. 3a. Under the CSB rule, the two smallest XE991 cells form a 5 pF bin with mean dVmin 0.80 V (file 57 has CL 5.93 pF and CLW 0.48 pF), and one cell of Fig. 3b has a negative CLWn. The paper's Fig. S4 fit includes only "Delta V min exp < 0.4 V", and its Discussion assumes "V 0.5 is constant and independent for Delta V min exp > 0.3 V (Fig. 3 a and Fig. S4)". The reading adopted here (y-1, an assumption) leaves bins or groups whose mean dVmin is not in (0, 0.4] V out of every Eq. 3a fit and every group built on the Fig. 3a and Fig. S4 data (fig3a-xe991-fit, fig3b-negative-clw-fit, mean-slope-half, fig4a-vh-rises-vd-constant, fig4d-cleq-two-limits and the claims on its groups, low-chloride-similar). It leaves out no bin of the Fig. 3a fit without XE991. With that bin, the XE991 fit has an adjusted R2 of -43, which the paper's 0.793 rules out, and the paper's smallest sensor area (51 nm2) rules out its C_Leq of 0.20 aF (21 nm2). All bins (y-0) and the cells within the range binned again (y-2) are shown as variants.

Checks of the description against the data:

  • 89 cells, as the description says; each HDF5 file matches one record of the MATLAB files on CL, cell number, turn, time after death and corrected V0.5. No capacitance trace is deposited twice. Thirteen original cell numbers are used twice, each time by cells from different dates (and mostly different researchers and colonies), so the original number is not a unique identifier.
  • Temperature 24 C and pipette pressure 0 kPa for every cell, as the Methods say. 144 mM chloride (140 mM CsCl + 2 mM MgCl2) in 86 cells and 10 mM (with 133 mM Cs glutamate) in 3, as the Methods say.
  • XE991 is stored as 60 uM for 20 pigmented cells. The MATLAB toxins field labels 15 of them XE991 and 5 yes. The Methods give 30 to 60 uM; no cell is stored with another concentration.
  • The stored QTm, alphaB and corrected V0.5 follow from the stored traces and means as the data set's analysis script computes them (differences printed above). The largest capacitance on the ramp lies within one ramp step (0.0031 V) of the stored raw V0.5 in 79 of 89 cells, at the f1 or the f2 trace (the script lets the user pick the peak on either), and within 0.0092 V in the rest, so the stored values and the traces share the voltage axis. This is consistent with the description; it does not show how the linear region was chosen, which the script leaves to the user. Five files pad their traces with NaN at the ends.
  • One cell (file 52) holds at +0.06 V before and after the ramp, where the stimulus description says -0.06 V.
  • The HDF5 I_mean of the I-V protocol has shape (N, 2) but stores its values in column order; read as given, the means and SDs are interleaved (a format error). It is reshaped here.
  • The stored surface area (83 cells) follows two formulas: 2 pi r l for 34 cells with a cell length, and 2 pi r LW + 2 pi r^2 (a closed cylinder on the lateral wall length) for 50 (one cell fits both). The description does not say so.
  • The stored surface area is used as AM for the stereocilia rule of Corbitt et al. (2012), which defines AM = 2 pi r (L + r) with L the lateral wall length. The 50 cells on the second formula match it exactly. The 34 cells on 2 pi r l match it only if the stored cell length l equals L + r, which the description does not say; the area recomputed as 2 pi r (L + r) from the lateral wall length gives the same root (5.100 against 5.104 pF, nlw-capacitance). The resulting CSB runs from 0.35 to 1.05 pF (43 cells at the 1.05 pF plateau).
  • Six cells have no stored area; their CSB is the median of their turn. Leaving them out moves the root from 5.104 to 5.137 pF.
  • The I-V protocol steps are in V (-0.13 to 0.09 V in 0.01 V steps for file 22) and the mean currents in pA; the chord conductance between neighbouring steps is about 37 nS in that cell, so the DC conductances of the data set are in the nS range.

cohort¶

The data set holds the paper's 89 OHCs, 39 from adult males (17 albino, 22 pigmented), 21 from prepubertal males (15 albino, 6 pigmented), 28 from prepubertal females (10 albino, 18 pigmented) and 1 pigmented cell from an adult female.

The HDF5 maturity is mature, Prepubertal or juvenile; the MATLAB files put the juvenile males in the prepubertal-male file, so juvenile is read as prepubertal.

nlw-capacitance¶

The capacitance of the non-lateral-wall region is about 4.82 pF, close to the 4.86 pF reported before for adult males, and it does not differ between phenotypes or between adult and juvenile animals.

QTm = gamma + beta ln(CL - CSB), root exp(-gamma/beta), CNLW = CSB + root. The data set stores neither CSB nor CNLW nor CLW, so readings (n-a) and (n-b) cannot be run as written. Run here are (n-c), CSB = 0, and the CSB of Corbitt et al. (2012) (the rule of reading L-b, see the header), on the stored area and on 2 pi r (L + r) recomputed from the diameter and lateral wall length; the Corbitt CSB is computed, not stored, and stands in for n-a as an assumption. Each is read as (n-ii) the root alone and (n-i) the mean per-cell CNLW, on all 89 cells and on the 86 with 144 mM chloride. Intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals (2000 resamples of cells, seed fixed; a standard choice).

def r2_origin(x, y):
    x, y = np.asarray(x, float), np.asarray(y, float)
    k = np.sum(x * y) / np.sum(x * x)
    sse = np.sum((y - k * x) ** 2)
    n = len(y)
    # Adjusted R2 with one coefficient: the residual has n - 1 degrees of freedom; the total sum of
    # squares has n - 1 about the mean (centred) and n about zero (uncentred, no mean estimated).
    cen = 1 - (sse / (n - 1)) / (np.sum((y - y.mean()) ** 2) / (n - 1))
    unc = 1 - (sse / (n - 1)) / (np.sum(y**2) / n)
    return k, cen, unc

def fig3a(s, col="CLW", width=5.0, lim="y-1"):
    """Eq. 3a and unconstrained fits on bins of `col`. lim y-1: bins with mean dVmin in (0, 0.4] V
    (ear1.in_range, the adopted reading); y-0: all bins; y-2: cells with dVmin in (0, 0.4] V, then binned."""
    if lim == "y-2":
        s = s[(s.dVmin > 0) & (s.dVmin <= ear1.DVMIN_MAX)]
    rows = []
    for e_, start in [("e-a", 0.0), ("e-b", s[col].min())]:
        b = ear1.bin_cells(s, col, width, start)
        if lim == "y-1":
            b = ear1.in_range(b)
        for w_ in ["w-a", "w-b"]:
            f = ear1.fit_eq3a(b.dVmin, b.V05, b.n, weighted_r2=(w_ == "w-a"))

def asym(cl, rd, th, clw="CLW"):
    s = cl[cl.ratio < th]
    dC = s[rd] - s.CL
    k = s[dC <= 5]
    dk = k[rd] - k.CL
    out = {"reading": rd, "fraction": th, "n": len(s), "mean dC": dC.mean(), "sd dC": dC.std(), "n dC<=5": len(k)}
    for reg in ["apical", "basal"]:
        g = k[k.region == reg]
        dd = g[rd] - g.CL
        out[f"n {reg}"] = len(g)
        out[f"dC {reg}"] = dd.mean()
        out[f"m-a {reg}"] = (dd / g[clw]).mean()
        out[f"m-b {reg}"] = dd.mean() / g[clw].mean()
    return out

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The per-cell CL, QTm, V0.5 and alphaB are the values the data set stores, which equal the Fig. S1 legend for its example cell. CLW follows the amended shared method of the claims file: CL - CNLW with CNLW = CSB + root per cell (reading L-b). The data set stores no CSB, so the stereocilia capacitance is computed by the rule of Corbitt et al. (2012), the paper the method cites, on the stored cell surface area, with the 6 cells without an area given the median CSB of their turn; this rule is an assumption, and every outcome on CLW, dVmin or C_Leq rests on it. It gives the Fig. S1 cell CLW 18.30 pF (legend 18.56 pF) and a root of 5.10 pF. Under L-b, against CL - 4.82 pF (L-c, shown for comparison), the Eq. 3a fit without XE991, the Eq. 6b R2 of Fig. 1 and the Fig. S4 fit without XE991 come within tolerance (the R2 of the Fig. S4 fit with XE991 does not), while the band of Fig. 1 fails. The CLW of the smallest cells shrinks (file 57: 0.48 pF), so the two smallest XE991 cells form a bin of mean dVmin 0.80 V, and one Fig. 3b cell has a negative CLWn. Bins and groups with a mean dVmin outside (0, 0.4] V are left out of the Eq. 3a fits and the groups built on them (an assumption from the Fig. S4 legend and the Discussion; all bins are shown as a variant). This makes the XE991 fit and Table 3 pass and the low-chloride cells pass in part, and puts the lower C_Leq limit 0.0006 aF outside its tolerance. CLn is not stored. Its rule (the two most negative ramp points, a plateau fraction chosen to give the paper's 62 cells) is an assumption. Group counts differ slightly from the paper's: 27 against 28 pigmented cells without XE991, 17 against 15 pigmented XE991 cells with 144 mM chloride (Table 1), and 69 against 70 cells in Fig. 2.

What was found about the data set. It holds all 89 cells, with the paper's group counts, and the derived values the paper reports. It does not hold CLW, CLn, CSB or the selection of cells for each table. The HDF5 I_mean array stores its values in column order under a row-major shape. The stored surface area follows two formulas. One cell holds at +0.06 V where the description says -0.06 V. The MATLAB toxins field labels 5 of the 20 XE991 cells yes. Original cell numbers are not unique (13 are used twice).
