Siegle, Jia et al. (2021): the passive viewing survey, from the paper's DANDI deposits¶

Paper. Siegle JH, Jia X, Durand S, et al. (2021). Survey of spiking in the mouse visual system reveals functional hierarchy. Nature 592, 86-92. doi:10.1038/s41586-020-03171-x. The claims file was written from the NIH author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC10399640): main text, Methods, the legends of Figures 1 to 4 and Extended Data Figures 1 to 10, and the availability statements.

Target dandiset. DANDI:000253, version 0.240923.1441, the most recent published version (the paper cites no version of it; it predates the dandiset). Its title and description are those of the OpenScope Global/Local Oddball project; it names this paper as IsSupplementTo. The first section shows that it holds a different experiment, so every claim the claims file marks elsewhere is tested on the archives the paper names for its data.

Where the paper's data are. The Data availability statement deposits the 58 passive viewing experiments on DANDI as dandisets 000021 and 000022. Pinned here: 000021 version 0.251116.2246 (Brain Observatory 1.1 stimulus set) and 000022 version 0.251116.2247 (Functional Connectivity stimulus set). The paper cites no version; these are the only published versions of each (the other is the draft), so they are pinned. The per-unit tables and the behaviour metrics tables of the paper's GitHub repository (AllenInstitute/neuropixels_platform_paper at commit 4f8bcc26) are read for the claims that the claims file says rest on them, and for the link claim.

This notebook tests the claims that do not need cross-correlograms. The CCG claims (Figure 2 and Extended Data Figures 7, 8 and 10c to e) are in ccg_siegle_2021.ipynb.

Checks of the description against the values. The 58 sessions split 32 and 26 between the Brain Observatory 1.1 and Functional Connectivity sets, one session per mouse, as the Methods give. Every session has 150 full-field flashes (75 dark, 75 light) of 250 ms every 2 s, and a Gabor block of 81 locations with 250 ms presentations, as the Methods describe; the Functional Connectivity sessions have the drifting gratings of 4 orientations at 2 Hz with 75 repeats per contrast. Times are in seconds and spike times share the clock of the stimulus tables: the receptive field test finds significant fields in most visual area units and almost none in the hippocampus, and flash latencies cluster in tens of ms (below), which a clock offset would not give. The electrodes table gives the CCF position in x, y and z, but y and z are equal on every channel of every session, so one of the three coordinates (the left-right one, z) is not the CCF value; x (anterior-posterior) is used below only to say whether a probe was mapped.

The timescale table was written by Figure3/timescale_calculation.py (response decay timescale over 40 to 290 ms after onset), whose procedure is recomputed. So the latency table's procedure is not known, and it is compared with every window computed here.

Figure 3: unit selection and the four metrics¶

The selection of Figure3.py: per area, p_value_rf < 0.01, area_rf < 2500, snr > 1, firing_rate_dg > 0.1 Hz; then flash time to first spike < 100 ms for all four metrics; then, for the response decay timescale, 1 < timescale < 300 ms, more than 50 spikes and SD of the estimate < 20. Area means are over units (log10 for the modulation index), and Pearson's r is against the eight hierarchy scores. Pairs of areas: two-sided rank-sum with 1e-5 added to each p, Benjamini-Hochberg over the 28 pairs at 0.05, as Figure3.py.

def pairs_test(values, sel, f=lambda v: v):
    vals = {a: f(values[sel & (U3.location == a)].dropna().values) for a in S.AREAS}
    rows = []
    for i, a1 in enumerate(S.AREAS):
        for a2 in S.AREAS[i + 1:]:
            z, p = stats.ranksums(vals[a1], vals[a2])
            rows.append({"pair": f"{S.NAMES[a1]}-{S.NAMES[a2]}", "p": p + 1e-5})
    t = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    t["reject"], t["p_bh"] = multipletests(t.p.values, alpha=0.05, method="fdr_bh")[:2]
    return t


def judge_pairs(t, expected_ns):
    ns = set(t.pair[~t.reject])
    exp = {p if p in set(t.pair) else "-".join(p.split("-")[::-1]) for p in expected_ns}
    disagree = (ns - exp) | (exp - ns)
    return sorted(ns), sorted(disagree)

def layer_test(D, sup, deep, tfs="tfs_all_250", ts=("timescale_ac", "err_ac", "spike_count_ac"), mi=np.log10):
    sel = (D.p_value_rf < 0.01) & (D.cortical_depth > 0) & (D.area_rf < 2500) & (D.snr > 1) & (D.firing_rate_dg > 0.1)
    out = []
    specs = [("time to first spike (ms)", tfs, lambda v: v * 1000, "deep-sup"),
             ("RF area (deg2)", "area_rf", lambda v: v, "deep-sup"),
             ("log10 MI" if mi is np.log10 else "MI", "mod_idx_dg", mi, "sup-deep"),
             ("response decay timescale (ms)", ts[0], lambda v: v, "deep-sup")]
    for k, (name, col, f, sign) in enumerate(specs):
        if k == 0:
            sel = sel & (D[tfs] < 0.1)
        if k == 3:
            sel = sel & (D[ts[0]] < 300) & (D[ts[0]] > 1) & (D[ts[2]] > 50) & (D[ts[1]] < 20)
        a = f(D[col][sel & D.cortical_layer.isin(sup)].values)
        b = f(D[col][sel & D.cortical_layer.isin(deep)].values)
        a, b = a[np.isfinite(a)], b[np.isfinite(b)]
        diff = (b.mean() - a.mean()) if sign == "deep-sup" else (a.mean() - b.mean())
        out.append({"metric": name, "difference": diff, "as": sign, "p": stats.ranksums(a, b)[1],
                    "n_sup": len(a), "n_deep": len(b), "median_diff": (np.median(b) - np.median(a)) if sign == "deep-sup" else (np.median(a) - np.median(b))})
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper took the receptive field, modulation index and drifting grating rate of each unit from the AllenSDK's precomputed metrics, which are not in the NWB files; they are recomputed here from the spike times as the Methods describe them. The preferred grating condition (the stimulus condition with the highest mean count) and firing_rate_dg (spikes over all grating presentations divided by their duration) are assumptions; firing_rate_dg enters only as the > 0.1 Hz cut, which removes 560 of 25,019 units (fig3-unit-counts). Latencies and response decay timescales follow the Methods' windows (250 ms; 0 to 250 ms); the scripts' windows (200 ms in get_flash_PSTH.py; 40 to 290 ms in timescale_calculation.py) are shown as variants, and they change the unit counts, the layer differences and some pairwise tests. The paper's latency table matches none of the procedures tried (at most 16% of units within 1 ms), and no script of the repository writes it. A unit needs at least 10 spikes over the Gabor presentations for a receptive field p-value, as in the AllenSDK.

What was found about the data. Dandiset 000253 is an OpenScope oddball experiment and holds none of the paper's recordings, though its metadata names the paper as IsSupplementTo. The paper's data are in 000021 and 000022, which match the paper's counts of sessions, genotypes and probes. In every session the electrodes' y and z columns are identical, so the left-right CCF coordinate is lost. The units tables carry no stimulus metrics (p_value_rf, area_rf, mod_idx_dg), which the paper's figures use. The behaviour experiments of Figure 4 are not deposited as recordings; only the paper's per-unit and per-session metric tables are public.
