def decode(X, y):
    ok = 0
    for i in range(len(y)):
        m = np.ones(len(y), bool); m[i] = False
        cs = np.unique(y[m])
        cen = np.array([X[m & (y == c)].mean(0) for c in cs])
        ok += cs[np.argmin(((cen - X[i]) ** 2).sum(1))] == y[i]
    return ok / len(y)

Trial selection and condition means¶

Every analysis below starts from condition means under a reading of the trial selection: the movement onset (a, the dataset's; b, from hand speed) and the proto-trial rule (Supplemental Experimental Procedures, Task design: "the subset of trials (typically ~90% of all trials) where the horizontal and vertical velocity profiles correlated with those of a reference 'proto-trial' with r > 0.9"), proto (a) the condition mean or (b) a representative trial (the medoid), and the profiles compared (a) separately or (b) concatenated. Only trials with delays above 400 ms are kept (Methods, Task design). The main reading is onset (a), proto (a) the condition mean, and the profiles concatenated (b): comparing the profiles separately keeps 70 to 82% of trials, against the paper's "typically ~90%", while concatenating them keeps 93 to 96% (table below). The condition mean over the medoid is an assumption (a template is usually a mean); both are run.

def corr_trace(prep, rate):
    """prep (c, n), rate (c, n, T) -> per-neuron correlation at each time (n, T)."""
    a = prep - prep.mean(0)
    b = rate - rate.mean(0)
    num = (a[:, :, None] * b).sum(0)
    den = np.sqrt((a ** 2).sum(0))[:, None] * np.sqrt((b ** 2).sum(0))
    return num / den

rng8 = np.random.default_rng(8)
eps = 2 * np.pi / 360
W = np.array([[1, -eps], [eps, 1]])
def simulate(x0, steps):
    out = np.empty((len(x0), steps, 2)); x = x0.copy()
    for t in range(steps):
        x = x @ W.T; out[:, t] = x
    return out

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper's J-array dataset has 108 conditions and about 2166 trials per neuron; this dandiset has 27 conditions and 6 to 10 kept trials per condition, so condition means are noisy (split-half reliability of preparatory tuning, median r = 0.374) and every PD performance is low (best 0.117 with S fixed, 0.226 recomputed). The tuning strength is read as the range across conditions; the SD reading leaves almost no neuron above 5 spikes/s. The proto-trial is the condition-mean velocity profile with the two profiles concatenated (the reading closest to the paper's ~90% of trials kept); the other readings are shown. S is fixed across folds where the two readings differ. AP location is read as the array, and the array from the unit id. EMG, the speed-task and single-electrode datasets are not in the dandiset. With 6 to 10 trials per condition the 5 spikes/s threshold passes a median of 59 of 152 units on shuffled labels (75 observed); only 43 of the 76 PD neurons have tuning distinguishable from noise, and on them the Figure 6 results are stronger.

What was found about the dataset. The units' electrode references all point to PMd rows of the electrode table (whose location column puts M1 on ids 97 to 192), although the unit ids give 77 units on a second array whose references (rows 14 to 94) look like M1 channels without the offset of 96. The electrode table has no positions. The test file's 100 trials have no condition labels or go cue times.
