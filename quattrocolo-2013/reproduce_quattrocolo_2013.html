Reproducing Quattrocolo & Maccaferri (2013) from CRCNS hc-9¶

Paper. Quattrocolo G, Maccaferri G (2013). Novel GABAergic circuits mediating excitation/inhibition of Cajal-Retzius cells in the developing hippocampus. J Neurosci 33(13):5486-5498. doi:10.1523/jneurosci.5680-12.2013. The claims file (claims/crcns-hc-9/) was written from the version of record, which has no supplement and links no code.

Data set. CRCNS hc-9, "Firing pattern of O-LM cells in mouse hippocampal CA1" (doi:10.6080/K0HQ3WTH), read from the public bucket at https://crcnsarchive.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/hc-9/live/. CRCNS data sets are not versioned, so the version is recorded as unversioned. The data are pinned by MD5: the one data file, Maccaferri_data.zip, has MD5 4e3175055681b99ad5be06ba542a93b8, as the data set's checksums.md5 gives it, and the data check below confirms the MD5 of the zip as read. This is the only published copy of the data (the bucket's hc-9/old/ folder holds only earlier documentation), so there is no version to choose between; the paper cites no data set. The description read is version 0.7 (Dec 2, 2016) of live/docs/crcns_hc-9_data_description.pdf.

What the data set holds. Current-clamp responses of three O-LM interneurons to 1 s current steps (15 sweeps each), the firing patterns of the paper's Figure 7. None of the recordings from Cajal-Retzius cells behind the paper's main findings (Figures 1 to 6 and 8 to 12) are in it, and the light-evoked responses of Figure 7C and 7D are not either. The zip is opened in place over range requests; nothing is written to disk.

The voltage must share the clock of the current channel. Two checks: the delay from the step onset in the current channel to the first departure of the voltage from its baseline, and the voltage change in the step window against the step level, with the window as the current channel gives it and shifted in time by 1.1 s (after the step).

def window_change(c, shift):
    on, off = hc9.step_window(c)
    t, v = c["t"], c["v"]
    a, b = on + shift, min(off + shift, t[-1])
    w = (t >= a + 0.2 * (b - a)) & (t < b)
    pre = t < on
    # median over the window, so spikes on depolarizing steps do not dominate
    return np.median(v[w], axis=0) - v[pre].mean(axis=0)

fig7a-olm-sag¶

O-LM interneurons recorded in CXCR4-EGFP slices show a sag in their voltage response to hyperpolarizing current pulses (pulses of -100 and +50 pA in Figure 7A), and the ChR2-expressing O-LM cell of Figure 7D has a similar firing pattern.

Criterion (claims file): the -100 pA step, judged under two readings. (a) The relaxation from the peak hyperpolarization during the step to the mean voltage over its last 100 ms is larger than twice the SD of the pre-step baseline. (b) The sag ratio (V_end - V_min) / (V_baseline - V_min) is at least 0.1. Every cell, the Figure 7D cell included, is judged alike. Reproduced when every cell shows a sag, partially when most do. The parameters are in hc9.py. Two details are standard choices: the pre-step baseline is all of the sweep before the step (0 to 75 ms), and V_min is the minimum of a 1 ms running mean during the step (the raw minimum is a variant; the noise checks below show why it is not the main measure).

How large a "sag" the measures find where there is none. Two checks of reading (a)'s false-positive level, with V_min from the raw trace and from the 1 ms running mean. First, each cell's 0 pA sweep, measured in the same step window: no current is injected, so any "sag" is noise. Second, a synthetic trace with no sag: a 40 mV exponential hyperpolarization (time constant 20 ms) in the same window, plus Gaussian noise of 0.2 mV SD, 100 draws from a fixed seed. The 0 pA sweep also gives a reference for the -100 pA sag in mV: the same measure with no current injected, and the SD of the voltage over the 1 s step window, which includes slow fluctuations that the 75 ms pre-step baseline does not. For reading (b) the 0 pA "sag" is also divided by the -100 pA hyperpolarization of the same cell (V_base minus V_min): this is the part of the -100 pA sag ratio that noise of the 0 pA size could make. The sag ratio of the 0 pA sweep itself is printed too, but its denominator, the 0 pA sweep's deflection below baseline, is noise as well, so it does not measure a false-positive level of reading (b).

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper shows the sag only in example traces and defines no measure; the two readings of the claims file are used, and both agree. V_min is taken from a 1 ms running mean, a standard choice. The 2 SD threshold of reading (a) is also passed by each cell's 0 pA sweep, so that reading alone does not separate a sag from noise in these recordings. On the 0 pA sweeps the noise "sag" is 0.038 to 0.061 of the -100 pA hyperpolarization, below reading (b)'s 0.1, and the -100 pA sags are 4.5 to 7.3 times the 0 pA value; on these references the outcome is the same. Which data set cell is in which panel of Figure 7 comes from the superseded description in the bucket's hc-9/old/ folder, not from the paper or the files; the criterion judges every cell alike, so the outcome does not depend on it.

What was found about the data set:

  • The data set holds only the current-step recordings of the three O-LM cells of Figure 7. None of the Cajal-Retzius recordings behind the paper's main findings are in it, nor the light-evoked responses of Figure 7C and 7D.
  • The description gives the current steps as -100:30:+250 pA; the files hold 15 steps from -100 to +250 pA in 25 pA increments.
  • The description says the injection usually starts about half a second into the sweep; in these files it starts at 75 ms and lasts 1 s.
  • The current description does not say which animal, age or mouse line each cell comes from, or which panel of Figure 7 shows it. The superseded description in hc-9/old/ does (P13 and P14 CXCR4-EGFP, P15 Sst-IRES-Cre;ChR2(H134R)-EYFP), and it matches the Figure 7 legends. That old description calls the files ABF; they are ATF text.
  • The zip's MD5 matches checksums.md5; no recording is deposited twice; the voltage and current channels share one clock.
