Clock check¶

The stimulus series and the responses carry different stated rates (25 kHz and 1 Hz), so the shared clock is checked on the data. The presynaptic spike peaks about 2 ms after the pulse onset in the same sample index (above). As a contrast, for every tested pair the averaged postsynaptic deflection in 1 to 15 ms after the pulse (against the 49 ms before it) is compared with the same measure taken 35 ms earlier, where there is no pulse. If the responses are locked to the pulse, the pairs the code annotates as connected stand out at the pulse and not in the shifted window.

def window_snr(avg, t0_ms):
    x = mp.lowpass(avg.astype(float))
    z = -mp._ms(mp.KEEP_MS[0]) + mp._ms(t0_ms)
    b = x[z - mp._ms(20): z - mp._ms(1)]
    w = x[z + mp._ms(1): z + mp._ms(15)] - b.mean()
    return np.abs(w).max() / b.std()

A failure to reproduce here may be a failure of this attempt, not of the paper: see the reasoning and the differences from the paper's method.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper gives no rule for calling a connection, so connections are the annotations of the paper's code table, not a detection from the traces. Cell types are the table's. The Na+ current peak is taken after a 1 kHz low-pass, a choice that reproduces the code's per-patch values. All sweeps of a file are averaged; the code plots round 2 of each .dat file, which the NWB files do not separate.

What was found about the dataset. The files carry no cell types, connection labels or soma positions, so Fig. 5 cannot be tested from the dandiset alone. The response series state a rate of 1 Hz, the stimulus rate is given with the unit "millisecond", the stimulus amplitude is 2 "amperes" (2 nA in the Methods), every stimulus series names Elec_8 as its electrode while the cell that fires is another in most files, and every file has the session start time 2018-01-01. The code's table, the only source of labels, names no file for 10 of its presynaptic blocks (8 match a file of the dandiset by the firing channel, 2 have none), counts fewer cells (298) and more pairs (1,403 linked, 1,416 in all) than the paper (377 and 1,292), and annotates 26 connections, not 31; per class its pair counts differ from the paper's by more than 10% in 6 of 8 classes. Of the other code tables read for claims on data elsewhere, several hold different numbers of cells or items than the paper states (23 eEPSP pairs against 26, 120 and 500 AIS values against 108 and 430, 14 and 15 VEN subtypes against 10 and 11, and more cells per type in the peak-index table than the legend states).
