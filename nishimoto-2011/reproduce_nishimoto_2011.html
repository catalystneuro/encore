Nishimoto and Gallant (2011) on CRCNS mt-2¶

Paper: Nishimoto S, Gallant JL (2011), "A Three-Dimensional Spatiotemporal Receptive Field Model Explains Responses of Area MT Neurons to Naturalistic Movies", J Neurosci 31(41):14551-14564, doi:10.1523/jneurosci.6801-10.2011.

Dataset: CRCNS mt-2, "Extracellular recordings from area MT of awake macaques in response to naturalistic movies", doi:10.6080/K0DN4374, read from the public bucket crcnsarchive/mt-2/live/. CRCNS data sets are not versioned; the version is recorded as unversioned and the next cell prints the MD5 sums of checksums.md5 for the 45 files read, so a later change can be detected. The live folder is the only copy in the bucket (there is no old folder), so it is the version pinned.

Claims: claims/nishimoto-2011.yaml, written from the version of record (main text, Methods and legends; the paper has no supplement beyond a movie of stimulus spectra and links no code).

The model cannot be fitted here at the paper's size (about 6000 filters at 11 delays, boosted five times, for 12 models and 45 neurons) within the time limit. The filter bank follows the paper's rules (about 7000 complex and 1100 simple channels), and the regression weights are constrained to be pooled over space onto 763 regressors (see mt2.py). To measure what this costs, the main model (compressive and divisive) is also fitted for every neuron with a finer pooling (1465 regressors), and for three neurons with a finer one still (3085 regressors, with every simple channel of the bank), in the second notebook, finer_pooling.ipynb. Every difference from the paper is listed in the summary and in the attempt record.

Fitting every neuron¶

Each neuron's file is streamed, its frames reduced to 32 x 32 pixels, the filter bank applied with and without luminance and contrast normalization, and the 12 models of Figure 2 fitted by boosting with early stopping (mt2.process_neuron). For the compressive and divisive model it also fits the readings the claims file names (delays 1 to 10, folds as random partitions, sigma chosen on held-out data, the 12 ms Gaussian as a full width), early stopping with a patience of 500 steps, the complex-only and simple-only models, the null model neurons of Figure 6 and the simulated neurons of Figure 8, and drives the fitted model with gratings and plaids. Two neurons run at a time.

Null model neurons (fig6a-null-on-plane): 150 neurons, the k-th on the movies of neuron k mod 45 (movie reading (a)), each under the four combinations of the rate reading (rectified (a), offset (b)) and the delay reading (all delays (a), one delay (b)); and 150 more on the movies of one neuron, ct0001 (movie reading (b)), with readings (a) and (a) only, for time.

How the analysis differs from the paper's:

  • The regression weights are constrained to be pooled over space (763 regressors at 11 delays), where the paper fits each of its 5956 filters; the filter bank itself follows the paper's rules (about 7000 complex and 1100 simple channels). Simple cells enter the regression only at 0.75 cycles per cRF and SF 0. A finer pooling of the main model (1465 regressors, finer_pooling.ipynb) raises the mean correlation from 0.448 to 0.518. Four of the Figure 2B models refitted with it (finer_pooling_fig2b.ipynb) give the same outcomes for fig2b-compressive-improves, fig2b-divisive-improves, fig2b-compressive-divisive-best and fig2b-no-interaction; the expansive models and the spectral analyses here rest on the 2 x 2 pooling only.
  • The paper does not give the temporal envelope of its Gabor filters. Here it spans 11 frames (SD 2 frames), centred on the frame, and the 11-frame delay window is moved 0, 3 or 6 frames earlier per neuron, chosen on held-out training data (0 for 31 neurons).
  • Five temporal frequencies (0 to 30 Hz) and five spatial frequencies (0 to 6 cycles per cRF), an octave apart; sigma of divisive normalization 1% of the mean pooled response (and chosen on held-out data as a reading); boosting step 1% of the response SD; early stopping after 50 steps without improvement.
  • 45 neurons where the paper has 52, and 14 with a natural-movie validation set where it has 15.
  • The simulated pattern indices are taken from the rectified model responses, which the paper does not specify.
  • Plaid and grating parameters, the plane search grid and the quadrant geometry of the horizontal-vertical ratio are this attempt's choices; the paper gives none.

What was found about the dataset:

  • 45 of the paper's 52 neurons (34 from monkey f, 11 from monkey v); no recording appears twice.
  • The files hold only responses averaged over repeats, so the explainable variance cannot be computed.
  • 14 files have an undocumented ranges.nrange, the first 2000 frames of a 4000-frame vrange; its frames carry less motion than the rest of vrange in all 14 (0.225 against 0.325, median frame difference over contrast), consistent with the natural movies of the paper's control. ranges.trange is empty in every file and not described.
  • The per-frame denominators of the averaged validation responses imply 10 to 33 repeats, where the paper reports 5 to 20; training frames carry denominators up to 5.
  • Training frames run from 17,908 to 40,000 per neuron (mean 26,894; the paper gives 20,000 to 40,000, mean 27,120). In ct0133, 12,729 of 19,842 training frames have no response.
