Reproducing Dehaqani, Michelon et al. (2024) from dandiset 000931¶

Paper. Dehaqani AA, Michelon F, Patella P, Petrucco L, Piasini E, Iurilli G (2024). A mechanosensory feedback that uncouples external and self-generated sensory responses in the olfactory cortex. Cell Reports 43, 114013. doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2024.114013.

Text. The claims file (claims/000931/10.1016-j.celrep.2024.114013.yaml) was written from the version of record (main text, figure legends, STAR Methods and the legends of Figures S1 to S6), and checked by a second reader. This attempt read the same text.

Dandiset. DANDI:000931, version 0.261011.1559 (doi:10.48324/dandi.000931/0.261011.1559). The paper cites no version of the dandiset; its data statement says processed data "will be shared by the lead contact upon request". The protocol then pins the most recent published version. All three published versions and the draft hold the same single asset (same asset id and SHA-256, printed below); only the most recent published version and the draft name the paper in their metadata. So the choice of version does not change the data.

What the dandiset holds. One NWB file, sub-001/sub-001.nwb: the spike times of 1013 sorted units from one mouse, recorded on 2021-10-27, with the keyword "Concentration Series". The paper recorded 22 mice in six groups (STAR Methods, Mice: 4 + 2 + 4 + 3 + 4 + 5). The file has no breathing signal or inhalation times, no odor trials, no artificial airflow pulses, no unit waveforms and no brain areas. Every claim needs at least one of these (printed below, per claim), so no claim can be tested.

Claims.

Claim Outcome
All 37 claims of the claims file (inhalation clustering, phase and speed decoding, odor responses and their dependence on inhalation speed, GLM and linear models, airflow tuning in anesthetized, awake, neurectomized and bulbectomized mice, motor cortex, population decoding of concentration, the simulation, and the waveform control) Blocked: data missing

This notebook lists what the dandiset and its file hold, checks the unit table against its description and the paper's spike-sorting criteria, and shows, per claim, which data it needs and which are missing.

The file holds only general metadata and the Units table. acquisition, analysis, processing, stimulus/presentation and stimulus/templates are empty; there is no intervals group (no trials or epochs) and no electrodes table. The units table has the 13 columns printed, none of them a waveform, a location or an event time. No dataset is stored anywhere else in the file, so no inhalation times, odor trials or pulses are held in a column or table outside the groups mechano.streams_present looks at. Besides the NWB core and hdmf namespaces, /specifications caches one extension, ndx-dandi-icephys, which defines only two metadata types (DandiIcephysMetadata, CreSubject) and is not used by any object in the file. The Methods describe four signals recorded besides the spikes, all at 30 kHz: the nasal airflow from a flow sensor, the odor signal from a photo-ionization detector, a TTL at each odor trial and a TTL at each detected inhalation onset (STAR Methods, Experimental signals). None is in the file.

The keyword "Concentration Series" places this mouse in the group of four mice given alpha-pinene and eucalyptol at three concentrations (STAR Methods, Mice), as far as a keyword can; the file does not say which odors were given, and the subject age (P56D) is within the paper's 6 to 9 weeks. The file's related_publications names the bioRxiv preprint (10.1101/2023.04.04.535405), not the version of record. The session description is the converter's default ("Auto-generated by neuroconv").

Each claim's needs are read from its criterion in the claims file and the STAR Methods sections it cites (the docstring of mechano.py says so). No claim can be tested: 33 need the breathing signal or inhalation times, 31 need odor trial times, 2 need the artificial airflow pulses of the tracheostomized mice, and 4 need unit waveforms. The claims on odorless inhalations in the ten control mice (Figures 3F and 4) count as needing odor trial times: the Methods define odorless inhalations as "those occurring within the -13 to -4 seconds window before the delivery of an odor" (Analysis of sniff-by-sniff responses), and the ten control mice are the mice of the odor experiments (STAR Methods, Mice: four concentration-series, two four-odor and four odor-identity mice), so odor onsets are needed to find those inhalations. The Methods do not say whether the neurectomized mice were given odors, so figs3-aen-neurectomy is not counted as needing them; it is blocked by the missing breathing signal and mice either way. Beyond that, every claim pools mice of one or more groups (the four concentration-series mice, the six of Figure 1, the ten control, four bulbectomized, five neurectomized or three anesthetized mice), and most select PCx layer 2 and 3 units, for which the file has no brain area. The spikes of one mouse alone cannot stand in for any of them, since the paper's every measure on spikes is aligned to an inhalation, an odor onset or an airflow pulse. No outside source holds these data publicly: the paper says the processed data are available on request, and its code deposit (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.10381293, read by the reader at commit 3bec502) loads preprocessed .mat files it does not contain.

The check of claim ids against the claims file reads it from the registry (../../claims/...); where that file is not beside the notebook, the cell prints the 37 ids in mechano.CLAIM_NEEDS for a comparison by hand.

With no trial table or event times in the file, there is no second stream whose clock could be checked against the spikes (protocol, Author, step 2); that check could not be made.

The Methods say the probe was lowered to "a region with high firing rates and breathing-coupled activity" (Stereotaxic probe insertion). Without a breathing signal this cannot be shown, but the summed activity of the 97 good units with depth at most 1000 has its spectral peak between 1 and 15 Hz at 2.78 Hz, 9.7 times the 15 to 40 Hz baseline, a frequency within the range of resting breathing in mice. By the channel map assumed above, these units are the ones nearest the probe tip, where the PCx would be; the file does not confirm the probe or its map. The largest power in a band can lie on a harmonic of the rhythm: on a regular synthetic 3 Hz train it lies at 6.01 Hz. mechano.spectral_peak moves the peak to its subharmonic when the power there is at least half the peak power, which gives 2.98 Hz on that train and leaves a 3 Hz Poisson train at 2.98 Hz. For the 0 to 1000 band the largest power and the checked peak agree (2.78 Hz). The bands further from the tip peak near 1 Hz (1.03 Hz at 1000 to 2000 and 3000 to 4000) or have a weaker peak (2000 to 3000: largest power at 2.93 Hz, checked peak 1.51 Hz, 2.9 times baseline). This is consistent with these units being in a breathing-coupled region such as the PCx, and does not show that they are.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. No analysis of the paper was run. The notebook checks the file against its description and against the spike-sorting criteria of the Methods.

What was found about the dataset.

  • The dandiset holds one NWB file from one of the paper's 22 mice: spike times of 1013 units and their Kilosort and Phy labels. The breathing signal, the inhalation and odor-trial TTLs, the odor signal, the artificial airflow pulses, unit waveforms and brain areas are not in it, and neither are the other 21 mice. None of the paper's findings can be tested from it.
  • The file does not say which experiment it belongs to except through the keyword "Concentration Series", which odors were given, or which units the paper analysed.
  • KSLabel is "good" and ContamPct is 100 for every unit, which contradict each other; quality is missing for 644 of 1013 units. The unit table's columns other than quality, spike_times and unit_name have no description, and depth, Amplitude and amp no unit.
  • The file's related_publications names the preprint, not the published paper; the two 2024 versions of the dandiset do not name the paper.
  • What could be checked is consistent with the paper: the good units' ISI violation rates and presence ratios fall in the ranges the Methods report, no unit is duplicated, and the summed activity of the good units nearest the probe tip (by the Neuropixels 1.0 channel map, which is assumed, as the file names no probe) has its spectral peak at 2.78 Hz, within the range of mouse breathing; the subharmonic check leaves that peak at 2.78 Hz.
