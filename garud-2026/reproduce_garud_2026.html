Reproducing Garud & Morris (2026) on DANDI:001552 and the Krausz et al. (2023) table¶

Paper. Garud S, Morris L (2026). Continuous Temporal Difference Learning as a Unifying Theory of Dopamine Function. bioRxiv preprint, doi:10.64898/2026.04.07.716149, version posted April 8, 2026. The claims file was written from this preprint's text (main text, legends and Methods); the supplement was not available.

Dataset. DANDI:001552, version 0.250828.1728 (Floeder et al. 2025), streamed from the archive. This is the version the paper's Data Availability cites, so it is pinned. A later version, 0.250903.1802, holds the same assets (same paths, asset ids and sizes; data check), so the choice does not change the analysis.

Outside data. The paper's Figures 2, 3 and 5 use the Krausz et al. (2023) hex-level table, which the paper cites at Mendeley Data doi:10.17632/m59zdjpm9h.1. Its file hexLevelDf.csv.zip is read into memory and checked against its SHA-256. These claims are data: elsewhere and are counted apart from the dandiset.

Random draws. No analysis here uses random numbers, so no seed is set.

The stated rate is wrong in part of the JF-FP files: 64 of them state 120481.9 Hz (or timestamps 8.3 microseconds apart), and the TTL2 channel gives 120.29 to 120.53 Hz (mean 120.41), a factor of about 1000. The other files state 130 Hz, which the TTL2 channel confirms. Every photometry series states a start of 0 (at most 8 microseconds), and the event log has its own zero. The only link between them is photometry_ttl2, described in every file only as "raw photometry channel ttl2". Each TTL2 rising edge falls on a cue onset of the event log, within 0.1 s once one outlying pair is left out in 6 sessions, so the photometry can be placed on the event clock; on it, the photometry starts 3.6 to 251 s before the event log's zero.

The extreme ΔF/F values come from short stretches where both channels fall near zero (listed above), where the fitted 405 signal is near zero too; they are kept, as the paper names no exclusion, and they touch few 1 s bins.

With the fitted clock, the reward response of long-ITI sessions is larger at the true reward times than at shifted ones, which supports the alignment. In short-ITI sessions the shifted windows land on other trial events, so that contrast cannot tell; the cue-locked trace does. Two recordings are deposited twice: in JF-dLight-ramp2-F2 the Day3 and Day4 files, and in JF-dLight-ramp2-F4 the Day5 and Day6 files, hold identical photometry, cues and licks. Each is counted once, at its first day, and the second file is left out (sessions are then numbered without it). Other files share a cue schedule with another mouse's session but hold different photometry and licks; these are a reused trial schedule, not duplicates.

fig4b-long-iti-dip-below-baseline¶

During the late inter-trial interval of long-ITI sessions, dopamine settles below baseline, while in short-ITI sessions a slowly decaying reward response does not resolve before the next trial.

Eight readings: correction (fit, own) x ITI reference (reward, cue) x observation (trials, samples). The long-ITI test is one-sample, one-tailed, across mice of each mouse's mean tonic measure; the short-ITI part is the last kept bin before the next cue. Of the ITI references, the paper best supports the one from reward: the median time from reward to the next cue is 7.6 s in short and 44.7 s in long sessions (data check), the Results' ~8 s and the Methods' ~45 s, while from cue to cue the long value is 53.7 s. The reward reading is adopted where one is needed; all are shown.

Baseline is ΔF/F of zero (criterion). Both corrections regress the 405 nm signal over the whole session, so the mean ΔF/F of a session is close to zero by construction (printed below), and stretches without transients come out below zero. A late-ITI value below zero is therefore partly set by this centering; the test cannot tell a tonic dip from it. Two comparisons outside the criterion are printed for this: the late-ITI level against the level just before the cue (a pre-cue baseline), and the short-ITI last bin against the long-ITI late-ITI level.

fig5bc-gaussian-ct-td-best¶

Of six models of approach dopamine, the Gaussian continuous-time TD model fits best summed across rats and wins in 8 of 10 rats by BIC.

The Methods fit each model by Nelder-Mead from many starts. Here the linear parameters (A, B, c) are solved by least squares for each value of the spatial scale, and the scale is found by a grid and a bounded refinement. As Nelder-Mead is unconstrained, λ is searched from -5 to 5 per hex (negative λ is a profile that rises with distance) and σ from 0.3 to 10,000 hexes (flat over the maze). A Nelder-Mead search over all parameters is then run from each optimum to check that it finds nothing lower. The first round's narrower search (λ from 1e-4 to 5, σ from 0.3 to 100) is shown beside it.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The photometry is placed on the event clock by a fit of TTL2 edges to cue onsets, as the files' stated rates are wrong in part of the sessions. Where the paper leaves a step open (the ΔF/F correction, where the ITI is measured from, what an observation is in the 20-observation rule, session numbering, how r is computed, which rows form "non-approach" observations, the minimum count per position in the value recovery, and which rows give the stationary estimates of the value recovery), every reading is run and shown; for Figure 4B the ITI from reward is adopted, as its medians (7.6 and 44.7 s) match the paper's ~8 and ~45 s. Two recordings deposited twice are counted once. The model fits of Figure 5 solve the linear parameters by least squares and search the spatial scale on a wide grid (λ from -5 to 5, σ up to 10,000 hexes), in place of multi-start Nelder-Mead, and a Nelder-Mead search from each optimum confirms it; the mixed models of Figure 4 are fitted with three optimizers and the fit with the highest REML likelihood is kept; the value recovery integrates with scipy's RK45 in place of Julia's Tsit5. Baseline for Figure 4B is ΔF/F of zero, and the session-wide correction makes the session mean ΔF/F zero, so a late-ITI value below zero is partly set by the correction.

What was found about the dataset. The dandiset holds the 18 mice of the paper with photometry and events, and 9 more mice (JF-VR-FP) with photometry only and no event log or task description. Two sessions are deposited twice under different days (JF-dLight-ramp2-F2 Day3 and Day4, JF-dLight-ramp2-F4 Day5 and Day6). The photometry series of part of the JF-FP sessions state a rate of 120481.9 Hz (or timestamps 8.3 microseconds apart) for a signal sampled at about 120.4 Hz, and in every file the photometry states a start of 0 on a clock that is not the event log's; the only link between the two is the TTL2 channel, described only as "raw photometry channel ttl2", whose rising edges fall on the cues. The files hold no depositor ΔF/F (processing/ has only the task parameters). Sessions are in blocks, 8 long-ITI then 8 short-ITI sessions in JF-FP, and 7 or 8 long, 8 short, then 7 or 8 long in JF-dLight-ramp2, not alternating across days as the paper describes. In the Krausz table the nominal rewards the paper calls µL are reward probabilities in percent, as the Krausz et al. code reads them.
