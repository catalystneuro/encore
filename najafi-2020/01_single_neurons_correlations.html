The traces under processing/Ophys/dFoF_* hold sparse values in 0 to 1 with each neuron's maximum at 1, as the paper's normalised inferred spiking activity (STAR Methods), not Delta F/F as their names say. The time axis of each trial series is stated by starting_time and an attribute named rate that holds the frame interval (0.0324 s), not a rate. The trial table's times are relative to each trial's start, so no inter-trial interval can be computed.

Clock check: the traces are aligned to the choice by the file's own segmentation, and the trial table gives event times within each trial only. The alignment is consistent with the data, not proven: single-neuron choice selectivity rises from the earliest choice-aligned bins to the bin before the choice in every mouse (fig2bd below), and the choice decoders rise from 59 to 70 percent in the earliest bins to 72 to 86 percent at the choice, against shuffles of about 50 percent (03_learning_decoders.ipynb, fig3b).

The imaging dataset has 135 sessions from 4 mice (45, 48, 7 and 35 sessions), with on average 480 neurons per session, about 40 inhibitory, 330 excitatory and 100 unclassified; inhibitory neurons are 11% of the population.

How the analysis differs from the paper's: the SVM regularisation is chosen from a grid with steps of 1 in log10 (the paper 0.2; the check in 02_decoders.ipynb shows differences of at most 0.44 points); excitatory, mixed and 2n-excitatory populations use 5 subsamples (3 in the learning analysis) where the paper uses 50; the time courses use 25 splits per bin and one C per session chosen at the bin before the choice; choices are matched once per session; and side labels are "high-rate" and "low-rate" choices, as the files do not name the side. 16 Hz trials are left out everywhere, as their choice side is unknown; the trial table has no flag for helped trials or the first trials of a session, so those exclusions of the code are not applied; notebook 04 repeats the fig3b time course and the per-bin fig3df weights for mice 1, 2 and 3 with C chosen at every bin and 50 splits.

What was found about the dataset: the session counts, trials per session and neuron counts match the paper; the traces named dFoF_* hold the normalised inferred spiking activity, not Delta F/F, and no fluorescence trace is in the files; the attribute named rate of each trial series holds the frame interval; trial times are relative to each trial, so inter-trial intervals cannot be computed; the lick trace does not say which spout was licked; some files store non-integer pulse rates; and the NWB session identifiers are the CNMF run timestamps, while the recording date is in session_start_time and session_description.
