Reproducing Mountoufaris et al. (2024) from dandiset 001131: activity, selectivity, decoding and persistence¶

Paper. Mountoufaris G, Nair A, Yang B, Kim D-W, Vinograd A, Kim S, Linderman SW, Anderson DJ (2024). A line attractor encoding a persistent internal state requires neuropeptide signaling. Cell 187, 5998-6015. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2024.08.015. The claims file (claims/001131/10.1016-j.cell.2024.08.015.yaml) was written from the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC11490375), with the supplementary figures; this attempt read the same text.

Dandiset. DANDI:001131, version 0.260601.1203. The paper cites the dandiset without a version (Key resources table, "DANDI:001131"). Two versions are published. 0.240826.1647 holds two files, one control and one experimental mouse; 0.260601.1203, the most recent, holds ten, and its files for mice 510 and 500 hold the same data as the two earlier files (checked below). The most recent version is pinned because it holds all the data of the earlier one and more, and so matches the claims file's description better.

What the dandiset holds. One file per male resident: 4 control (scrambled gRNA) and 6 experimental (Oxtr/Avpr1a gRNAs) mice, each with a z-scored trace per unit at 10 Hz and the annotated sniffing, attack and dominance mounting towards a male intruder (two files only sniffing). Two files hold the authors' rSLDS weights of each unit on "latent factor 1". There are no female-intruder sessions, no mark of the pre-intruder baseline, the intruder's entry or removal, and no raw dF/F. The behaviour and photometry cohorts (Figures 2, 3), the slice imaging (Figures 1F, 2E, S7C-L) and the outside RNA-seq atlas (Figure 1E) are not in it. The atlas, of Kim et al. 2019, is read from Mendeley Data (doi:10.17632/ypx3sw2f7c.1) for the fig1e test.

This notebook tests the claims that need no rSLDS fit. The rSLDS claims are in rslds_seeds_0_1.ipynb and rslds_seeds_2_3.ipynb (the four seeds the claims file asks for do not fit in one notebook's time limit), and rslds_checks.ipynb holds the sigma-normalized and held-out checks of seed 0.

The clock of traces and annotations¶

The traces and the behaviour annotations are given on one clock (traces start at 0 s at 10 Hz, interval timestamps in seconds). To check that they line up, a decoder of "attack or sniff" against all other frames is trained on the first half of each session and tested on the second, with the labels shifted in time. If the clocks agree, the decoder does best at a shift near zero.

from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score

shifts = [-30, -10, -5, -2, 0, 2, 5, 10, 30]
rows = []
for m in mice:
    Y, n = m["Y"], len(m["Y"])
    lab = np.zeros(n, bool)
    for b in m["bouts"].values():
        lab |= vmh.frames(b, n)
    # frames from the first to the last annotated behaviour, in four blocks: train on 1 and 3, test on 2 and 4
    a = int(min(b[0, 0] for b in m["bouts"].values()) * m["rate"])
    z = int(max(b[-1, 1] for b in m["bouts"].values()) * m["rate"])
    block = np.arange(z - a) * 4 // (z - a)
    train = np.isin(block, [0, 2])
    r = {"mouse": m["mouse"]}
    for s in shifts:
        y = np.roll(lab, int(s * m["rate"]))[a:z]  # label of frame t is the annotation at t - s
        if y[train].mean() in (0, 1) or y[~train].mean() in (0, 1):
            r[s] = np.nan
            continue
        clf = LogisticRegression(C=0.1, max_iter=2000).fit(Y[a:z][train], y[train])
        r[s] = roc_auc_score(y[~train], clf.decision_function(Y[a:z][~train]))
    rows.append(r)
clock = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("mouse")
print("Test AUC of the behaviour decoder by label shift (s), within the interaction; positive shift = labels delayed:")
print(clock.round(3).to_string())
print("\nshift of the best AUC per mouse:", clock.idxmax(axis=1).to_dict())

"Mixed" is taken literally from the claims file, units with CP at most 0.7 both ways (tuned to neither). About 78% of Esr1+ VMHvl cells express Oxtr mRNA, and about 61% of these also express Avpr1a mRNA, in a single-cell RNA-seq atlas of VMHvl.

Rounds 1 and 2 left this claim blocked (other), as the atlas had not been found; it was tested after round 2, and round 3 agreed with the outcome. Readings run: the cell set (all cells of those clusters; male cells only, as the paper's mice are male; cells of those clusters in which Esr1 itself is detected) and the threshold for "expressed" (CPM above 0, 1, 5 or 10).

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The dandiset gives z-scored traces only and marks neither the pre-intruder baseline nor the intruder's entry, so the baseline is taken as the frames before the first annotated behaviour, and the interaction as starting at that behaviour. One control and one experimental mouse are not in the dandiset, and two mice have no attack annotation, so tests across mice use 3 to 6 mice per group (4 and 6 where every mouse enters, 3 and 5 for attack measures). "Nested" tests are not defined in the paper; both readings are shown. The estimator of the autocorrelation and the summary of "attack probability" are not given; every reading run is shown. Mice 497, 510 and 500, whose clock alignment could not be established, are left out in a sensitivity check of each sigma, decoding and ACHW claim.

What was found about the dataset. One mouse per group is missing; units per group differ from the Methods' counts; the files of mice 510 and 500 describe z-scores as "DF/F" from "GCaMP6m" (the paper names GCaMP8s) and hold only sniffing annotations; the baseline, the intruder's entry and removal and the female-intruder sessions are absent; session start dates are placeholders; the earlier published version's two files duplicate mice 510 and 500.
