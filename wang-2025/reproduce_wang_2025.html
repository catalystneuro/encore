Reproducing Wang et al. (2025) from DANDI:000363¶

Paper. Wang ZA, Kurgyis B, Chen S, Kang B, Chen F, Liu Y, Liu D, Svoboda K, Li N, Druckmann S (2025). Brain-wide analysis reveals movement encoding structured across and within brain areas. Nature Neuroscience. doi:10.1038/s41593-025-02114-x. The claims file (claims/wang-2025.yaml) was written from the version of record (main text, legends, Methods, Extended Data legends) and the Supplementary Information.

Dataset. DANDI:000363, Mesoscale Activity Map Dataset, version 0.230822.0128. This is the version the paper cites (its data reference, doi:10.48324/dandi.000363/0.230822.0128), so it is pinned. The most recent published version, 0.231012.2129 (the one the code's README cites), holds the same 174 sessions in much larger files (65.7 TB in total, against 53.6 GB): 169 of its files add the raw electrophysiology (ElectricalSeries), and the 71 of them renamed +image also add the raw video (28.9 TB). The video, from which the paper's autoencoder embedding and end-to-end networks are computed, is in no other version, and it covers 34 of the 106 sessions the authors used for Figs. 6 and 7. The difference, a check that the units, trials and markers of four sessions are the same in both, and the cost of reading the video are shown in the data check.

What the dataset holds. 174 sessions of 28 mice in the delayed-response task: sorted units with CCF annotations, the trial table, behavioural events (licks, epochs), and three side-camera DeepLabCut markers (jaw, nose, tongue, with a likelihood) per frame. It does not hold the paper's 16-dimensional autoencoder embedding, nor the trained networks.

Outside inputs. From the authors' processed-data deposit, Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.16740783 (named in the claims file), two small members read by HTTP range requests: area2anno.json, the code's grouping of CCF annotation names into areas, and the session names of cal4cat-delay.pickle, the authors' Fig. 6 and 7 file, which name the 106 sessions the paper used. Both are tested by the link claim link-authors-deposit.

The marker timestamps advance by exactly 3.4 ms within a trial, a frame rate of 294 Hz where the Methods say 300 Hz; the video is recorded per trial (one gap per trial). The 3.4 ms step is the stride the Methods give for binning, so the paper worked at the same step. The timestamps are regular to the microsecond, so they were most likely written from a nominal rate, not measured; whether they hold the camera's true timing cannot be told from the files.

In five (SC011 on 24, 25 and 26 February 2019, SC022 on 27 and 28 February 2019) the tongue is visible at 6 to 13% of lick events as deposited and at no shift of the lick times within 1 s does this reach 0.49, while stretching each trial's video timestamps by a factor of 2 from its first frame puts the tongue at 99.9 to 100% of the licks. Their trial videos hold about 940 frames, against a median of 1904 over all sessions. The cell above checks this without the licks: in the same two mice, the trials last as long in the five sessions as in the others (median 4.85 to 4.90 s) and each trial video starts at the trial's start, but holds 934 to 946 frames against 1864 to 1886, so if the camera ran as long per trial as on the mice's other days, the frames are 6.8 ms apart. The video itself cannot settle it: the later version's side-view series of these sessions hold the same frame counts and carry no timing (a starting time with a rate of 1.0, no timestamps). In these five sessions the frames are 6.8 ms apart, not the 3.4 ms the timestamps give, so their markers are on a time base half as long as the trial table's; nothing in the files shows this. In SC026_080719 the tongue is at 60% of lick events: 214 trial videos match the licks and 117 do not, and the session has 539 trial videos for 630 trials, so some videos are likely attached to the wrong trials (the cause is not established). The paper's embedding is computed by a convolutional autoencoder trained on each session's side-view video, and its end-to-end networks are trained on the frames themselves (Methods, Convolutional autoencoder; End-to-end learning). The pinned version has no video. The later version has it in 71 files, from 16 of the 28 mice (66 recorded in 2019); the other 103 files of that version have no +image in their names; 98 of them add the raw electrophysiology, and the largest of these, opened above, holds no ImageSeries. Of the authors' 106 sessions, 34 (from 8 of their 21 mice) have video and 72 have none in any version, so for most of the paper's sessions the input is missing (data_missing). For the 34 that have it, reading the side view alone takes more than an hour a session and tens of hours in all (at the rate printed above), before any of the autoencoders and end-to-end networks is trained on four CPUs, which is also beyond the five-hour run and the sixty-minute notebook limit (too_large).

The authors' processed-data deposit does not supply the input either. Its session_latents.pkl holds, for each of the 106 sessions, 16 images of 120 by 112 pixels (the decoder's components, as printed above), not per-frame values. The go-cue-aligned embeddings the code's README links are in a Google Drive folder, which has no DOI and no fixed version; the protocol (author step 1.4) allows outside data only when it is pinned, so they are not used, by that rule. The deposit does hold the authors' own results: per-neuron explained variance (final/r2_marker_single_split_noshift.pkl, final/r2_embed_single_split_noshift.pkl, combined_methods_r2_new.pickle, the time-shift files r2_embed_cv_timeshift*.pkl) and choice AUCs (auc_analysis.pickle, auc_delay_data_new.pickle). data-scale¶

The analysis covers more than 50,000 neurons recorded in more than a dozen cortical and subcortical structures, simultaneously with high-speed video of orofacial movements.

Criterion: units passing the dataset's quality labels in the sessions with video, before and after the 2 Hz filter; more than 12 distinct structures at the level of the areas the paper names (the criterion's examples: ALM, other isocortex, striatum, thalamus, midbrain, medulla, pons, cerebellum). Structures are counted without nesting, each unit once: its major division of the Allen CCF, from the authors' structure tree in their deposit, with isocortex split into ALM (units on probes targeted to ALM, as the code's get_single_area_inds takes them) and other isocortex. The 16 areas of the code's grouping (area2anno.json) are also shown; they nest (Thalamus holds VM, VAL and MD), so they are not a count of distinct structures. The rate is the dataset's avg_firing_rate (see the notes); the quality labels are read two ways, unit_quality good and classification good.

fig6e-marker-clustering¶

With single markers the mouse-specific structure of uninstructed movements remains; for some mice the nose is more informative than the jaw and for others the jaw more than the nose.

Per session, choice is decoded from the jaw marker alone and from the nose marker alone in the delay epoch (correct trials, 20-fold stratified cross-validation, L2 logistic regression with the penalty chosen by nested cross-validation; decoder reading (a), the five-frame window, and (b), one frame). The session's AUC is the mean over the time bins of the delay epoch (-1.2 to 0 s, every 68 ms) of the AUC at each bin. The paper does not say how the AUC at a bin is formed from the 20 folds; the main reading averages the test-fold AUCs, as the Methods do for the other cross-validated measures (see the helper's docstring), and one AUC over the pooled out-of-fold decision values is a variant. Kept: sessions with jaw or nose AUC of at least 0.65, and mice with two or more such sessions. def clustering(A, rng, thr=0.65, n_null=1000):
    keep = A[(A.jaw >= thr) | (A.nose >= thr)]
    mice = keep.mouse.value_counts()

    if keep.mouse.nunique() < 2:

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper's embedding, its end-to-end networks and its embedding-based choice decoder could not be computed: the cited version has no video, the later version has video for 34 of the 106 sessions the authors used and none for the other 72, and the authors' per-frame embeddings are in no pinned deposit. Only the claims that rest on the markers or on unit counts were tested. Structures for data-scale are the major Allen divisions with isocortex split into ALM and other isocortex, each unit counted once. The marker choice decoder of Fig. 6e is not described in the paper and its code is not public; the claims file's two readings were run (five-frame window and single frame, L2 logistic regression with a nested choice of penalty) and give the same outcome. The session AUC averages the test-fold AUCs (pooled out-of-fold AUCs are a variant with the same outcome), computed every 68 ms in the delay epoch, not every frame. The 2 Hz filter uses the dataset's whole-session avg_firing_rate, not the rate over included trials. The main session set for Fig. 6e is the authors' 106 sessions, from their deposit; the 167 sessions that pass the clock check (166 of them decoded) are a variant.

What was found about the dataset. In five sessions (SC011 and SC022, February 2019) the marker timestamps run twice as fast as the video: the frames are 6.8 ms apart, not 3.4 ms, and only with the stretch do the tongue markers meet the lick events; their trial videos hold half the frames of the same mice's other sessions over trials of the same length. In SC026_080719 117 trial videos do not match the lick events, SC066_041321 has markers for 7 trial videos only, and SC065_050521 lacks marker frames for 140 of its 354 correct trials. Elsewhere the timestamps step by exactly 3.4 ms (294 Hz, where the paper says 300 Hz). Only units with classification good have a CCF annotation, so 92,769 of the 154,948 unit_quality good units have no location. The version the paper cites has no video and no embedding; the later version adds the raw video for 71 sessions of 16 mice, 34 of them among the authors' 106. The dataset holds 174 sessions of 28 mice, of which the authors used 106 sessions of 21 mice for Figs. 6 and 7, a selection the paper does not describe.
