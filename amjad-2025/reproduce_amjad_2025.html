description: This dataset contains chronic neurochemical and electrophysiological recordings from the striatum of two adult female rhesus monkeys (Macaca mulatta; Monkey P and Monkey T), obtained with shaft-assisted micro-invasive probes (s-µIPs) and collected by the Schwerdt Lab at the University of Pittsburgh. It includes the processed data published on Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14826579) together with the raw recordings from which they were derived. Recordings were made while the monkeys performed reward-biased eye movement tasks, in which the animal fixates a central cue, is shown a value cue signaling a large or small reward, and makes a saccade to a left or right target to obtain reward; trials include forced and free-choice conditions. Electrophysiology was acquired with a Neuralynx Digital Lynx SX system through a unity-gain headstage (HS-36, ±1 mV input range) at 30 kHz (Monkey P) or 32 kHz (Monkey T), bandpass filtered 0.1–7500 Hz. FSCV was performed with a triangular waveform (−0.35 to 1.34 V, 400 V/s) applied at 10 Hz, with current sampled at 25 kHz. For Monkey P (session 08/13/2018), the dataset includes raw broadband electrophysiology from striatal site cl3, spike-sorted units from Plexon Offline Sorter, and threshold-detected spikes with 48-sample waveforms. For Monkey T, session 09/13/2024 includes raw FSCV current and applied voltage from site c8dg together with trial-aligned, PCA-extracted dopamine, pH, motion, and oxidation current (at 0.6 V) time series (10 Hz, 60 s per trial); session 09/26/2024 includes raw broadband electrophysiology from sites c3bs and c3a and a bipolar beta-band LFP (c3bs referenced to c3a, 1 kHz, 60 s per trial centered on initial cue onset). Behavioral data are stored as a trials table with trial types and an annotated events table of task events (e.g., fixation onset, value cue onset, reward delivery, and error types) decoded from 8-bit Neuralynx event codes. The Neuralynx and FSCV systems were synchronized using shared trial-start event codes sent to both systems, and all data are expressed relative to the start of the Neuralynx recording.

release notes of this version:
## Changes since version 0.261012.0921

The three session files were re-converted from the lab's source files with the corrected conversion ([catalystneuro/schwerdt-lab-to-nwb](https://github.com/catalystneuro/schwerdt-lab-to-nwb), commits [4d716c5](https://github.com/catalystneuro/schwerdt-lab-to-nwb/commit/4d716c5) and [40752c4](https://github.com/catalystneuro/schwerdt-lab-to-nwb/commit/40752c4)). The conversion fixes problems found by a reproduction attempt ([issue #31](https://github.com/catalystneuro/schwerdt-lab-to-nwb/issues/31), triaged in [issue #32](https://github.com/catalystneuro/schwerdt-lab-to-nwb/issues/32)). The dandiset holds the same sessions under the same file names: Monkey T on 09/13/2024 and 09/26/2024, and Monkey P on 08/13/2018.

**Corrections**

- **One clock for every stream (Monkey T).** The raw ephys, the eye tracking and the spike times started at 0 s, while the trials, events, LFP and FSCV were on the session clock (9831 s later in 09/26/2024). They are now all in seconds from the session start, the time the Neuralynx recording was opened (`recording_opened` in the .ncs header).
- **One clock for every stream (Monkey P).** The spike times were 6800.666 s ahead of the raw signal, which started at 0 s. The Neuralynx records of this session count from the start of the acquisition software, and the raw signal now follows that clock too, so spikes and raw data line up.
- **Time zone.** The lab's timestamps hold New York wall-clock time stored as if it were UTC. The earlier conversion decoded them in the time zone of the converting machine, which placed the trials, events, LFP and FSCV 2 h late. They are now decoded as UTC, whatever the machine's zone.
- **Sampling rate.** The raw ephys and eye tracking are written at the rate measured from the .ncs record timestamps, not the nominal rate, which drifted from the spike times by about 4 ms per hour.
- **Raw FSCV.** The scan times were interpolated against a Neuralynx timestamp, so the whole series sat on a 0.1 s grid from the first trial and could not be matched to the trial-aligned FSCV. Each scan is now placed on the session clock by a line fitted to the lab's pairs of FSCV-clock and Neuralynx times (residual at most one scan, 0.1 s).
- **Events.** Each event was stored once for every overlapping 60 s trial window, about five times (85,665 stored times for 17,686 events in 09/13/2024), and out of order. Each event is now stored once, in time order. Codes missing from the lab's event map are kept as `TTL code <code>`.
- **Plexon spikes.** The Plexon units are read with neo 0.14.6, which reads the PLX timestamps as unsigned; earlier versions placed some spikes 4294.967 s early and dropped others. Spike times are sorted within each unit, and units are named by their Plexon id (`ch<channel>#<unit>`, unit #0 is the unsorted unit). In Monkey P, unit 0 merged the unsorted and the poor-quality spikes; only the units of channel `csc12_100` are now written, and the 7 thresholded spikes that came before the first raw sample are left out.
- **Units and scaling.** The bipolar LFP carried the raw conversion factor (3.05e-8) for values already in volts, which read about 3e7 times too small; it is now written with a conversion of 1. The raw FSCV current is in nanoamperes with a conversion of 1e-9, so it reads as amperes. The thresholded spike waveforms are converted from microvolts to volts.
- **Descriptions.** The LFP is no longer called beta-band: it is broadband, and its preprocessing is not documented in the source. The subject and session descriptions describe one monkey each and say which clock the times use. The raw series gives the .ncs filter settings (high cut 8000 Hz in 2024, 7500 Hz for Monkey P). The files cite the published article as well as the preprint.

**Additions**

- **Monkey P events.** Monkey P's file had no events. The strobed event codes of `csc12.plx` are now stored, by code, since no code map exists for that session.
- **Trial-aligned FSCV** has one row for every trial with data, a `trial_index` column, and the `good` flag as the lab gives it. Its columns say that their units are not known.

**Known gaps**

- The second FSCV site of Figure 3A (c8ds) is not in the dandiset; 09/13/2024 holds c8dg only.
- The trial-aligned FSCV of 09/13/2024 covers the first 734 of its 1468 trials, as in the lab's source file.
- Monkey P's 36 event codes have no names, so its trials and reward conditions cannot be identified.
- The units of the PCA-extracted dopamine, pH and motion components and of the oxidation current are not given in the source, nor the window they are referenced to.
- The trials tables hold 60 s windows around each trial start; trial conditions are only in the events.
- The implant dates are not given, so the days after implant that the paper reports cannot be checked.
- Monkey P's site is named cl3 in the files, where the paper names it c33.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The paper reports no statistics for Figures 3 to 5; the tests are those the claims file fixed (Mann-Whitney and Wilcoxon tests on per-trial values), and its figures show trial averages and single trials. The trial-aligned dopamine is the PCA output stored in the dataset, re-referenced at the value cue as the claims file says; the PCA was not re-run. The beta power is computed here from the stored bipolar LFP with the paper's filter description, as the claims file reads it (the squared Hilbert envelope); it matches the paper's example trace (r = 0.94). The paper's order read literally (squared, then enveloped) matches it less closely (r = 0.91) and gives the same outcomes for fig3b, fig4b and fig4c. For the burst claim the paper does not define a burst, and the claims file's definition was used; with the 0.25 s smoothing its events last about 0.2 s, longer than the paper's bursts of one to a few cycles. The raw FSCV voltammograms average all successful forced large-reward trials, where Figure 3A shows one trial.

What was found about the dataset. The events, the trials tables, the bipolar LFP, the raw EPhys and the raw FSCV current agree on one clock (raw against LFP at 0 to 1 ms; raw FSCV against the trial-aligned table within 0.1 s), and Monkey P's spikes sit on its raw signal. The trial selections of 09/26/2024 match the paper's Zenodo lists. The dataset lacks the second dopamine channel (c8ds), the implantation dates and the list of implanted sensors, and a code map for Monkey P's events, which blocks four claims. The stored acquisition filter is 0.1 to 8000 Hz where the paper and the description say 7500 Hz; the stored FSCV waveform runs from -0.347 V where the paper says -0.4 V. Monkey P's site is named cl3 in the electrodes table and ch33 in the unit names (c33 in the paper). The trial-aligned FSCV series have no units and cover only the first 734 of 1468 trials. The raw FSCV current shows no dopamine redox signature after large-reward cues, while the stored PCA dopamine of the same trials shows the reward effect (p = 0.0226, weak, with most variants not significant). In the direction task the side of the big reward is fixed within blocks of 50 to 93 successful trials, so within a block large against small is the same contrast as left against right. The side effect on beta power holds with the reward fixed (large-reward trials, and small-reward trials, p = 1.48e-10), and the reward effect holds with the side fixed (p = 0.00164 and 9.91e-08); both of these tests compare trials from different blocks. Within blocks the data cannot tell a reward effect from a side effect: of the 12 of 16 blocks that go the expected way, 8 are all the blocks with the big reward on the left, where both effects predict the same sign, and 4 are half of those with the big reward on the right, where they predict opposite signs. The criteria as written are met. The release notes of the pinned version list the gaps that block four claims.
