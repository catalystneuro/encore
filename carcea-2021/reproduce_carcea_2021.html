Reproducing Carcea et al. (2021) from dandiset 000114¶

Paper. Carcea I, Caraballo NL, Marlin BJ, et al. (2021). Oxytocin neurons enable social transmission of maternal behaviour. Nature 596, 553-557. doi:10.1038/s41586-021-03814-7. This attempt used the full text, which is open access (CC BY, PMC8387235), with its Source Data spreadsheets for the reported numbers.

Dandiset. DANDI:000114, version 0.230602.1643. The paper cites the dandiset without a version (it was published in 2021, before this version). This is the only published version. The draft holds the same 30 files; each is 240 bytes or 6 KB larger, and the check below compares the spike times, the events and the shape and rate of the voltage series (the photometry trace itself) of every draft file with the pinned version.

What the dandiset holds. PVN recordings from five virgins co-housed with a dam and litter (V85, V110, V111, V116, V118): raw voltage from a 16-wire bundle, sorted units for four of them in 8 of 14 files, and video annotations. Fibre photometry from six non-co-housed virgins during observation of dam retrievals (ROV40 to ROV49). It does not hold the optical tagging that identifies oxytocin (OT-PVN) units, the behaviour of Figure 1, the unit recordings during observation, or the auditory cortex data of Figure 4.

Outside input. The files do not name their behaviour labels. The paper's code (GitHub ic283/Carcea-et-al.-2021, commit 2db2a75) uses "Mom agressing" for shepherding, "Virgin in rest" for nest entry and "Mom retrieval" for dam retrieval; claims that need these names are tested through the claim label-link. The analysis follows the Methods and, where they leave a choice open, the code's calc_spike_freq_behavior_significance.m; the choices are listed in the claims file and in pvn.py.

The session_start_time does not match the date in the session id in several files: V116 03082018 and 03102018 start in May 2018, V116 03092018 starts on 11 March, and V118 03182018 starts on 23 March, after V118 03192018 and on the same day as V118 03222018. V111's three sessions each hold 31 units whose spike counts are correlated unit by unit (0.93 to 0.97), so they are probably the same clusters on consecutive days. The paper counts units per day (541 units from 17 virgins); here too each session's units are counted separately, and results are also given per session and per mouse.

# Shared clock: firing change at annotation onsets (5 s after minus 5 s before), with the onsets
# shifted in time. If units respond to the onsets and share the annotations' clock, the contrast
# is largest near shift 0.
shifts = np.arange(-120, 121, 2.0)
clock = []
for s in with_units:
    for lab in ("Virgin in rest", "Mom agressing", "Mother in nest"):
        st, _ = pvn.episodes(s["events"], (lab,))
        if len(st) < 4:
            continue
        c = pvn.onset_contrast(s["units"], st, shifts)
        c0 = c[shifts == 0][0]

                      "median_over_shifts": round(np.median(c), 2), "best_shift_s": shifts[np.argmax(c)],
                      "rank_of_0": f"{int(np.sum(c > c0)) + 1}/{len(c)}"})

def split_entries(s, window):
    st, en = pvn.episodes(s["events"], pvn.NEST_ENTRY, pvn.NEST_CAP_S)
    ss, se = pvn.episodes(s["events"], pvn.SHEPHERDING)
    shep_mask = np.array([np.any((x >= ss) & (x <= se + window)) for x in st], bool)
    return st, en, shep_mask

def shepherded_vs_voluntary(window, units_filter=None):
    rows = []
    for s in with_units:
        st, en, m = split_entries(s, window)
        if m.sum() == 0 or (~m).sum() == 0:
            continue
        for i, u in enumerate(s["units"]):
            if units_filter is not None and (s["session"], i) not in units_filter:
                continue
            a = pvn.modulation(u, st[m], en[m], "equal")[0]
            b = pvn.modulation(u, st[~m], en[~m], "equal")[0]
            rows.append({"session": s["session"], "mouse": s["subject"]["subject_id"], "unit": i,

    ok = t.dropna(subset=["shep", "vol"])
    w = stats.wilcoxon(ok.shep, ok.vol) if len(ok) > 1 else None
    return t, ok, w

How the analysis differs from the paper's.

  • The dandiset has units from 4 of the paper's 17 virgins (194 units in 7 sessions with nest entries, against the paper's 147 units), and no OT-PVN identity, so OT-PVN and PVN units are pooled and the false discovery rate is applied over each session's units together.
  • The label names come from the paper's code. The definition of a shepherded entry, the session selection for the rates, and the readings of each reported fraction were fixed in the claims file before the analysis.
  • Where the Methods and the code differ (the 40 s cap, the 10 s baseline), the Methods are followed and the code's choice is shown; for nest entry the cap changes the fraction from 21.6% to 1.0%.
  • Units of V111's three days are counted as separate units, as the paper counts units per day, though their spike counts suggest the same clusters; counting V111 once is shown as a variant.
  • As in the code, real episodes without spikes are left out of a unit's mean while shuffled ones count as 0; counting both as 0 is shown as a variant (nest entry 16.0% against 21.6%).
  • Poisson units with no response give 1.0 to 2.3% activated on the same episodes and test, the Fisher test against zero, as in the paper, does not take this level into account.

What was found about the dandiset.

  • The behaviour labels have no description; "Mom agressing" (shepherding) and "Virgin in rest" (nest entry) can be read only through the paper's code. One file carries both naming schemes.
  • The OT-PVN identity and the optical tagging recordings are not in the dandiset, nor are the behavioural cohorts, the observation-session unit recordings, or the auditory cortex data.
  • Units in V110 and V111 have a median 7.0 to 9.2% of inter-spike intervals under 2 ms, and 79 to 90% of their spikes coincide within 0.1 ms with another unit's, against the Methods' removal of such spikes.
  • Session start times disagree with the dates in the session ids for V116 and V118, and the spikes and events of V116 03102018 run to 13.4 h while its raw voltage lasts 4.46 h.
  • No test here could show that spikes and annotations share a clock. Without V116 03102018, whose spikes and events outlast its raw voltage, nest entry gives 14.9% and the shepherded-entry comparison p = 0.0237.
  • In V111 03032018 the 9 units activated by the dam's single retrieval share a median 71% of their spikes pairwise within 0.1 ms, and the retrieval falls on one of the session's largest synchronous bursts (14,346 population spikes in 5 s against 1,838 on average).
  • Subject descriptions say every animal was co-housed, including the photometry animals of the observation experiment; electrode and fibre locations and the fluorophore are "unknown"; the photometry trace is labelled in watts; the commanded_voltage series is not a command signal.
  • Which photometry animals learned to retrieve is known for 2 of 6.
  • The draft holds the same spike times and events, the same shape and rate of the voltage series, and the same photometry traces as the pinned version in all 30 files; the voltage values were not compared.
