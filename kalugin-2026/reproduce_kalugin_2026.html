Reproducing Kalugin et al. (2026) from dandiset 001074¶

Paper. Kalugin PN, et al. (2026). Simultaneous, real-time tracking of many neuromodulatory signals with multiplexed optical recording of sensors on a micro-endoscope. Neuron. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2026.07.008. The claims file was written from the accepted author manuscript in PubMed Central (PMC13568791) with its supplement (Figures S1 to S6); the version of record was not read.

Dandiset. DANDI:001074, version 0.260928.0807. The paper cites the dandiset without a version. Three versions are published (0.260804.2042, 0.260807.0137, 0.260928.0807); their asset lists hold the same 713 paths with the same blobs and sizes (checked through the API while preparing), so they do not differ in anything the analysis uses. The most recent is pinned, as the protocol asks when the paper cites none.

What the dandiset holds. For each MORSE recording, the CaImAn output of the paper's pipeline: a trace per cell mask (the denoised trace and its residual) and the 3D masks. There are no sensor labels, no well, wash-on, injection or stimulation times, no region or experiment labels, and no deformation fields, raw imaging, photometry, ELISA, histology or choroid plexus traces. Subjects are named by date and probe, with the context (wells, slice, mouse).

Outside input. The well order of the ten revision probes' identification and dose-response dipping, the mixture concentrations, and the baseline and normalization wells of each fit come from the paper's code, github.com/pkalugin/kalugin-et-al-morse at commit fa4526ac02eaa2c5a24c4cfdcaab5c36ed5ae4ed (6_trace_analysis/morse_figs3_rev.ipynb). They name the frame blocks of recordings the dandiset holds and bring in no measurements, so the claims that use them are with_labels and depend on the link claim link-revision-well-order.

Sensor labels (SENSOR IDENTITY of the claims file, the dataset giving none). The Methods' procedure: dF/F0 (Eq. 27), the jump artefact removed, an exponential background fitted before activation, peak normalization (Eq. 28), a Savitzky-Golay derivative over 1 minute (31 frames; the claims file's header gives 30 frames, shown as a variant), HDBSCAN (min_cluster_size 3) on correlation distance, and clusters matched to ligands by correlation with the time course of ligand administration. Reading (a) pools the cells of all ten probes in one clustering; reading (b) adds, for cells (a) leaves unlabelled, the label of their cluster in a clustering of their own probe, as the code's second pass does (morse_figs3_rev cells 62-69). The code then corrects clusters by hand (cells 73-78), which cannot be repeated.

Two details of the cluster matching are assumptions, since the Methods do not give them: the time course of ligand administration is a boxcar over the first minute of each well, and a cluster keeps its ligand only when its mean derivative also peaks in that ligand's well (the peak rule, the author's own, meant to drop weak matches). Both are shown changed below: without the peak rule, and with a boxcar over the whole well. Reading (b) is used in the sections that follow, and every section also shows its result under reading (a).

def ec50_table(L):
    rows, fits = [], {}

        r = L == g[0]
        out = {"sensor": g[0], "ligand": g[1], "cells": int(r.sum()), "paper nM": EXP_EC50[g]}
        for name, PL in [("i", PLm), ("ii", PLc)]:
            c, y = M.group_points(PL[r], g, "code")
            kd, f, n = M.fit_group(c, y)
            fits[(name, g)] = (kd, f)
            out[f"({name}) nM"] = round(kd * 1e9, 1)
            out[f"({name}) F"] = round(f, 2)
        keep = M.noise_keep(M.normalized_dose_trace(DX[r], g))
        c, y = M.group_points(PLm[r][keep], g, "code")

About 70 to 130 active sensor cells per probe can be tracked across 2 to 8 recordings in different contexts.

A matched<k> file holds the masks matched between a reference recording and one other recording of the probe (the k-th pair), and both files of a pair hold the same number of cells. The code supports this reading: the mask matching (5_caiman_fit_qc_matching/8_mask_matching.ipynb cell 8) writes the matched masks of one pair to both the reference and the moving recording, and morse_figs2_2 cell 3 loads ..._matched{k} for the k-th pair of a probe. The same matching notebook (cells 15-17) loads the matched1 file of 20230922-PL28 wells001 and saves it again as matched, which may be why that probe's suffixes do not fit its pairs. The matched count of a pair is that number. Files deposited twice are counted once, and the refit copies (-refit-matched, the same masks) are left out. Per probe, the mean over its pairs.

How the analysis differs from the paper's. The sensor labels are made by the Methods' clustering without the code's hand corrections, so the labelled cells are not the paper's 506. Matching clusters to ligands uses two assumptions (a boxcar over the first minute of each well, and the peak rule); both are shown changed, and every labelled claim is shown under both labelling readings. Of the outcomes, only figs3e-tau-on-under-one-minute depends on the labelling reading. The dose-response normalization follows the code's baseline and peak wells, since the Methods do not fix it, without the code's per-cell background fits over hand-set windows; the code's noise cut is shown as a variant. The time base is the code's 31 frames per minute, since the files' 30 Hz is a placeholder. tau-on uses the Table 1 EC50s to pick the well and convert to concentration.

What was found about the dataset. The files hold traces and masks only: no sensor labels, no well, wash-on, injection or stimulation times, and no region or experiment labels, so the slice and in vivo claims cannot be tested. The timing metadata are placeholders (30 Hz, every file starting at 0 on 2023-01-01), and the indicator is given as OGB-1. The 3D masks are stored scrambled (Fortran-order data in a C-order array). 67 files of an unnamed subject 0 carry no context; 16 of them repeat named files.
